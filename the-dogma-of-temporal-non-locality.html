The Dogma of Temporal Non-Locality

N. Vivian Pope

The Dogma of Temporal Non-Locality A transmitter transmits 10 one-second clock pulses, one pulse every other second. How many seconds long is that train of pulses? Answer: 19 seconds. No problem! Now let there be a receiver of those pulses, moving away from the transmitter at a steady speed. After the receiver (Let’s call him ‘R’) has received from the transmitter (T) the first of those pulses, he moves a distance s further away from T before he receives the next one. And then he moves away another distance s before he receives the next one … and so on, until he receives the last of the ten pulses. The number of pulses R receives tallies exactly with the number T has sent. No doubt about that! But now, we have to take account of the fact that every metre of these distances s that interposes between the receiving of one transmitted pulse and the next is a time in the ratio of units c. So by the time R receives the last of the 10 pulses, 9 time-intervals will have interposed between the first and the last of the train of pulses. In other words, for R, the pulsetrain will have been stretched to the extent that each pulse received is one second plus the intervening distance-time, which means that it is spaced longer (or slower) than the nominal one-second transmitted pulse. It simply is not true, then, that just because a transmitter transmits ten pulses and a receiver receives that same amount, the duration of that sequence of 10 pulses and of the pulses themselves has to be the same for R as for T. “Ah, but despite these appearances,” says someone, “all properly regulated clocks at both T and at R are still ticking at the same rate, no matter how far apart they may go.” But how can we know that? In what possible viewing-frame can that be ascertained? Not in any observational frame known to man, that’s for sure! “Ah, but it is logical to assume it,” says our anti-relativist. But what does “logical” mean here, if it is not used – as it so often is, unfortunately – as just a more emphatic verbal variant of “it seems obvious to me”. Well, okay, it is, strictly speaking, logical to assume that these clocks tick at the same rate wherever they may go. This follows logically from the proposition that the clocks are all identically functioning and properly regulated. (If they aren’t then they are rejected as unreliable timereading instruments). What is not logical is the carried-over, hidden assumption that this same proposition automatically implies that there is a non-local “time of the universe”, or “cosmical present moment”, defined by any “simultaneous” readings, or readings in unison, of these widely separated well regulated clocks. For that assumption to be logical there has to be some clearly stated premise or set of premises from which it follows as a deductive conclusion, otherwise it can be no more than a dogma. So, what, here, might be the hidden premise? What else but that there is some superhuman, super-instrumental viewing-frame in which that “whole phalanx” of clocks can be observed all ticking “at the same rate” throughout. So, what evidence can there possibly be for the existence of that superhuman, superinstrumental observer-frame? By definition, it cannot be evidence provided by any human or instrumental observation. On what, then? The answer is: on no evidence at all. And the word used correctly to describe an assumption doggedly adhered to on the basis of no possible evidence is, of course, dogma. And it is this dogma – not any logical reason, as so often claimed – that opposes, in the name of “Dissidence”, all arguments and demonstrations in support of the relativity or locality, of time. Relativity makes it plain that in all finite observational-instrumental viewing-frames the interposition of the distance-times involved in relative motion destroys any assumption of temporal non-locality, that is, of an underlying ‘level of reality’ in which all time-processes everywhere remain in unison even when far apart. So without any justification in these discussions for the continued dogged denial of temporal relativity, why don’t we just dump that debilitating dogma and let the relativistic revolution continue? This would be away from the presumptuous, ‘God’s-eye-view’ physics of the past towards a more humane, observation-based and communications-based physics of the future. 
