Angular Momentum IAAAD: The Way it Works Viv Pope

Question: How do we reconcile quantum instantaneity and relativistic time-delay? Angular momentum is, by definition, an automatically paired and balanced reciprocal relation between distance-separated masses. It is also quantised in ultimately irreducible measures of Planck's constant i divided by 2n, or h. These fundamental angalar momentum units are pairs of masses traditionally called'electrons' and'protons'. A single pair of these (a protium, atom) is a quantum of angular momentum rh. When the atom loses some amount of angular momentum (nz - n1)h, some other resonant atom somewhere else has to gain precisely that amount. This is not by any agency travelling in between but simply in accordance with the law of conservation of angular momentum, which permits no delay in preserving the overall balance.

In this directly resonant transaction between the two atoms the balance of the overall angular momentum (of the whole system of atoms and so on) shifts immediately from the atom that loses the quantum to the one that gains it, so whatever angular momentum those atoms had in their former relations with other atoms, within the molecules etc. to which they belong, also changes. All those changes, between parlicles in atoms, between atoms in molecules, between molecules in bodies, between bodies in orbits around one another ... and so on, are holistically and immediately accountable in a unified angular momentum vector formula which is described in a paper entitled 'Gravitational Action-at-a-Distance: Conservation of Angular Momentum', by A. D. Osborne & N. V. Pope, to appear soon in Galilean Electrodynamics. (See handouts) When one atom affects another in this way, the effect is immediate and instantaneous, by the conservation law So is any further, knock-on effect that the second atom may have on a third, and that third on a fourth ... and so on. Not so, however, the sequence itself. These 'knocks' are not transitive in the way we think of by analogy with snooker balls or Newton's spheres. An atom obviously cannot both gain and lose a quantum in the same instance. So between the gain and the loss, there has to be a delay, during which the relaying atom, in its temporarily 'pumped-up' state, lies dormant for periods consistent with the statistical half-life of atoms in that condition. During this time, a non-interacting atom, in its dormant, 'dead weight' state, so to speak, remains in balanced angular momentum relations with other atoms within molecules and with other particles in many-body angular momentum systems where the quartum 'pairing' becomes lost among statistical numbers of other, similar linkages. On these macro-levels, the reciprocal angular momentum balances are, of course, those between things like the sun and the planets of our solar system.

But if all these at-a-distance interconnections are instantaneous, then how is it that, according to Special Relativity, nothing can travel faster than the finite speed c? The answer is that c is not a speed but a dimensional constant.
