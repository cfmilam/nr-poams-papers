The Issue: In fundamental theoretical physics, can we sensibly speak of only one point at an instant in time (a 'locality') or can we speak of more than one point at a single instant (a'non-locality')? According to Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity there can be no instantaneous linkages between diffbrent locations because according to that theory nothing can travel faster than the finite and constant speed of light. This implies that there are no physi cal wholes - that is to say, no objects or volumes whose parts exist together at the same instant. This poses a problem, because in systems of angular momentum, bodies have to balance; and balance is a holistic, reciprocal relation in which masses on opposite ends of a moment-arm mutually influence one another. The problem with Special Relativity is that it rules out this well-confirmed balancing relation and therefore violates the law of angular momentum conservation.

Logically, however, there is no need for such violation of the conservation law. It is possible, without contradiction, to think of light-interaction as a 'movie' type action.
