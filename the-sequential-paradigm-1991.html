The Sequential Paradigm

N. Vivian Pope & Anthony D. Osborne — 1991
Presented at the Alternative Natural Philosophy Association (ANPA) Meeting No. 13, Department of History & Philosophy of Science, Cambridge, September 12th–13th, 1991

Abstract

The paper presents the case for a radical shift in the foundational paradigm of physics, from the classical 'mechanistic' paradigm to what is here called the 'Sequential Paradigm'. In this new paradigm, the ultimate elements of physical reality are not material particles interacting by forces but informational events occurring in time-sequences. The basic model is cinematographic: the world is like a movie in which individual frames (quantum events) are instantaneous and their time-ordering produces the appearance of motion, continuity and mechanical causation.

1. The Problem with the Mechanistic Paradigm

The classical mechanistic paradigm, founded on the atomism of Democritus and the mechanics of Galileo and Newton, conceives the ultimate reality as consisting of material particles moving in continuous space and time under the influence of forces. This paradigm has been spectacularly successful in many respects but has led to increasingly severe problems at the fundamental level:

  • The 'EPR paradox' — the irreconcilable conflict between relativistic locality and quantum non-locality.
  • The 'measurement problem' — the insoluble conundrum of how observation can 'collapse' a quantum state.
  • The proliferation of 'fundamental' particles and forces, with no end in sight to the reductive regress.
  • The failure to unify gravity with the other 'forces' of nature.

All these problems, we argue, stem from the same root cause: the assumption that the ultimate elements of physical reality are mechanical. This assumption, however natural it may seem, is not logically obligatory. There is a coherent alternative.

2. The Sequential Alternative

In the Sequential Paradigm, the ultimate elements of physical reality are not particles but events — specifically, quantum events. These events are:

  • Discrete — they come in irreducible units of action $h$ (Planck's constant).
  • Instantaneous — in their own proper-time, each event is a single, timeless occurrence.
  • Sequential — the appearance of motion and time-evolution arises from the time-ordering of these events in the observer's frame.

The analogy is with cinematography. A movie consists of a sequence of still photographs, each of which is, in itself, motionless and timeless. The appearance of motion arises from the rapid succession of these stills. In the same way, the physical world consists of a sequence of quantum events, each of which is instantaneous, and the appearance of continuous motion and mechanical causation arises from their time-ordering.

3. The Role of $c$

In the Sequential Paradigm, the constant $c$ is not the 'speed of light in space' but the rate of quantum sequencing — the 'frame-rate' of the cosmic movie, as it were. It is the dimensional constant converting spatial extension (in metres) into temporal succession (in seconds).

The Pythagorean space-time formula:

$$t_R = \sqrt{t^2 + (s/c)^2}$$

then expresses the composite time $t_R$ of an observation as the resultant of two orthogonal components: the proper-time $t$ of the quantum event (the 'still') and the distance-time $s/c$ of its spatial extension. For light ($t = 0$), we have $t_R = s/c$, the conventional 'light-travel time'.

4. Angular Momentum

In this Sequential Paradigm, the natural mode of free motion is orbital, not rectilinear. Angular momentum, measured in quantum units of $h/2\pi$, is the fundamental conserved quantity. The paired angular momentum relation between an electron and a proton constitutes the basic quantum 'accumulator' — the fundamental unit of information-storage and exchange.

On the macroscopic level, the discrete quantum angular momentum interactions are statistically smoothed into the continuous balancing of astronomical angular momentum systems. This provides the universal reference-frame of Mach's Principle.

5. Conclusion

The Sequential Paradigm offers a coherent alternative to the mechanistic paradigm that has dominated physics since Galileo. It dissolves the EPR paradox, provides a natural interpretation of quantum phenomena, and unifies relativity and quantum theory within a single conceptual framework. The price is the abandonment of the 'speed of light' interpretation of $c$ and the mechanical conception of ultimate reality. The reward is a physics that is both simpler and more comprehensive than anything the mechanistic paradigm has been able to offer. ■
