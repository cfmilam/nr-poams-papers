In response to Van Flandern's new figures.

$$\frac{t_R}{t} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{GM}{rc^2}}} \quad \text{(POAMS time-dilation formula)}$$

Comparison with Van Flandern's Proportional Dilation from Ground to Orbit

V-F says: 'The target value is (a gain of) +38,700 ns/day relative to ground clocks'. There are $86{,}400{,}000{,}000{,}000.0$ nanoseconds in one day, so in one day the satellite clocks register $86{,}400{,}000{,}000{,}000.0$ nanoseconds plus $38{,}700$ nanoseconds, which is $86{,}400{,}000{,}038{,}700$ nanoseconds.

The proportional time-dilation for V-F is therefore $86{,}400{,}000{,}038{,}700$ nanoseconds per $86{,}400{,}000{,}000{,}000.0$ nanoseconds, which is $1.00000000044791666666666666666667$.

Conclusion

The POAMS formula therefore predicts, for the time-dilation of an average GPS satellite orbit, $0.9999999996$ times the empirical time-dilation on the basis of the 'empirical' data provided by V-F. Claims that 'the size of these numbers is wrong by nearly a factor of three' have not been substantiated. In fact, subject to any definite fault in these calculations being shown, the match of the philosophical formula to the practical data is as near perfect as can be reasonably expected.
