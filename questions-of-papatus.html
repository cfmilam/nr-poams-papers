The Questions of Papatus

N. V. (Viv) Pope

A Critique of Modern Physics and Cosmology
(With Apologies to Voltaire)

The eighteenth century French writer, Voltaire1 was so critical of the religious establishment of his time that he was forced to spend many years in exile. One of his most critical essays was 'The Questions of Zapata',2 supposedly based on questions asked by an earlier critic of the establishment, a Spanish professor of theology. In the following parody, questions are directed, not at the present-day Catholic Church but at the current Scientific Establishment whose orthodoxy nowadays, some innovators feel, is no less mystical, stultified and repressive than the Church was at the time of Voltaire.

Wise Masters

1° How ought I to proceed with the object of showing why it was necessary for Isaac Newton to state, as a First Law of Motion, against all evidence to the contrary, that the natural tendency of a body with no forces acting upon it is either to remain stationary in space or else travel in a straight line? Why, students ask me, did no-one point out that nowhere are bodies observed to behave in that way? And when Newton devised another Law, his Law of Universal Gravitation, to explain the obvious fact that nowhere do bodies travel in accordance with his First Law, why was it not pointed out to him that those two laws exactly cancel each other? Why, these common critics say, should we think of one 'force' by which a body seeks to travel in a straight line (the so-called 'centrifugal force') and another whereby the body is 'drawn' towards another body by an opposite, 'centripetal force' called 'gravitational'? Surely, they will say, since those alleged 'forces' are exactly equal and opposite, they can both, logically, be dispensed with.

2° Why, then, people will ask, did no-one point out to Newton what his predecessor, Aristotle, observed, that the natural force-free motion for a body is just cyclic, or orbital. Surely, someone should have seen that all free orbital motion takes place in automatically paired and balanced systems of conserved angular momentum of finite radius, with no need whatsoever for Newton's equally opposed centrifugal and centripetal 'forces'. So why was it not made known that the angular momentum equation3 is, very plainly, sufficient in itself to account for the orbital trajectories of bodies without involving Newton's unnecessary 'Gravitational Law' and its associated inscrutable 'forces'?4 Surely, they will say, someone should have pointed out that only in an ideal special case of a circular orbit of infinite radius (hence of infinite angular momentum, which is impossible) does the orbital trajectory of a freely moving body lie in Newton's straight line – which, with the velocity v then being zero, is the same as no motion at all? My learned masters, in my teachings to my students of Physics I shall need some help with these things.

3° In the same way, how shall I explain why no-one challenged Einstein, when he said (in the second axiom of his Special Theory of Relativity) that the light by which we see things travels invisibly at a constant and finite speed in a vacuum? How shall I respond to those who ask me why no-one objected that in a vacuum, there is literally nothing to which a motion can be referred, constant or otherwise? And if I say to them, as some professors have advised me, that what Einstein meant was that the speed of light is constant relative to all instruments of reference in a vacuum, then common people will ask me why Einstein didn't say that instead of talking confusingly about a speed in or relative to the 'vacuum'.

4° Moreover, since Einstein's theory implies that no physical body or influence can ever reach the 'speed of light', that this 'speed' is the unreachable limit, or asymptote, of all real speeds, how shall I convince these common critics that c can sensibly be regarded as a 'speed'. They will point out to me that people like Herman Bondi have demonstrated that in Relativity, c is, in effect, no more than a constant, having the dimensions of a speed but none of a true speed's definitional characteristics – that, for instance, it does not conform to the law of the addition and subtraction of velocities. And what shall I say to ordinary intelligent thinkers who tell me that it is a logical 'howler' to infer that because a speed has the dimensions of distance divided by time, any measure of distance divided by time is a speed? They will tell me that this is like saying that because all ants are insects, all insects are ants. Please advise me, my revered mentors, as to how I might avoid ridicule on that score.

5° And how shall I answer these common folk when they ask me what possessed Einstein to think of the irreducible quantum of light-energy as a space-travelling elementary particle, which he called the 'photon'? They will complain to me that for anything to be sensibly regarded as a particle, it has to have some substance, that is, some mass. But according to Einstein's own Theory, for any particle, properly so called, at the 'speed of light' its mass becomes infinite. So if the 'photon' were truly a particle, then at the 'speed of light' its mass would have to be equal to that of the universe itself. I must say, my noble teachers, that I find it difficult to deal with these rude objections on that score. Further, when you tell me to defend the ingenious concept of the 'photon' against these vulgar objectors by pointing out that in its own rest-frame the mass of the photon is zero, how shall I answer their protest that in the Theory, light, hence the 'photon', has the speed c in all reference-frames bar none (that there is no reference-frame whatsoever in which the 'photon's' speed is zero)? How shall I convince these disagreeable people that what I am telling them is not, as they see it, just pure nonsense?

6° Can you advise me, my dear masters, how I can convince these offensive people that the whole universe, in all its dimensions of space, time and mass, all its properties and qualities known to us, not only in physics and astronomy, but also in biology, psychology, sociology, art, literature, ethics … and so on, were once all locked-up, some 13 billion years or so ago, within a volume no larger than the size of a pea? They are bound to ask me: without there being any space prior to that primordial pea, what can any talk of its 'size' or 'volume' possibly mean in that situation? And without there being any space for it to explode into, how can that 'primeval atom' possibly 'explode'? And into what, nowadays, is it still expanding? Is that expansion simultaneous all over? But how can that be when Relativity says there is no such thing as overall simultaneity, especially among things that are travelling so vigorously relatively to one another as those galaxies? And – I am bound to be asked – what is it, among these relativistically different-aged remnants of the alleged 'Big Bang', that still constitutes a 'universe'? How can so many galaxies, with so many different endurance-rates form such a unity, and at what common instant?

7° Moreover, what evidence shall I show these people for that miraculous pristine explosion? I have been taught that the observational evidence is that all the distant galaxies are receding from one another at speeds that are proportional to their distance. But whoever actually observes these galaxies receding? By what means do we measure those speeds? We are told that the evidence is plain, that the spectrum of a distant galaxy displays a uniform shift towards the red in a manner analogous to the way in which the sound emitted by a police siren is lowered in pitch as the vehicle speeds away from us. But, again, how shall I convince the commonsense thinkers that because the pitch of sound or of light is lowered by the speed of recession of its source (known as the Doppler effect), any instance of witnessing the lowering (reddening) of the pitch of the light from a distant galaxy is the same as witnessing that galaxy receding. They will surely ask me, for what possible reason should we choose to think of those galaxies as receding, with such an utterly bizarre inference as that it all started with a primordial 'Big Bang'. Why choose that altogether weird explanation when there is plainly no logical necessity for doing so? They will point to the fact that there are other possible explanations of the galactic redshift. For example, someone is bound to point out that relativistic time-dilation causes the same sort of spectral reddening, hence that motions of the galaxies that cause relativistic reddening need not be uniformly recessional. An increase in purely random motions of those sources with distance would have the same effect, but without the reduction to absurdity implied by uniform galactic recession.5 Please tell me, then, my good masters, how I can make palatable the story of the 'Big Bang' to these stubbornly commonsensical, untutored people?

Can you also tell me how I can convince these same rude folk that they should be as enamoured as we, the intelligentsia, are of the idea that the universe is a space-time continuum, or a continuous underlying wave-function? 'A continuum of what?' they will ask – or, even more crudely, 'What is it that is supposed to be waving?'

A wave, they will say, is a time-sequence of discrete, locally confined movements of something that is not a wave. In a surface water-wave, for instance, the water molecules describe relatively small circles around a fixed point, without any one of them travelling in the way the wave travels.

So in space, that is, the vacuum or void, what shall I say is moving in the manner analogous to those water molecules, or in the bumpety-bump manner of a pressure wave, like sound in a liquid or solid medium? They are bound to say to me, 'For there to be a wave, there has to be a wave medium.

And a wave,' they will assert, 'cannot be its own medium.' So, they will ask me: 'In a vacuum, what is there to wave?' Shall I inform them as to what you good teachers taught me, that there is no substance to those waves, that they are purely abstract mathematical wave-functions, or waves of pure probability?

Shall I tell them that these 'waves' are not real waves in any ordinary sense but that the opposite is the case, that reality, as known ordinarily, is an observational 'collapsing' of that abstract wave-function? And what will they make of that? If it is a function of mathematical probabilities, why is it not just called just that, a 'probability function', instead of a 'wave function', with all that that unnecessarily entails?

How shall I deal with the derision that my answers are bound to provoke among these ordinary critical folk? 'Is this what we are funding you for?' they will say. How shall I reply to that? How shall I convince them that, as a teacher of Modern Theoretical Physics and Cosmology, I am giving true value for their money?

Shall I appear before them in my cap and gown and reassure them that it is alright for them not to understand these things so long as I do? Shall I tell them to set their minds at rest because Nature speaks vicariously to them through me, that they can rest assured that I am their trustworthy mediator and advocate?

But how can I do that when, really, I am no more confident of what Nature is telling us than they are? And how shall I persuade them of the usefulness of this academic knowledge that I am passing on to them, of the advantages in sending their children to study this sort of knowledge at university?

Now I ask you all of this in view of the disturbing fact that, as you well know, so many departments teaching Modern Physics and Cosmology are currently being closed down in favour of more popular and therefore more cost-effective subjects such as Aromatherapy, Beatles History and Flower-Arranging? I am wondering, therefore, whether I should persist in my trained profession of casting these pearls of academic wisdom before an unappreciative public or else take voluntary redundancy and accept the remunerative offer of becoming a professional bingo caller.

I await the honour of your reply

VIVIANUS PAPATUS
