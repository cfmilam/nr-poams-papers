Now our textbooks tell us that an object's weight is the product of its mass m and the local gravitational acceleration, g. That is to say, weight (symbol W) in units of force (newtons) is $W = mg$, where $g = GM/r^2$ and m is the mass of the body in question.

In the London Sunday Telegraph of 21 Sept. 1997, reporter Robert Matthews writes; A team of Japanese scientists have spun up a gyroscope to 18000 rpm and dropped it through a distance of 63 inches in vacuo. The time taken to fall this distance was 1/25000 sec. longer than when the gyroscope was not spinning, corresponding to a weight reduction of 1 part in 7000.

How does POAMS account for this The only answer that is consistent with our POAMS synthesis, is that it has to be a misinterpretation of the fact of weight-change with spin. For instance, assume, just for the sake of argument, that the change in pressure of water with depth might be discovered by a highly intelligent abyssal fish.

That fish might assume that the datum level of that pressure change was the level it was at when it made the discovery. In that case, it might report, not that water pressure increases with depth but that it decreases with height. It is only when it discovers that those decreases in pressure with height terminate, high above, at sea-level, which it never knew about, that it realises its mistake in thinking that the reference-zero for water-pressure was at the depth it had assumed, and that there were pressure increases with depths far below that assumed reference level.

In that same way, those who report instances of ‘levitation’ in their spin experiments might well be mistaken in assuming that ordinary ground-level is the datum zero for changes in the measure of g, and that they are unaware that the limit of g is not at ground-level but in space where the weight of the body reduces to nothing.
