The Logical Redundancy of Einstein's Second Postulate

N. Vivian Pope & Anthony D. Osborne — International Journal of Mathematical Education in Science and Technology, Vol. 18, No. 2 (1987), pp. 191–198

A New Approach to Special Relativity

Abstract

All the standard consequences of Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity can be deduced more simply without Einstein's Second Postulate regarding the alleged 'constant speed of light in vacuo'.

1. Introduction

It was shown by Olaus Rømer in 1676 that the distance of any object in an observer's spherical frame of reference is a time in the ratio $c$ of conventional units (metres to seconds). This has since been generalized to express the distances of all events, at any distance in an observer's frame, as time-equivalents in the same ratio, $c$. In other words, a general result in observational physics is that all distances in space are times in the ratio of units $c$.

Einstein, in his 1905 Special Theory of Relativity (STR), took this as meaning that 'light travels in vacuo at a constant speed $c$'. However, Einstein's teacher, Ernst Mach, opposed this, essentially because physical objects are not separated from observation, so that there is no 'vacuum' in which light can be supposed to 'travel'. More formally, this objection of Mach's rests on the logical principle that in order to call something a 'speed' it has to obey the law of composition of velocities. However, since $c$, as established by Michelson and Morley, does not obey this law (being constant in all relatively-moving frames of reference), $c$ cannot logically be called a 'speed'. Mach preferred to call $c$ an 'observational constant', which is dimensionally the same as a speed, that is, distance divided by time.

Following Mach's original thinking in this direction, it can be shown that Einstein's Second Postulate, which interprets $c$ as 'the speed of light in vacuo', is logically redundant, in the sense that all the standard results of STR can be derived more simply without it, solely from the first Postulate, the Principle of Relativity, together with the empirical fact that $c$ is a constant relating observational distance and time units.

2. The Basic Formula

Let $O$ be any observer and let $P$ be a body at a distance $s$ metres from $O$ in the radial space-time projection, of the observer's frame of reference. According to the general result identified in §1, the distance $s$ of $P$, in metres, in $O$'s frame of reference, is also a time, $s/c$, in seconds, in the same frame. This is true for all observations of $P$ at any distance $s$.

The relative time, $t_R$, in which $O$ observes $P$ to move freely over a distance $s$ in the radial dimension of $O$'s frame, has therefore two components: the proper-time $t$ (being the time of the motion as registered by $P$'s own clocks) and the distance-time $s/c$. The resultant of these two co-exclusive (geometrically orthogonal) time-measures is then, by the Pythagorean theorem:

$$t_R = \sqrt{t^2 + (s/c)^2} \tag{1}$$

It may now be seen that all the standard results of STR follow from this simple formula, more directly than from the Second Postulate of Einstein, as follows.

3. Time-Dilation

Since $v = s/t_R$ (where $v$ is the velocity of $P$ relative to $O$), we may replace $s$ in (1) by $vt_R$, whence:

$$t_R = \sqrt{t^2 + (vt_R/c)^2}$$

which simplifies to give the standard Einsteinian time-dilation formula:

$$t_R = \frac{t}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} \tag{2}$$

4. Length-Contraction

When a body $P$ is moving relative to an observer $O$ at relative velocity $v$, the length $L$ of $P$ in the direction of motion, as observed by $O$, is related to the rest-length $L_0$ (measured when $P$ is at rest relative to $O$) by the standard Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction formula:

$$L = L_0\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2} \tag{3}$$

This follows directly from (2) together with the definition of relative velocity.

5. Mass Increase

From (2), the mass $m$ of a body moving at velocity $v$ relative to an observer is related to its rest-mass $m_0$ by:

$$m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} \tag{4}$$

6. Mass-Energy Equivalence

The kinetic energy of a body of rest-mass $m_0$ moving at velocity $v$ is:

$$K = mc^2 - m_0c^2 = m_0c^2\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} - 1\right) \tag{5}$$

For $v \ll c$, this reduces to the Newtonian formula $K = \frac{1}{2}m_0v^2$. For $v = c$ (the case of light), the total energy is $E = mc^2$, which is Einstein's famous mass-energy equivalence formula.

7. The Doppler Effect

The relativistic Doppler formula for the observed frequency $\nu$ of a source receding at velocity $v$ is:

$$\nu = \nu_0\sqrt{\frac{1 - v/c}{1 + v/c}} \tag{6}$$

where $\nu_0$ is the rest-frequency. This follows directly from the time-dilation formula (2) together with the geometrical considerations of approach and recession.

8. Conclusion

All the above standard results of STR have been derived from the single Pythagorean formula (1), without any reference to Einstein's Second Postulate. The constant $c$ appears throughout, not as the 'speed of light in vacuo' but simply as the observational conversion-factor between metres and seconds, as first empirically established by Rømer. This establishes the logical redundancy of Einstein's Second Postulate and, with it, the redundancy of the entire theoretical superstructure of 'light travelling in vacuo' on which that Postulate depends. ■
