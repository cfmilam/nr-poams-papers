Pope's Response to Bastin and Kilmister

On page 3, Ted Bastin reports 'An active and extremely voluminous correspondence' involving himself, Clive Kilmister and Viv Pope, plus others at what Bastin refers to as Pope's 'Blue Anchor redoubt'.

The 'Blue Anchor' is a pub on the South Wales coast, where informal philosophical discussions among a small group of non-academics take place on most Tuesday nights. These discussions focus on the central ANPA issue of 'projection space' and the latest results of the Thomas Young two-slit experiment, which are described in Kilmister's review article on page 5 and in the above Times extract.

As Bastin reports, it all started from Pope's attempt to winnow-out the basic ANPA philosophy and present it 'before the bar of commonsense' at the Blue Anchor. Central to the prevailing ANPA way of thinking, of course, is the conception of space, time and matter as projected, in some manner to be agreed, out of informational constructs of otherwise discrete mathematical and/or physical elements. The fact that the philosophical justification for this constructivist approach to theoretical physics and cosmology cannot simply be taken for granted makes it clear why it became the focus of philosophical enquiry in the correspondence Bastin mentions.

'Projection space', it was agreed, is basically a quantum discretum (Bastin's term), as opposed to the classical continuum, and the fundamental philosophical issue is that of how these discrete and dissociated quantum elements are associated to form the world as we know it. The elements out of which this space is projected are, for Pope, Planck's quanta h - although he now embodies in his method (see following) the combinatorialists' contention that 'measurement is counting' and also Noyes' principle that the paradigm case of this is 'counting things in bubble chambers'. But who does the counting? The dissociated elements of a discretum cannot count themselves; and if it is only we who count them, while they are dissociate in nature, then the disastrous consequence of that is subjective idealism. Is there, then, a God who does the counting - the associating of the dissociated or the continuising of the discontinuous? This, of course, takes us into deep and dark domains where no refuge can be sought, as in classical physics, from worries about philosophy, psychology and even outright theology. It is this disconcerting blurring, in modern physics, of the conventional academic demarcation lines in contemplating those 'basic troubles' Kilmister introduced concerning the two-slit experiment and so on, which makes ANPA such an adventure. It is these deeper issues which the 'voluminous correspondence' is all about.

Now perhaps at this juncture we leave those physicists in the Association who, as Bastin says, want no 'philosophical palaver' (although one hopes that they will stay and appreciate its relevance). This 'palaver' concerns Bastin's point about Pope's 'linguistic' approach to these problems, which would have seemed irrelevant to classical physics. The language Pope takes as his standard of reference for linguistic analysis is, as he describes it, that taproot of all commonsense language which was codified by Aristotle. Its essence is what the Greeks called logos, which we now call 'logic and which is international in the same way that mathematics is international. Indeed, logic and mathematics are inseparable aspects of the Greek logos, which are common not only to human languages (including art and music) but also to nature itself. This, of course, is why language and mathematics are inextricably linked in the business of science in general and physics in particular.

The point Pope was emphasising, therefore, was that insofar as we and nature 'speak the same language', the true aim of empiricists should be simply to 'listen to nature' rather than follow the usual academic course of proposing more and more competing theories to add to the conceptual clamour. This, he argues, is what commonsense, or 'ordinary language' philosophers like Moore sought to do with 'linguistic analysis'; that is, to analyse out of evolving common language the sorts of

THE TWO ASPECTS of SPECIAL RELATIVITY

In 1926, in an article in Nature, Gilbert Lewis showed something which Einstein never fully realised, which is that there are two separate aspects to his Special Theory of Relativity. In the one, there is a finite, unreachable upper speed-limit to all mechanical motion. This is c, which Einstein called the 'speed of light'. In the other, the limit of those same motions is infinity, as in pre-Einsteinian mechanics.

It all depends which clock you use to time the distance, your own clock or the clock of the object. If you use your own clock you get the relativistic limit c in Figure 1; if you use the object's clock there is no limit, as in Figure 2. So a light-signal which travels at the finite speed c in the one way of measuring has an infinite speed in the other.

Had Einstein fully realised this, his tussle with Bohr over the so-called EPR paradox need never have happened. For, why would quantum theorists have had to postulate 'spooky superluminal action-at-a-distance' to satisfy Newton's third law of instantaneously balanced or reciprocal action and reaction when light, in its own time and in its own right, is absolutely instantaneous?

Pope replies, then, to Bastin, that his 'Popish' language is by no means pontifical in the manner of Linguistic philosophy. He argues, however, that the disrepute into which Linguistic Philosophy fell does not invalidate the point those philosophers should have been making, which is that there is no way in which the logical and mathematical systems of science can be made independent of language in general. In other words, Pope says, a truly common or commonsense language, if we do not 'set it in aspic' in the way the Linguistic philosophers did – and as some scientists now tend to do with their terminology – is one in which that commonsense language and the language of the scientists evolve together, with the common ideal of becoming a logically coherent whole – or, at least, as near as can be managed.

Scientific language, Pope argues, is thus as subject to linguistic analysis as any other. Such analysis reveals that some areas of evolving science, particularly modern physics, have become sources of general infection by the disease-organism Wittgenstein identified as the Scheinproblem. As prime examples of this, Pope cites those conceptual conundrums that have been generated by the latest results of the two-slit experiment as described in the Times article and in Kilmister's review.

Needless to say, Pope's updated 'linguistic analysis' approach to conceptual problems of physics differs radically from that of most theoretical physicists, which is simply to generate more and more theories to 'fill the gaps' in the prevailing paradigm – postulating, for instance hidden variables such as wave functions, superluminal pilot-waves, etc., acting ahead of photons and guiding them 'tele-

pathically' towards their assignations in the two-slit experiment. But, Pope asks, why postulate these 'spooky superluminal influences' acting ahead of particles to explain the results of the two-slit experiment when quantum light-interactions are, themselves, proper-time-instantaneous? For instance, in 1926, in Nature, Gilbert Lewis showed that there are two different but concurrent aspects to light. In the one, light travels at Einstein's finite and limiting speed c, so that no causal influence (so Einstein supposed) can reach anywhere in a time less than the distance divided by c. In the other, light is distanceless and timeless (instantaneous),

The seventy year long hang-up in accepting this indisputable demonstration of the immediacy of light-quanta stems from the conceptual difficulty of putting these two situations logically together in the context of the present concept-constellation, as described in the 'To Hell With Education' poem (see page 7). As Basil Hiley once commented, it seems paradoxical to think of things being both together and apart in the same instance.

However, as Pope explains, logically there is no paradox. For instance, if you express the velocities of bodies as the distance they travel relatively to you in the time measured by your observatory clock, then you get the sort of distribution of velocities shown in Figure 1, on page 10, which is a graphic depiction of the standard Einsteinian formula for relative velocity and consequent time-dilation. However, if you express those same motions as the same distances travelled in the time measured by the clocks of the travelling bodies (proper times) you obtain the classical or Newtonian sort of distribution shown in Figure 2, which is a plot of the distance against relativistic proper time. These diagrams clearly show how the limit of all velocities, for the same motions, is both finite (Fig.1) and infinite (Fig. 2). As Pope explained to Hiley, there is no paradox involved in this dual aspect of motion because the two diagrams are no more than different-

dimensional aspects of a single space-time graph. The details of this can be read in a paper by N. V. Pope and A. D. Osborne (1987). 'A New Approach to Special Relativity'. Int. J. Math. Ed. Sci. Tech. 18, 2, 191. This shows how both these Einsteinian and Newtonian aspects of motion are deducible from the very simple Pythagorean formula

$$t_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+(s / c)^{2}=t_{\mathrm{R}}^{2}$$

where s is the observationally projected distance shown in Figs. 1 and 2, tp the time registered by the travelling body, shown in Fig. 2 and tR is the observer time shown in Fig. 1. Mathematicians will recognise this formula as the standard formula for a rectangular cone, so that when the three variables are plotted against one another the normal flat graph of motion becomes a conic surface of which Fig 1. is the side-elevation and Fig. 2 the end-elevation.

[For the mathematicians, the distance (distance-time s/c) is the conjugate axis and the time is the transverse axis of the hyperbola in Fig.1 which appears vertical in Fig.2. Since the units along both axes are fixed and equivalent (i.e., metre/c = 3.3 nanoseconds) the hyperbola is equilateral (the cone is rectangular), so that its limit, or asymptote, is at 45° to the horizontal in Fig.1 and 90° in Fig. 2. Note that c here is Bondi's distance-time conversion factor, not Einstein's 'velocity of light'.]

Is this rendering of Pope's, then, as Bastin says, 'right outside relativity as usually understood'? Well, if Bastin is correct, then, surely, that is precisely what any new theory should be, a new structure of thought which includes the old. For instance, substituting v for s/c in the Pythagorean formula produces Einstein's time-dilation equation in just a few lines of algebra, whilst expressing the velocities as s/tp rather than s/tR produces the classical Newtonian distribution whose limit is infinity, showing that both Newton and Einstein derive from Pythagoras applied to distance-time and time.

These diagrams should make it clear why Pope 'flies his kite' about the photon. From his linguistic analysis point of view, to call an instantaneous atom-interconnecting quantum jump a 'photon' - which the textbooks define as a light-particle travelling at the speed c - is a literally devastating example of Wittgensteinian 'misuse of language'. Pope regards this as the central Scheinproblem which prevents us from reaching what would otherwise be a plain commonsense solution to the all-attested 'basic trouble in physics' concerning those 'strange results' of the latest versions of the two-slit experiment.

Pope's 'plain commonsense solution' to this problem of how, as the Times correspondent says, 'photons can tell in advance, before they even reach it,' that there are things waiting for them en route from source to screen, is simply to see how completely inappropriate that sort of 'clairvoyance' language is when referring to instantaneous light-quanta. However, the usual hang-up in recognising this, apart from having radically to revise our already familiar 'photon' language, is the aforementioned problem of trying to conceive how a three-dimensional space can be projected out of dissociated distanceless and instantaneous quantum-elements. This introduces the other part of the 'voluminous correspondence', about projection space. Pope's empiricist's solution was that in space as we perceive it, distances as depicted along the vertical axes in figures 1 and 2 are relativistically 'video-projected' by the observer out of informational patterns of Manthey's co-occurring quanta (which Pope renames phota), in the way that a hologram is projected by the observer out of informational patterns of visual elements on a flat card. In a Blue Anchor circular on 'Projection Space' (Nov 11th/95), Pope explains this as follows.

... the intensity of a standard image (that of a unit candle, say) systematically increases or decreases in such a way as enables the observer to project from what he sees, a measure called the distance of an object. That distance s is a function of the change in observed intensity of the standard light-source, by the equation.

$$s=\left(I_{\mathrm{ST}} / I\right)^{1 / 2} s_{\mathrm{ST}} \quad(1)$$

where IST is some standard intensity (e.g., one candela) at a standard distance sST

and 1 is the measured or actually observed intensity.

This phenomenon of distance involves, of course, other bits of information such as the angle subtended by the object at the point of observation (perspective). These various bits of information form the three-dimensional sphere of perception of that particular observer at that particular moment, which conforms to what Bastin calls "projection-space". Further sensory information, such as those changes in perspective we call parallax, also the information we get from reaching for things, viewing them in mirrors, communicating with others who compare and contrast their own points of view, and so on, further enables the observer to project a de-centralised, Cartesian system of rectangular, frame-type dimensions called (inter-communicational) blocspace.

Pope goes on to explain that sequences of these projection-spaces in the time of the observer form the fourth, kinematical dimension of ordinary physical phenomena. Manthey, who concurs with this 'video' model of projection-space, calls this the dimension of coexclusion. All physical processes, such as signals and motions of bodies up to the speed-limit c shown in Fig.1 are thus, Pope says, kinematical sequences of co-excluded sets of co-occurring quanta whose proper 'speed' of interaction is the instantaneity shown in Fig.2.

Light quanta (photo), then, are instantaneous (distanceless), and sequences of them (signals) are propagated in projection-space at the speed c. There is no more of a paradox in that, says Pope, than in the length, breadth, depth and motion we see on a TV screen being projected by the viewer out of patterns and sequences of dimensionless dots. This is his answer to Bastin's point that he does not provide the details as to how his version of projection space is projected. Of course, the combinatorialists of ANPA, with their contention that, as Bastin says 'measurement is counting', might require something more mathematically structured than mere intensities of quantum co-occurrences.

However, with regard to Bastin's point about ANPA and Noyes' principle that 'the paradigm case of counting is counting things in bubble chambers', Pope points out that his photo do not just magically appear in perception. They have to originate in something other than themselves, and that is in atoms. This atomic and sub-atomic substructure that Noyes and his colleagues are concerned with at SLAC and at ANPA is nevertheless, Pope says, a discretum not a continuum. He explains this in a recent paper entitled 'Instantaneous Gravitational and Inertial Action-at-a-Distance' (N.V. Pope and A.D. Osborne, Physics Essays 8, 3, Sept. 1995, pp 384-397. Copies on request). He stresses, however, that he does not consider himself competent to deal with the mathematical details of the Combinatorial aspect of this constructivist methodology as discussed among the principals of ANPA. He insists that his contribution to ANPA, as a philosopher rather than a physicist, is to deal with issues such as that of the role of cognition in associating the otherwise unspeakably dissociated elements of the quantum/combinatorial discretum.

To return to the associated issue of the two-slit experiment, the fact that light-quanta are intrinsically instantaneous is an obvious answer to the problem of their imputed 'clairvoyance'. However, Kilmister stressed the difficulties of explaining, in Pope's instantaneous 'photom' terms, the sort of interference he, Kilmister, describes in this Newsletter, involving conveyance through the slits of 'slower-than-c' particles like electrons and sodium atoms. After much pondering and after making some abortive suggestions about these particles being accompanied by a Cherenkov-type radiation, Pope wrote:

I have just realised that all this worry about "pilot radiation", in terms of photo "scouting ahead of the particles" and so on, in 2-slit types of experiments, is redundant. In the new sequential paradigm (See Proc. ANPA 13, p.55) the apparatus, like everything else, is a neo-Newtonian discretum - that is to say, a statistical proper-time-instantaneous whole, of the sort Mach envisaged for "gravitation" but in which the "gravitational continuum" is replaced by a gravitational discretum consisting of gravitational quanta, or grávita, as I call them.

The reason, then, why there is no need of pilot radiation is that as a neo-Newtonian [i.e., statistical] proper-time-instantaneous whole, the apparatus is in itself a "pilot" for any radiation, photumnic or particulate, that may be beamed in it from source to sink.

In previous correspondence Pope had pointed out that as a physical form in the world conceived as a quantum discretum, the two-slit apparatus is relativistically projected, like any other form, from relative intensities in the manner described. As designed and manufactured, it is therefore a set of built-in geometrical potentials for subsequent atomic interactions between a source and sink. Atoms interact only when they are in resonance with one another, and when they interact, a whole quantum of pure energy $h\nu$ is instantly transferred – there being no distance, as we observationally video-project it, at that individual quantum level. Those lengths defining the dimensions of the apparatus, we must remember, are projected by the observer, not out of single, isolated quantum-interactions between atoms but out of statistical groups or ensembles of those interactions in the manner already explained. Since those macroscopically projected lengths $s$ are also times, in the ratio of units $c$, the whole group-projected distance-time $s/c$ between the source and screen, as between any part of that apparatus and any other, has two distinct yet complementary characteristics. One is that it has the instantly resonating, or contiguous features of the individual atoms. The other is that it also has, in its formal or the macroscopic, observer-projected aspect, the features of the $c$-speed-propagated, quantum-sequential 'wave' of classical and quantum wave-dynamics – except, of course, that there is now no 'Einstein separation' involved at the quantum level nor any question of 'spooky superluminal' interconnections between source and sink. The apparatus, like every other object in the world is a form in the quantum discretum; and, as such, it is a neo-Newtonian (quantum-

statistical) whole. Thus, while at the ordinary level of phenomena we see three-dimensionally extended things (qua forms) moving and interacting over distance and so on like the action on a TV screen, at the microphysical level every quantum element out of which those forms are projected is a single, irreducible event like a momentarily lighted screen pixel. That is to say, every quantum interaction between forms (as opposed to the kinematical $c$-speed-propagated signal by which they causally influence one another in the projection-space they define) is a jolt – a Lewisian quantum touching – affecting the formal 'producer' and end-user as a single contiguous event satisfying Newton's third law of instantaneous reciprocal interaction. In answer to the criticism that these quantum contacts cannot be both instantaneous and time-retarded in the same instance, Pope says that the instances are not the same. The case in which the reciprocal quantum-interaction is instantaneous is one instance and the case in which the contact is time-retarded (i.e., between forms as opposed to individual atoms) involves sequences of those instances. Thus, while the interactions between forms as such is kinematically time-retarded and statistical (entropic) the individual quantum-interactions out of which these forms are informationally projected are instantaneous. The statistical continuity of these sporadic, instantaneous contacts between their component atoms is, Pope says, what keeps the physical forms together in projection space, defining their trajectories and maintaining their balance with respect to one another. This, he says, is the so-called action-at-a-distance which quantum physics requires. He sees no more need to think of these elements of action-at-a-distance as bound by the geometry and kinematics of projection space than to think of the elements of a computer's random access memory (RAM) as bound by the geometry of what appears on the computer screen.

How, then, specifically, does this help us to understand the latest results of the two-slit experiment? First, to the question of how this periodic, wave-like group response applies to those one-at-a-time 'photons' which, in the two-slit type of experiment seem to 'interfere with themselves', Pope says that it is the paths that 'interfere' not the 'photons'. Those paths, as already discussed, are not to be conceived as lines or trajectories traced on or in some classical spatial continuum. They are projected from differences in statistical amounts, called intensities, of those otherwise distanceless quantum twinkles. This means that those paths from source to screen, via the slits, in relativistic projection-space are not necessarily lengths of any process actually taking place along those paths. They are no more than what may be truly and commonsensically be called geometrical potentialities for those actions, according to the design of the apparatus. That potentiality of the apparatus has the dimensions and other parameters of a 'wave' but, of course, without a real wave's actual dynamic properties.

Like a real wave, also, those potentials extrapolated through the slits in the Thomas Young apparatus have geometrical phase-relations. That is, due to the macroscopic geometry of the apparatus, for each point on the screen the wave-like probability-function is now a characteristic of the apparatus itself, not necessarily of anything 'travelling' in it. For quantum contacts of intrinsic wavelength λ, therefore, there are, in the apparatus, geometrically prepared paths for interaction which, as multiples of lengths ½λ, are in-phase or out-of-phase with one another in terms of the wave-like group-extrapolated probability function. This means that the interference will be the same, no matter how many or how few source-screen quantum interactions of that wavelength are actually taking place.

These potentialities, i.e., paths, will be the same, of course, for any sort of periodic interaction, whether of photo, electrons, mesons, sodium atoms, the rolling of mapping-wheels or whatever, and no matter at what 'speed'. All that matters is that the intrinsic space-time lengths λ = h/mv for particles, or h/mc for photo, of those periodic interactions are 'in phase' or 'out-of-phase' with the lengths of the paths. The consequences are the same as in conventional 'wave-dynamics' – in every respect, that is, except one. This is that these purely geometrical in-phase or out-of-phase conditions, in projection-space, now control the distributions of single particles over extended periods of time in the way experiment attests but which it has proved impossible to explain in terms of classical and quantum wave-dynamics – other than by postulating 'spooky superluminal' clairvoyance, of course.

Perhaps it can be seen, then, how the two issues, of projection space and the two-slit experiment were connected in the correspondence Bastin reports. Pope concludes his reply to Kilmister as follows:

What I had forgotten is that ... blocspace (projection space) is a statistical version of the classical "absolute space" of Newton and Mach. In this neo-Newtonian quantum discretum, proper-time-instantaneity is invariant in the usual Relativistic sense of being the same in all observer-frames, which makes it no more than a discretised or quantised, observer-sequential version of the absolute space of Galileo and Newton.

... In this suggested neo-Newtonian way of thinking, the classical continuum is replaced by a quantum discretum, which is holistic in the sense of Heraclitus rather than of Zeno. ... The Heraclitan conceptual model for this [particular] sort of whole, or discretum, is the candle flame burning steadily in still air but whose parts are in continual violent motion.

For the more philosophical members of ANPA he adds the following cosmogonical argument:

Like any other physical phenomenon, the candle-flame as such is not, logically, one of its parts, any more than any one of those parts is, logically, the whole. Logically, then, the existences of the whole [which is cognitive] and the part [which is physical] are categorically different [i.e., they are not reducible to one another]. Moreover, again logically, there is no reason to suppose that these wholes, or forms of things ... form an ultimate continuous Whole of the ... sort classical physicists have called "The Universe". In other words, in a discretum there is at least as much room for diversity, change and even conflict among the wholes (forms) as there is among their parts. Indeed, the concept of interaction and even conflict, of forms as such, is essentially Heraclitan.

Any idea of the discretum as a single overall-interrelated whole, or 'Universe' is therefore rejected by Pope in favour of a pluriverse or polyverse (manifold) of interacting wholes, or forms. Moreover, as he argued with Manthey, in the latter's Blue Anchor visit in 1995, there is no possibility of the sort of association of elements that we find in cognition emerging from a discretum (of numbers or other elements) by pure chance. This is because it is axiomatic that nothing can emerge from a system which is not potentially there at the start. Thus he questions not only the idea of an emergent evolution but also that of the single, overall 'Creation' described by authors of the Old Testament and paraphrased by the modern exponents of 'The Big Bang'.

As Bastin says, 'the debate continues'.

Eddington's Search for a Fundamental Theory: A key to the universe C.W. Kilmister, C.U.P. 1994

A Review by H. Pierre Noyes

This book, by one of the founders of ANPA, explores a mystery in the history of science. Sir Arthur Eddington was a brilliant astrophysicist and one of the leaders of British science in the 1920's. He was one of the most important figures who brought Einstein's work on general relativity to the attention of the British scientific community. He was responsible for organising the solar eclipse expedition whose observations gave dramatic evidence for the prediction of the bending of light as it passes the sun. His book, Mathematical Theory of Relativity, published in 1923, was recognised at the time as the definitive textbook in English on the subject. It was a model of lucid presentation. In 1936 he published Relativistic Theory of Protons and Electrons (RTEP), which most readers found obscure and unhelpful; some felt it was completely on the wrong track. His last book Fundamental Theory (FT) – essentially complete before his death, but published posthumously in 1946 – went even further toward destroying his reputation with most physicists.

The mystery is attacked by Clive Kilmister by asking and answering three questions about these two controversial books.

  1. What made Eddington write RTPE (and then FT)?

  2. Why is RTPE (and also FT) obscure?

  3. What important and valuable aspects does each work have?

Since a reviewer is not supposed to reveal the plot of a mystery story, I will not summarise the answers here. I urge you to learn for yourselves by reading this delightfully written volume which clarifies an important piece of scientific history in non-technical language.

This work is, of course, required reading for anyone interested in ANPA. Ted Bastin and Clive Kilmister met originally because of their common interest in Eddington's theory, and in the early 50's started the collaboration which has continued to this day. ANPA was originally founded in 1979 in order to attract more attention to their efforts to forge a new foundation for physics. As Clive remarks, "For all its faults, RTPE remains, because of its wealth of revolutionary ideas, one of the most important scientific books of the first half of the twentieth century. It puts at the head of its statement of purpose the outstanding problems of physics – the establishment of a sensible relationship between general relativity and quantum mechanics. An enormous amount of work on somewhat more orthodox lines than Eddington's toward quantum gravity in some sense of the words has still left this problem unsolved. This suggests that perhaps the wrong question is being asked.

As those who have attended recent meetings of ANPA at Cambridge know, there is as yet no consensus within ANPA as to what is the right question, let alone how to ask it! How to formulate the problem of quantum gravity is a matter of lively debate among us, and not just in the community at large. This debate will certainly continue.
