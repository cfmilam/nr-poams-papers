An ANPA Scoop - Aug. 2006

N. Vivian Pope & Anthony D. Osborne — 2006

An ANPA Scoop This is a full Report on the talk and ensuing discussion of a paper presented at ANPA (the Alternative Natural Philosophy Association) Wesley House, Cambridge. The talk was entitled ‘A Truly Alternative Natural Philosophy’ and was communicated to the Association by Viv Pope, on Saturday, August 5th, 2006. Considering the animosity towards POAMS (the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis) that has been so much a feature of the recent ANPA e-mails, the level of hostility towards the presentation of my paper at the Association’s meeting, on Saturday morning, was not nearly so great as I had anticipated. There were one or two members of the audience who remained implacably opposed, but in general I think it can truly be said that there was something of a consensus on the vital issue of the two possible interpretations of the constant c leading to the two divergent and mutually exclusive paradigms of Natural Philosophy, namely, Einsteinian Realism and Machian Phenomenalism. Those few who, as I said, remained hostile could not see the dialectical, or Socratic, critical method of eliminative selection of what otherwise creates a veritable ‘slum’ of theories, as anything more than what one of them called a ‘slagging-off’, by POAMS, of all other ‘theories’ but its own. And this was despite the careful description of that well-known dialectical method, delivered right at the start! As for the ten reasons that were advanced for eliminating the customary Einsteinian, ‘velocity’ interpretation of c, a general agreement was reached on the seminal POAMS proposition that to interpret c as a ‘velocity’ is illogical. These ten questions had already been circulated on e-mail, prior to the meeting, so that anyone who wished to do so could prepare answers. Here, again, are those ten reasons.

1. The undeniable fact that c has the dimensions of distance divided by time explains all that is known about the times taken for communications over distance. But the fact that all velocities are distances divided by time by no means entails that all distances divided by time are velocities, which would be as absurd as saying that because all bachelors are men, all men are necessarily bachelors. [This point was accepted without demur.]

2. If the non-velocity interpretation were false, then how could it possibly support such a tightly structured logical edifice as POAMS? How could such a large and healthy tree grow from allegedly barren ground? Added to this is James Lindesay’s experimental support of Pope’s contention, at an earlier ANPA meeting, that ‘there is no such thing as the “photon”.’ (See Lindesay’s contribution to our recently published book: Immediate Distant Action and Correlation in Modern Physics: the Balanced Universe, by Edwin Mellen Press, USA and Britain (2005), Chapter 11, page 271.) [There were no objections to this.] These above two arguments were aimed to prove that c need not necessarily be a ‘velocity’. The following eight arguments contend that c cannot possibly be a ‘velocity.

3. For light to be seen, photographed or detected in any possible way, it has to shine on something. In a vacuum there is, by definition, nothing on which it can shine. So, logically, light cannot be seen, photographed or in any other way be detected in the vacuum of space, which signifies a reduction to absurdity of Nils Abramson’s claim to have photographed ‘light travelling in vacuo’. [This was not contested and was, in fact, heartily endorsed by some members of the audience.]

4. To be seen or otherwise detected travelling in a vacuum, light would have to give off light. And for that secondary light to be seen in vacuo it would also have to give off light; and in order for that tertiary light to be detected it, too, would have to give off light … and so on, ad

infinitum, in a logical regress to absurdity. [This, too was generally accepted. At least, there was no argument offered in opposition and there was complete silence from the Abramson support sector.]

5. If c is interpreted as a ‘velocity in the vacuum of space’ (pace Einstein’s Second Postulate), then in a vacuum to what can that ’velocity’ possibly be referred, constant or otherwise? So the concept of light as having a ‘velocity in space’ is just another absurdity. [There was, again, no opposition to this argument.]

6. Light is quantised in units of Planck’s constant h. These quanta have been interpreted as ‘flying photons’, claimed to have been photographed ‘in flight’ by Abramson, a claim which has been vigorously supported by some members of ANPA. However, since the ‘photon’ is defined as a single, irreducible light-quantum, it has no energy to spare in manifesting itself anywhere between its point of emission and point of absorption. A quantum interaction between a pair of atoms therefore has to be instantly consummated, with there being no sensible question either as to where it is or what it does between its source and sink. There are simply no parameters to describe that ‘motion’. Any attempt to photograph or otherwise detect it absorbs its whole packet of energy at that point, so that there can be no question of how it exists or travels when undetected. [This, also, was accepted, unopposed.]

7. In order to conform to the law of conservation of energy, the alleged ‘photon’ cannot just hang around unconsummated in limbo, waiting to be absorbed. As Tom Phipps (Jr.) put it, ‘the ‘photon’ sure don’t have a holding pattern!’ So, what is a ‘photon’ when it is supposed to be travelling, say, between galaxies or, as it might be, en route to nowhere? The whole concept is meaningless. [To this there was much nodded agreement. The Abramson support sector, however, reiterated the claim that the light-in-flight photograph was that of a hologram in space. In my reply I pointed out that this had been discussed twenty years ago, with the laser-hologram specialist, Michel Grossman and others, at the Louis Pasteur Institute, University of Strasbourg, circa 1988. This was after a paper I read at the Conseil de l’ Europe. In these discussions, following a study of Abramson’s claim, it was decided that the hologram could not have been projected objectively onto a vacuum but was an illusion created by different observational perspectives of light reflected off different surfaces into the eye or a camera lens. It is certainly not something existing objectively and self-sufficiently in space independently of all optical and photographic perspectives.]

8. Can light be scattered by light, as some experimenters have claimed? If a powerful laserbeam is shone across the path of another, do their ‘photons’ collide or their ‘waves’ interfere? In a simple experiment devised and carried out at Brunel university, in 1980, by myself and Peter Louwerse, plus some laboratory assistants, two powerful lasers were beamed across each other’s paths and also shone head-on at each other. No blocking or interference whatever was detected. If any such interference were to take place, then that light would suffer dispersion. Considering the amount of light that is allegedly ‘criss-crossing’ around, it would be amazing if visual acuity was possible over the length of a single metre. All the light that is allegedly shooting around in all directions would be as much a barrier to vision as the densest fog that can be imagined. The fact, then, that there are photographs of the farthest galaxies that display awesome clarity militates against the validity of any such experimentalist claim, especially in view of the caveat already provided, that no true experimental results can confirm something which is not logically possible. [This issue of ‘light scattering light’ had already been answered in the preceding paper by James Lindesay, entitled ‘Few Particle insights into Combinatorial Physics’. In his talk he had concluded, on the basis of algebraic quantum logic applied to evidences of particles being scattered by other particles, that there could never be any true evidences of photon-photon scattering. In my talk he reiterated this

point in answer to those who had claimed experimental evidence for light interfering with light. After some discussion, this point of James’ was eventually accepted.]

9. All velocities, properly so called, obey the rule of the composition of velocities, according to which the velocity of an object is different relative to differently moving observers. But c is, eminently, the same for all relatively moving observers,. Therefore, logically, it cannot be a velocity. [There was no opposition to this argument.]

10. For a velocity to be a velocity it has to be the velocity of something that is physically identifiable. In physics both ancient and modern, there is nothing that can be physically identified as light travelling in vacuo, especially in view of Heisenberg’s Indeterminacy Principle, which makes the ‘track’ of an alleged ‘photon’ absolutely indeterminate. If we think of what ‘travels in vacuo’ as ‘waves’, then what can possibly ‘wave’ in a vacuum? And if we think of what ‘travels’ as ‘photons’, then if those ‘photons’ travel at the ‘speed of light’, then their mass has to be relativistically infinite at that ‘speed’ – the mass of a single photon would be as great as that of the whole universe. To escape this consequence by assuming that the ‘stationary mass’ of the photon is zero – as Jean Pierre Vigier claimed, in a conference I attended at Toronto – is, as I pointed out at the time, to raise the inevitable question of how that ‘zero mass’ can it ever be conceived as a ‘particle’? And, anyway, when is a photon ever regarded as stationary, since c is c in all observational frames? Vigier had no answer to this. [There was just one objection here, based on a point of semantics. This was that the word ‘velocity’ could be applied to the purely disembodied apparition of motion created by the time-sequential switching of light-bulbs, as seen on some advertising hoardings. My answer was that this is precisely the way POAMS describes the time-delay associated with light; that it is, fundamentally, a ‘cinematic’ or observational sequencing of proper-time-instantaneous (i.e., Lewisian) quantum touchings, in a manner similar to that in which single photographic frames, or ‘stills’ are sequenced in the running of a movie. The ‘objection’ was therefore no more than a confirmation of the POAMS contention that light involves nothing travelling (i.e., ‘photons’) but is simply a very fine-grained instance of what perception psychologists call the ‘phi phenomenon’. This point was not challenged further.] Following these ten proofs of the non-velocity interpretation of c, the general discussion (which someone described as a ‘free-for-all’) was, as one member summarised it afterwards, greatly to his taste in being ‘much enlivened’ by the introduction of this issue of c. Various questions were raised. For instance, one of the members challenged me to explain, in consistent POAMS terms, the time-differences in the readings of clocks set at various distances apart from one another and from the observer. My rejoinder was to ask the questioner whether he could answer these questions in terms of standard orthodox relativistic light-theory. His answer was, ‘Yes’. ‘Well, then,’ I replied, ‘the answer POAMS gives to your question is the same as that given by your standard orthodox relativistic light-theory, since c is the same c in both cases.’ [This seems to have been accepted.] Another point was raised regarding quantum instantaneity. It was pointed out that in standard quantum theory, as the CERN physicist, John Bell had proved, it was the ‘up’ and ‘down’ spins of fundamental particles that had been found to be instantaneously coupled at a distance, which seemed to have nothing to do with the sort of distant instantaneity POAMS was talking about. In reply I pointed out that since there is no possibility of any observer or observing instrument being in two distance-separated places at once, these instantaneous quantum spin-couplings could never be observed, other than at a distance by the agency of light, in which those distant couplings are instantaneous in proper time and time-delayed in relative (or observer) time. This was demonstrated by the use of an overhead projected diagram of the basic Pythagorean time-dilational formula, showing that when the quantum connection is intrinsically instantaneous, the relative time is delayed, without there being any

suggestion of a paradox. So, in short, since there cannot be (not in science, at any rate) any ubiquitous ‘God’s-eye-view’ of the distance-separated spins together, simultaneously, the quantum theorists’ claim that these spin-couplings are instantaneous has to fall into line with observational relativity – as it does in the Angular Momentum Synthesis of Pope and Osborne. Of the core members of ANPA one declared afterwards that it had been a ‘good paper’ and another that it had given him a much better understanding of the POAMS position. This was also the view expressed by other members in conversations afterwards. During the talk, however, there were some questions asked as to what, for instance, constituted a ‘thing’ (i.e., a physical object) in the philosophy of Normal Realism. This was reminiscent of Samuel Johnson’s famously abortive reaction to the Phenomenalism of George Berkeley, which was to kick at a stone and declare ‘Sir, I refute thee thus!’ The answer I gave was to say that all science starts in the world of ordinary reality, our experience of which begins at birth in what William James called ‘a big buzzing and booming confusion’. As we grow, we begin to sort out that confusion, gradually classifying and dividing it into things like – at first, ‘Mum’, ‘Dad’ ‘rattle’, brother’, ‘sister’ and so on, and then, later, into categories such as ‘hard and soft’, ‘pleasant and painful’, ‘right and wrong’, ‘late and early’, ‘brave and cowardly’, etc., in accordance with the standard language of whatever happens to be the society in which one is born and raised and which is taken in, as it were, with one’s mother’s milk. In a discussion on this afterwards, it was remarked how so many ‘hard-nosed experimentalists’ seem to be unaware of the extent to which their native language predisposes them to perceive, conceive and interpret the world prejudicially, far less of what the philosopher Wittgenstein meant by the truism that ‘the world is language’. Rather, they tend to react by thinking of language as ‘mere words’ as opposed to what they presume is some kind of absolutely objective, ‘God’seye-view’ of ‘things’. In short, then, the Normal Realist answer to the question ‘What is a “thing”?’ was simply that it is something of which we have sensory and/or instrumental information and which we describe in language – a classifying and dividing of phenomena, no more and no less. So the objection was reversed by posing the question ‘What else can a “thing” possibly be but something we learn or are conditioned into describing according to our linguistic conventions? Any question of what a ‘thing’ is ‘in itself’, behind and beyond all language is a metaphysical conundrum.’ [To this there was no answer, except for a deep, attentive contemplative silence.] A point was made by another member, regarding the Thomas Young two-slit experiment. This was that physicists were now able to observe single photons emitted by a source and counted by a distant detector. How, I was asked, does POAMS account for this? My answer was that in the two-slit experiment, even in the case of what are described as ‘single photons’ the ‘wave’-like pattern of detection is never a characteristic of ‘the photons themselves’ – whatever that might mean – but a proper-time-instantaneous function of the apparatus as a whole in the way that is described in the POAMS literature (See, e.g., ‘The Tantalising Two-Slit Experiment’, by N. V. Pope, in Recent Advances in Relativity Theory, vol. 2, ‘Material Interpretations, Eds. M.C. Duffy and M. Wegener, Hadronic Press, Florida (2001)). It was pointed out that nothing on the quantum level occurs in the manner envisaged by Laplace and Boltzmann, that is, as sets of events which, if we were God, we would see as perfectly determinate. It was pointed out that in the more recent interpretations of the Second Law of Thermodynamics, by Shannon and Weaver, et al, causality is not a determinate, mechanical atom-to-atom interaction but something involving statistical numbers of atoms, in which case the idea of a single atom ‘causing’ something to happen in another atom in a mechanically determinate way is nonsense.

So, it was concluded, since causality is governed by the Second Law of Thermodynamics, according to which a cause is a function only of statistical numbers of quantum events, never of just one single event, then even in the case of what had been claimed as ‘the counting of single photons’ it is the statistical law applied to the whole apparatus that determines this counting of a ‘single photon’, never the ‘photon’ itself as a discrete entity. [This was agreed, particularly by one of the members who said he would take it up in further discussions after my wife and I had left, which we had to, fairly soon after my talk, so as to meet some pressing domestic obligations.] At least one member remarked, afterwards, his realisation, from the e-mail exchanges, of the vital connection, in POAMS, between the issue of the interpretation of c and the phenomenalist philosophy of Mach. One of them said that in his experience, most physicists knew next to nothing about Mach other than in connection with ‘Mach’s Principle’ and the ‘Mach number’. These physicists, he said, were almost totally ignorant of Mach’s radically different philosophical approach to their subject, to say nothing about Mach’s fundamental disagreement, as a phenomenalist, with the classical ‘Realism’ of his follower, Einstein. At one point, the question ‘What is logic?’ was raised. In answer I recounted a conversation I had recently with someone who asked me what I meant by ‘logic’. ‘Well,’ I replied, ‘suppose I tell you “The biscuits are in the tin” and then tell you that “The tin is in the cupboard”.’ From these two statements, I said, ‘you may deduce a third, namely, that “The biscuits are in the cupboard”. And that’s logic,’ I said. After a moment’s uncomfortable silence he responded by shaking his head and saying: ‘I’m sorry but that “does my head in”.’ The laughter with which this report was met was reassuring. However, the point I was trying to make by reporting this incident was to reveal the extent to which the gradual withdrawal, in popular Education, of any training in the discipline of logical reasoning, had produced such examples of what would once have been clearly seen as an appalling ignorance of what ‘logic’ is. My own experience, as an erstwhile teacher of Logic and Scientific Method, is that of the degree to which this sort of ignorance of logic has now infiltrated into science, which makes so much of what is claimed as ‘science’ scarcely distinguishable from outright political chicanery! Surely, I felt, some of the recent correspondence within the ANPA e-mail circuit had demonstrated just how deeply this ignorance of logic has now penetrated into physics. The fact that so few people remarked on this in the e-mail exchanges had been, to me, most disconcerting. However, on this point of logic, no matter how compulsively the train of logical implications can be seen to lead from the non-velocity interpretation of c into phenomenalism (POAMS) it seemed that most people felt free not to be ‘railroaded’ along that track into accepting phenomenalism as a complete alternative to the current eclecticism. Much of this is undoubtedly because all too many people still associate Phenomenalism with the subjective idealism of George Berkeley, unaware of the famous (in Modern Philosophy) refutation of Berkeleyan Idealism by G. E. Moore. This is due, undoubtedly, to the artificial Educational schism between Arts and Science bemoaned by the likes of C.P Snow and A.J. Ayer. So the upshot of all this seemed to be a preference for keeping ‘all the options open’, without any burdensome philosophical concern for logical coherence. This is as expressed in the old saying: ‘I see and approve the better course but follow the worse’. So, unable to fault the logic that leads irrefutably from the non-velocity interpretation of c to Normal Realism and its modern-physics spin-off, POAMS, the general preference seemed to be to abandon any concern for ultimate truth and just go along with the current flow of non-philosophical, non-logical eclecticism. In this way of thinking, all theories are regarded, for the most part, as just ‘horses for courses’ in the unsystematic collecting and accumulating of ‘experimental facts’. In opposition to this it was pointed out that there are no absolute facts of any kind, that

all facts, even those revealed by the most meticulous scientific experiments are, in the last analysis, interpretations of phenomena, hence prone to revision in the light of logical advances in ongoing experience. However, I had to agree that a pragmatic eclecticism is sometimes a necessary ‘survival mechanism’ in the workaday world. I recounted some of my own experience as a trouble-shooter in the telephone industry, where I had to earn my bread in situations which were eminently non-philosophical. Nevertheless, as I argued, it seems disingenuous for this policy of pragmatism to be invoked as a correct attitude to be adopted as a philosophical approach to truth, especially in an Association such as ANPA, with the word ‘Philosophy’ so resplendent in its title. Anyway, as already remarked, as it turned out, my talk on ‘A truly Alternative Natural Philosophy’ was far better received than I had anticipated, and this has, to a large extent, restored my faith in the philosophical integrity of ANPA. There was a bit of vociferous silliness at the end, from someone who has never seen these arguments about c as anything but ‘a load of guff’. However, all in all, I think it can be fairly said that the logical demonstration of the impossibility of the constant c being interpreted as a ‘velocity’ was wellreceived and therefore a qualified success. It may be concluded, then, that since there were no successful refuting arguments, the point is proved that the constant c need not, and indeed cannot, logically be interpreted as a ‘velocity’, hence that the logical choice of the proper course for the future of relativity is not that of Einstein but that of his philosophical mentor, Mach, of which the relativity of Bondi, Pope and Osborne (POAMS) is, currently, the historical cutting edge. This is not least because the relativity of POAMS is also quantised, thereby cutting off the notorious ‘EPR paradox’ at its very root. Now if I may be allowed a short departure, I have to say that the general reluctance among ANPA members to study this truly alternative approach in depth is perfectly understandable, since what used to be called Natural Philosophy is now, fashionably, the truncated ‘Physics’ of contemporary convention, in which the ‘Philosophy’ part of Natural Philosophy has now traditionally dropped out of account. This means that in contemporary Physics the sort of overall logical coherence which natural philosophers have always sought in their interpretations of natural phenomena has been officially abandoned – all too often together with logic itself. In place of that logical coherence, the habit has been established of simply picking and mixing theories to suit a particular situation, regardless of any philosophical concern. This is in the vague and entirely vain hope that, somehow, in the end, ‘experimental facts’ will eventually ‘write their own story’. That habit is now set and, like all habits is difficult to break. So, what psychologists call ‘tolerance of ambiguity’ remains the present order of the day. This is manifest even in ANPA whose aim, one would think, from its title, would be to restore to science the Natural Philosophy element which eclectic ‘Physics’ has all but completely removed. So, even when the opportunity of a coherent logical synthesis of natural philosophy presents itself we, like a caged animal suddenly released, are afraid to venture out from our all-too familiar eclectic surroundings which, as I say, is all very well for the workaday world but scarcely in Philosophy. But at least, the light-barrier that, in his false interpretation of c as a ‘velocity in vacuo’, Einstein inadvertently placed in the path of philosophical progress, has now been removed. This, at least, is now established as a terminal falsehood in the ‘Realistic’ tradition of science. The way is therefore now wide open for the long-awaited breakthrough from the truncated (that is, the EPR-inhibited) relativistic physics of Einstein into the wholesale relativistic philosophy of Mach. Fortunately, for anyone wishing to embark on it, much of this relativistic philosophy has already been investigated and set out in detail, by the POAMS group, so that no-one need

enter that ‘unknown region’ map-less and unprepared. The starting-point for that exploratory journey is in the following websites: www.poams.org www.vivpope.co.uk www.nrig.org www.vivpope.org If critics of POAMS were to read these websites, studiously and unprejudicially – which, for the most part they plainly don’t – then there might well be a resurgence of Natural Philosophy to replace the modern quantum-relativistic physics which, as one of its foremost exponents, Richard Feynman, has said, ‘No-one understands’. What, then, could be more appropriate than for this opportunity for a resurgence in Natural Philosophy to be hosted by and therefore accredited to ANPA? Best wishes to all fellow members, Viv Pope.
