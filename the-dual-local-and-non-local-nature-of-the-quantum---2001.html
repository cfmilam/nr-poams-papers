However, despite the undoubted success of Einstein's ideas in other respects, this idea of the 'photon', as a particle whose speed is a constant, c, not only with respect to the vacuum itself - whatever that may mean - but also to everything in it, whether relatively moving or stationary, has not proved particularly helpful, as Einstein himself was the first to admit. The trouble is that the word 'photon' over-vividly suggests a travelling object, to be listed as just another 'fundamental particle'. The confusion this creates is as though a traffrc accident involving two vehicles were to be reported as an accident involving three vehicles, the two colliding (interacting) vehicles and the collision (interaction) itself. This kind of misuse of language raises strange questions as to what speed the interaction travels at, what its mass and momentum are, and so on, which precipitates us into depths of mystery.

Logically, then, the terminology of particles should be kept distinct from that of the interactions between them. Only at the risk of creating chronic confusion, should we continue conflating the two in the manner of Einstein. Happily, there is a more logical and natural way of depicting the quantum which avoids that Einsteinian confusion. This is to think of the two interacting atoms is forming, at first, an automatically paired and reciprocally balanced angular momentum relation which, like the action $e/\nu$ is irreducible below the limit $\hbar$, or $h/2\pi$. Being irreducible, the angular momentum quantum may be conceived as a virtually static configuration, as in a single cinematographic frame, or 'still'. 'Electric charges', 'gravitation' and in vacuo'forces' It should by now be plain that the traditional conception of particles being possessed of'charges' and other static capacities for attracting and repelling one another at a distance cannot be fitted to our angular momentum account of atomic interaction, any more than can the 'fields' associated with these postulated 'capacities'. There is, in any case, a logical circularity involved in defining these esoteric 'capacities'. For instance, if we ask what a 'charge' is, we are told that is a capacity of one charged particle for creating or changing the motions of other charged particles; and if we ask what evidence there is of these underlying 'charges' we find that the only evidence of their existence is the motion and change of motion in the particles we started with. Since the energetic motions of those particles are measured in mechanical units of joules, it seems bizarre to take these mechanical measures as evidence of some underlying, static 'capacity' of those particles, measured in coulombs, whose only 'effect' is to manifest those original mechanical measures in joules.

A more straightforward account of those mechanical motions is, surely, to express them all in terms ofjoules, dispensing altogether with the esoteric 'capacities' in coulombs. What remains is then an un-elaborated description of the orbital motions of masses in terms purely of angular momentum, which proves sufficient in itself to account for the closed trajectories of free-moving bodies without having to postulate the existence of any in vucuo 'field-forces' of 'electrostatic', 'gravitational' or any other description. t{l Fo, instance, Newton's straight-line ('inertial') momentvm mv can be taken as being, implicitly, an angular momentum mvr with r at theoretieal infinity, which is a theoretically infinite angular momentum. Interpreted in this way, as configurations of pure angular momentum, from astronomical bodies to microphysical particles, there can be no question of bodies in vacuo 'forces' of any kind, called 'gravitational', interacting by means 'electrostatic' or whatever. As Mach argued, masses can interact only with other masses, never with a void, which means that the interaction (correlation might be a better word) is direct and immediate (non-local) with there being no question of mediation by 'fields' or 'ethers'. The connection is, in other words, what some physicists accept as 'action-at-a-distance'. The only forces I makes sense to speak of in this context are those required to prevent masses from travelling in their natural force-free orbital trajectories. These are real, that is, measurable forces, unlike those fictitious or metaphyiical ones supposed to be acting in vacuo.[6]

The main disadvantage, as many will see it, of this suggested removal of 'photons', 'waves', 'charges', 'force-fields' etc., from relativistic physics is that despite its obvious economising of concepts in accordance with the principle of 'Occam's razor', and the simplifuing of the time-dilation formula, it calls for a radical switch from thinking of physics in the 'realistic' manner of Einstein, Podolski and Rosen, towards thinking of it in the phenomenalist or information-statistical mode of Bohr, Heisenberg and Schr6dinger - and. of course, Mach. Usually, we think of cinematography as a secondary and phantasmagorical representation of nature. It is by no means easy to manage a paradigm switch from thinking of the 'cinematographie' depiction as basic (a priori) and the usual mechanistic (or'realist') one as secondary or representational (a posteriori). However, philosophers from Berkeley to Mach have seriously considered such a paradigm switch in our approach to physics, and it is hoped we have demonstrated that it remains, even today, a viable alternative to our customary mechanistic approach.

Which of these paradigms will eventually prevail is, of course, too soon to say. However, two things are cerlain. One is that the two paradigms are logically divergent and cannot sensibly be regarded as 'complementary'. The other is that no selfrespecting physicist should prejudge in favour of the one or the other, far less on grounds of no more than a conceptual conservatism.
