'Simon Says' - A Challenge to the NPA

N. Vivian Pope

Dear friends and Fellow NPA members I think it is time to remind readers of what the issue was that kick-started all this ‘Dissidence’ controversy. It was a paper I sent out in response to John Chappell’s talk, at Storrs, about the need for a ‘new paradigm’ of physics with which to unite the Association against the now veritably collapsing Orthodox Paradigm. My suggestion was that there is, for the taking, a logical paradigm which might be considered. Its advantage is that it has been developed, tried and tested for almost fifty years, based on a critical correspondence with Albert Einstein himself. But a new paradigm is a new paradigm. You can’t make a new paradigm by splitting-up an old and ailing one into warring factions. Yet that is precisely what has happened in these NPA discussions. The whole thing went pear-shaped, with the dissident energy that might have been directed against the Establishment merely turning in on itself. This was in a tizzy of sheer fractiousness conducted in the same old-paradigm jargon, with scarcely a glance spared to look beyond it. Needless to say, in all this antler-clashing furore, any chance of uniting against the Establishment was thrown to the winds. For myself, the logistics of dealing with the avalanche of emails which my original paper precipitated have now become impossible to cope with, although, mercifully, they are now not directed entirely at me. However, couched as they invariably are in the same old jargon and demanding that I respond in those same stultified terms, my answers to those questions are now going round for the umpteenth time with, as it now seems, no hope of getting anywhere. In desperation, therefore, I have composed a blanket reply to my inquisitors, which is a last-ditch attempt to obtain some just and objective assessment of my proposed way out of the morass. This suggested ‘way forward’, may I remind you, was the completely ahistorical, and anti-scholastic one I described in both my original paper at Storrs and in the follow-up paper that was circulated afterwards to those people on what has since become the ‘Dissidents egroup’. Of course, I am aware that there may be, out there, some natural philosophers, truly so called, who can sensibly evaluate - and may even approve my suggested way forward. But if there are, then I don’t hear from them – at least, not in these present discussions. So, instead of continually beating my head against a brick wall, I have written a paper on which there should be some sensible response. (I was going to put this on ‘Attached’, but I don’t know how this would work on the present provision, so I shall send it as an ordinary (separate) e-mail.) A suitable attempt at LOGICAL refutation of this would not come amiss! But in a situation where logic becomes ‘illogicality’ and ‘black’ is made to appear ‘white’, where the whole thing begins to look like the Mad Hatter’s Tea Party, what else can anyone do with truly logical argument but, however regretfully, to doff one’s cap and move on? Nevertheless, the experience has been nothing if not informative. And bystanders may make of it what they will. But no matter which way it goes from

now on, I sincerely hope that those personal friendships I have made within the NPA will continue, even though I have become a dissident among the ‘Dissidents’. So I leave you with the next (following) e-mail, with my apologies for its length. If , like a long ‘shaggy-dog’ story, it is listened to only in bits, then, it is scarcely surprising if you don’t laugh! All the best, Viv Pope P.S. If there are any questions arising from this, I shall try to answer them but can no longer guarantee to do so. No offence whatever is intended.

‘Simon Says’: A Challenge to the NPA Viv Pope

1. INTRODUCTION My original proposal for a new paradigm somehow got lost and virtually mugged in the labyrinthine streets of the internally fractious NPA. Innocently, I thought everyone knew what a paradigm is. It seems I was wrong. As I learned it, a paradigm, in the context of natural philosophy, is a whole organised way of thinking, a conceptual Gestalt which may share nothing in common with another paradigm except for the natural phenomena (observational and experimental data) on which they both are based. I also imagined, in my innocence, that it would be as obvious to all NPA members as to logicians and scientists throughout history, that the observational/experimental information which is common to different paradigms can, in itself, in no way dictate which paradigm to choose. What a shock it was to find that no-one in the Association seemed to understand that fundamental scientific principle! And what a shock, again, it was to discover that no-one seemed to know that the way to choose between paradigms is not only on the basis of making refutable physical predictions (Popper’s ‘refutability criterion’) but also – and mainly - on the basis of the well-known principle of ‘Occam’s razor’, according to which the paradigm or thesis which requires the least number of supporting suppositions in interpreting the common information is the one we ought, objectively, to choose. Something else I thought everyone knew is that no paradigm can be refuted simply by propounding or stressing another. Seeking to do so is the fallacy known as ponendo tollens (which means that in a choice of alternative propositions, P and Q, it is impossible to negate P simply by affirming Q, or vice versa). Popper’s point was precisely to emphasise the opposite of this fallacy in his advocacy of tollendo ponens (negating P in order to affirm Q or vice-versa) These principles were once common knowledge in science until it launched itself on its Gadarene descent into Modern Physics, with its characteristic eclecticism and sophistry. However, in presenting to the NPA my own suggested new paradigm I have found, in that organisation, no recognition whatever of those fundamental logical principles. So I suppose, looking back on it, it was inevitable that my proposal would not be understood, far less logically evaluated. What happened was that, from the first, it was taken as an arrogation of the political constitution of the NPA, in which the ‘association’ seems to be not so much a collection of true natural philosophers but more of a political huddle of ‘mechanists’ and ‘materialists’ against what they intuitively regard as the ‘idealism’ of logical and philosophical reasoning – and, of course, against any form of relativism, which they dread as being somehow synonymous with ‘subjectivism’. Yet, despite the obvious confusion over what ‘paradigm’ means, there was nevertheless much talk of an anticipated ‘new paradigm’ to replace the old one, which was why I thought it fitting to present my suggestions for the same to the NPA members. Unfortunately, what I found was that by a ‘new paradigm’ the disputants within the NPA meant nothing of the sort I was contemplating. They saw it as no more than a sectioning-off of the old paradigm into a militant sub-group who were ‘against’ the other half on the

grounds that those others were ‘followers of Einstein and Lorentz’, These they regarded ‘idealistic’ or ‘subjectivistic’, whereas they themselves, the self-fancied ‘hard-headed experimentalists’, were ‘absolutistic’ and ‘realistic. It reminded me of nothing so much as the splittings of street gangs into ‘Mods’ and ‘Rockers’, ‘Hell’s Angels’ and so on, in the 1960s. What I most found disconcerting, however, was that in defence of that ‘materialism’, people who should have known better were prepared to commit just about every fallacy there is. And despite my having reminded everyone about those old and well-tried, scientifically hallowed logical principles, no matter how careful I was to avoid it, my arguments got continually snarled up with contentious ‘experimentalist’ issues internal to the Old Paradigm which have all but halted progress in natural philosophy. Well alright, at first there did seem to be a common consensus that time was running out on the existing paradigm, and that attempts by establishment physicists to repair it and shore it up with more and more eclectic theory-saving hypotheses could continue only just so far. In his piece for ‘Foundations of Science, Vol. 3, No. 2, Curt Renshaw remarks: ‘How much simpler to go back to the first principles when experiment contradicts theories, rather than to keep building “castles in the air” to rescue a doomed theory.’ Of course, whole paradigms, if well-formed and logically consistent, may be refuted by a single experimental result; and Popper’s point was precisely that all theories and hypotheses should be framed so as to be refutable in that way. But no such experiment, or number of experiments can, in themselves, create a paradigm. A paradigm is like a painting. To imagine that a paradigm can come about as a result of pure experimentation is like imagining that monkeys dabbing around with paint-brushes could produce a Mona Lisa! As I was taught, paradigms are as distinct from one another as paintings or orchestrations. Jumble them together and you create a grey bodge or a cacophony. So I could not see how The NPA could ever create their anticipated new paradigm simply by splitting-up the old paradigm into those warring factions and then, opting arbitrarily for one of them and shouting at the other across the divide, which was why I proposed my completely new and independent alternative. Presenting this to the NPA, however, was like tying to roll a steel ball through a field of magnets – or, as it later turned out, like taking a stroll through a preceptual minefield. In desperation, therefore, at being unable to obtain from the NPA anything like the clean, honest and objective assessment of my proposed alternative, I had a flash of inspiration as to how I might possibly get the Association to see my proposal in its proper logical perspective. What happened was that I recalled the old children’s game of ‘Simon Says’, where to stay in the game one obeys only those commands which are prefaced by those words and anyone obeying a command which is not so prefaced is ‘out’. In the same way that in the game, players lose by failing to control their habitual impulses, there are members of the NPA who, I have discovered, cannot, it seems, control their knee-jerk impulses to talk about ‘Einstein’, et al., in discussing natural philosophy, and would be ‘out’ at their very first utterance. My idea, then, was to produce a scientific variant of that game in which the rule is to describe all the known phenomena without having to drag in Newton, Einstein, Lorentz, Faraday, Maxwell, Weber, Helmholtz, ‘gravity’, ‘electrodynamics’ ‘fields’, ‘waves’, ‘ethers’ … and so on. However, it occurred to me that since, in presenting my paradigm to the NPA this condition has so far proved impossible to fulfil, I would have to try something along similar lines as the ‘Simon says’ game, but in a different form. I therefore constructed in my mind a situation in which an extra-terrestrial scientist came to visit the NPA. Such a visitor, despite being immersed in the same natural world as we are, could in no way be expected to think of it in the way we do, conditioned as we are by the theories loaded upon us by the luminaries of our terrestrial history. In that way we would have to quell our conditioned impulses to talk about ‘STR’, ‘LT’, ‘EPR’ ‘GR’ and so on. It would be no use yammering on to this extraterrestrial about ‘Faraday’ and his ‘fields’, or Maxwell and his ‘electrodynamics’. And you might as well talk to him about the cosmical influences of the Kings and Queens of England as about the Laws of Newton or the Theories of Einstein. Only in this way, it seemed to me, could we listen to what such a paradigm-presenter had to say without assuming his ‘incompetence’ or ‘hostility’ towards what currently goes under the name of ‘physics’. NPA members would scarcely be able to accuse him (or would they?) of being ‘partisan’ towards or against this or that terrestrial theory. Here, then, is my ‘last ditch’ attempt to gain, from the NPA, some proper logical assessment of the paradigm I presented. Here, I suggest, is how it might go. And if this does not work, then I pass!

THE NPA VISITOR from ANDROMEDA Switching on our television we find ourselves in the middle of an interview between an extra-terrestrial natural philosopher (ENP) and a terrestrial physicist (TP):

1. LIGHT TP: So for you, light, you say, is no more than what you see, the opposite of dark, not something travelling in space, as it is for us. ENP: Yes, we find it strange that you should imagine that anything can travel at a constant speed with reference not only to a void but also to everything moving about in that void. Very strange indeed! That is why we prefer to interpret what you call the ‘constant c’ as simply just that, a constant, no more nor less, like your constant relation of 39.37 inches to the metre. Like c, this is the same for all observers at rest or in motion with respect to one another – but not, of course, in space. In the measures we employ, our units for measuring distance are equivalent to those for measuring time. So for us there is no pure space nor pure time, as you understand these concepts, but only what you might call spacetime, which has not just three but four geometrical dimensions, all measured in the same units, like your ordinary three-dimensions, vertical, lateral and longitudinal. So there is no ‘c’ in our equations of motion, because for us that ‘constant’ is unity, which means that it would make no sense for us to talk about it, any more than it would make sense for you to keep talk about a ‘constant’ of unity when measuring distances north-south against distances east-west. TP: So how did you get to know that constant distance-time relation in the first place? ENP: The same way as you did, by watching the eclipses of satellites as they move around their parent planets. The only difference is that when our equivalent of your Olaus Römer discovered this relation between clock-measure and rod-measure, we adjusted our units of both dimensions to suit. So for us there was never any question of anything ‘travelling’ in our viewing of distant objects, as there is for you. TP: I can see that that is more conceptually economical than our accustomed way of thinking, but you must understand that for us, your way of thinking seems very strange. For instance, how can you explain the interactions between light-sources and detectors if there is nothing crossing the space between them? ENP: Well, since there is no possible way of knowing what happens in a true void between the source and the detector, other than by interposing another detector between those two particles, and then two more between those three .. and so on, until the void becomes a continuous medium, which is a contradiction, I cannot see how you could even hope to explain what happens in the void. So, like your terrestrial philosopher, Wittgenstein, we say that what there is no possible way of explaining is not worth talking about. TP: Nevertheless, that is the way some of us terrestrials do think about light, as something conducted continuously in that way – in imagination, of course, since there is no logical nor practical way of establishing it observationally or experimentally. So the continuous substance that would be represented by such a continuum of detecting agents cannot be material, like those detectors, but has to be conceived as non-material, or quasi-material in the form of what some people call ‘ether’ and others call ‘field’ . ENP: Now that seems to be a very contrived way of thinking. TP: Undoubtedly, but how else can you possibly explain the interactions between distance-separated bodies other than in those, admittedly, purely theoretical or metaphysical terms? In other words, how can you explain what we call ‘radiant energy’? ENP: My short answer is that we see such interactions as happening, basically, not first to the one particle and then to the other, as you do, but as happening to both particles at once – as in charging a capacitor, where the energy it absorbs is absorbed by the both plates at once, in perfect symmetry, without having to think of anything ‘travelling’ in between. In other words, such a fundamental interaction is what you would call a single quantum-event, a mutual and reciprocal resonance between the two particles which, in that interaction-event, act as one. Since a quantum is a complete and

irreducible amount of action, and since action is a conserved quantity, we cannot see how it can be conceived as open-ended – that is, as leaving a source and then swanning around in the void until, if ever, it hits something … TP: You mean in the form of what we call a ‘photon’? ENP: Yes, precisely! That, again, would have to be what you call metaphysical, because how, logically, can an interaction between particles be itself a particle? To us, that would make no sense! TP: But then, again, how else, without resorting to such ,undoubtedly purely theoretical, postulates, can the time-factor in distant interaction be explained? ENP: My answer is that over the distance between bodies, the sequences of instantaneous quantum connections between the bodies are like those of the stills in a movie projector – but random, of course, not like the photographs on a reel of film. The statistical law of what you call entropy, determines the direction of that flow from the one macrophysical body to the other. And since the distance between those bodies is, for you, also a time in the ratio of units c, that flow will have the dimensions of your constant c. And because the dimensions of c are the same as those of a speed, then you assume that c is a speed, as of something travelling between the bodies. And, further, since the instantaneous quantum connections are those of resonance, as of a ‘wave’, you assume that the interaction is caused by some kind of space-travelling ‘wave’, whose in vacuo speed is c. However, we extra-terrestrials have no more need of that ‘wave’ hypothesis than for your ‘ether’ or ‘field’. The two concepts, of ‘wave’ and ‘wave-medium’ imply each other, so to dispense with either one is to dispense with both. NP: So for you there is no wave, as such, in the space between the two interacting bodies, only those instantaneous and reciprocal quantum resonances occurring at both ends, simultaneously. That’s interesting, because it’s the same as what some of us think of as ‘instantaneous action-at-a-distance’, about which there has been much fuss, nowadays. And I can see that in your way of thinking, there is no contradiction in thinking of ‘instantaneous’ and ‘time-delayed’ no more than components of the same interaction. I see what you say, and I can’t fault it, but it will take a bit of digesting. But now I’d like to talk to you about ageing. Do you think that we all age at the same rate, that our chemical, biological, psychological and other processes are basically the same, all measured, as it were, in a single, overall or cosmical time? ENP: No, because given that space is also time, then in moving about in space we are also moving about in time. So there is no more likelihood of you and me ageing at the same rate than there is of us both, in driving different routes, clocking the same mileage between two towns. By that same token, If I left here, now, and went back to my home star-system, and then returned at some future earth-date, it would be impossible for you and me to have aged at the same rate during the two visits. TP: Why do you say that? ENP: You’re not telling me that you don’t know this? TP: Let’s say we do. But tell me about it all the same. ENP: Well, if you accept the fact that for all observers, distance is also time in your constant ratio of units, c, then there’s no escaping the consequence that if you and I travel apart, the distance each of us travels with respect to the other is also a time in that ratio of units. So, assuming that throughout the motion we have spectroscopes trained on each other, then during that away-motion each of our spectroscopes will record a uniform slowing – that is, a shift towards the red – in all the processes viewed in that way, chemical, biological, you name it. I don’t suppose that any of you colleagues are likely to deny that. TP: I wouldn’t bet on it! But no, in general, we wouldn’t deny it, It’s what we Terrestrials call the ‘Doppler effect’, which is the same, in essence, as that observed by Römer, in his determination of c. ENP: Whatever! So, okay, when one or the other of us reverses his motion so as to rejoin the other, whichever of us is the one to take that reversing action will know precisely when that motion is reversed, because it will be signified by the shift from red to blue in his spectroscope. But that event, which is known immediately by the reversing observer will not be known by the other till after the lapse of the extra distance-time s/c separating that event from him. So, up till the time of that event, the spectroscope of whichever of us is the out-and-back observer will have recorded slowed (i.e., reddened) periods up until that instant and increased (blued) ones from then on. By contrast, the spectroscope of the other will record reddened periods for a time which is longer by that extra distancetime s/c separating him from that turnaround event. So at the end of the out-and-back motion, the

number of periods registered by the respective spectrometers will obviously not tally. Whichever is the one taking the reversing action will have counted less reddened periods and more blued periods than the other, and the spectroscope of whichever one of us has not taken that action will have counted the opposite. So, there is, as I say, no way that in that situation of out-and-back motion with respect to each other, our living-times, at the end, can be the same – except, of course, by denying c. TP: I take your point. But there are, among us, those who will say that this effect is no more than apparent, that in what they call ‘real’ terms, nothing changes; that no matter what we observe in that way, the number of spectral periods recorded, ‘behind the scenes’ , as it were, by the one has to be the same as that recorded by the other, otherwise the whole thing (in their view) becomes absurd.’ ENP: But that’s very puzzling, because those differences are irrecoverable. What’s gone is gone. And it would be very strange indeed if those spectroscopic records of our chemical, biological and other life processes became out of synch with some other underlying ‘real’ or ‘absolute’ time. On what possible grounds would those times have to be, ‘underlyingly’, the same, anyway? TP: On the grounds, as I understand it, that the reversal of the one observer immediately entails that of the other, so that the change from red to blue in the spectroscopes has to be simultaneous for both observers. ENP: But they plainly aren’t! How could they be? TP: Well, all I can say is that there are those among us who think that way, for what reason I don’t know. You’d have to ask them. But now I’d like to know how you extra-terrestrials quantify these effects. How, for instance, do these relational time-differences bear on the motions of ordinary bodies? We terrestrials are told that our constant c places an absolute upper limit on all physical speeds of motion and interaction. Is there such a limit in your extraterrestrial way of thinking? ENP: No. As we see it, an object may, in principle, travel from A to B in whatever time it likes – in next to no time at all if it has the resources to do so. However, as seen by the observer of that motion – the one in whose view the points A and B are stationary – the time of that same motion, which we will say is $t_P$ as registered by the travelling observer, is the geometrical resultant of those two coexclusive (i.e., orthogonal) component time-measures, s/c and $t_P$. Thus, $t_R$ is, by Pythagoras, equal to the sum of $(s/c)^2$ and ($t_P$)-squared. Here it can be seen that as the real time $t_P$ of the motion approaches zero, so the apparent, or observational time, $t_R$ approaches s/c. In other words, as the true speed $V = s/t_P$ approaches infinity, so the observed, or ‘apparent’ speed $v = s/t_R$ approaches c. TP: So, what would you say about claims, by some of our experimentalists, to have sent signals many times faster than light? ENP: I would say that it is strange on two counts. First, for us that would be like someone claiming that they could get more than 39.37 inches into a metre, which is plainly impossible, and second, it would imply that if what they claimed were possible, they could make the real time $t_P$ of a body run backwards. So what these experimentalists of yours must be claiming, it seems to me, is that in the true-time, or real time of these signals themselves, they may be sent as fast as you like, which is no more than a truism, so far as I am concerned. But since your c is not a speed, anyway, but an asymptotic upper limit on a certain kind of velocity, then how can anything be said to go ‘faster’ than it? It makes no sense – not in our way of thinking, at any rate. TP. From your point of view, then, our speed-limit c is no more than a myth. Some of us will be pleased to hear that, just as there are others who won’t. Anyway, I now see how differently you and we interpret the same phenomena. I must say, I find it difficult to take in, being so very different from what we are accustomed to. But, of course, one must expect that. One particular difficulty I anticipate, though, is that some of my colleagues will see these deductions of yours as purely phenomenological, or ‘kinematical’, which, for them, means no more than ‘apparent’ and therefore as having nothing to do with ‘physical reality’ as they understand it. For these people, that ‘physical reality’ of theirs is essentially dynamical. They say dynamics has to come first and kinematics (the purely observational stuff) second, as a kind of incidental ‘spin-off’ . ENP: Has to? , Why? TP: Gee, I don’t know! You might as well ask me why some people vote Conservative and others vote Liberal or Labour. It’s sheer anthropomorphism, I suppose.

ENP: Hardly a universal scientific principle, then! So let’s get on, shall we. Is there something else you would like to ask me? TP: Yes, I would like to ask you about what we call mass? How does mass configure in your system? ENP: The same as in yours, the property of a body by which it resists acceleration, that is, $m = Ft/v$. TP: But from what I now understand about your extra-terrestrial paradigm, by ‘acceleration’ you can’t mean the dynamical, or mechanical acceleration, the visceral, or ‘seat-of the-pants’ sort of acceleration that our a priori dynamicists think of as such.’ ENP: Well, no, that’s not exactly right. We don’t exclude that visceral phenomenon. It’s just that since we, as beings build differently from you, don’t have those same ‘visceral’ sensations which you find so compelling. So we have no reason to regard them as a priori in your anthropological way. For us, being more sight-orientated than tactile, we are what I suppose you would describe as natural phenomenalists, or kinematicists. That is why, in our account of physical phenomena, kinematics naturally comes first and dynamics second. Changes in velocity are easily perceived visually, and so are those accelerating affects of those bodies on one another, by which, without ever getting near those bodies, far less being having to be one of them or handle them, we can judge that dynamical property of theirs which you call their mass.’ TP: By purely kinematic comparison, as you would say.’ ENP: Yes. But of course, that does not make us ‘partisan’ against those of you who, you say, prefer to do it all in their a ‘priori-dynamicist’, or ‘materialist’ kind of way. It’s simply that we don’t have to do it that way, which means that there can’t be any cosmical compulsion to do so. TP: Point taken! Anyway, for the moment, let’s just carry on with this interview. I’d like to know more about this ‘acceleration’ of yours. How does it relate, in your naturally kinematical terms to what we think of as physical force and kinetic energy?’ ENP: Well, in the same way that we can judge the comparative masses of bodies at a distance, by the accelerating affects they exert on one another, we can also judge, by similar comparisons, their interactions in terms of those measures you call ‘energy’ and ‘force’ . Moreover, having now established that the observational speed v of a body is always smaller that its intrinsic, or ‘real’ speed V, we know that there are diminishing returns on any investment of force and energy that may be made by any one body in accelerating another. In other words, as the speed of a body with respect to another approaches the asymptotic and unreachable observational ‘speed c’ it gets more and more difficult, by any interaction between the bodies, to accelerate it further, which signifies, by definition, an increase in the relative mass of the body (that is, the measure of its observed reluctance to move in response to an applied impulse). So as the relative speed v/c of the body approaches unity, the more energy has to be spent on accelerating it, which means that the faster it goes the more the accelerating energy is turned, by definition, into mass. So, in the interactions of bodies, energy is mass and mass is energy. The two are, in those interrelations, inter-convertible. What else can I tell you?’ TP: Well, okay, so now I understand – or at least, I think I do – how you interpret physical interaction. What you call the real mass and the real time of a body are those measures of the body which the body possesses in itself – in isolation, as it were, from other bodies, while in their interactions with one another those masses and times undergo observational variation. That is certainly different from our terrestrial science. It is only in that relational scenario that time-dilation (distension and contraction, as you call it) and mass-energy-conversion exist. But by ‘relational’, here, you don’t mean merely apparent? ETN: No. of course not. If you are killed by a bullet, the fact the speed and the force of the bullet exist only in its relation to you doesn’t mean that you are only ‘apparently’ dead. What you are as an individual, separate from everything else, you really are as that individual, and what you are in your interactions and interrelations with other people and other objects in the world you also really are. There is no way that you can regard the one reality as the true reality and the other as ‘merely apparent’. TP: - as many of our self-styled ‘realists’ do, I’m afraid! But, talking of ‘interaction’, let’s get back to that subject. Now light’ you say, is not just the single uniformly one-way process that it is for us terrestrials. It is a two-component process, one of which components is an instantaneous quantum action-at-a-distance connection between an atom in the source and an atom in the receiver, and the other is a movie-like, phenomenological time-sequencing of these instantaneous connections,

propagating entropically at a rate which is (for us) c. But now, if I may, I’d like to know how you interpret what we call ‘gravitation’, the in vacuo force which pulls masses together into orbits against the opposed ‘centrifugal force which seeks to tear them out of those orbits and make the travel in straight lines.

2. GRAVITATION ENP: Well, for us, there is no such thing as that in vacuo ‘gravitational force’. Nor is there any straight-line motion of the sort you call ‘inertial’. All we have is what you would call angular momentum, in which bodies are automatically paired and balanced, and in which the only straight-line momentum mv is an infinite angular momentum $mvr$ with an infinite radius r. All finite angular momentum therefore has a finite radius r, hence is naturally orbital. The only forces to be considered are those real (i.e., measurable) ones which are required to prevent bodies from following their naturally force-free orbits. What you call the ‘weight’ of a body is thus the pressure the earth’s surface exerts of a body to prevent it from orbiting freely, which would be many miles below the earth’s surface [1]. TP: That’s a truly remarkable re-interpretation of what we call ‘gravity’. But what about other orbits, such as that of an electron around a proton, where the force that has to be exerted in order to tear them out of their orbit is many orders of magnitude greater than ‘gravity’ ?’ ENP: Again, that is all angular momentum. In the case you cite, with your ‘electron’ and ‘proton’ , the difference is that those particles are significantly spinning, with an angular momentum far greater than that of their orbital motion. For us it is all a matter of ‘wheels within wheels’ of overall-conserved angular momentum [2]. TP: Okay, sometime I will have to study the fine details of this angular momentum synthesis of yours [FN]; but for the moment, something puzzles me. On the one hand you speak of all motion and time as being relative and on the other hand, of your angular momentum connections as being, as it seems to me, absolute. How do you reconcile those two things, absolute and relative? ENP: Well do you agree that among a whole mess of objects which are moving about all over the place, with some being time-distended and others correspondingly time-contracted with respect to one another, there has to be, amongst it all, some datum state of position or motion which is the least timedistended of all.’ TP: Like the average frame known to our earth-astronomers, as the ‘sidereal frame’ , the frame of the ‘fixed stars’ . ENP: Precisely! And that, by default, is your ‘absolute’ frame - or as near to absolute as you are ever likely to get without going into metaphysics.
