Is Relativity Quantised

Speculations in Science and Technology Volume 9, Number 4, 1986.

Abstract: Is relativity quantized?

N. Vivian Pope

209 Newton Road, Burton on Trent, Staffordshire DE15 0TU, UK (Complete copies of the paper will be distributed during the EASST Conference at the Council of Europe, Strasbourg, 29 September to 1 October 1986.)

The unification of relativity and quantum theory is undoubtedly the 'Holy Grail' of the present century. It is so elusive, perhaps, because we look for it too hard. Our attempts are beginning to make nature appear more and more unnatural.

Relativity has to do with time, and the quantum with frequency. If we simply invert the time-formula of relativity and replace its controlling variable, v², with its dimensional equivalent e/m, then solving for e gives the energies of the spectrum – which is what quantum theory is all about.

The paper demonstrates this, with some further reasoning to show that these energies are discrete (quantized). This follows from the fact that the product of relative mass-energy and time gives a coefficient with the dimensions and discreteness of Planck's constant h.

When we think about it, this is what we should expect, because in observing objects (vide Heisenberg) we do so at the expense of some microscopic portion of their relative mass-energy. Since this is the energy that appears on the spectrograph, then it seems natural to think of the product of that energy and the relativistic time as giving the action-values of the spectral lines. In this case, relativity turns straight into quantum theory, without recourse to any specially contrived rationale.

IS RELATIVITY QUANTISED?

by N. VIVIAN POPE

209 Newton Road, Burton on Trent, Staffordshire, DE15 0TU, England

ABSTRACT

The aim of this short paper is to demonstrate that the formulae expressing the discreteness of action and frequency, which have been presented on empirical grounds by Planck and Rydberg, are logically deducible in a simple and straightforward way from the formulae expressing the variations in mass, length and time in special relativity.

If the argument is correct, then relativity and quantum theory are connected in such a way as needs no supplementary hypotheses. They are simply different parts of the same dimensional axiom-system.

A physical object, relativistically speaking, is a mass-energy phenomenon relative to an observer. In observing an object we partake of some microscopic portion of that mass-energy in action-units h. Since this is the energy that appears on the spectrograph then it seems natural to think of the product of that portion of relativistic mass and relativistic time as giving the action-values of the spectral lines. In this case, the theoretical connection between relativity and the quantum should be direct and axiomatic. The following is a demonstration of that direct logical connection.

Listed below are four equations from the theoretical domain of special relativity. They are not strictly axioms, since (2), (3) and (4) are standard derivatives or corollaries of the first. However, for the sake of the present argument let us regard them as axioms of a purely theoretical kind.

$$t = t_0 / \sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2} \tag{1}$$

$$n = n_0 / \sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2} \tag{2}$$

$$e_0 = n_0 c^2 \tag{3}$$

$$e = n c^2 \tag{4}$$

In these formulae, $v$ is the speed of motion of a body of initial mass $n_0$, $n$ is the relativistically increased mass in accordance with this motion, and $t$ is the correspondingly dilated period of time which initially (i.e., when $v = 0$) is $t_0$, a standard unit such as a second measured by a clock travelling with the body. The measures $e_0$ and $e$ are then the energy-equivalents, according to the theory, of the masses $n_0$ and $n$, respectively. In the first two formulae, $c$ is the conversion-constant for converting length-measure into time-measure* (i.e., $3 \times 10^8$ metres/c = 1 second) and in the second pair of formulae, $c^2$ is the constant for interconverting measures of mass and energy (i.e., $9 \times 10^{16}$ joule/$c^2$ = 1 kilogram).

Now let us consider another set of formulae. These are from a different domain entirely, the domain of experimental spectroscopy and thermodynamics. They constitute the empirical basis of quantum physics and are as follows:

$$e = h\nu \tag{5}$$

$$\nu = N^2 c R (1 - (1/m^2)) \tag{6}$$

Respectively, these are the Planck relation and the Balmer-Rydberg relation. In (5) $e$ signifies, as previously, energy but appears here as a function of frequency,\nu$, with $h$ as the constant for interconverting the two measures (i.e., $6.626 \times 10^{-34}$ joule/h = 1 hertz). In (6) this same frequency,\nu$, appears as a set of discrete fractions of a frequency-coefficient $cR$, called the Rydberg constant, with the controlling variables $n$ and $N$ being natural numbers.

The measures thus expressed are those found in the light-spectra of chemical elements, $n$ being the principal line-number of the spectrum

and $N$ the ordinal number, in the periodic table, of the element in question.

At first sight, then, there appears to be little similarity between the two sets of formulae. The one set, for instance, is theoretical (albeit well-authenticated in that regard) while the other set is empirical. The first set has to do with the mechanical motions of ponderable bodies, while the second deals with frequencies in the spectrum of pure light-energy. Moreover, in the second set of formulae, due to the fact that the controlling variables $n$ and $N$ are natural numbers, the values of $\nu$ are discrete, or quantised. For example, if $N$ is taken as unity and $n$ takes values in the sequence 2, 3, 4, ..., then the values of $\nu$ become $(\frac{3}{4})cR$, $(\frac{5}{8})cR$, $(\frac{15}{16})cR$, ..., which expresses the frequency-values of the spectral lines in the Lyman (i.e., basic) series for hydrogen. If $N$ takes values in the sequence 2, 3, 4, ..., then the sequences $n$ for each value of $N$ give the Lyman series for (ionised) helium (\text{He}^+$), lithium (\text{Li}^{++}$), beryllium (\text{Be}^{+++}$), ..., and so on up the atomic scale. The relativistic formulae, by contrast, yield continuous quantities $t$ and $n$ - or at least, so we generally suppose. This is because $t$ and $n$ may be any multiple (integer or non-integer) of $t_0$ and $n_0$, respectively, with $\nu$, the controlling variable, being continuous.

However, from a purely axiomatic point of view, the two sets of formulae are similar insofar as they both deal with energy and time (in the latter case with the inverse of time, or frequency). So far as quantisation is concerned, the difference is easily removed by supposing that $t$ and $n$ and hence $\nu$ in (1) and (2) take rational values instead of all real values; that is, by supposing that $t$, $n$ and $\nu$ are discrete rather than continuous quantities. In (1), for instance, the amount of time-dilation may be made as small as we wish by choosing some arbitrary unit $t_0/N$, where $N$ is some natural number, of which unit $t$ is then some integer multiple $n$. A corresponding choice of unit $n_0/N$ in (2), with the now discrete values of $\nu$ common to both formulae then produces the same multiple $n$ in (2) as in (1). Under this hypothesis, (1) and (2) can be restated as:

$$n(t_0/N) = (t_0/N) / \sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2} \tag{7}$$

$$n(n_0/N) = (n_0/N) / \sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2} \tag{8}$$

Multiplying these formulae together then gives

$$n^2 n_0 t_0 / N^2 = (n_0 t_0 / N^2) / (1 - (v/c)^2) \tag{9}$$

From (3) we have $\epsilon_0 / \kappa_0 = c^2$. Multiplying (9) by $\epsilon_0 / \kappa_0$ produces:

$$n^2 \epsilon_0 t_0 / N^2 = (\epsilon_0 t_0 / N^2) / (1 - (v/c)^2) \tag{10}$$

Inverting and solving for $N^2 (v/c)^2 / t_0$ gives:

$$N^2 (v/c)^2 / t_0 = (N^2 \epsilon_0 / (\epsilon_0 t_0)) (1 - (1/n^2)) \tag{11}$$

In this formula we note that there are three coefficients: one for energy (i.e.,\epsilon_0$), another for action (\epsilon_0 t_0$) and the third for frequency ($1/t_0$). This gives us an a priori, or purely definitional, relationship between the measures energy, action and frequency, arising out of the relativising of time and mass-energy. For instance, if we denote the term on the left of this last equation by the appropriate frequency-symbol $\nu$, and the energy $N^2 \epsilon_0 (1 - (1/n^2))$ by $\epsilon$ (signifying the same energy as in the relativistic formula (4)) then (11) becomes:

$$\nu = \epsilon / \epsilon_0 t_0 \tag{12}$$

And if, further, we give to the action-expression $\epsilon_0 t_0$ the empirical value of Planck's constant $h$, then we have

$$\epsilon = h\nu \tag{13}$$

precisely as in the quantum formula (5).

All we have to do now is to give to the frequency-coefficient $1/t_0$, on the right of (11), the value of the empirical Rydberg constant $cR$ and (11) becomes:

$$\nu = N^2 cR (1 - (1/n^2)) \tag{14}$$

which gives us a relativistic facsimile of the quantum formula (6).

In this demonstration there has been recourse to nothing but non-arbitrary dimensional substitutions and legitimate transformational procedures. If the argument is correct then it appears that despite their differences in all experimental and other circumstantial respects the two domains, the relativistic and the quantum, are logically connected. It appears, in other words, that the two traditionally separate areas form parts of a single dimensional axiom-system in which the quantisation of action follows from the relativising of action's components, mass-energy and time.
