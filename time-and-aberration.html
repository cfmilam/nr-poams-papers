Time? – Gentlemen, Please! Why do we have to wax metaphysical about time? What else is time, but one of the three fundamental measures of physical process, length, time and mass, each being inseparable from the other two And in the way that measuring-rods or tape-measures measure length, so the instrument for measuring time is a clock. While length is what we measure with instruments such as rods and tape-measures, and mass with weighing-scales and dynamometers, so time is what we measure with clocks and other chronometric devices. What can possibly be puzzling about that? Yet people say things like, how did time come about? How did it start? When did it start? At what time did it emerge? How quickly or slowly? What went on before it? … and so on. If we ask questions like that, then how can we not be puzzled. Ask silly questions and you get silly answers. To ask such questions and try to answer them may be all very well as a kind of intellectual ‘Trivial Pursuit’. But now let’s get real about it. Take the simplest case of a pair of clocks ticking at different rates. There is no logical contradiction in saying that at one instant the clocks are both set to read zero and then, at another, later instant, the one clock registers one second and the other reads, say, two seconds. In that case, which of them is right? How else can that be decided except with reference to some other clock measuring some standard rate by which the rates of those two clocks are judged? And what is it that uniquely measures that standard rate? What else but the statistical norm among all the other clocks arbitrarily selected as the standard for measuring the rates of clocks and physical processes generally? Who, then, decides that statistical norm? Who else, we reply, but oneself – in communication of course with other observers in an accredited social group who agree on some arbitrary standard? If this is not enough, then who else shall we turn to? God? What are we to conclude from all this but that time is completely and utterly relative in the sense of Mach, SR and POAMS [1]. Time is, of course, a succession of instants, of present moments. Is this present moment confined to just one point of observation? If not, then how far into the space around one does the present moment, or now, extend? In POAMS, this present moment is the overall balance of angular momentum as experienced by any observer at any particular point in his orbital trajectory, as this is defined in relation to the orbits of other bodies in the overall-conserved angular momentum nexus. This is the ‘felt’ connection that manifests itself as the resistance to practically every movement we make. It is the phenomenon of the reluctance exhibited by all bodies to start or stop their motion, that is commonly called ‘inertia’. This defines the universal now of things, which is independent of the readings and different rates of clocks and other time-processes, any contrived synchrony among which is soon destroyed by the timedilations that their prolific motions and changes in motion incur relatively to the norm and to one another.

As Phipps picturesquely puts it (quoting what Mach is alleged to have said): ‘When the subway (train) jerks, it is the fixed stars that throw you down.’ Now plainly, if ‘inertia’ is the felt instantaneous presence of other bodies in the universe, then what can that felt correlation with the motions of other bodies possibly have to do with the readings of clocks? In that situation of the simultaneous all-over, felt presence of other free moving bodies, the readings of clocks may be as fortune finds them. Neither in SR nor in POAMS does there have to be any permanent synchrony among clocks or any other chronometric devices – or, indeed, among the living-rates of organisms and ‘lifetimes’ of inorganic processes. If ‘inertia’, then, is to be taken in the Machian way as manifesting the instantaneous presence of distant bodies (in systems of correlated angular momentum, according to POAMS), then the instantaneous correlation of the felt universe and the time-synchrony of clock-readings are by no means the same. By contrast, let us now consider the seen universe. Astronomers after Bradley describe a phenomenon they call aberration in which, by subtracting from the time of seeing an astronomical object its distance-time s/c, what they regard as the ‘real’ position of that object at the instant the light left it is pinpointed in relation to other objects behind its apparent or observed position. (The method used is that of measuring the difference in angle between the direction in which a telescope has to be pointed in order actually to observe the star and the direction which was judged (retrospectively) to be the star’s true, instantaneous position.). However, at the instant that the star is observed, its ‘present position’ among the other stars at that retrospectively judged instant is long past, so that retrospectively judged instant cannot be the same as the one that is immediately felt by the observer. In other words, as Phipps emphasises, the felt universe and the seen universe are not the same. In POAMS that distinction is maintained by referring to the felt connection as correlation (UICAAD) [2] and the seen as UIAAAD [3]. As POAMS describes it, the seen connection consists of two dimensionally orthogonal at-a-distance components, compounded in a movie-like way according to the Pythagorean space-time equation. In utilising the phenomenon of aberration to pinpoint what they extrapolate as the ‘instantaneous positions’ of the stars, what astronomers do, in effect, is to subtract from the observer-time tR in the Pythagorean equation, the distance-time s/c to obtain the proper-time tP of the event, which (for light) is zero, viz.: [tR2 - (s/c)2] = tP = 0. The phenomenon of aberration thus provides observational evidence of the true proper-time-instantaneous positions of the stars relatively to one another in an overallconserved angular momentum nexus corresponding to their instantly felt (UICAAD) positions. It also provides, of course, observational evidence of the earth’s orbital motion with respect to that angular momentum frame as a whole; that is, on the basis of measuring the angle of aberration. So now we have, in POAMS, a logical tie-up between the felt and the seen presence of other bodies in the universe. A correct plot, therefore, of the positions of bodies at a single instant in the overall angular momentum nexus is as defined by the objective determination of tR – (s/c) = 0, which is the same in all systems of reference, regardless of relative motion.

With this analysis in mind, let us consider how these principles should apply in practice. Take, for example, an orbiting spaceship. In its coasting, ‘zero-gee’ state, the passengers are floating freely around in the ship’s cabin, unaware of any connection whatsoever with those distant bodies whose presence nevertheless determines, via the angular momentum nexus, the trajectories of the spaceship and of their own bodies. Then, with the motors suddenly switched on, a torsion is applied to the ship which changes its orbital angular momentum. And it is at that moment that the passengers become aware of the correlating angular momentum influence of those distant bodies that had kept their own bodies in their undisturbed trajectories – until they strike the cabin wall, from which point on, their bodies will share in the new, changed angular momentum trajectory of the spaceship. This immediately felt influence of distant bodies on the passengers, when the spaceship’s trajectory changes, manifests itself in the passengers being flung across the cabin. This does not imply some mediating influence travelling from the passengers to the ‘fixed stars’ and back, or vice versa. It is a completely unmediated connection corresponding to what Phipps describes as the influence of the ‘felt’ universe in contrast to the one that is ‘seen’ – the one being immediate (at the point of the felt jerk of the subway-train or space-ship) and the other being time-delayed. This immediately felt connection with the distant universe also corresponds to what Mach and the Graneaus call ‘inertia’, except that in POAMS, inertia is a tendency to remain in force-free orbits rather than in Newton’s force-free straight lines. Of course, most people are aware that we do not see distant events immediately, but only after the delayed time which is due to the (classically conceived) ‘speed of light’, as manifest in the phenomenon of aberration. So let us assume that the passengers, having felt their disturbed correlation with distant bodies in being ‘flung’ against the cabin wall, now look for the compensatory effect of that same disturbance of angular momentum on the orbit of some distant body that is relatively nearer than the others. (We may imagine that at that location some suitably sensitive instrument is designed to detect the effect by triggering some visual signal that is visible to the spaceship passengers.) However, being au fait with Einstein’s Special Theory, or with POAMS – which, in this present context is practically the same – those spaceship passengers will know that in their common observational frame, all distances between any one body and another are also times, such that every metre of distance in space is 3.3 nanoseconds of observer time. With that same knowledge of relativity (in POAMS as in SR), they will also know that no physical influence – that is, no action (as opposed to correlation) – can travel from any one place to another in observer-space in an observer-time less than the distance s divided by that constant, c. Logically, then, it follows that the immediately felt action effect of the change in the correlated motion of the ship and its passengers, cannot be conveyed to those distant bodies in observer-space in an observer-time less than s/c, which, if it were less, would be in direct contradiction to both POAMS and SR. And then, when that one-way, observationally time-delayed effect occurs at the distant detector, the response of that detector will be an event occurring at another distance s in the observer-space of the passenger, who will therefore see that distant reaction-event only after a further delayed-time s/c. Altogether, then, in the observational frame of the passengers, there are two distances s involved, hence two distance-times s/c. Starting at that basic commonsense level, we now turn our attention to the microphenomenal level down to which angular momentum analyses into irreducible quantum elements h/2. These are what have traditionally been called ‘atoms’. They are not single, self-sufficient bits of mass, as Democritus envisaged them, but pairs of masses. Each pair (traditionally called an ‘electron’ and ‘proton’) forms a balanced angular momentum relation which, in POAMS is called a quantum accumulator, storing and exchanging action in discrete quantum units h over macroscopically defined distances s in the form commonly known as light. This is in a compound, movie-like process in which the proper-timeinstantaneous distances s, in those atomic interactions into which all macrophenomenal interactions ultimately analyse, occur like the ‘stills’ (still frames) in a movie, in observational time-sequences tR according to the Pythagorean space-time equation. The geodesics thus projected in observation between the sources of lightsignals and their points of observation correspond to the ‘light-rays’ in terms of which aberration is conventionally explained; and the ‘particulate’, or ultimately quantised nature of these interactions, as manifest in certain kinds of experiments, correspond to what Einstein called ‘photons’. (This, according to POAMS, is a misnomer, insofar as it suggests little corpuscles of light travelling in a bullet-like way, making complete mysteries of the way light-geodesics behave in situations like that of the two-slit experiment, where it is the geodesics or quantum stills as such, not telepathically guided quantum missiles, geometrically extended between source and screen that create patterns of wave-like ‘interference’.) It seems, then, that in POAMS we have a natural commonsense-logical tie-up between the felt and the seen presence of other bodies in the universe – in effect, between what are traditionally called ‘gravitation’, ‘electrodynamics and ‘inertia’. This also ‘unifies’ all the various mediatory ‘fields’ and ‘ethers’ by dispensing with them altogether in favour of a logically cleanshaven interpretation of actual phenomena.
