Angular Synthesis

N. Vivian Pope

An Angular Momentum Synthesis of Gravitational and Electrostatic Forces N. V. Pope Correspondence address: ‘Llys Alaw’, 10 West End, Penclawdd, Swansea SA4 3YX, UK. E-mail: n.v.pope@swansea.ac.uk Website: http://members.tripod.co.uk/Vivpope/

ABSTRACT Are all the truly radical, flat-earth-to-round-earth and earth-centred-to-sun-centred types of scientific solutions now exhausted? Are all the revolutions in that direction now completed? It would surely be calamitous for science if, short of a complete understanding of nature, all efforts of that radical kind were curtailed due to an over-attachment to current conventions.

Some of these conventions are very deep-seated. Since the time of Democritus, it has been assumed that material particles basically float about in space, independently of one another, either at rest or in straight-line trajectories of the sort described in Newton’s first law of motion.

The fact that no particles are ever observed to behave in that way is traditionally explained as due to various kinds of influence that the particles exert on one another across the void on account of certain ‘powers’ they are assumed to possess, of static attraction and repulsion which oppose their otherwise natural tendency either to remain where they are or else travel in straight lines.

Since the only evidence there can ever be of these static ‘powers’ and the ‘influences’ they are supposed to project across space is in the observable motions of bodies, we explore the possibility of dispensing with these traditional expedients and deal with the motions direct, in the context of a holistically integrated system of motion.

In this system, the positions and motions of particles are fundamentally correlated, so that the motion of every free-moving particle is immediately balanced with that of each and every other, in an overall-conserved nexus of angular momentum interrelations.

We demonstrate that angular momentum is sufficient in itself to explain the orbital trajectories of particles without involving the traditional in vacuo ‘forces’ of ‘gravitation’ and ‘electrostatic’ attraction and repulsion. The result is a theory of pure, unmediated action-at-a-distance as the datum state or substratum of physical interaction in which disturbances are transmitted dynamically at an instant and kinematically in time-delayed sequences of those instants at speeds limited to that of the so-called ‘speed of light’.

1. Introduction What are gravitational and electrostatic orbital systems without field forces? Answer: pure angular momentum, what else? What, then, are gravitational theory and electrodynamics if not, in the end, just pure mechanics? But what do we mean, here, by ‘pure mechanics’? Our answer is: Newton’s laws of motion minus his Universal Law of Gravitation, which we regard as not only redundant, but also as both unempirical and wrong.

2. Gravitation Newton’s first law of motion states: Law 1. Every body perseveres in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line except in so far as it is compelled to change that state by forces impressed on it. This may be regarded as a definition of force [1].

Now when this law first appeared, someone should have pointed out that no bodies are ever observed to behave in that way, so that the statement is, in that sense, purely oracular and unempirical. Not even a ball on the flattest-ever billiard table could be seen to move in that way if the table was large enough for the curve of the earth’s surface to be taken into account. What is actually observed is not that bodies in force-free motion in space move independently of one another in straight lines with self-sustaining momentum, or ‘inertia’ but that they move in such a way that each mass is automatically paired and balanced on opposite ends of a common moment-arm with each and every other, in overall-conserved angular momentum interrelations. In this way, the motion of every body in the system is coordinated, holistically, with the motions of all others. Newton’s independent, straight-line momentum, mv, customarily called ‘inertial motion’ is then, implicitly, the special case of an angular momentum $mvr$ with r at theoretical infinity (a circumference of infinite radius being the same as a straight line). But of course, with r at infinity, so is the angular momentum $mvr$, from which it follows that for all finite angular momentum, r is also finite, so that the force-free trajectories of the bodies involved are naturally orbital. Only at vast distances from one another do they approach the straightness of the trajectories described in Newton’s first law. Needless to say, there are no ‘forces’ required to keep these automatically paired and balanced bodies in force-free motion. What, then, is that force we measure on a weighing-scale and which Newton is said to have observed in the falling apple? Bodies, if allowed, orbit one another in the way they do in accordance with their angular momentum, as described in formula (2), which is sufficient in itself to describe orbital motion without raising any question of forces, either of centripetal or centrifugal acceleration reaching across the vacuum [2].

This fits Aristotle’s description of force-free (i.e., orbital) motion as naturally cyclic. As he argued, all force-free motion is, by definition, un-interrupted. Such motion in a straight line, he said, is therefore either infinitely long, which is unreal, or else it begins somewhere and ends somewhere, in which case it is interrupted and therefore not free.

Thus, the eminent logician concluded, all free motion is naturally cyclic, which means that it has no distinguishable beginning nor end. And since it is free, then by definition there is no question of any ‘forces’ being responsible for those orbits.

That is to say, all forces supposed to be acting on those free orbits are ruled out by definition. In that same way, what our formula (2) describes is natural, force-free orbital angular momentum. This is pure unmediated, instantaneous action-at-a-distance, in which the influence of each body on the other is instantaneous and reciprocal by definition.

By contrast, in the Newtonian account, orbital motion is accredited to an assumed ‘universal force of gravitational attraction’, an invisible static drawing-power which all bodies are supposed mysteriously to exert on one another across the void. As the Penguin Dictionary of Physics puts it [3]: ‘In 1687, Newton presented his law of universal gravitation, according to which every particle attracts every other particle with a force F given by $F = Gm_1m_2/x^2$, where $m_1$ and $m_2$ are the masses of the two particles a distance x apart (and) G is the gravitational constant.’ By contrast, in our ‘Aristotelean’ account, true gravitational force is not an inscrutable in vacuo force which is required to draw bodies together into orbits around one another.

It is the actually manifest and measurable force of exertion required to remove a body from its natural orbit or in any other way to disturb that natural force-free motion. When the disturbing force is

The fact that this law does not actually specify that these bodies A and B have to be actually physically touching has been exploited by a group of modern physicists, notably, Peter and Neal Graneau, Tom Phipps (Jr.), Andre Assis and many others, including (in their train) this present author and his colleague, Anthony Osborne.

In the view of these people, that same law of instantaneous and reciprocal interaction applies every bit as much to bodies which are separated by large distances in vacuo as to bodies whose distances apart are zero. In other words, for these physicists, Newton’s third law is as much a principle of what they call far action (from the German, Fernwirkung, meaning ‘distant effect’) as of physical contiguity.

Typical of the members of this sub-group of instantaneous-action-at-a-distance (IAAAD) theorists is that although some of them believe in the delayed-time mediation of ‘electrodynamical’ interaction by the customary ‘fields’ and ‘ethers’, they eschew this kind of conception at the level of traditional ‘gravitation’ and ‘inertia’, which they distinguish as a kind of substratum of pure and instantaneous, unmediated action-at-a-distance.

We propose, then, that it is this overall instantaneous interconnection that all bodies have with one another, in accordance with Newton’s (distance-extrapolated) third law, within the angular momentum nexus as a whole, that gives them their characteristic inertia, which is their reluctance, proportional to their mass, to be moved or prevented from moving in response to our externally applied efforts.

This is the well-known Mach’s Principle which Phipps reports Mach as having once described in the following words: ‘When the subway (train) jerks, it is the fixed stars that throw you down’ [5]. However, if the angular momentum nexus were a complete continuum, then to move one body would be simultaneously to move all the others, so that there could be no motion within the continuum, hence no angular momentum as such. It follows, then, that angular momentum, to be mobile, cannot be a continuum; that is to say, all its instantaneous quantum

However, continuity and fluidity are not necessarily opposed. For instance, there is a wellknown game called ‘tag’ or ‘touch’ in which each touch is immediate and reciprocal in accordance with Newton’s third law, as stated above, without in any way restricting the fluidity of the game.

This is because those touchings between A and B, B and C …, et seq., do not take place all at the same time, but only sporadically. The touching relation in this case is what logicians call intransitive. In the game, there is no loss of overall continuity, as there would be if any one of the players, after being touched, just walked away and touched no-one else.

In that same way, there is no loss of continuity in an angular momentum system that is quantised, because in that system, every atom is paired and balanced with some other atom or atoms, with no possibility of any one of those atoms being paired and balanced with just nothing.

In that sense, both the absolute continuity and fluidity of overall angular momentum are assured. The macrophysical connection Mach envisaged between the ‘fixed stars’ and the subway train is therefore instantaneous and reciprocal according to Newton’s third law, not in terms of any single monolithic interaction, but only in terms of countless numbers of discrete and intransitive, overlapping quantum instantaneities.

In that way, the moment-arm on which a pair of macroscopic bodies like the moon and earth are balanced consists not of a single, solidly enduring strand, but of statistical numbers of quantum relays in which, as soon as any one of those fleeting connections finishes, another takes over. In that way, continuity is maintained without loss of fluidity, which is why the instantaneous far action of Mach is inertia, not stasis.

The angular momentum nexus is therefore as fluid as needs be to avoid the Eleatic ‘gridlock’ which is the dire consequence of all continuity theories, such as those of Weber and Helmholtz and Feynman and Wheeler – and, of course, Einstein’s ‘underlying space-time-continuum’.

4. Electromagnetic propagation Now, plainly, an action between bodies that are separated in space cannot be both instantaneous and time-delayed. Or can it? We usually think of the surface of a lake as present all over, all at once, at every instant during the propagation of a wave across it. But of course, in the case of an angular momentum nexus, there is no interconnecting continuum analogous to the surface of the lake. What, then, is it, in the case of angular momentum, that facilitates the propagation of those energies we call ‘light’ at the measured rate c? In the void separating the interacting atoms there is nothing to convey those energies in the form that physicists, after Huygens, envisaged as ‘waves’. What, then, can those resonant interactions be, between atoms, as manifest in instances of ‘interference’, ‘diffraction’ and so on, that are traditionally regarded as ‘wavular’? What else, we reply, but direct and unmediated resonant interactions between the atoms themselves in accordance with Newton’s extended third law – i.e., regardless of distance, as more and more neo-Newtonian physicists now understand it. In other words, for angular momentum to be conserved, in order for one atomic pair of micromasses $m_e$ and $M_p$ (see above) to lose some quantum of angular momentum $nh/2\pi$, some other pair in some other atom, somewhere else, must gain precisely that same amount in order for the interaction to take place. There is no limbo of ‘space in itself’ or ‘vacuum in itself’ in which that interaction may languish between the one atom and the other, for perhaps centuries (if not forever), in anticipation of some ultimate consummation of interaction. This, of course, rules out not only ‘waves in vacuo’ but also ‘photons’ conceived as bits of energy thrown off ad lib into the void by some atoms in the mere hope of being consumed by others, all in a completely hit-and-miss, take-it-or leave-it, fashion.

What is it, then, if it is neither travelling waves nor particles – nor ‘wave-particles’ (‘photons’) – that are conveyed in these instantaneous atomic interactions? Our answer is that it is action, the product of the orbital energy and period of the subatomic micromasses, in units of Planck’s constant h (as opposed to $h/2\pi$).

And where does the delayed-time speed $s/t$ of this atomic interaction come in? To answer that, let us think of the distinction we normally make, in cinematography, between the way in which objects are separated in the individual photographs, called ‘stills’, and in the sequences of those stills, which is called ‘action’ in a movie.

That is to say, in watching a movie we know that throughout the whole scenario there is both instantaneity, in the dimensions of the stills, and time-delay, in the movements of the characters and other objects, as the action unfolds. And, of course, there has to be a limit to the speeds at which objects move about in that movie scenario.

That limit is the time (which has to be finite) that it takes for one cinematic quantum, or still, to be replaced by the next – sporadically, of course, in the case of atomic interaction. But why, in the case of these atomic interactions, are the speeds so strictly limited to that of the peculiar value of the constant c, of $2.99792458 \times 10^{8}$ metres to the second?

The reason is simply that this is the consequence of our circumstantial history in which these sorts of units, of metres for length and seconds for time, were selected arbitrarily without our modern empirical knowledge given to us by Römer, et al., of how these dimensions are connected.

Had we been so miraculously fortunate as to have chosen those units, originally, such that their ratio would have been unity, then all our speculations about ‘luminiferous ethers’ and ‘ether winds’, ‘fields’, ‘light-waves’, ‘wave-particles’ and so on, need never have arisen.

Instead, ‘instantaneous’ and ‘delayed’ distant interaction might naturally have been conceived as no more nor less than the dynamical and kinematical components of the same phenomenological interaction.

5. Summary This paper has to be little more than a condensed description of our angular momentum synthesis, the full mathematical exposition of which is being published elsewhere. Its writing is prompted by the philosophical truism that nature is one thing and our ideas of it another. Every view we may ever have of nature, even through our clearest microscopes, telescopes or whatever, is also through our minds; and if our minds are clouded, then so is every instrument of observation we may ever employ. Unfortunately, even in our most purist approaches to science our idea-systems all too often settle for an agenda of their own. This is to preserve an habitual order of association among themselves which all too often, far from aiding our perceptions, makes it so that in looking through our microscopes and telescopes we might as well be looking through the bottoms of stout bottles. At such times, the cosy satisfaction with our habitual idea-systems may need to be shattered for any clarity to be restored. That is, the scientific agenda may need to be changed from that of seeking to preserve and embellish our conceptual conventions towards sweeping aside as many of them as may be necessary to let in some light.

Some of these obscuring conventions have now been revealed. They are, in particular, the attribution of metaphysical (i.e., directly unobservable) ‘powers’ to bodies, such as that of Newton’s purely static power of attraction which he called ‘gravitational’ and Coulomb’s powers of ‘attraction and repulsion’ which he called ‘electrostatic’. These working-hypotheses which were contrived by those undoubted geniuses to fit the needs of their time have now long outlived their conceptual usefulness. Since the only evidence that can be produced for the existence of these ‘powers’ is that of their capacity to affect the mechanical movements of bodies and since, in angular momentum systems, the mechanical equations prove sufficient in themselves to explain orbital motion, we have sought to remove those ‘powers’ from the physical description of motion and consign them respectfully to history, where they so eminently belong. We may add, however, that from the way this reasonable suggestion has been received so far, it seems about as welcome as if this mobilising of ‘Occam’s razor’ was not so much for shaving-off unnecessary and outworn hypotheses as for slashing academic throats. ■
