The Complete Physics Heresy (2)

The Complete Physics Heresy

Viv Pope 'Llys Alaw', 10 West End, Penclawdd, Swansea. U.K., SA4 3YX

The shift from Ptolemaic to modern, post-Copernican cosmology bears witness to the fact that a system of ideas that is bequeathed to us at any stage by the circumstances of history, no matter how prestigious and well-institutionalised it becomes, is not always, nor necessarily, the best there is. The same applies to systems of thought, or paradigms, in physics. This paper argues the feasibility of a wholesale (in some cases, the long-awaited) paradigm shift from the customary analog conception of fundamental physics into a relativistic, quantum-digital mode. Such a shift offers a novel solution to the problem of how to unify the various 'field' concepts with which physics is historically encumbered. Since the common factor in all these concepts is that of angular momentum (orbital, spin, oscillatory or whatever), then the logical solution to that problem of unification would be to dispense with those 'fields' entirely, in favour of an overall-conserved angular momentum nexus that fits, without further elaboration, the phenomena that those 'fields' have been traditionally invoked to explain The fact that angular momentum is both non-local and quantised makes it the natural choice of datum in the recommended switch from the traditional analog paradigm of physics to a consistently digitalised alternative.

The same paradigm switch also introduces a novel way of deducing, from a 'Galilean' account of motion, the formula for relativistic time-dilation and, as a direct consequence, the Balmer Rydberg formula for spectral frequencies. This is in terms of proper-time-instantaneous action-at-a-distance instead of traditional 'electrodynamics'.

1. GENERAL INTRODUCTION

That the physical world is composed of atoms is generally agreed. Also generally agreed is that it is the interactions between these atoms that lie at the root of all physical phenomena. What is not generally agreed is what these interactions are. That is to say, there is no firm consensus as to how they are to be conceived and properly described.

In some cases these interactions are conceived as mediated by intervening agencies variously described as 'fields' or 'ethers' This is by analogy with the way in which, on ordinary levels of experience, objects interact via media such as air or water – that is to say, by means of 'waves' of one kind or another. Let this be called the analog, or traditional paradigm of fundamental physics. Antithetically opposed to this is what may be distinguished as the digital paradigm, according to which, on the most fundamental levels of physics there can be no processes analogous to those observed on ordinary mechanical-engineering type levels. At this 'digital' level of pure physical data the whole analog scenario of physics, whether it be that described by a Galileo, a Newton or an Einstein, is a phenomenon generated out of information-statistical sequences of quantum-events, like the discrete events that make up what we see on a TV or cinema screen.

From the point of view of this digital paradigm it is as nonsensical to think about 'waves' mediating interactions between atoms as to project the world of a film cartoon into the interstices of the TV pixels or cinematographic frames. The language used to describe physical phenomena and that used to describe the events into which these are ultimately analysed simply do not gel. Nor is there any logical reason why they should. They are what the philosopher Wittgenstein called different 'language-games'. To confuse these, he said, is to fail to understand the way language works, to commit a 'category-mistake'. The view to be developed in this paper is that the language-systems appropriate to these two different levels of physics, the analog and the digital, should be kept clearly distinguished and that our failure to do this has created a veritable Tower of Babel of pure jargon in which we talk about atomic processes as though they took place in the same observational space that separates ordinary physical objects.

What is becoming increasingly plain, however, is that behind all this jargon we still know virtually nothing about how atoms interrelate. The more we experiment the more confused we get and the more contrived becomes the language used to report our findings. As a result, any prospect of logical integration of scientific thinking has been all but completely abandoned, leaving experimentalists about as philosophically organised as woodworm in a beam.

However, there are, as always, philosophical souls unwilling to accept any dogma of unrestrained eclecticism. To these heretical few we suggest that the increasing confusedness of the collected experimental evidence and the abstruseness of the language used to describe it should now be seen clearly for what it is, a problem to be systematically addressed as such. We propose that the solution should be sought, not by devising more and more experiments (there are already enough of these) but by a logical analysis of the experimental results we already have. In other words, we consider that it is now time for our physics to mobilise Occam's razor in shaving off the hoary accretion of hypotheses which have now long outserved their original circumstantial purposes.

Take, for instance, the analog notion of 'fields' as mediators of interaction between elementary particles. Prior to the establishment of the electromagnetic field-theory of Faraday and Maxwell, the formulation of electrodynamics by Weber was taken as successfully describing the relevant experimental information.¹ Weber's approach to electrodynamics was mainly empirical, that is to say, based on information gleaned in the actual detection and measuring of the energies manifest in the behaviour of the particles involved. For him, hypotheses as to what goes on in vacuo between the sources and sinks of these measured energies were of little consequence. (Hypotheses non fingo as Newton used to say.) In contrast, the approach of Faraday and Maxwell was almost entirely concerned with the invisible conduction of those energies in space. The choice of this more theoretical 'field-conduction' approach was then, it seemed, sealed by Hertz's acclaimed 'discovery' of the invisible electromagnetic waves predicted on that hypothesis.

This, of course, was all in keeping with the precept, originated by Empedocles and promoted by Galileo and Newton, of light being something that travels in space with a finite mechanical velocity. What the Hertzian discovery seemed to confirm was that this velocity was that of a wave-like sequence of alternate electric and magnetic field-disturbances in an 'æther', conceived by analogy with the way wave-disturbances travel in air or water. The 'electric' and 'magnetic' vectors involved in that theoretical process of wave-propagation were (and still are) conventionally conceived as orientated successively at right-angles to each other in space and to a line abstractly joining the emitter and absorber – 'abstractly' because in the electrodynamics of Faraday and Maxwell there is no longitudinal vector to the wave. In that orthodox electrodynamical rendering, the wave propagates in vacuo by means of this procession of lateral vectors, in a mechanical-causal, 'bumpety-bump' fashion, at the finite and constant speed c. This is conceptually problematical, not only because in a vacuum there is, by definition, nothing there to wave (i.e., be displaced) but also because according to conventional special relativity (SR) there is an absolute upper velocity-limit c on all causal interconnections, so that at every stage in the wave's motion the source, the sink and the wave are all completely unconnected – in effect, existentially dissociated. That is to say, according to this Faraday-Maxwell-Einstein (FME) wave-motion analog, when the wave leaves the source it has to be conceived as carrying on in the void, regardless of whether, in the meantime, the source and sink may have been annihilated. Nothing need exist either ahead of the wave or behind it. This is what is meant in modern physics by the completely nonsensical notion of the 'locality' of that process of wave-propagation in the FME analog.

The trouble is that this localised wave-propagation hypothesis makes a complete mystery of certain natural phenomena. For instance, it fails completely to explain the results of Thomas Young's 19th century two-slit experiments on light-interference, to say nothing of the modern advances on these experiments using elementary particles. These experiments demonstrate very clearly that it is neither waves nor particles (far less 'wave-particles') that 'interfere' where they meet at the screen but the path-length differences – purely geometrically, of course – in half-units of λ = h/mc for light or h/mv for particles. Wave-propagation also fails to explain the results of the sorts of experiments and observations that are carried out by a growing number of researchers such as Aspect, Assis, Phipps, Graneau, Van Flandern, and others, which similarly demonstrate that there is a natural, non-local, interaction between distance-separated bodies which takes place directly in accordance with Newton's third law.² This glaring deficiency in the FME analog has prompted theoretical physicists such as Whitney, Evans and Vigier,³ Chubykalo and Smirnov-Rueda⁴ and others, to revise that analog and re-interpret the relevant phenomena in a manner more in keeping with the electromagnetic theory of Weber than of Faraday, Maxwell and Einstein.

From analog to digital

It is scarcely too much to say, then, that this problem of the alleged 'locality' or dissociation of events in analog space is becoming more and more recognisable as a crucial issue of modern theoretical physics. In Newtonian physics there could be no such problem. This is because in that classical rendering of natural philosophy there is no finite limit of the sort Einstein set to the speed at which bodies may interact, so there are none of those strange 'localist' or 'Einstein-separation' implications that confound modern physics. However, it seems not to have been realised, not even by Einstein – not fully, at any rate – that in his special theory 'locality' and 'non-locality' exist side by side. That is to say, as Whitney points out, in SR there are two different but complementary descriptions of velocity. One is the 'Galilean' velocity, which is the distance between A and B that a body travels in the time recorded by the body itself. The other is the 'Einsteinian' velocity, which is that same observational distance divided by the time registered by the observer of the motion. Whitney's notation (which we shall follow) for these Galilean and Einsteinian velocities is V and v, respectively. The one tends to a limit of infinity, as in classical physics, while the other tends toward the finite limit c, as in Einsteinian relativity.⁵

It seems surprising then, that in the infamous 'EPR' disagreement over whether distant interaction takes place at an infinite or a finite speed neither Einstein nor Bohr seem to have considered this simple logical solution. This is doubtless due to the persistence of the traditional analog way of thinking conceived by Maxwell and Hertz and fostered by Einstein as to how light travels in the 'void', separating electromagnetic emitters and absorbers. Nor was this helped by Einstein's interpretation of Planck's (and his own) discovery of light-quanta as mediating wave-capsules, which he called 'photons', travelling in space by analogy with ordinary projectiles. To think of waves and/or photons travelling in this independent, projectile-like (i.e., localised) way rules out any possibility of them having, in the same instance, both local and non-local components. How, for instance, is it possible to think of a material particle as having both a limited and an infinite speed, let alone the twin features of waves and particles?

With wave-motion, as ordinarily understood, things are different. A 'wave' is a kinematic phenomenon in which the wave-medium extends as a whole, both in front of and behind the wave – like a plucked string anchored at both ends while the wave travels along it. In this case it is plain commonsense that there are both local and non-local components of the phenomenon. The natural non-local component is the connection between the ends of the string and the local component is the motion of the wave along it.⁶

Normally, then, there should be no contradiction whatsoever implied by the juxtaposition of locality and non-locality in true wave-motion. What creates the difficulty is simply the sheer inappropriateness of the theoretical modification which makes the wave travel like a particle. This removes the non-local, or longitudinal, component of naturally conceived wave-motion and leaves only, nonsensically, the local component. Thus we are called upon to think of waves, in the FME way, as absurdly making their own medium to travel in as they move along.

This theoretical conflation, then, of wave and particle concepts of distant interaction has been a disaster for modern physics, to say nothing of the relation between physics and commonsense. What makes matters worse is the extent to which some physicists, unable to cope with the situation in any rational way, have seen fit to make a virtue of the confusion, claiming it as some new and 'higher' form of knowledge – which, of course, mystics of science and religion have welcomed with open arms.⁷

The source of that confusion, however, is simply our scholastic insistence on 'conserving' the intrinsically nonsensical 'wave-particle' notion of light-interaction, making the local and non-local features of distant resonant interaction seem logically antagonistic. Otherwise, without the least suggestion of conflict, we may think of the interactions between light emitters and absorbers as consisting of purely kinematic sequences of instantaneous extensions, as in the phenomenon of a wave travelling along a stretched string, or the sort of action we see in a movie, which consists of a finite-rate cinematographic sequence of instantly extended frames or 'stills'

It is to be stressed, however, that there is a vital difference between these two examples of kinematic action. In the example of the wave along a string, the action may be conceived as continuous, whereas in the cinematographic example, the action has to be conceived as quantised, or discontinuous. This essential distinction is signified, here, by the words analog to denote continuous, and digital to denote the opposite. This usage is as applied to computers and compact-disk systems, where 'digital' signifies a process that is discrete, as opposed to one that is – or is assumed to be – continuous. In relation to what follows, we also distinguish the analog continuous-wave example as kinematic as opposed to the discrete process movie example, which is cinematic. To think of light-interaction in this cinematic way is therefore to switch from the classical, analog way of thinking about light travelling in space into a digital mode in which the whole analog scenario we call nature analyses-out into finite-rate sequences of discrete, instantaneous extensions or 'quantum stills'.

A Short Philosophical Digression

It may be prudent at this stage to interpose a short philosophical digression. This is to set at rest the minds of those physicists who, not being particularly au fait with developments in modern philosophy, are disposed to imagine that there are two, and only two, competing philosophical approaches to physics, so that to reject the one is automatically to adopt the other. These adversaries are, on the one hand, the 'Positivists' of the Copenhagen school of physics and, on the other, the 'Realists' of the opposed or anti-Copenhagen school.

This, however, is a fork in physics of a branch which, in philosophy itself is now quite dead. It was a bifurcation which Descartes introduced, in the 17th century, according to which there is not one real world but two. The one he saw as the world of mindless and self-sufficient matter, existing relatively to nothing but itself. The other, in contrast, he saw as a non-material world of 'mind', consisting of nothing but our conscious thoughts and ideas. These, he reasoned, include our observations of matter which, but for the beneficence of the Creator and Coordinator of these two 'realities' and the links between them, such as our sense-organs, light and sound, would be a world of completely subjective 'illusion'. In this bogus bifurcation the most practically-minded physicists have, not surprisingly, opted for Descartes' 'material' reality, dismissing the other as not only non-material but immaterial. Those who accept this artificially truncated conception of reality are the self-styled 'Realists' of modern physics, for whom anything which seems to compromise that view (such as, e.g., the 'Positivism' of the Copenhagen school) seeks to embrace a 'subjective idealism' which threatens to turn everything into dream.⁸

Our heterodox theory, however, replaces that classical two-worlds view of matter and observation with a one-world view of physical reality based on a relativistic, or information-theoretical account of matter, space and motion.⁹ This dispels the futile conflict between 'positivism' and 'realism' and brings physics up to date with the modern developments in philosophy which have demonstrated that the dualism of Descartes, sustained by the so-called Einstein-separation between material objects and our subjective perceptions of them, is completely spurious.¹⁰

This philosophical 'digression' has been necessary so as to make it clear that what is here presented is a philosophically revised model of a physics – especially relativistic and quantum physics – that is based upon objective constructs of observational/ informational data rather than upon the customary analog-type projections. A quantum datum, as we present it, is therefore not split into a distance-time separated material 'beginning' and observational 'end', as in the 'Realist' FME and 'photon' interpretations of light-quanta. It is an immediate connection between objects and our direct or instrumental observations of them in which beginning and end are indistinguishable. In other words, it is an event which the parties to the interaction share in common, the sort of event that in the jargon of current experimental physics is called 'non-local'.

The essential point about this, for what follows, is that this digital, or information-based (i.e., stochastic) approach to physics makes it no more sensible to try to explain the 'interactions' between one datum and the next, in the usual analog way, than to speculate about what sorts of mechanical or chemical 'interactions' there may be between the letters of the alphabet in the construction of language, or what 'hidden mechanisms' of the pieces explain a game of chess.

2. GALILEAN QUANTUM RELATIVITY

Our deductions so far have been based purely on Galilean dynamics – 'Galilean' because in some ways this radically new theory branches out, logically speaking, from nearer the roots of the evolutionary tree of scientific ideas than even the 'Gravitational' theory of Newton. In that case, needless to say, it logically precedes those theories which have taken their departure from the Newtonian gravitational theory, namely, the Electrodynamical 'force-field' theories of Faraday and Maxwell and the Einsteinian theories of Relativity, both Special and General.

However, since the basis of our heterodox theory is angular momentum, which is the same whether expressed relativistically or non-relativistically, no clash with essential relativity may be anticipated. Indeed, in the following sections it may be seen, not only how very simply and logically the Einsteinian formulae for relativistic time-dilation and mass-energy follow from this neo-Galilean (cinematic) interpretation of motion, but also how directly these deductions lead to the well-known Balmer-Rydberg formula for the frequencies of the spectrum – that is to say, by the direct transfer of relativistic mass-energy from emitter to absorber, in quantum amounts, without mediation by 'fields' or any other intervening agency.

The natural non-local or longitudinal component of distant interaction

What, then, if not the customary 'æther' and/or 'fields' of FME electrodynamics, are we to think of as constituting the non-local or instantaneous connections, or 'stills', of this cinematic or quantum-sequential model? To answer this, let us free our minds of traditional precepts about 'gravitation', 'inertia', and so on, and take another good commonsense look at what we ordinarily perceive as the motion of a body in space. In the first place we note that no body, even when completely free of any external force, moves independently of other bodies, i.e., 'inertially', in the way Newton assumed. Every mass tends to orbit every other, so that all momentum (mV) is really angular momentum (mVr) in which each body in the system is paired and (non-locally) balanced with every other on opposite ends of a moment-arm r. Newton's straight-line 'inertial' motion, V of a mass m is thus a special case of a theoretically infinite angular momentum mVr in an orbit of infinite radius r.

It follows, then, that all finite angular momentum is, at least incipiently, orbital, with no need to postulate fictitious in vacuo 'forces' of centripetal or centrifugal acceleration. Logically, therefore, the description of angular momentum and the fundamental law of conservation concerning it should be sufficient in itself to explain the orbital motions of bodies. For instance, the normal description of angular momentum is that of an automatically paired relation between some mass m and a countermass M, according to the equation (for simple circular motion):

$$J' = m V_m r_m + M V_M r_M \tag{1}$$

where J' is the angular momentum as viewed from a standpoint with respect to which the centre of mass of the two bodies is stationary – that is, with respect to which $V_m$ and $V_M$ are the (Newtonian) speeds of m and M in their orbits around the common centre of motion, and $r_m$ and $r_M$ are the distances the masses from their common centre.¹¹

A typical case of this circular angular momentum is that of a body situated somewhere near the earth's equator. The angular momentum of the circular motion of that mass m with respect to the earth's centre is simply

$$J = m V r \tag{2}$$

where V is $2\pi r$ divided by the period t of the motion cycle, and $r = r_m + r_M$ is the length of the moment-arm separating the centres of the two masses

We may suppose, then, without logical contradiction and with apologies to Newton, that the body weighs what it does on the earth's surface simply because its angular momentum with respect to the earth's mass is not sufficient for it to orbit at that distance from the earth's centre. Consequently, the body presses on the surface which is preventing it from orbiting where it should, way down beneath our feet, with a real (i.e., actually measured) force of 9.8 newtons per kilogram.¹²

However, in that branch of the evolutionary tree of scientific ideas which culminates in Newton, this real force has been interpreted as due to some fictitious vacuum-extended pulling-power of the planet which causes bodies to be drawn towards it. This imputed 'power', which Newton named 'gravitation', cannot, as such, be measured. Like all the other 'powers' conceived on similar lines, variously named 'electric', 'magnetic', 'nuclear' and so on, 'gravitation' is measurable only by its effect on the motions (e.g., the orbits) of real objects, such as falling apples, planets and satellites. According to Newton's hypothesis, then, every piece of matter attracts every other piece with a force that is the product of the two masses divided by the square of the distance between their centres (Newton's universal law of gravitation). That is to say, the measured force, in terms of the masses and distances involved, is

$$F = G m M / r^2 \tag{3}$$

where G is an empirical factor relating to the more or less passive masses of ordinary and astronomical bodies.

Not all bodies, however, are as passive as these ordinary and astronomical ones, the effects of whose extra-orbital energies of spin, mechanical oscillation etc., are usually minute in comparison to those of their orbital motions.¹³ Those bodies we call 'magnetised', 'electrically charged' and so on are much more volatile in terms of the strengths of the forces they exert on one another compared to those of gravity. So spectacularly different are the strengths of these (real) forces that in our circumstantial history they have been imputed to 'powers' that differ not only quantitatively but also qualitatively from that of 'gravity'. This is why these powers have been allocated special units, such as coulombs for electric charge, webers for 'magnetic strength' ... and so on.

All these other forces, however, are conceived along the same Newtonian lines – that is to say, as the product of the two imputed 'powers' divided by the square of the distance between them. For instance, as compared with the 'gravitational' force in Equation (3), the measured force between two 'electrically charged' bodies is

$$F = k q _ { 1 } q _ { 2 } / r ^ { 2 } \tag { 4 } ,$$

where $k$, like $G$, is a 'constant', but of an experimental value $8 . 9 8 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 9 }$ newton-metre$^2$/coulomb (i.e., $k = 1 / 4 \pi \varepsilon _ { 0 }$), and the $q s$ are charges in coulombs. It is to be noted that a textbook giving these formulas and the values of the constants includes a footnote to the effect that no attempt is made to explain 'what' electric charge 'is'.$^{14}$ This, as these writers concur, is because any such explanation would have to be metaphysical. In fact, like all these esoteric 'powers', 'electric charge' is defined by nothing but its ability to influence the motions of objects and is therefore ultimately cashable in mechanical units of joules.

Our heterodox thesis, then, is that these different-strength forces can be unified by expressing them all in the same dynamic units of kilograms, metres and seconds – that is, without complicating the account by involving 'coulombs', 'farads', 'henrys', 'webers' or whatever. This is by the use of a parameter G which, presumably, should be called the Pope-Osborne parameter (from the 1995 paper by these authors in which it first appears).$^{15}$ $\mathcal { B }$ has the same dimensions as $G$ and the same value for relatively inert (e.g., spinless) masses.$^{16}$ Note also that unlike $G$,\mathcal { B }$ may be either positive or negative, according to whether the vector $r$ of the radial real-force on a constraint is centripetal or centrifugal. All the different strengths of the various (real) forces may then be explained in terms of pure angular momentum, of one kind or another, in accordance with the following formula:

$$J = m V r = 2 ( K + K _ { \mathrm { X } } ) r / V = \mathcal { B } m M / V \tag { 5 }$$

where $V$ is, again, the Galilean orbital velocity (pace Whitney) of the mass $m$, $K$ is the purely orbital kinetic energy of a body and $K _ { \mathrm { X } }$ is any extra kinetic energy the orbiting body may possess, such as that of spin, mechanical oscillation or any other involution or convolution of angular momentum.$^{17}$

The following are two examples of how this angular momentum formula works for fundamental masses at the level of what are commonly conceived as 'electrons' and 'protons'. In addition to any orbital angular momentum those masses may have with respect to one another, they also have an angular momentum $\frac { 1 } { 2 } h / 2 \pi$ of the sort that Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit have identified as intrinsic 'spin'. Without that spin and with only the orbital angular momentum $h / 2 \pi$ (the so-called Dirac quantum), the electron mass $m _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 1 }$ kg would orbit the equally spinless proton-mass $M _ { p } = 1 . 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 7 }$ kg, in accordance with the usual Newtonian equation:

$$J = m _ { \mathrm { e } } V r = G m _ { \mathrm { e } } M _ { \mathrm { p } } / V = h / 2 \pi \tag { 6 } ,$$ where $h$ is Planck's constant of action (energy $\times$ time). This would be with the extremely small (Galilean) speed $$V = G m _ { \mathrm { e } } M _ { \mathrm { p } } / J = 9 . 6 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 4 } \mathrm { m e t r e s / s e c } \tag { 6 a }$$

at a truly immense distance of

$$r = J / m _ { \mathrm { e } } V = 1 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 2 9 } \mathrm { m e t r e s } \tag { 6 b }$$

and with a kinetic energy of negligible value

$$K = \frac { 1 } { 2 } m _ { \mathrm { e } } V ^ { 2 } = 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 9 7 } \mathrm { j o u l e } \tag { 6 c } .$$

This motion (such as it is) might be regarded as a near-enough approximation to Newtonian straight-line motion in 'inertial space'. However, with its 'charge' $q = 1 . 6 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 1 9 }$ coulomb converted into joules (i.e., by multiplying by the ionization-potential $I _ { 0 } = 1 3 . 6$ volts) we obtain a kinetic energy $K _ { \mathrm { X } } = 2 . 1 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 1 8 }$ joule, which is the ground-level kinetic energy $h c R$ of the electron in the Bohr atom ($h$ is the Planck constant and $R$ is the Rydberg constant). This we may assume is mainly the kinetic energy of the electron's intrinsic spin. (Without that spin, $K + K _ { \mathrm { x } }$ is, as we have seen, negligible.) So, with that spin-energy substituted for 'charge' we may replace the usual 'electrodynamical' angular momentum with the following purely dynamical angular momentum formula:

$$J = m _ { \mathrm { e } } V _ { \mathrm { e } } r = 2 ( K + K _ { \mathrm { X } } ) r / V = \mathcal { B } m _ { \mathrm { e } } M _ { \mathrm { p } } / V = h / 2 \pi \tag { 7 } ,$$ where $\mathcal { B }$, as explained, is the variable version of the conventional 'gravitational constant' $G$. In this case,\mathcal { B }$, which is equal to $G$ when $K _ { \mathrm { X } }$ is zero has the value $1 . 5 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 2 9 } \mathrm { N } \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { k g } ^ { - 2 }$ with $K _ { \mathrm { X } } = h c R / I _ { 0 }$).

In principle, then, all motions of all particles should be accountable in terms of different forms of angular momentum (with suitable step-changes, of course, in the value of $\mathcal { B }$) all within an overall-conserved system of the same, without having to postulate all the different 'powers' and 'fields' of circumstantial convention. In other words, the traditional concept of 'electric charge' is redundant, since, as demonstrated above, that postulated 'static' form of energy, measured in coulombs, can be cashed-out without remainder as, for example, a spin-energy in mechanical units of joules. For instance, with the qs in (4) replaced by the masses of the electron and proton, and with $\mathcal{G}$ given the value $1.52 \times 10^{29} \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{2} \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}$, as in (7), the (real) force acting against any restraint on those masses 'orbiting' where they should is

$$F = \mathcal{G} m M / r^{2} \tag{8}$$

which is the same force as given by the much more complicated orthodox formula (4).

It may also be noted, in passing, that the law of conservation of angular momentum demands that not only are the amounts of angular momentum strictly conserved but so, also are its vector orientations. Indeed, Blanchard et al. point out that the vectors involved in angular momentum follow the same rules that apply in electromagnetism (namely, Flemming's rules).$^{18}$ In that case, since every characteristic of so-called 'electrodynamic' interaction, even its features of attraction and repulsion, can be replicated in terms of pure angular momentum, without having to postulate the metaphysical influence of 'charges' and the invisible mediation of these influences by 'fields', then what need do we have of the traditional esoteric talk about 'electrodynamics'? Why not, plainly and simply, dynamics?

The natural local component of distant interaction

It has been argued, so far, that there is no logical need of the 'æther' and 'field' analogs of classical physics, that the common factor in all these conventional 'field-force' conceptions is angular momentum, which proves sufficient in itself to explain the orbital tendencies of bodies without having to endow those bodies with all sorts of strange properties whereby they influence one another at a distance. This leaves the angular momentum quanta $h/2\pi$ and their multiples $nh/2\pi$, as the ultimate quantum 'stills' of the cinematic model. It is to be stressed, however, that there is no more sense in thinking of these fundamental masses, lengths and times as anything like those we usually think of as defining the orbital parameters of stars, planets and satellites. That would be as absurd as thinking of objects in a movie behaving in the cinematographic stills in the same way that they do in the film. In other words, the quantum $m_e V_e r = h/2\pi$ in (7) is the complete, irreducible packet of dimensions mass, length and time in which the moment-arm $r$ may be regarded as an elementary bit of space and the masses at its ends as the elementary bits of mass. The irreducible 'periods of rotation' $2\pi r/V$ are then the elementary bits of time.

The combined locality and non-locality of relativistic interaction

In our quantum-Galilean cinematic model, then, the quantum jump from one quantum still $n_1 h/2\pi$ to some other still $n_2 h/2\pi$ – or, of course, the reverse – is a unit of cinematic sequence into which the physical scenario ultimately analyses on the theory in question.

What constitutes that sequence is as follows. In that cinematic scenario, since angular momentum is a conserved quantity, the change in angular momentum in one place (i.e., one atom) from, say, $n_2 h/2\pi$ to $n_1 h/2\pi$, cannot occur without a compensatory change by that same (reverse) amount $n_1 h/2\pi$ to $n_2 h/2\pi$ in some other atom elsewhere. Moreover, the law of conservation brooks no delay. (As Phipps once expressed it, the interaction cannot be suspended, like an aircraft awaiting landing instructions).$^{19}$ That quantum jump, therefore, has to be instantaneous and immediate (i.e., non-mediated) in accordance with Newton's third law of immediate and reciprocal action/reaction. Also, since angular momentum is quantised, those quantum jumps have to be discrete and dissociate (indeterminate). That is to say, being 'digital', they have to be devoid of any connection between them that is in any way analogous to the sort that is manifest in the physical phenomena that are generated out of them. The situation is somewhat like in banking where some number of units (e.g. coins) are shifted from one account to another, with no question of the speed at which any particular coin travelled nor what physical track it took (remembering, of course, that the transaction might be made by computer without involving any coins as such).

Let the atom in the initial surfeit-state $n_2 h/2\pi$ in this sequence be called the 'emitter' of the action and the atom in the initial deficit-state $n_1 h/2\pi$ be called the 'absorber'. Transacted between them is a quantity of resonant energy (energy $\times$ time) called action in units of Planck's constant, $h$. Now we need to remember that there are no 'waves in vacuo' with this model nor 'fields' or 'ethers' associated with them, any more than on, say, the screen of a cartoon movie there are such entities connecting one cartoon-frame and the next. In this 'movie'-type quantisation of physical action therefore, the only true 'void' there can be at this ultimate quantum level is a null-and-void. The quanta, in other words, do not 'travel' in any analog-type space as analog-type waves, particles or 'photons'. They simply occur as instantaneously, i.e., non-locally, extended events in which the objects concerned are spatially and non-temporally distributed and connected like those in an ordinary still photograph. The phenomenon of space-time displacement of objects which we call motion, with all that this entails in terms of energy, momentum and so on, is then provided by the purely temporal (i.e., non-spatial) quantum sequences of these quantum stills.

What we have here, then, as the basis of our cinematic model of quantum physics, are two rectangular components of the action. One is that of all-distance-and-no-time (the three non-local dimensions of the stills, measured in conventional metres); the other is the dimension of all-time-and-no-distance, the component of pure sequence of the quantum stills, measured in seconds. The situation is the same as that of the Galilean paradigm of science except for three altogether vital differences. One is that instead of being continuous, the instantaneous, non-local distance-extensions, or quantum present moments, are discrete. Another is that behind the quantum jumps that occur between these discrete present moments there is no 'universal' time-background, or cosmic GMT, of the sort envisaged by Galileo and Newton. – the jumps are the times, the only times that there are. The third difference is that these respective measures of distance and time are no longer independent measures (i.e., vector to scalar) as in the paradigm of Galileo and Newton but are dependent measures (vector to vector) as in the geometrical relation between ordinary length and breadth or vertical and horizontal.

That relation between the two different sets of conventional units, metres of distance and seconds of time is, of course, c, as determined macroscopically by Römer and confirmed by others such as Bradley and Michelson. However, instead of interpreting c in the usual analog way, as the 'velocity' of something travelling, we now interpret it in Bondi's way, simply as a constant relating units of metres to units of seconds, in the way that would be found necessary if some tradition had demanded that length be measured in metres and breadth in yards.²⁰ Indeed, this is the same way that in the Standards Institute in Paris, the metre is now defined in time-units of 3.3 nanoseconds.

The situation described so far may therefore be portrayed as in Figure 1. In this diagram, S is the axis of all-distance-and-no-time, or extended instantaneity, measured in metres and T is the axis of all-time-and-no-distance, measured in units of metre/c, or 3.3 nanoseconds, as in the Paris Standards Institute. (Note that these axes represent the orthogonal dimensions of non-locality and locality, respectively, of the commonsense 'movie'-type model of physical action.) The length s portrayed in the diagram is that of an angular momentum moment-arm on the ends of which are bodies containing the emitting atom A and an absorbing atom B between which a quantum of light-energy h is transacted. In other words, the situation in which the atom A is in the initial quantum state n₂h/2π and B in the initial state n₁h/2π is one instantaneous or non-local frame s in the sequence, and the opposite situation in which it is B that is in the state n₂h/2π and A in the state n₁h/2π is the next frame or still, s in the succession-dimension T.

Now the dimensions portrayed in the figure are precisely those of the Galilean conception of motion, in which the velocity V of an object (pace Whitney) may be anything from zero to infinity. The only difference between this quantum version of Galilean space and time and the traditional one (apart from those other three differences already mentioned) is that since the orthogonal measures of distance and time are now dependent variables, related by the dimensional constant c, the measures s and t in this velocity-relation

$V$ produce a resultant that has vectorial significance. A consequence of this is that as applied to ordinary motion, where $s$ is the distance between $A$ and $B$ travelled by a body in a time $t$ registered by the body, there is a spatio-temporal resultant $t_{\mathrm{R}}$ of those two rectangular components which is, by Pythagoras,

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = [(s/c)^2 + t^2] \tag{9}$$

From this it is plain that in the extreme case where that motion is instantaneous, as when the time $t$ in (9) of a quantum jump from $A$ to $B$ is zero, the resultant, or relative time $t_{\mathrm{R}}$ of that transition is the finite time $s/c$ – thus confirming, once again, that physical interaction may, without contradiction, combine both instantaneous (non-local) and finite-speed (local) components.

Since the time $t$ in this formula is not necessarily zero, the formula is a general one applicable to all (Galilean) velocities $V$, from nought to infinity. Moreover, far from being antagonistic to conventional relativity, this theory confirms it – in fact, contains it, as we shall see. For instance, the Einsteinian version of velocity, in the terms of Equation (9) is $s/t_{\mathrm{R}}$ which, in Whitney's notation is distinguish as $v$. If, therefore, we substitute for $s$ in this formula the Einsteinian equivalent $vt_{\mathrm{R}}$ and then simplify the result we obtain

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = t [1 - (v^2/c^2)]^{-s} \tag{10}$$

which, of course, is the standard Einsteinian formula for relativistic time-dilation. (Note that this relativistic facsimile has now been deduced without any reference whatsoever to the customary analog conception of the velocity of light, 'photons', 'electromagnetic waves' or whatever, in vacuo.)

The Galilean Quantum-Relativistic Action-at-a-Distance Spectrum

Now $v^2$ in this equation, (10) is, dimensionally, $e/m$, where $e$ signifies energy in joules and $m$ signifies mass in kilograms. Substituting accordingly and expressing the formula in terms of $e$ we have

$$e = mc^2[1 - (t^2/t_{\mathrm{R}}^2)] \tag{11}$$

which, as $t_{\mathrm{R}}$ becomes greater than $t$, approaches the familiar Einsteinian mass-energy relation $e = mc^2$.

Let $mc^2$ in this formula be the limit of the kinetic energy $K + K_{\mathrm{X}}$ of the mass $m_{\mathrm{e}}$ in equation (7), which is $hcR$ ($= 2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ joule), the total energy lost or gained by an electron-proton pair in atomic interaction. The fractions of that total energy, determined by the values of the variables in the square-bracketed expression on the right of equation (11) are the values $e$ of the energy manifest in that loss of angular momentum, as measured by the gain in angular momentum of some other atom in some detecting instrument such as, say, a spectrograph. The value of the coefficient $m$ in (11) is thus $hcR/c^2$, which is

$$m = 2.3 \times 10^{-35} \text{ kilogram} \tag{11a}$$

which, in the usual analog terms eschewed in this theory, would be called the 'mass of the photon'.

What we have, then, as a direct consequence of the Pythagorean relation expressed in (9) is a spectrum formula which, suited to the immediacy of the quantum contact, is independent of any medium of conduction between emitter and absorber – and, once again, of any of the usual 'electrodynamic' concepts. The requirement of resonance between the emitter and absorber ensures that these immediate contacts, in statistical cinematic profusion, will have the 'wave'-like character observed by Huygens and Fresnel, plus the 'velocity' characteristic $c$ noted by Römer and Bradley – but now, of course, without requiring any analog-type wave-conducting medium or 'field'. In fact, if we make $t_{\mathrm{R}}$ in these equations whole multiples $n$ of discrete fractions $1/N$ of some basic natural unit $t$, and divide these energies by Planck's constant $h$ to express frequency (\nu$) then equation (11) becomes

$$\nu = cR[1 - (N^2/n^2)] \tag{12}$$

For some fixed fraction $1/N$ and varying values of $n$, where $N$ and $n$ are independently varying integers, we have the following series of discrete frequencies:

$$\nu = N^2 cR[(1/n_1^2) - (1/n_2^2)] \tag{13}$$

which is equivalent to the empirical Balmer-Rydberg formula for the frequencies of the spectra of elementary hydrogen-like atoms of atomic number $N$. $^{21\ 22}$

This cinematic interpretation of quantum phenomena, then, goes further than even Weber went in the direction of empirical electrodynamics. Not only does it dispense with the esoteric 'fields' that were postulated by Faraday and Maxwell but also because it dispenses with Coulomb's no less esoteric notion of 'electric charge' as sources of these 'fields' and even with Newton's power of 'gravitational attraction'. It retains, however, the essential empirical characteristics of resonance, observed by Fresnel et al. as well as the finite-speed propagation-characteristics observed by the likes of Roemer and Hertz. It eschews as unnecessary, however, all attempts to interpret these phenomena as 'waves' travelling inscrutably in an inscrutable 'void', whether by the conductive agency of 'fields', 'æthers' or any other device whatsoever.

An Addendum Regarding Doppler Shifts and the Hubble Redshift

Before closing this section on the spectroscopic implications of the quantum cinematic model, some remarks might be in order concerning the measured relativistic Doppler shifts in these spectral lines due to motion between the emitter and absorber. When the emitting and absorbing atoms are parts of different macroscopic moving objects, say a star relative to a spectrograph, the relativistic changes in mass and time due to that macroscopic motion are reasoned to shift the spectral lines in accordance with the above formulas and with the well-known relativistic Doppler effect.

So far as the cosmological (Hubble) redshift is concerned, since, according to convention, light has a constant speed relative to the vacuum, the amount of vacuum (so to speak) is the same between one wave-node and the next, no matter how straight or bent might be the route taken – in the same way that the pace-lengths of a hiker are the same no matter what route he takes. According to the conventional interpretation, then, there can be no possible explanation of the Hubble redshifts of distant galaxies other than as a Doppler effect due to real recessional motions of those sources, with the usual 'expanding universe' and 'Big Bang' implications.

In our cinematic model, however, that Doppler explanation is no longer obligatory because in our theory it is the path-lengths we are concerned with, not 'waves' in space.

Now Van Flandern, on grounds of astronomical observation using radar, distinguishes, in effect, two such paths. One he calls the 'present position' path, which he identifies as the 'gravitational' connection. The other, as he sees it, is the 'electromagnetic' connection. The one is instantaneous (it exhibits no aberration) and the other is time-retarded (it does exhibit aberration).²³ This distinction, but for the differences in the language used to describe them, is equivalent to that between our instantly extended angular momentum stills and the time-retarded sequences of stills in our cinematic model.

We concur with Van Flandern, then, that there are, in effect, two 'distances' involved in our observations of remote objects, one being the proper-time-instantaneous or non-local distance, which Einstein called the 'null geodesic', which cannot be anything but straight, and the other being the retarded relative-time geodesic of the movie-like motion of the light-signal.

Thus, whereas Van Flandern remarks the observational time-difference between these two geodesics (the one being susceptible of aberration, the other not), we may also consider the possibility of a length-difference in the two paths due to some bending of the time-retarded path relatively to the straight.

Any such bending due to small but cumulative non-linearities in the time-retarded geodesic, of the kind postulated in General Relativity, might therefore be considered as a possible alternative to the current 'Doppler' explanation of the Hubble redshift, which is made to seem obligatory by the conventional 'light in vacuo' interpretation. The implications of this possible alternative to the current dogma of the 'expanding universe' are being explored as part of an ongoing project at the Maths Department, Keele University, England.

So far, however, these particular projections, concerning the Hubble redshift are no more than tentative, especially in the light of doubts raised by some researchers as to whether the Hubble parameter really is what it claims to be.

CONCLUSION

Forty years ago, when the record of these ideas begins with a short but encouraging piece of correspondence between a young telephone lineman (i.e., the author) and Albert Einstein,²⁴ there was no question of there being anything like the instantaneous, non-local, longitudinal connection that has here been described. At least, so it seemed. However, in 1992 the writer learned that as early as 1926, in an article in Nature, the chemist Gilbert Lewis had identified this relativistic proper-time-instantaneity as a physical touching.²⁵ It then emerged that this same contiguity feature of light-interaction had been disclosed, independently, by other thinkers. One of these was Herman Bondi, who informed us that he had presented a non-light-velocity interpretation of SR, similar to ours, in 1965.²⁶ So it seems, all-in-all, that precedence in this theoretical discovery of what, in some places, has come to be known as 'quantum touching' has to be conceded to Lewis.²⁷ The trouble is that no-one, not even Einstein it seems (nor Bondi), had any more idea of what to do with this theoretical result than, as it is said, 'a cow with a musket'. Nor does there seem to be any record that it was ever raised in the discussions between the Copenhagen group and the Einstein (EPR) group over the 'spooky superluminality' Einstein imputed to Bohr's interpretation of quantum physics.

But what need is there of 'superluminality' when 'luminality' is intrinsically instantaneous? Besides, to assume that there are superluminal interactions between distant bodies creates the nonsense of the proper-time of those interactions running backwards! The notion of 'superluminality' is therefore a complete mess, as are all those strange speculations to which it leads.

Yet there are growing numbers of experiments nowadays ostensibly testifying to the existence of this supposed 'superluminality'. Some physicists identify it as 'gravitation', others as evidence of the kind of connection Mach called 'inertia'. Others again identify it as some longitudinal 'electromagnetic' vector of a sort neglected by Maxwell. In any case, as Grunhaus et al observe: 'While experiments are still open to criticism, it is (now) generally accepted that ... in nature there exist nonlocal correlations as predicted by quantum mechanics.' 28 29

What we need to ask ourselves, then, is this: are these non-local correlations the same as those predicted by relativity in the way Lewis and others have shown or are they something different? And is it really possible that there are as many of these longitudinal connections as have been differently named.?

From a logical point of view, the latter is hardly likely. Unification demands that among so many different conceptions with the same empirical implications, no more than one should suffice. But which? Well, what else should unify those concepts but the one which is common to them all, namely, that of angular momentum, in which masses are automatically paired, instantly separated, reciprocally balanced and quantised? And of what else could a digital account of physical process consist but descriptions of informational time-sequences of those irreducible bits of non-locality, h/2π? And in what possible way might the locality of these time-sequences and the non-locality of the quantum bits themselves be combined, other than in the cinematic way that has been described?

Complementarity in physics is therefore truly, we suggest, not that of manifestations of wave-motion and particle-motion but of locality and non-locality in the context of a cinematic model of physical process. As those who first embraced Bohr's deliberate distortion of its meaning well recognised, the term 'complementarity' applied to the evidences for waves and particles in atomic interaction was an expedient which would never have been entertained had it not seemed that there was no rational alternative. With that eminent expedient we may sympathise. Let us hope, however, that that ad hoc bit of nonsense and the mysticism it engenders have not become so institutionalised that when a rational solution finally appears it will be automatically rejected as heresy.
