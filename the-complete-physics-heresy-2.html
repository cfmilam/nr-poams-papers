It may be prudent at this stage to interpose a short philosophical digression. This is to set at rest the minds of those physicists who, not being particularly au fait with developments in modern philosophy, are disposed to imagine that there are two, and only two, competing philosophical approaches to physics, so that to reject the one is automatically to adopt the other. These adversaries are, on the one hand, the 'Positivists' ofthe

Copenhagen school of physics and, on the other, the 'Realists' of the opposed or anti-Copenhagen school. This, however, is a fork in physics of a branch which, in philosophy itself is now quite dead. It was a bifurcation which Descartes introduced, in the 17th Gntury, according to which there is not one real world but two. The one he saw as the world of mindless and self-sufficient matter, existing relatively to nothing but itself. The other, in contrast, he saw as a non-material world of 'mind', consisting of nothing but our conscious thoughts and ideas. These, he reasoned, include our observations of matter which, but for the beneficence of the Creator and Coordinator of these two 'realities' and the links between them, such as our sense-organs, light and sound, would be a world of completely subjective 'illusion'. In this bogus bifircation the most practically-

minded physicists have, not surprisingly, opted for Descartes' 'material' reality, dismissing the other as not

only non-material but immateial. Those who accept this artificially truncated conception of reality are the self-styled 'Realists' of modern physics, for whom anything which seems to compromise that view (such

then emerged that this same contiguity feature of light-interaction had been disclosed, independenfly, by other thinkers. discovery of what, in some places, has come to be known as 'quantum touching' has to be conceded to Lewis.27 The trouble is that no-one, not even Einstein it seems (nor Bondi), had any more idea of what to do with this theoretical result than, as it is said, 'a cow with a musket'. Nor does there seem to be any record that it was ever raised in the discussions between the Copenhagen group and the Einstein @pR) group over the 'spooky superluminality'Einstein imputed to Bohr's interpretation of quantum physics.
