The Atom in Normal Realism

N. Vivian Pope

The Atom in Normal Realism The quantum discretum Any attempt at understanding what happens at the quantum level of analysis is bedevilled by an automatic tendency to think of these fundamental events as occurring in some dark and dismal, metaphysical nether-region of existence underlying the world of ordinary everyday perception. To the question, then, of where it is that these events happen we must recall that in Normal Realism there are no such metaphysical domains. The only answer consistent with Normal Realism is that where these events happen is in the light: that is, in the full spectral range of the informational pixels from which the one and only real world is observationally projected. This spectrum is essentially a quantum discretum in which the separation between one pixel-event and another involves statistical numbers of other intervening pixel events and, as we have seen, it is from patterns and sequences of these separated events that ordinary space and time are projected. The space in which these events, are separated is therefore the real space of ordinary phenomena, and their time separation is, likewise, that of the same ordinary phenomenal world. Beyond this, there is nothing that it makes sense to speak of. In short, the space and time of quantum interactions are those of ordinary everyday space and time as measured in the normal way by physical processes, organic and inorganic (clocks, phases of the moon and sun, ageing and so on). So much, then, for the quantum pixels, those interactions between atoms measured in units of the action-constant h. So, what about the atoms themselves? Where do these quantum accumulators abide, between which action is transacted in units of the quantum h? Our answer is: where else but in the same real space as the quanta, which is the only space that there truly is? (Any suggestion that there is more than one overall reality is, surely, absurd.) Now as we have already seen, in Relativity the interaction between an atom in a distant nebula, say, and another in the observatory spectroscope, has no intrinsic, or proper-time separation, being literally the same event in both places. Recall also that when the proper-time of this interaction is zero, its observational or relative time is the measure of the distance s of the nebula from the spectrograph divided by c, so that there is no contradiction between instantaneity and time-delay as components of any single atomic interaction. So everything, so far, exists and acts in real space – real, that is, as defined in Normal Realism. Let this be called mono-realism, or holo-realism, as distinct from the mind-matter duo-realism, or dualism, of Descartes and the classical physics that is based upon it. However, no matter how holistic that real space may be, we must remember that in order to avoid the consequence of an Eleatic stasis, it has to be, essentially, a discretum, not a continuum. This means that there have to be fundamental discontinuities on the ultimate quantum level. Where then, are those discontinuities, and what form do they take? We have seen that those discontinuities are not the quanta, as such, since they are the measurable energetic connections between distance-separated atoms. Also, we have seen that the separations between these quanta are defined on terms of the statistical numbers of other intervening and overlapping quantum events in the space of ordinary physical experience, which is the only continuity they have. However, that statistical continuity is not absolute, otherwise it would not be the required discretum but an Eleatic stasis. What, then, are

the ultimate discontinuities? Are they voids, in any spatial sense, like the void inside a Thermos flask or vacuum chamber? The answer is no, because that vacuum-space has measurable or, at least describable dimensions, which the quantum ‘void’ or ‘gap’ does not. Words like ‘void’, ‘space’, ‘gap’, etc., are therefore singularly unsuitable as names for these ultimate discontinuities. So, whereas the quantum itself has dimensions of energy, duration and so on (i.e., t = h/e), except in terms of intervening such quanta, there is no space, gap, void or whatever, that it makes sense to think of. All quantum interactions between atoms are direct and instantaneous, without any question of there being any mediating ‘space’, ‘void’, ‘vacuum’ or whatever, involved in these interactions. So much, then, for the interactions between atoms.What is an atom doing between it is not interacting with another atom? By definition, it is doing nothing. Does it, then, in that meantime, exist all alone, permanently in time, like the atom conceived by Democritus? In Normal Realism such existence, without interaction, is impossible. There is just no way in which, to use a phrase of J. L. Austin’s, the atom can persist, ‘ticking over metaphysically’, behind the scenes, that is, behind the actual phenomena. This means that the existence of this entity, in real time and space, is never more than sporadic. This accords with Heisenberg’s Indeterminacy Principle, which forbids any determinable existence of a fundamental particle. Is it the same atom that pops into and out of existence, on these separate occasions? That is indeterminable in the same way. This reminds us of the top-down methodology of Normal Realism, according to which it is not the whole that takes its character from the parts but the parts that take their character from the whole. In this view, the atom is not the solid, permanent, physically enduring piece of matter that Democritus envisaged. It is an event, or rather, a succession of discrete events in phenomenal space and time. Its position in space, on these separate occasions is therefore no more than probabilistic in accordance with the Schroedinger probability function. This gives the particle permanence of a sort. However, there can be no ‘collapsing of the wave-function’ to winkle-out the atom from metapjysical inscrutability, as in standard quantum ‘wave-mechanics’. In Normal Realism, there is no such underlying wavecontinuum or substratum. The probabilities themselves, of atomic manifestations in great fine-grained profusion, have to be conceived as self-supporting. Is the atom to be conceived, then, as no more than an illusion, a kind of insubstantial will-o-the-wisp? No, because it takes part in chemical processes as an entity in itself, and in ‘atom-smashing’ experiments as a solid object, a mass which can be broken up into component masses, such as electrons, protons and neutrons. However, to be solid, in any meaningful sense of the word, the atom would have to have a continuous material inside, whereas experiments reveal that it is a configurtion of compoents, the sizes of which are so much smaller than the atom that by far the larger proportion of the atom itelf is pure emptiness. What, then, are those components of the atom? Are they permanent and solid all-through, like the atom of Democritus? Again, their durations, in physical manifestations, are finite, the ‘lifetimes’ of those manifestations being functions of their frequency, ν, i.e., t = e/h, where e is the intrinsic energy of the interaction.
