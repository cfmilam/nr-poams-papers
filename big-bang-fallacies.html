Big Bang Fallacies

N. Vivian Pope

The Big Bang Fallacies 1, Where has the energy gone ? A question I was asked, recently was this: If an object is moving away from the observer, and emits electromagnetic radiation of a given wavelength, that radiation is received by the observer at a longer wavelength, as in the case of the Hubble redshift. But it is said that radiation of a longer wavelength has lower energy than that of a higher wavelength. So my question is - where has the energy gone in the case of the galactic redshift? The stock answer to your question, I said, is that in classical terms, the energy lost in the spectral frequency-shift towards the red is swallowed-up by the kinetic energy of the motion of the light-source (the galaxy) away from the observer. This, of course, is the standard explanation of the redshift, that it proves the recession of the galaxies, hence the ‘expansion of the universe’ and its original ‘Big Bang’ implications.. In this classical way of thinking, the ‘lost energy’ is that of the alleged 'universal expansion' in the same way as kinetic energy (heat energy) of the molecules of a gas decreases (cools) as the gas expands.

2. The receding galaxies There is, however, a rather stark logical fallacy in assuming that because all receding light-sources exhibit a redshift, seeing a redshift is the same as seeing a body receding. In most cases, this assumption is correct. Nevertheless that assumption is not logically true. There may well be, in nature, instances where a source exhibits all the characteristics of a spectral redshift without there being a recessional motion. According to Special Relativity, for instance, a distant source may exhibit a spectral redshift on account of a time-dilation due to motion in any direction, even in a direction towards the observer …. There is no way of distinguishing, by direct spectral observation, this time-dilational effect from a galactic recession. So, you will ask, where does the energy disappear to in the POAMS explanation of the Hubble Redshift? The answer POAMS gives is practically the same. The energy is absorbed by the energetic motions of the light-sources (the galaxies in this case). The only difference is that in POAMS those motions are not uniformly recessional but are randomly distributed in an overall steady-state situation. Relativistically, of course, for those large velocities, some of the kinetic energy turns into mass at the rate m = e/c^2. In POAMS this is the same, regardless of the direction of the motion. This entails that a body's atomic periods are slowed relatively to the observer, and the faster the body goes, the slower (the more relativistically time-dilated) become its atomic periods. This relativistic lengthening of the spectral periods of the atoms of time-dilated randomly moving bodies is the POAMS explanation of the Hubble Redshift. So why, you may ask, do these energetic galactic motions increase with distance? In other words, why is it that ‘the further they are the faster they go’? The answer POAMS has given is based on the fact that in a simple angular momentum system consisting of a small mass orbiting a large central mass – like the earth around the sun, say – any loss of angular momentum reduces the orbit radius, increasing the orbital velocity, hence the kinetic energy of the orbiting mass. In other words, it can be said that in general, the nearer to the central mass an orbiting body gets, the faster it goes. When the NASA space-probes Pioneers 10 and 11 received the ‘slingshots’ by which by which they left our solar system, they ended-up travelling faster around

those planets than they ever did when they left earth. Presumably, in travelling through interstellar space they will be travelling at even greater velocities, and even greater again as they approach other galaxies. If there is some very large, supergalactic mass out there, then as they lose momentum with regard to it [i[i]], so the nearer they approach it and the faster they will travel around it. As a result, their spectra become more time-dilated relative to a terrestrial observatory. Extrapolated to distances on an intergalactic scale, the statistical accumulation of masses expands roughly spherically, hence, the greater the observational distance and the greater the time dilation on that same statistical scale. So, where in the universe is that greatest mass? Certainly it is not on our earth nor in our solar system, nor in our galaxy, even. Nor, in a steady-state, Gaussian distribution of matter, can it be located anywhere among them. However, in such a random distribution of bodies, the statistical accumulation of their masses increases volumetrically with the distance from any particular point of observation. This omnidirectional accumulation of mass with distance means that the further away from us a galaxy is, the faster it goes – not necessarily radially but in any direction, as I say. Since this explanation contains no logical contradiction it stands as a valid alternative to the ‘expanding universe’ theory and is therefore a viable option for a steady-state alternative to the ‘Big Bang theory of cosmical creation. Insofar as the ‘Big Bang theorists ignore this viable logical alternative, that theory is, by definition, a dogma. Analysis of the ‘supporting evidence’ for the Big Bang theory. Logically speaking, empirical evidence for a theory is never a proof of that theory. This, of course, is because that same ‘evidence’ may be equivalent ‘proof’ of some other, altogether different theory. It is surprising that so many theorists seem not to understand this! Nor are many of those theorists aware that, logically, the only proof of a theory is for it to be the one theory that refutes all the alternatives. And how are those alternatives refuted? Either i) by producing evidence to the contrary or, ii) by logical analysis, by which is meant the discursive method instituted by Socrates, to prove, by logical deduction, that a set of premises, or propositions leads, dialectically, either to an absurd or a self-contradictory conclusion (what logicians have named reductio ad absurdum). For instance, one of the fallacies in the ‘Big Bang theory of cosmic creation is this: Given that, due to their different motions relatively to one another, the times registered over their individual histories have to be immensely different, so that there can be no overall, unified ‘winding back’ to a single, punctiform ‘moment of creation’ or cosmical ‘ground-zero’. This reduces the ‘Big bang’ theory to an absurdity. Another example of a reduction to absurdity is in that same theory. This is where it is assumed that the whole universe started off as a tiny dot called the ‘primeval atom’ and then expanded hugely into the universe as we know it astronomically today. But since the word ‘universe’ means everything there is, there can be nothing outside that ‘primeval universe’ whereby it can be judged large or small. So, to describe it as being ‘tiny’ is absolutely meaningless. Yet another example of fallacy in the Big Bang Theory is as follows. It is a matter of fact that the heavier isotopes of the elements are the most unstable, that they are the ones which break up spontaneously into more stable configurations of nuclear particles and, in so doing, produce all sorts of exotic entities which test the capacity of ordinary language to classify, categorise and name.

From this tendency of the elements to downgrade, entropically, from uranium, plutonium and so on towards the lowest, least volatile forms, of iron, for example, it is assumed that at one time, some thirteen billion years ago, there was just the one ‘Adam‘ particle. This alleged particle is called the Higgs boson or ‘God Particle’ from which, in the theoretical ‘Big Bang’, all the subsequent generations upon generations of ‘particles’ have descended. One fallacy in this (compounded by the other, above-mentioned fallacies regarding the relativistic time-staggering), lies in the assumption that entropy, the inevitable downgrading from order into complete randomness or chaos, takes place universally, from everywhere in parallel, all at the same time (God’s Ubiquitous Time). In fact, however, a loss of order (heat loss) in one place can create an increase in order somewhere else. A case in point is that of photosynthesis, where the energy lost by the sun turns inorganic material into living matter. The same may happen when the loss of stellar energy in one place builds up energy in another place, in the same way that the energy lost by one atom is transferred immediately to another with zero entropy in the transfer. To say, then, that the Second Law of Thermodynamics (the Law of Entropy) is true is not the same as saying that it takes place everywhere all at once, that the ‘whole universe’ is uniformly consigned, as some cosmologists have suggested, to a ‘cosmic heat-death’ with the same temperature everywhere. Logically speaking, without this ‘universal, God’s-eye-view assumption there can be no single backwards extrapolation, either to a ‘Big Bang’ moment of creation or to a single ancestor, or ‘Adam’ of all the present particles. That is analogous to seeking a single Adam for all the different life-forms there may be on all the different planets throughout the universe. In the same way that there may be a single life-principle throughout the universe there may well be a single principle applied to the scatter-products of particle collisions or spontaneous fissions and fusions If so, then what else would that be but entropy, the Second Law of Thermodynamics. By this law, the lineage of any particle that is abroad at any moment may be traced back to some source of lower entropy (higher order), but not necessarily the same source in all instances. In that case, the ‘Big Bang’ moment of all creation of all particles everywhere – the Higgs boson, or ‘God Particle’ is pure metaphysics. In POAMS, every particle has a resonant frequency, like a wine-glass which shatters when a certain note is struck. Each of these sub-particles has, in turn, its own resonant frequency, its own harmonics and sub-harmonics, whence the aim of Particle Physics would be to determine the order and limits to which these reductions may be taken. This, however, is less a process of ‘finding’ new particles and more a process of creating them. As a late colleague of mine once joked: ‘The size of the newest particle to be discovered in these atom-smashing laboratories is inversely proportional to their electricity bill!’ As for the recent experiment at CERN to recreate the ‘Big Bang’, next to the failed attempts by Michelson and Morley to determine the ‘ether drift’, this present experimental hunt for the ‘Big Bang’ particle, by means of the LHC (Large Hadron Collider) has to be the biggest non-event in the history of experimental physics. There is much talk, of course, about the spin-offs to society of these sorts of experiments, as if some new understanding of nature must be determined by such means. However, if Physics were to be funded to build more and more huge experiments to determine the existence of angels and fairies, there would be bound to accrue to society some

beneficial spin-offs of one kind or another, the occurrence of which would in no way prove the existence of these theoretical beings. But how, one may ask, did that ‘Big Bang’ idea come about in the first place. The answer is that it was based on the Hubble Redshift, a shift towards the red in the spectra of the most distant galaxies in direct proportional to their observational distance. Since this kind of redshift is usually associated with a Doppler recession of a light-source, like a speeding car in a police camera, it was assumed, straight away, and quite uncritically, that observing this redshift was tantamount to observing the galaxies receding. This is yet another fallacy, like saying that since all frogs jump, whenever you see something jump, it is a frog. However, on that fallacious assumption that the galaxies are receding, it was assumed that ‘the universe is expanding’. Extrapolating backwards in time, from the alleged ‘speeds’ of those galaxies as they are now, one arrives at a metaphysical ‘time-zero’, of about 13.7 billion years in the past, when the whole universe, with its prodigious mass and energy, its stars, planets, galaxies and everything, including space and time, was packed into a tiny, immensely volatile dot called the ‘primeval atom’. Plainly, this should have been taken as the reductio ad absurdum of the ‘Expanding Universe’ theory and its fallacious premise that seeing a redshift of the galactic spectra is the same as seeing those galaxies receding. This should have left the way clear for exploring some more rational explanation of the redshift, the possibility of which, strictly speaking, is by no means logically precluded by the customary ‘recessional’ interpretation of the Hubble redshift. But custom is custom, and once established, fallacious or not, that custom of thinking of the universe as ‘expanding’ has become practically embedded in the public psyche. This is to the extent that any logical alternative now seems unthinkable. This conservative blocking of alternatives is, of course, scientifically and philosophically dysfunctional. What makes it worse is the Experimentalist tradition of physical science according to which the language in which some experimental discovery is described at the time and the concepts it evokes become, we tend to think, ‘fixed for all time’. This Experimentalist approach to physics is like a tunnel dug by a series of moles, one after another, each advancing the tunnel further than his predecessor with none of them having any question of where they are going or why. This ‘mole’s-eye-view’ of science contrasts greatly with what, by comparison, may be called a logical or philosophical ‘eagle’s-eye-view’, from where that series of molehills randomly appears, or where that tunnel is heading in the geographic terrain. Another example of a demonstrably fallacious theory is that of Dark Matter Unfortunately, at this present time, the notion of ‘political correctness’ in politics has crept into Physics to the extent that it becomes ‘impolitic’ for one theorist to refute another. This puts the fashion of theoretical physics on a par with ‘religious tolerance’, which defeats the whole point and purpose of the physics which, since its inception in Natural Philosophy, has always been to determine by rational selection the best, most coherent and irrefutable of theories from among the various contenders In this way, the meaning of ’truth’ has become as lost to Modern Physics as it has to contemporary politics and society generally.
