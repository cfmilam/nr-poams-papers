The Tantalizing Two-Slit Experiment (as Published)

N. Vivian Pope — 1996

Published in Recent Advances in Relativity Theory 2: Material Interpretations, Hadronic Press, Palm Harbor, FL, USA. Eds. M.C. Duffy & M. Wegener, pp. 218–226.

Summary

The continued inability of physicists to explain the mysteries of action-at-a-distance without invoking metaphysical hypotheses about 'hidden variables' and so on, suggests that something is radically wrong with our present ways of thinking about nature. If that is so, then nothing less than a radical readjustment in those current ways of thinking may be necessary in order to solve those problems. Accordingly, this paper presents a radical, ahistorical alternative to the present conceptual course. This, it is claimed, is more logical, more direct and more conceptually efficient than the usual historical route.

Introduction

For every motion there are two velocities. One is the distance a body travels between A and B in the time measured by the observer of that motion; the other is the same distance as seen by the same observer, but registered by the time — called the proper time — of the body itself. The first of these is what we may call the Einsteinian velocity, which tends to the finite limit $c$. The other is precisely Newtonian and tends to a limit of infinity.

Now it needs to be stressed that these Einsteinian and Newtonian accounts of the motion of a body are two aspects of the same motion. That is to say, they are equally applicable. Unfortunately, the circumstances of the history of our academic science have made it seem that the two are in some sense antagonistic; that the Einsteinian theory of motion has somehow 'superseded' the Newtonian. In fact, however, the two theories are 'complementary' and inter-derivable, each with its own special application for the conduct of physics, the Einsteinian for the relativistic aspect of physics, and the Newtonian for the quantum aspect.

For instance, on the evidence provided by Römer et al., all observational distances travelled by bodies in space — e.g., Jupiter and its principal satellite in the original observations of Römer — are times in the constant ratio of units $c$. This enables us logically to deduce the relativistic time-dilation of Einstein and Lorentz in the following unusual but very simple way.

Let $s/c = t_s$ be the distance-time travelled by an object P in the time $t_p$ of that object, as seen by an observer O. The measures $t_s$ and $t_p$ are now axes of a graph whose units are fixed. That is to say, unlike an ordinary graph, the axes of this time-graph of $t_s$ by $t_p$ are dependent variables, measured in the same units, which means that there is a geometrically significant resultant of those two orthogonal time-components, as shown in Fig. 1. [1] This is, by Pythagoras:

$$t_\tau = \sqrt{t_p^2 + t_s^2} \tag{1}$$

That this is the same time-dilation as that of Lorentz and Einstein can be seen if, in this equation, we substitute for $t_s$ the equivalent expression $s/c$ and then, for $s$ in this expression, substitute the equivalent $t_\tau v$, where $v$ is the Einsteinian velocity $s/t_\tau$. This yields:

$$t_\tau = \sqrt{t_p^2 + t_\tau^2 (v/c)^2} \tag{2}$$

which simplifies directly to the standard relativistic equation:

$$\frac{t_\tau}{t_p} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}} \equiv \gamma \tag{3}$$

The first thing to notice about this ahistorical, neo-Newtonian derivation of relativistic time-dilation is that it requires no mention whatsoever of the Einsteinian 'light speed in vacuo'. The notoriously perplexing notion of light as something travelling invisibly relative to a vacuum and to everything else in that vacuum, moving or stationary, is made unnecessary and redundant. [2] Instead, the empirical constant $c$ is taken simply as what Bondi calls a conversion factor for converting Römer's distance-measure $s$ in metres into a number $t_s$ of seconds. We get: [3]

$$1 \text{ metre} / c \approx 3.3 \text{ nanoseconds} \tag{4}$$

So, in this neo-Newtonian depiction of motion, there is no question of the 'Einstein separation' which makes a mystery of the seeming 'clairvoyance' of particles in the Thomas Young two-slit experiment and other experiments dealing with the phenomenon of action-at-a-distance. In this non-standard account of the facts of modern physics, such mysteries simply don't arise. Nor, by implication, should they arise in the other, Einsteinian account, since the two accounts are no more than alternative aspects of the same theoretical (i.e., Pythagorean) system.

The Pythagorean formula (1), which is basic to both theories, contains three variables. Hence the usual two-dimensional depiction of motion as a plot of distance by time is inadequate. For instance, if, instead of plotting a motion against the axes $t_s$ and $t_p$ as in Fig. 1, we plot it against $t_s$ and $t_\tau$, we obtain the typical relativistic or hyperbolic depiction of the same motion, shown in Fig. 2, where Fig. 1 is shown dotted in the background.

A full depiction of motion in accordance with the Pythagorean equation (1) is therefore not flat, but three-dimensional — that is to say, not a chart, but a model. Nor does that model need to be fanciful since mathematicians will recognise formula (1) as the equation for an equilateral cone, of which Figs. 1 & 2 are the end-elevational and side-elevational projections, respectively. In Fig. 3, the motion vector $s/c$ (the equilateral hyperbola of Fig. 2) is shown as a conic section.

This Pythagorean time-cone should not be confused with the light-cone of Eddington. It is simply a graphic portrayal of the geometric relations between the dimensions of motion, of which there are five, comprising the three dimensions $t_s$, or $s/c$ (two of these suppressed in the diagrams for reasons of simplicity), the dimension $t_p$, and the dimension $t_\tau$. [4]

With the assurance that in dealing with the one aspect of motion we are also dealing, by implication, with the other, let us take a look at the neo-Newtonian aspect as pictured in Fig. 1. We refer to this as neo-Newtonian because it incorporates Bondi's distance-time conversion factor in a way which, due to circumstances of history, could never have occurred to Newton. Also it rejects Newton's dictum, as expressed in his First Law of motion, that all force-free, i.e., inertial, motion takes place in straight lines.

The fact that, in nature, the trajectories of freely moving bodies are not straight has made it obligatory, in accordance with the historical way of thinking, to invent a plethora of in vacuo 'forces', such as 'Newtonian force', 'Coulomb force', etc., responsible for accelerating those bodies away from their fictitiously straight trajectories. The Scheinproblem this has bequeathed to physics, namely that of 'unifying' these fictitious 'forces', has enervated and defeated the very best of intellects.

Besides, as Aristotle argued, a force-free — i.e., uninterrupted — motion cannot be rectilinear because a straight line, unless it is infinitely long, has a definite beginning and end and is thus interrupted. All finite force-free motion, Aristotle thus concluded, is therefore cyclic, or orbital, which is far more in direct agreement with the natural facts than Newton's eminently unempirical First Law.

Newton's conception of rectilinear inertial motion as momentum $mv$ is thus, from the Aristotelian point of view, the limiting case of an angular momentum $mvr$ where $r$ is implicit and, in this case, infinite; that is to say, an infinite angular momentum. From this it follows that, in all finite measures of angular momentum, $r$ is also finite and the motion non-rectilinear.

Another advantage of this angular momentum interpretation of force-free motion is that, unlike Newton's truncated concept of momentum, angular momentum is quantised in units of $\hbar$ (that is, Planck's constant $h$, divided by $2\pi$). Newton's conception of space as a Euclidean continuum, 'inertial space', is thus replaced, in our neo-Newtonian theory, by that of an angular momentum discretum.

This obviates any need to suppose that the influences exerted by bodies on one another are due to forces exchanged between them in vacuo on account of those masses possessing some static metaphysical vis gravitata, vis electrica, or whatever. By definition, angular momentum is sufficient in itself to explain those influences since, (a) it signifies an automatically balanced, paired relation between masses and, (b) conservation demands that in a paired angular momentum relation no mass can change its motion without immediately affecting the other, regardless of the length of the moment-arm.

All distant actions of bodies on one another, therefore, due to these holistic shifts in angular momentum, have to be instantaneous. This, as we have seen, presents no problem from the standpoint depicted in Fig. 1, in which no historical question of 'Einstein separation' arises.

In this neo-Newtonian account of motion and space, then, all masses interact automatically and instantaneously (i.e., proper-time-instantaneously), with no question of fictitious 'forces' being responsible for those interactions nor of their being delayed or suspended for any length of time. The different strengths of the real forces which are required, according to Newton's Third Law, to prevent masses from following their natural force-free orbital trajectories are explained by the conservation of angular momentum, according to which the orbital parameters of bodies, with extra-orbital angular momentum such as spin, alter the value of $G$ in the following equation:

$$J = m v_p r = \frac{2(K_o + K_s)\, r^*}{v_p} = \frac{\mathcal{G}\, mM}{v^*} \tag{5}$$

In this equation all variables are Newtonian (non-relativistic), $m$ being the mass of some atomic particle, or ensemble of particles, and $v_p = s/t_p$ being its proper orbital velocity when $K_s = 0$ ($K_o$ is the orbital kinetic energy, while $K_s$ is any additional kinetic energy). $\mathcal{G}$ is a factor which is equal to the gravitational constant $G$ when $K_s$ is zero, and $r^*$ and $v_p^*$ are the changed values of $r$ and $v_p$ for $\mathcal{G} $\neq$ G$. $M$ is the balancing countermass, taken for convenience to be infinite. [5]

Let us now look at the adjacent Einsteinian aspect of the Pythagorean time-cone, shown in Fig. 2; that is to say, the relativistic, or time-retarded aspect of these.

Now, angular momentum, as is well known, can be lost or gained only in integer multiples of the fundamental quantum $n\hbar$. Moreover, conservation demands that for an amount of angular momentum $n\hbar$ to be lost at one place, exactly the same amount of angular momentum must be gained somewhere else and since the transfer is immediate, there is no distinction, at the quantum level, between cause and effect. The connection between the 'emitter' and the 'absorber' of that action is, as Gilbert Lewis once described it, a physical 'touching'. [6]

This 'touching' takes place in accordance with Newton's Third Law of equal and opposite action and reaction which, since it is immediate, involves no question of mediation by independently space-travelling waves or wave-particles, called 'photons', 'gravitons' or whatever. Every bit of mass is, inextricably, part of a holistic angular momentum system in which there can be no isolated, independent condition of 'locality'. All trajectories are proper-time-instantaneously connected — albeit not continuously, as in classical physics, but sporadically in statistical numbers of these different sorts of quantum touchings.

In these quantum touchings the angular momentum is conserved in the manner of a currency, where (barring the counterfeiting and destruction of coins) no matter how their distributions might change, the number of coins in actual 'circulation' — in banks, cupboards, people's pockets, etc. — remains the same. The angular momentum nexus as a whole, like a monetary currency, is therefore a Heraclitan rather than a Parmenidean continuum. That is to say, it is not an absolute continuum in which everything is instantly, simultaneously and permanently connected. It is a discretum, whose permanence or continuity, to employ a simile of Heraclitus, is like that of a candle flame burning steadily in still air while everything within it is rapidly and continually changing.

But if everything is connected in this immediate, proper-time-instantaneous way, albeit sporadically, then of what do distance and motion consist? The answer to that is as follows. There is, as we have seen, no distance nor time involved in a single quantum touching. Distance at the ordinary macrophysical level is an informational projection from patterns and intensities of these quantum events as they impinge (proper-time-instantly) on an object such as a photographic plate or the eye of an observer, according to the equation:

$$\frac{s}{s_o} = \frac{I_o}{I} \tag{6}$$

where $s$ is the distance, $I_o$ some standard intensity (e.g., one candela) at a standard distance $s_o$, and $I$ is the actually measured intensity. [7]

These informationally projected distances $s$, since they are composed of instantaneous quantum touchings, are also instantaneous, and since that proper-time instantaneity is the same in all observational frames, the distance $s$ is invariant, and thus an absolute feature of the neo-Newtonian discretum.

There can, of course, be no question of physical causality in a quantum event as such. Each quantum jump from A to B is a completely consummated and irreducible event in which the 'emission' and 'reception' of that quantum are co-determinate. Nor can there be any motion or causality in the patterns of co-occurring quanta from which the distances $s$ are projected. These instantaneous three-dimensional projections are comparable to the photographs called 'stills' in cinematography, which consist of informational patterns and distributions of photographic grains, in which there is no question of motion or causality between one grain and another.

Cinematographic motion, then, consists of sequences of photographic 'stills' or grain-patterns. With this comparison in mind, physical motion at the ordinary macroscopic level, including that of light-signals in experiments of the sort conducted by Michelson et al., may be said to consist of kinematical sequences, $t_s$, $t_p$, $t_\tau$, as the case may be, of such three-dimensional quantum projections or 'stills'.

However, unlike a film sequence, in which the succession of 'stills', as the viewer sees it, is predetermined by the film-maker, these quantum sequences are indeterminate and they propagate entropically, i.e., causally, at speeds the upper limit of which is the asymptote $c$ (the 45° slope of the cone) shown in Fig. 2.

For 'cinematographic sequence', in these passages, now read 'wave'. A wave, in these terms, is a temporal sequence of wave-elements which, ultimately, are instantaneous quantum jumps, or touchings. The wave sequence is a succession of these quantum touchings in the dimensions $t_p$ of the object, and $t_\tau$ of the observer of that object for whom the time has an additional component $\Delta s/c$ as in equation (1). The frequency of the space-time sequence is, of course, that of its component quanta,\nu = e/h$, where $e$ is the energy of the quantum transition, and its amplitude is the probability amplitude $\psi$ as in the Schrödinger wave-equation.

Our quantum-sequential wave is therefore, in effect, the wave-function of conventional quantum wave-dynamics, but with the essential difference that there is now no question of any 'Einstein separation' between the emitters and absorbers of the wave-energy (i.e., the atomic 'boluses' of the angular momentum discretum). Nor is there any need for the notoriously perplexing notion of the space-travelling wave-particle. The elements of the quantum-sequential wave are the quantum jumps between atoms in the emitting and absorbing objects, and there can be no such thing as a one-ended, or open-ended quantum jump.

A quantum, therefore, cannot be transacted between the emitting body and the absorbing body unless there is a mutual and reciprocal agreement between the two parties. That is to say, the action can take place only when the two atoms involved are in resonance. Any idea that this resonance is mediated by some vehicular 'wave-particle', called the 'photon' or whatever, accompanying the wave, clairvoyantly, at the same speed as the wave — namely, $c$ — is therefore, to say the least, redundant.

The wave travelling from the emitting to the absorbing body is a completely kinematic sequence of immediately and completely consummated quantum resonances between the atoms of those objects. This means that those objects, macroscopically speaking, are in continuous instantaneous contact throughout the whole period of the wave's evolution.

The 'spooky superluminal' influences which are supposed to act ahead of the light signal to consummate the quantum interaction, as observed in action-at-a-distance experiments of the sort conducted by Alain Aspect and Carroll Alley, are therefore not something additional to light, but constitute an inseparable part of light. In other words, far from being 'extraluminal', or 'superluminal', these instantaneous actions-at-a-distance are, in a definite sense, 'intraluminal'.

The Two-Slit Experiment of Thomas Young

Like all waves, then, these quantum-sequential waves are causally (entropically) propagated in space (space-time) at speeds up to the limit $c$ which is constant in each and every reference-frame of the sort depicted in Fig. 2. They are reflected, refracted, diffracted, attenuated, and so on, in all those ways familiar to students of wave-dynamics.

A natural phenomenon, which was first reported by Thomas Young, in 1801, is that of the patterns of interference in light shone on a screen through a pair of slits in an intervening diaphragm. This is perfectly explicable in terms of standard wave theory, yet it has since become one of the biggest mysteries of modern physics. The mystery is that of how this wave-type interference can be caused by 'waves' whose ultimate constituents are little discrete packets of energy $h\nu$.

But why should this create a mystery? All waves are kinematical sequences of such events. For instance, the most familiar kind of wave, a water wave, is constituted of motions which are nothing like that of the wave itself, but are discrete circular motions of the water molecules around relatively fixed points.

The reason why light-interference has become a mystery is that, historically, these energy packets $h\nu$ were arbitrarily interpreted as particles, namely, 'photons', travelling at the speed of the wave. This generates mystery on two counts. First, a 'wave' whose constituents are particles travelling at the same speed as the wave is not a wave, but a moving solid object. Second, particles cannot 'interfere' with one another, far less with themselves, to exhibit the sort of interference which is typically observed in the two-slit experiment. Indeed, the two-slit experiment was originally regarded as providing decisive evidence for light being waves, and not particles.

However, instead of accepting this phenomenon of wave-interference as plain evidence that the wave-quanta cannot be particles, theoretical physicists have thrown all logic to the winds and taken the results as evidence of the existence of a conceptual chimera called 'wave-particle', or even 'wavicle', thereby elevating an absurdity to the level of an esoteric discovery.

Whoever subscribes to this 'photon' interpretation of light quanta is therefore stuck with that chimera and all the mystery associated with it. To dispense with the chimera, on the other hand, is to dispose straight away of the mystery, since there is no mystery whatsoever in regarding light waves as kinematical time-sequences of instantaneous transactions of energy in quantum packets $h\nu$.

In any case, the notion that the elements of a wave can be particles conveyed at the speed of the wave is absurd, which becomes plain if we try to think of the molecules of water in a water wave as travelling along at the speed of the wave, or the molecules of air in a sound wave accompanying the wave at the speed of sound, which would signify a disaster for the recipient in either case.

Obviously, then, if we refrain from interpreting light-quanta as travelling particles, the mystery of how those particles can cause patterns of wave-interference is automatically solved, since those 'particles' can scarcely be accused of causing 'interference' when they are nonexistent.

Moreover, since the light-wave as it develops is anchored (statistically) at both ends, any postulate of so-called 'superluminal' influences acting ahead of it is redundant. As relatively permanent forms in the quantum discretum, the source and screen in the two-slit experiment are in (Heraclitan) continual proper-time-instantaneous contact even when no light-waves are travelling between them — the contact, of course, being that of the neo-Newtonian angular momentum nexus already described.

Typically, therefore, in the two-slit apparatus, as designed and manufactured, even before it is put into use, there are as many whole geometrical proper-time-instantaneous lengths as a draughtsman may draw between the source and the screen via the two slits; and every one of these lengths is potentially a free path for a quantum transaction between inter-resonating atoms at the two places.

Let us now, in our minds, send an imaginary wave through the apparatus. This divides the lengths of the paths theoretically into positive and negative half-units of the quantum length $\lambda = hc/\Delta E$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the imaginary wave, and $\Delta E$ is its energy. This means that in terms of those positive and negative half-lengths, the paths, where they intersect at the screen, are now either congruent or incongruent. That is to say, where two positive or two negative half-lengths coincide, the sum of the potentials at that point is doubled (quadrupled in area terms), and where a positive half-length coincides with a negative one, the sum of the two potentials at that point is zero.

Now this purely imaginary wave is virtually the Schrödinger wave-function, which is cast in terms of probability, as are the imaginary interference-patterns projected from it. The only difference between our imaginary wave and the equally imaginary probability-wave of Schrödinger is that there are now no hidden variables, such as an underlying wave-function which 'collapses' into actuality, as in current quantum wave-dynamics.

Our wave-substratum is no more nor less than the apparatus itself as an absolutely extended neo-Newtonian whole; and the probabilities of, or potentialities for, interaction are provided by those paths that are geometrically projected though it. These probabilities, of course, are precisely in accordance with the pattern of interference that is actually observed when energy is beamed through the apparatus.

The 'interference' we are now discussing is therefore not any actual interference, as of waves passing between source and screen, but a pure probability potential for that interference as defined by the Schrödinger equation, but whose substratum is no longer any metaphysical continuum but simply the geometry of the paths themselves, as neo-Newtonian, proper-time-instantaneous wholes.

But, of course, while the geometrical potentiality of the apparatus for wave-interference is instantaneous, any waves that actually pass through it are relativistically time-retarded, in the same way that everything in the space of a cinematographic still is connected instantaneously while the cinematographic, or sequential, motion remains finite.

Predictably, then, to place in either of those paths a detector of any kind is to bar the path to source-screen interaction by appropriating that particular potential to itself, thus effectively turning the two-slit into a single-slit apparatus in which there can be no expectation of light-interference. This is confirmed when the apparatus and detector are switched on, and the fringe-pattern is seen to have disappeared, together with any mystery attaching to this result.

Predictable also is that these path-potentials will be the same, regardless of how many quantum transactions actually take place. With a probability amplitude so low that even if only one quantum is transferred per month, the cumulative effect has to be the same as for many transactions at once — since, of course, it is the paths that interfere, not the actual transactions.

No less predictable, on the same grounds, is the reputedly mysterious fact that electrons and other 'slower-than-$c$' particles, in beams or individually, distribute themselves in the final detection area in the typical two-slit interference patterns. This, once again, is because it is not the particles that interfere with one another, or with themselves by duplicating themselves and traversing both slits, as if that were even remotely possible. What 'interferes' are the paths, as proper-time-instantaneous wholes, that is, in congruent or incongruent numbers of halves of the lengths $\lambda = h/mv$, where $m$ is the mass of the particle and $v$ ($v < c$) its velocity, according to the usual de Broglie relation.

Philosophical Conclusion

A critic complains that there seems to be some ambiguity in this neo-Newtonian conception of distance. In the one case, he says, distance is defined in terms of the lengths $r$ in the angular momentum relation $mvr$. In the other case, distance is defined as a dimension projected from informational patterns of intrinsically distanceless action-events, or quantum touchings.

There is, however, no logical ambiguity. The constituent masses of atoms, the so-called protons, electrons, and so on, possess (neo-Newtonian) angular momentum separation in terms of the length of their moment-arms, but in those angular momentum linkages they have no distance-time separations of the sort signified by $s/c$. The only distance-time separations they may have are as components of those ensembles of atoms we call bodies, the distances between which are projected from patterns and intensities of co-occurring and otherwise distanceless interactions in action-units $h$, as stated in (6).

So, there is no discrepancy between the two descriptions of distance: in both, it is the dimension $s$. Indeed, distance can be projected in the way it is because it is there absolutely, in angular momentum terms, behind and beyond the informational projections; otherwise, the implications of the projection concept would be totally idealistic (solipsistic).

The fact that this angular-momentum nexus underpins the informational projections means that there are none of those absurd subjectivist or observationalist consequences that have been embraced by physicists and philosophers of the Logical Positivist persuasion. This underpinning, moreover, does not precipitate the theory into the equally absurd 'hidden realities' consequences of reactionary anti-positivism, or 'realism'.

What this theory promises, in general, is a return to physical objectivity from the runaway subjectivity of observationalism in modern physics. This it does without compromising true relativity or quantum theory. The minimal adjustment required is simply to dump the customary, 'analog' conception of the quantum elements of light-waves as travelling-particles, or 'photons', in favour of a digital conception of those waves as patterns and time-sequences of proper-time-instantaneous quantum jumps. Those time-sequences are, of course, stochastic.

The theory is nevertheless holistic (in the Heraclitan sense of that word, not the Parmenidean), which means that it is non-deterministic.

The approach to physics advocated by this theory may now be summarised as follows. A metre rule is a hundred centimetres long because the centimetre is a hundredth of a metre, not because the metre is a hundred centimetres. In the same way, the atomic and other elements of our world are regarded in this theory not as the ultimate realities out of which our world is generated but simply as the terminal irreducibles of the one and only reality which is the basis of all true science, the reality of our immediately perceived, ordinary everyday world.
