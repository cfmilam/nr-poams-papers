Proofs of the Non-Velocity of c — Nev's Question

N. Vivian Pope

Nev writes:

Dear Viv,

Regarding your electrical demonstration that $c$ is not a speed. I can't see what the 'problem' is. To me the situation you describe is self-explanatory.

Think of a circuit consisting of a length of garden hose. At point A, at the beginning of the hose, water is pumped through a flow meter. At the opposite end, B, the hose is connected back to the input end of the pump through an ordinary tap, so as to complete the circuit. Now it is obvious that with the tap in the shut position, if the meter registers no flow, this signifies that the hosepipe is already full of water from A up to the tap, so that the water is ready to flow straight into the input end of the pump when the tap is opened. And when that tap is opened at the B end, the flow meter at the other end, B, immediately registers the flow.

By analogy, then, the electric circuit you have drawn in Figure 2 is already full, throughout its length, from the negative terminal of the battery at A right up to the switch at B, so that as soon as the switch is switched on, the current flows into the battery through the positive terminal. There is, then, in my view, no question of any mysterious 'reversal of causality' to be addressed.

Best wishes, Nev.

Viv replies:

Dear Nev,

It is this that makes it a mystery that the flow meter registers nothing while (supposedly) the pipe 'fills up'.
