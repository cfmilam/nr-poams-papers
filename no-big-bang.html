No Big Bang

N. Vivian Pope — 2011

A Logical Alterative to the ‘Big Bang’ (524 wds.)

The well-publicised intention of the large hadron Collider (LHC) experiment at CERN was to discover the so-called ‘God Particle', or Higgs Boson, as evidence of the alleged ‘Big Bang’. So far, this search has not succeeded, so perhaps it is time to consider some alternative to the ‘Big Bang’ theory, just in case it continues to fail. One logical alternative is that the whole ‘Expanding Universe’ assumption underlying the ‘Big Bang’ theory is false. This is because it is based on the fallacious assumption that since all receding light-sources are red-shifted, seeing a light-source red-shifted is the same as seeing that source receding – which is like saying that because all kippers are fish, any fish you see is a kipper. This underlying fallacy of the ‘expanding universe’ interpretation of the Hubble redshift opens the way to other interpretations which do not involve galactic recession. One such alternative is that the redshift is the effect of relativistic timedilation with increases in the random velocities of galaxies with distance. This is for the same reason that in an angular momentum system like our Solar System, the orbital speeds of bodies increase as they approach the System’s angular momentum barycentre, which, lies within the Sun’s mass. But, of course, our Sun cannot be the angular momentum barycentre of the whole universe. That universal barycentre must therefore be somewhere else. Indeed, there is no logical contradiction in assuming that in a normally distributed (i.e., non-expanding) universe there is no ‘barycentre’, or centre of mass, as such, but that the angular momentum countermass is the accumulated mass of bodies in proportion to the line-of sight distance from wherever the observer may be situated. Typically, then, of orbital angular momentum, the speeds of the galaxies increase in proportion to the distance relative to the observer on account of their increasing ‘closeness’, as it were, to that distant accumulated mass, hence their increased remoteness from the observer. Think of it this way: if, instead of occupying the small space that it does, the sun were infinitely more massive and spread all over the universe, then with the same consequence as is normally observed with the Sun, the nearer the bodies orbit to that distant spread-out mass the faster they go, not in any particular direction, i.e., radially, but in any direction according to the local angular momentum relations of the galaxies with one another. This is as befits the basic concept of relativity according to which all masses and other physical measures are relative to and centred on the observer, as opposed to absolute and universal, as in the ‘Big Bang’ theory. Such, then is a logical alternative to the Big Bang theory, based on a consistently relativistic interpretation of the Hubble redshift. The observational consequences of these two alternative theories, the radial and the relativistic, are shown to be the same. The only difference is that in the logical alternative there is no ‘Big Bang’, no ‘God-Particle’ (Higgs boson) and no need for any multi-billion dollar investment in searching for such a nonentity.
