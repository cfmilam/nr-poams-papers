The Radical Theory of 'Quantum Touching'

N. Vivian Pope — 1984

Background

The concept of 'Quantum Touching' — otherwise called Quantum Immediacy — was formulated to address the problem of the seeming incompatibility between the implications of Relativity and those of Quantum Theory. Einstein's Relativity, through his Second Postulate of the 'constant speed of light in vacuo', implies that no physical influence can travel faster than the speed of light. Quantum Theory, on the other hand, requires that certain interactions between distant particles be instantaneous — a requirement dramatically confirmed by the Aspect experiments of the 1980s.

The Lewis Insight

The key to resolving this paradox was provided by the physical chemist G. N. Lewis, who showed in 1926 that according to Einstein's own Special Theory of Relativity, the 'proper time' and 'proper distance' of any quantum interaction (i.e., any interaction involving the exchange of a single quantum of energy) are identically zero. This means that in their own intrinsic frame of reference, the two interacting atoms are at the same place at the same time — they are, in effect, touching — regardless of how far apart they appear to the observer.

Lewis's conclusion was that the 'photon' is not a particle travelling through space but an event simultaneously involving both the source atom and the detector atom. As he put it: 'I therefore take the liberty of proposing that an atom never emits light except to another atom... I propose to eliminate the idea of mere emission of light and substitute the idea of transmission, or a process of exchange of energy between two definite atoms.'

The POAMS Development

In the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS), this insight of Lewis's is developed into a complete theory of 'Quantum Touching' or Quantum Immediacy. The essential point is this: if the proper time and proper distance of a quantum interaction are zero, then the constant $c$ cannot be the 'speed' at which something travels between the atoms. Instead, $c$ is simply the dimensional conversion factor between metres and seconds in the observer's frame — the ratio in which distance-time $s/c$ relates to the observed time-delay.

The time-delay we observe is not due to light 'travelling' but is simply the difference between the observer's local clock-readings and the distance-time of the event. This is expressed in the Pythagorean formula:

$$t_R = \sqrt{t^2 + (s/c)^2}$$

For quantum interactions (where $t = 0$), this gives $t_R = s/c$, which is the observed time-delay conventionally attributed to 'light travelling at speed $c$'.

Implications

The implications of Quantum Touching are profound and far-reaching:

  1. The EPR paradox is dissolved. There is no contradiction between Relativity and Quantum Theory because the 'speed-limit $c$' is not a speed-limit on physical interaction but a dimensional constant. Individual quantum interactions are instantaneous (non-local); the time-delay is in the sequences of these interactions (local).
  2. Wave-particle duality disappears. The 'photon' is neither a wave nor a particle but an instantaneous quantum event common to two atoms. The 'wave' aspect arises from the sequential, movie-like time-ordering of these individual events.
  3. 'Action-at-a-distance' is rehabilitated. Since quantum interactions are proper-time-instantaneous, they are genuine cases of unmediated action-at-a-distance. No 'field', 'ether', or other mediating device is required.
  4. The observer is restored to physics. Since the time-delay $s/c$ is an observational time — a feature of the observer's frame, not of the quantum interaction itself — the observer becomes an integral part of the physical description, as Mach and the phenomenalists always maintained.

Conclusion

Quantum Touching is not a new theory added to the existing ones. It is a radical reinterpretation of existing physics — a recognition that the implications of Einstein's own Relativity, when followed through consistently, lead to a unification of Relativity and Quantum Theory that makes the notorious 'EPR paradox' simply vanish. The price of this unification is the abandonment of the customary 'speed of light' interpretation of $c$. The reward is a physics that is both simpler and more coherent than anything currently on offer. ■
