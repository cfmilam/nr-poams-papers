A New Angle on Angular Momentum

NASA's 'Pioneer Anomaly'

Introduction

In January, 2008, after publishing their joint research at Keele University, UK, in a book on Light-Speed, Gravitation and Quantum Instantaneity $^{[1]}$ the authors, Anthony D. Osborne and N. Vivian Pope were presented with something of a challenge. This was when a retired space-scientist, Rod Goodman, once connected with NASA, suggested that their work on angular momentum might be mobilised to solve the so far unsolved problem of the 'Pioneer Anomaly, a mystery that was plaguing scientists of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) at NASA. The Anomaly, they were informed, was that over a period of over three decades travelling through the solar system between the planets, NASA's space-probes, Pioneers 10 and 11 in particular, were found to have veered off the trajectories they should have followed according standard 'gravitational' theory. What NASA had discovered was, In effect, that the 'gravitational constant' $G$, whose constancy has long regarded as sacrosanct, seemed to be a variable. Neither Newton's nor Einstein's theories of gravitation were able to explain this anomalous variation other than to assume that some strange and invisible, unknown 'force', in addition to gravity, was accelerating these bodies off course, towards the sun and the planets they visited as they travelled away from earth through the solar system. Since the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS) predicted just those sorts of changes in the value of $G$ for spinning bodies, such as NASA's probes, it seemed to be a perfect candidate for explaining those variations in orbital trajectories that NASA had discovered.

These changes in $G$ are uniquely predicted by Pope and Osborne as due to the inclusion of the spins of bodies in their total orbital angular momentum, which standard 'gravitational' theory does not do, But all NASA's space-probes have to spin in order to maintain the orientation of their radio antennae with respect to earth. So the omission of spin from the total angular momentum account seemed a rather obvious anomaly – not in the trajectories of those space-probes but in the whole 'gravitational' theory that has been applied to them.

Undoubtedly it was this quite conspicuous fit between the Angular Momentum Synthesis and the phenomenon of the Pioneer Anomaly which prompted our NASA informant, Goodman, to suggest developing POAMS along those lines. The first flush of enthusiasm about this prospect, however, was extinguished by the information, supplied again by Goodman, that the spins of NASA's probes were, in fact, orientated in such a way, with respect to the ecliptic, that the changes in orbit of those probes predicted by the equations of the Angular Momentum Synthesis failed miserably as a possible explanation of the Pioneer Anomaly. This was on account of the spin and orbit vectors of the probes summing practically to zero.

Yet it seemed that the Synthesis should, in all qualitative respects, have provided the perfect explanation. Everything fitted so nicely except for those vectorial equations. Something just had to be wrong. If POAMS did not solve the Anomaly, then what did? The consensus answer to that was, by all accounts, nothing.

At this point POAMS became split in two over the question of what was wrong. The negative view on this was that despite its seeming suitability, something else, other than POAMS, would have to explain the anomaly – in effect, that the suggestion of mobilising POAMS to explain the Pioneer Anomaly should be abandoned, leaving NASA to its own, far more expert, devices. The positive view, on the other hand, was that failure of POAMS to consummate its rather obvious qualitative fitness for solving the Pioneer Anomaly had to be on its quantitative account, that is to say, its vectorial equation. The question then arose, concerning the law of angular momentum conservation, as to what was conserved, the vectors or the magnitudes. If it was the magnitudes that were conserved, then the fact that the vector equation had failed was inconsequential since, demonstrably (see following) the magnitudes alone could account for the anomaly, regardless of vector orientation.

There thus arose, within POAMS, the serious question of which of the two 'understandings' of angular momentum was the 'correct' one the 'vectors' argument or the 'amounts' (magnitudes) argument. Indeed, so polarized did these opposite views become, with so much evidence presented for both sides, that one of the POAMS group, David Howells, of Swansea, was prompted to say that in his view:

'No-one really understands angular momentum, not even the experts'.

This view is supported, in the above-mentioned book, Light-Speed, Gravity and Quantum Instantaneity [1], (henceforward referred to simply as 'the book') This reveals that until now it has not been recognised by the science-community that angular momentum in itself can explain the orbital motions of bodies without reference to Newton's in vacuo 'gravitational force'. This, in itself, makes us suspect that even in the standard textbooks, the phenomenon of orbital motion is still not completely understood.

Then trouble with 'gravitational force' as the agency of orbital motion is that it is the same for all bodies, purely on account of their mass, hence is assumed to be the same for orbiting bodies which are spinning as for bodies which are not. But this 'gravitational' theory is the theory NASA uses to track their spinning space-robes. Replacing 'gravity' with angular momentum as the basis of orbital motion is necessarily to include the angular momentum of spin with that of orbit in the total angular momentum account of an orbiting spinning body. This inclusion of spin, in the aforementioned book, entails that Newton's 'universal gravitational constant G' is, in truth, a variable, which, as already mentioned, provides a viable explanation of the reported 'anomalous' trajectories of NASA's space-probes.

In view of the continued irresolution, within POAMS, of the argument over 'the correct understanding' of angular momentum, and taking into consideration both Howells' opinion and the proof (in the book) of the neglected fact that angular momentum in itself can account for orbital motion, the following investigation was undertaken. The aim of this dialogue, is to unpick the current concept of angular momentum right down to its barest practical elements and then reassemble these on purely commonsense-logical lines. The dialogue is between a POAMS member, who shall be named 'N', and a completely unprejudiced enquirer, a non-mathematical, purely intuitive Arts graduate named 'M'.

'M': You claim that the Pioneer Anomaly is a problem created by a deficiency in the current understanding of angular momentum. What is angular momentum?

'N': Angular momentum is, essentially, a physical quantity, that is, an amount.

'M': An amount or quantity of what?

'N' An amount or quantity of motion, with the dimensions – that is, the combination of measures – energy multiplied by time. For instance, the earth, with its mass m in kilograms, orbits the sun with a velocity v in metres per second at a radial distance r in metres. That is to say, the earth's angular momentum is the quantity mvr.

That's fair enough. I can understand that.

But, of course, the 'v' in mvr signifies a velocity of the mass m, and velocity is defined as a speed in some given direction. So as well as being a quantity, angular momentum is also a vector quantity.

What does that mean, 'a vector quantity'?

Well, take again the earth and the sun. As a very simplified case, think of the earth's orbit around the sun like the rim of a wheel rotating around an axle. And as a wheel can rotate in either direction so can the earth around the sun – theoretically, at least, just for the sake of the argument.

That's easy enough.

However, like all true quantities, angular momentum is conserved, which, as described in the book, means that all the orbits of all the various bodies in a system such as, for example, the solar system, form an interconnected angular momentum whole in which each and every body or particle is in a balanced and paired angular momentum relation with each and every other. This makes the system as a whole, extremely complex. However, this system can be broken down into much simpler constituents which are much easier to understand, such as our example of the earth circling the sun in an ideally circular orbit around an ideally motionless centre.

Okay so far. Bur what about the vector. Where does this 'vector' come in?

Well now, when we look at a body orbiting, say, the sun we do not see a vector – not because it is invisible but simply because it isn't really there. What we see is simply the motion of the body in its orbit, like the moon around the earth and the planets around the sun. The vector is an abstract mathematical projection of the measure of the angular momentum of that body along an imaginary line at right-angles to the plane of the motion, either on the one side of the plane or the other. There is a whole geometry of these imaginary vectors, pointing in various directions and intersecting at various angles to one another representing the compositions of the various rotations and directions in an abstract but effective mathematical way. In some cases those compositions of vectors are very complex. However we can ignore those complexities for present purposes and concentrate on the bare principles, For this we need a simple practical demonstration.

Now you witnessed that experiment with the bicycle wheels on the rotating beam, in Burton on Trent. Here (on the laptop (Fig. 1) is a diagram of that experiment.

Elements of the Angular Momentum Amounts Experiment

Now recall that the beam in this apparatus was freely rotatable and that the wheels were spun in the same plane as the beam by electric motors with their axles fixed to opposite ends of the beam. These, if you remember, were connected to a remote twelve-volt battery through flexible cables via a reversible switch.

Yes, I remember that well. When those motors were spun in forward or reverse I thought they would take my head off!

Well, now, in order to illustrate the principle of angular momentum, think of the beam as an orbit radius, like that of the earth around the sun. The only difference, of course, is that for the sake of the exercise we now have to think of the 'sun' and the 'earth' as being, like the wheels, the same size and mass, balanced around the common fulcrum in the diagram. Otherwise, the angular momentum principle is the same.

Now you saw how, when the wheels were spun in the same direction, the beam rotated in the opposite direction around the fulcrum.

Yes, and when the wheels began to spin, the beam rotated immediately as if by magic. That was very impressive!

And you saw that when the spins of both the wheels are reversed, they orbited – that is, the beam rotated – at the same speed but in the opposite direction.

Yes, reversing instantly, again as if by magic.

... and this, you recall, was without any over-run or follow-through of the beam. Then, when the wheels were spun in opposite directions to each other, the beam didn't move at all, or, when the beam was already rotating, it stopped turning immediately.

Yes, as if it had been seized by an invisible hand. As I say, it was most impressive.

You have seen, then, how in the experiment the total angular momentum of the orbit (the beam) plus the spin (the wheels) is conserved. That is to say, when the wheels are spun in the same direction as each other, their vectors (directions) add and the rotation of the beam (the orbital angular momentum) is at maximum. And then you saw how, when the wheels were spun in opposite directions to each other, the orbital angular momentum (rotation of the beam) ceased. Now what is important to note, here, is that in this instance, the vectors of the wheels subtracted to zero, immediately. But although the beam had stopped rotating, the wheels kept spinning. In other words, when the wheels were spun in opposite directions the total amount of angular momentum didn't just vanish off the face of the earth; it was converted into spin thus conserving the overall amount of angular momentum. So, as I think you'll agree, this is a realistic demonstration that the amount, or magnitude of the angular momentum is conserved regardless of direction – that is, of vector orientation.

Yes, absolutely. The demonstration was so plain and simple. But I understand that in POAMS there has been opposition to this statement that the magnitude of angular momentum is conserved. The opposition claims that the magnitude, or amount is not conserved, that what is conserved are the directions, or vectors, not the amounts. I don't understand that. This experiment with the wheels demonstrates very clearly that the amounts definitely are conserved. So why can't the opposition accept this?

A lack of practical, hands-on engineering-type experience, perhaps. But there is some excuse for this opposition. In my visit to the Physics Department at Swansea [2] Professor Tim Hollowood stated emphatically that it is 'both the magnitudes and the vectors (directions) that are conserved'.

Now this information is undoubtedly correct, and indeed, in POAMS that logical solution had already been suggested. However, it revealed that there had been a misunderstanding on both sides of the conflict.

In what way is that?

Well, it meant that in :POAMS angular momentum had not been completely understood. In my discussion with Hollowood, I realised that what he meant by the conservation of vectors was simply that whatever might be the direction of the spin motion imparted to an orbiting space=probe at the start, unless acted upon by external forces, is retained or conserved thereafter, throughout its journey. This was not meant to imply that the vector, as such, is conserved in any magnitude sense. In any case, thinking of abstract vectors as possessing in themselves any real motion, like those spins and so on that they represent is a fallacy.

Yes, if they are abstract, or unreal, then how can they have any real magnitudes of angular momentum – or indeed of anything real. That’s just got to be a contradiction in terms! But now it occurs to me: what about relativity? Aren’t these motions supposed to be different in different observer frames – when orbiting the various planets, for instance – and doesn’t this change the values of the angular momentum?

Well, actually, no. It is well known that angular momentum is one of the few absolute measures there are. If it is conserved in any one observer-frame, then it is conserved in all observer frames, the same relative to them all. So as the velocity of the probe increases relatively to a planet, the value of the radius component of the angular momentum decreases to compensate for that increase in velocity. In other words, for Newtonian, non-relativistic mass and velocity – which is what we are talking about in relation to the space-robe – the mass is constant, so as the probe approaches the planet – that is, as its radial distance from it diminishes – the increase in velocity makes up for that decrease in orbital distance so that the angular momentum remains the same throughout its journey. In relativity it’s the same, but we needn’t go into that here, since the velocities we are concerned with are nowhere near relativistic, .

CONCLUSION

The orbital dynamics NASA uses to track its space-probes is Newtonian, that is, 'gravitational', which takes no account of the spin angular momentum of an orbiting body. The salient difference, in the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS), is that the total angular momentum of an orbiting body, such as a spinning space-probe, includes that of both orbit and spin.

The effect of that inclusion is, logically, to reduce the amount of orbital angular momentum by the amount due to the spin, so that a spinning space-probe has less orbital angular momentum, hence a smaller orbital radius, than a non-spinning one.

Thus, while the orbital trajectory of a non-spinning space-probe is exactly Newtonian, the orbital trajectory of a spinning space-probe is non-Newtonian. This is because the orbital amount or magnitude is reduced by the amount of spin (the brown zone in the four diagrams 2a to 2d). This reduction in the amount of orbital angular momentum (in the blue zone), due to the amount of spin angular momentum in the brown zone, is either increased or decreased by the differences in the angular momentum (yellow zone) due to the directions of spin, or vectorial angle, as described in Chapter seven of the Osborne-Pope Book. The effects of these spin vectors in the apportioning of the angular momentum in the various bands or compartments are illustrated in Figs. 2b to 2d.

When there is no spin of any kind, the orbit is exactly Newtonian. In this case, there is no brown zone to be depicted. The whole of the band representing the total angular momentum is then blue. Otherwise, any amount of spin, in any direction whatsoever, even when the vectors cancel to zero, increases the brown zone and shrinks the orbit (the blue zone), making it less than Newtonian. With this consequent reduction of orbital angular momentum the probe 'veers' or 'accelerates' away from its ideal Newtonian trajectory towards the centre of motion, be it the sun and or any other planet it visits in its journey throughout the solar system.

The widths of these bands in the four diagrams vary according to the composition of the vectors, as described in Chapter 7 of the book. These variations are, of course, set at the launch of the probe and are maintained invariant throughout the journey thereafter.
