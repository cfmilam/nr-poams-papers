The evidence and consequences of Newtonian Instantaneous Forces

Neal Graneau - Dept. of Engineering Science, Oxford University, Parks Rd. Oxford, OX1 3PJ, UK Peter Graneau – 205 Holden Wood Rd.. Concord, MA 01742, USA

Newtonian Gravitation Although banded around in loose conjecture for more than 2000 years, the concept of force has only gained solid mathematical interpretation since the publication of Newton’s Principia [1] in 1686. Until then, a long and eminent list of philosophers from Aristotle to Descartes had tried to quantify force which was an essential prerequisite in the search for the causes of motion and changes of motion.

It must have been perplexing that observations of the regular movements of astronomical bodies seemed to conflict with the irregular motions of objects on earth. It was difficult to reconcile that while all celestial bodies were found to move continuously in an orbital manner, on earth unconstrained bodies seemed to move in straight trajectories and usually came to a stop.

Possibly, the most crucial step towards our conquest of the mysteries of motion was an appreciation of the difference between velocity and acceleration. While it seems so obvious to us now in our world of rockets and high speed cars, this subtle distinction was not quite appreciated even by some of the early heroes of celestial mechanics up to and including Kepler.

However, Galileo, the Italian astronomer whose name stands as an icon of scientific revolution was probably the first to make clear this distinction and thus foresee the paradigm shift that was to be caused by Newton’s work. However, Galileo was not able to represent his concepts in a suitably mathematical manner so that the principles could be tested in a general way and written down in a condensed form.

The quantitative nature of the dynamics of bodies on earth and in the heavens was to be Newton’s ingenious discovery. The ramifications of this unique achievement have led directly to our modern world of engineering and technology because of his successful linkage of precise mathematics with empirical measurement.

His universal law of gravitation united the cause of changes of motion of celestial objects with the same mechanism that described how an apple falls to earth from a tree branch. While Newton logically induced his laws of motion from large amounts of observation by his predecessors and colleagues, he did not perform controlled laboratory experiments which would be the only way to fully confirm his law of gravitation.

He made no attempts to measure the mass of the earth or any other astronomical body, but simply relied on the assumption that the forces they exerted and felt were proportional to their mass. The measurement that finally confirmed the numerical accuracy of Newton’s universal law of gravitation was performed in a laboratory by the eccentric noble Englishman, Lord Henry Cavendish in 1798. His experiment which has now been repeated with ever increasing accuracy over the last 200 years [2], measured the very weak

gravitational force between large and small spheres of known mass in the laboratory. He confirmed the inverse square nature of Newton’s force law and measured the dimensional constant, G, now known as Newton’s gravitational constant. With this, he achieved his aim of being the first to measure the mass of the earth.

As a result of a successful controlled laboratory experiment, Newton’s law of gravitation had now acquired an irreproachable status. It had become a mathematical statement that in conjunction with his 2nd law of motion allowed mankind to describe the relative acceleration of gravitational attraction between every pair of bodies in the universe.

Newton prescribed no mechanism for this attraction for he feared the danger of unfounded hypotheses. To this day, no description of how this attraction is manifested has been attempted. However the relative acceleration undoubtedly occurs and the phenomenon is known as a form of Instantaneous-Action-At-A-Distance (IAAAD).

Modern physics books pay great regard to Newton’s laws as a notable historical achievement that has now been completely overthrown by Einstein’s theories of Special Relativity (SR) and General Relativity (GR). One of Einstein’s motivations that led to his revolutionary theories was the apparent non-invariance of Maxwell’s laws of electrodynamics which in order to remain valid required an adjustment to our notion of the measurement of motion and also to the properties of light.

These assumptions eventually led to the theory of SR. One of Einstein’s motivations in the development of his gravitational theory, (GR), stemmed from philosophical problems regarding whether it was possible to have a stable infinite homogeneous universe being controlled by a law of universal gravitation based on an inverse square interaction strength.

This paradox had been a hot topic of debate since Newton’s time and Einstein suggested that a possible solution was to require that the GR universe have a finite amount of matter in a finite and curved space. Unfortunately, GR, which differs in its predictions only very marginally from Newton’s law of gravitation, has never been tested in a controlled laboratory experiment.

Until this is achieved, Newton’s law will retain more validity on the grounds of its solid empirical foundation. No act of controllable human engineering or measurement has ever required the use of anything other than Newton’s laws to successfully predict the effects of gravity.

Newton’s theories did suffer from one major weakness, for which we can hardly blame him given his limited knowledge of the vast extent of the universe. Throughout the Principia, motion is discussed in absolute terms relative to a conceptual Absolute Space.

Ernst Mach in1893 [3], argued that when two bodies attract each other and the distance between them decreases, the only way in which their net momentum can be conserved is if their velocities are measured with respect to what he called the “fixed stars”.

He defined this frame using astronomical objects, precisely to avoid Newtonian Absolute Space. However like Newton, Mach’s knowledge of the extent of the universe was still very limited compared to what we now have observed. Nevertheless, his concept of stars that in the human time scale stay fixed in their positions around us also applies to the vast sea of galaxies that exist throughout the universe.

Therefore his statements are still valid. He argued [3] that “…we see that even in the simplest case, in which apparently we deal with the mutual action of only two masses, the neglecting of the rest of the world is impossible.”

This last statement comes nearest to what is now generally referred to as Mach’s Principle. Since the magnitude of the accelerations of any two interacting pieces of matter is linked in this way to their motions with respect to the distant universe, it seems a small step to accept that Mach’s Principle is responsible for the force of inertia, which is nature’s mechanism for controlling acceleration. To complete the paradigm suggested by this principle then still requires a law of nature which describes the nature of the acceleration dependent interaction between a particle in the laboratory and another in the distant universe. We will call this the Machian inertial particle interaction law which must provide a finite and non-zero force of inertia on every object that accelerates with respect to the Machian frame. Several attempts have been made this century to find this law based on a faulty electrodynamic analogy. However we are proposing a form of this law based purely on Newtonian gravitation. The most recent observations of the universe demonstrate that the universe is not homogeneous but has a fractal distribution of matter. Effectively, this means, that in an infinite universe, the mass density decreases in all directions from any point of observation. Observations show that this density decreases in just such a manner that allows us to retain Newtonian gravity in a Euclidean geometry and avoid ill defined gravitational forces. Our proposed new law of inertial force is presented at the end of this chapter.

IAAAD in Electrodynamics The history of the physical theories that have led to our present understanding of electromagnetism is not well known and is an illuminating mixture of human ingenuity and failings. Although it is only a small window in the long history of this subject, there was a period between Newton and Maxwell (1670-1870), when IAAAD theories were fully accepted to explain all observable mechanical forces. However even during this period, the study of light was concluded to be a separate issue. Maxwell [4] proposed the connection between electrodynamics and light and set the stage for the unification of the two concepts. The reality of Maxwell's proposed waves were famously seen to be confirmed by the results presented by Hertz [5]. However Maxwell's theory was soon found to be imperfect, and Einstein's theory of relativity was created when it became clear that Maxwell's field equations were not invariant under Galilean transformations. As a result of the subsequent discovery of the quantum nature of the universe, it has become clear that Maxwell's purely continuum theory is certainly not a complete description of electromagnetic radiation. However there is a general attempt now to retain both the Maxwellian wave and quantum particle theories of light even though they seem to be fundamentally conflicting, implying that light phenomena are observed to possess either wave-like or particle-like properties but never both at the same time. The physics community has decided to accept this paradox as part of nature, but it is proposed here that since theories are only man made devices, then it is more likely that the theories are incomplete than that Nature is internally paradoxical. Recent empirical discoveries show that neither the Lorentz force nor the field energy concept deep in the heart of modern relativistic electromagnetism are consistent with simple laboratory experiments [6]. The resolution of these inconsistencies is found not by seeking

yet more esoteric multi-dimensional descriptions of nature, but rather by returning to the pre-Maxwellian electrodynamic theories of Ampère, Neumann and their contemporaries. Their laws, which belong to the Newtonian IAAAD tradition are naturally Galilean invariant, and are shown to be consistent with all known observations involving electric conductors.

Our interest over the last 20 years has been to develop these mid-19th century concepts and equations into 21st century form. This has required reinterpretation of some of the modelling involved in the laws. This is to be expected for there was no knowledge of the electron or the mechanism of electric conduction when these theories were created.

Similarly, calculations involving these formulae are now performed on computers in a manner that was not possible when they were first published. Not only is this analysis informative, but since these laws describe interactions between discrete elements, solutions to most problems are only achievable by computer.

In a parallel vein, there are now high current sources that allow experimental testing of some of the predictions of Ampère’s IAAAD force law. Thus we presently have two major advantages over those scientists who at the end of the last century made the apparently arbitrary choice of adopting a field theory for the description of electromagnetism when they could just as easily have retained the existing IAAAD theories which had never disagreed with a single experiment.

While the choice may then have been arbitrary, and it may have been wiser to keep both options open, nevertheless field theory was accepted and IAAAD theory rejected. It will be shown that Lorentz, Einstein and their colleagues seem to have made the wrong decision.

A computer calculation will be described that demonstrates that these interpretations of electromagnetic forces are intimately related to the phenomena which include optical effects. It employs nothing more than Ampère’s force formula, first proposed in 1822 [7], and now tested extensively for its ability to predict electromagnetic forces of attraction or repulsion between current element pairs.

It will also be shown that it creates mutual torques on them which attempt to rotate the direction of current in each element. The results from the computation indicate that this law may be able to predict all of the observable effects that we ascribe to the radiation and detection of oscillating electromagnetic signals which naturally include light.

This is quite a radical new step to take even inside the world of IAAAD theory. From Newton through to Ampère and Weber, who all maintained that mechanical forces were described by IAAAD forces, there was still conviction that light was a separate phenomenon and involved the transmission and detection of a travelling entity, loosely called radiation.

While these calculations will be seen to be in their infancy, there seems to be strong correspondence between their predictions and the rather ill defined but nevertheless extremely useful quantities called probability amplitudes in the theory of Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) [8].

Due to the overwhelmingly successful predictive ability of QED, it is proposed that this new IAAAD theory based on Ampère’s force law may be able to explain all known optical effects. This would prove to be a large surprise to the physics community, that has previously assumed that a theory built on instantaneous interaction could never predict the observed time delays that are normally associated with the constant c = 2.998×108 m/s, that is known as “the speed of light”. However this constant is shown to have a long and chequered history and actually fits very neatly into the realm of IAAAD

theories, by whose creators it was first proposed and measured, even though at that time it was not called the speed of light. Recent experiments [6] have shown that some laws such as the Lorentz electromagnetic force law may have to be replaced, however many well tested laws such as the Lorentz transformations, atomic quantum theory and indeed QED will remain with their equations intact. However conceptually the universe would no longer appear to be threaded together by photons or waves, and we would be pushed into an IAAAD vision of the universe with truly empty spaces between matter that has not been considered in the western world for over 2500 years. Newton’s Principia inspired his peers and colleagues to search for other universal laws of matter interaction. The first success came in 1750, when another Cambridge physicist, John Michell, proposed an inverse square law describing the force between a pair of magnetic poles with the same form as Newton's law of gravitation. Charles-Augustin de Coulomb provided confirmation of this finding a few years later and also added his own force law predicting the attraction or repulsion between electric charges. Both of these two new laws predicted mutual attractions or repulsions equal to the product of the elemental quantities (be they quantity and sign of electric charge for Coulomb's law or polarity and strength of pole for Michell's law) multiplied by a constant and divided by the square of distance of separation. The Newtonian form of the force law gained credibility from these subsequent laws for it was now seen to be applicable to more situations than just gravity. The final classical IAAAD force law was discovered by Andre Marie Ampère in

1822. In Maxwell's treatise [4], he describes Ampère as the “Newton of electricity”, and his equation as “the cardinal law of electrodynamics”. This law, which is precisely analogous to the three previously presented, will be discussed in more detail later in this section. Like its predecessors, its form was the product of two current elements and a constant divided by the inverse square of distance, and this was also multiplied by a dimensionless angle function due to the fact that the current elements have direction. All of these IAAAD force laws were developed without theory and entirely based on experimental results. Due to their empirical nature, they are easily confirmed and have never been found to be in disagreement with experiment. Some have argued that the Newtonian gravitation law does not predict the correct precession of the perihelion of mercury, but this result is shrouded in controversy and will be briefly mentioned later. Between these four force laws, (Newtonian gravitation, Michell's force between magnetic poles, Coulomb's law of electrostatics and Ampère’s law of electrodynamics) related by their Newtonian inverse square form, physicists could predict all of the observable forces between objects which contained the only properties that could be measured, namely mass, charge, magnetic strength and electric current strength. Thus apart from optical effects, the 19th century almost witnessed a unification of the physics required to explain all known phenomena.

The delay of radiation effects In the mid-19th century, it was well known that there was a time delay between the transmission and detection of an optical signal, which was consistent with both wave and particle models. It seemed a natural guess that one should associate this delay with the transit of some mechanical entity with a measurable velocity, which we now call c.

This constant is probably the most interesting number in physics. Incredible ingenuity has been exploited in its measurement. What is remarkable is that different successful attempts to quantify it have been investigating apparently quite different quantities.

The first successful attempt to measure the delay caused by the speed of light was performed by Olaf Römer in 1676, by a clever analysis of the irregular period of the eclipse of a moon of Jupiter. He proposed a value which was two thirds of today's accepted figure.

Since this time there has only been one other one-way light speed measurement, by James Bradley, who in the early 18th century exploited a measurement of stellar aberration of a nearby star to deduce an even more accurate result. The first series of experiments to precisely measure this speed of light by well calibrated earth bound experiments were performed by Fizeau and Foucault in 1849.

These measurements all aimed to time the two way speed of light by reflecting light off a distant mirror a known distance away. These results soon became as precise as the surveying accuracy would allow producing figures very close to the value used today.

Even by 1851, Fizeau and Foucault had already measured that the speed of light is slowed down when it goes through a transparent substance. During the period when these delay measurements were being performed, several important milestones passed by, some more well known than others, and for this reason they will be presented in order of decreasing entries in the citation index rather than chronologically.

The first event was the publication of Maxwell's Treatise [4] in 1873. Even though he believed that it would probably be impossible to prove by experiment, the notion of the wave theory of light as an electromagnetic phenomenon was born. This theory was not immediately popular for it did not sit easily with the IAAAD theories currently prevailing in continental Europe.

However, the radiation aspect of Maxwell's theory was posthumously championed by a group of English and Irish scientists calling themselves “The Maxwellians” [9]. They were, most notably, Heaviside, Fitzgerald, and Lodge. These gentlemen were trying to perform experiments to test Maxwell's theory.

Their happiest moment was the announcement of the second milestone, namely the publication of Heinrich Hertz's experimental results in 1888. By means of an oscillatory spark discharge and a large metallic plane, Hertz [5] found nodes and anti-nodes of electrical activity at different locations in his laboratory and thus deduced a wavelength.

Since in wave theories the velocity of the propagation was always the product of frequency and wavelength, he estimated the frequency of his discharge from a circuit theory analysis, and as a result made the first measurement of the speed of electromagnetic radiation for non-optical frequencies.

That his value agreed with the figures arrived at by the delay measurements, encouraged physicists to assert that light and electromagnetic radiation were the same phenomenon which could be described by a wave propagating through an invisible ether.

The third milestone in the development of c came 30 years earlier in 1857, and is the least well known part of the story. This was in fact the first measurement of a constant called c. It was not called the speed of light, but was a dimensional constant that had fallen into Weber's rendition of the IAAAD electromagnetic force law [10].

Weber was attempting to combine Ampère’s law of electrodynamics with Coulomb's law of electrostatic force into a unified equation. His attempts suffered from not yet knowing any details of individual charges and their motions inside electric conductors.

Nevertheless in his theory, c was purely a factor of proportionality which had to be introduced when electrodynamic phenomena are described in terms of units which have been defined electrostatically or vice versa. That the factor which is introduced on such occasions must be of the dimensions (length / time) may be easily seen, for the electrostatic force between point charges is a quantity of the same kind as the electrodynamic force between two definite lengths of wire, carrying currents which must be specified by the amount of charge which travels past any point in unit time.

The value of this constant was measured in 1856 by Weber and Kohlrausch [11] who charged up a Leyden jar with a known capacitance and voltage and thus worked out the stored charge in electrostatic units. They then discharged the jar into the coil and measured the force that the transient current impressed on a magnet, thus measuring the charge in electromagnetic units.

They found the ratio of electrostatic charge to electromagnetic charge to be 3.1×108 m/s. This was the first measurement of the constant, c, taken from an empirically grounded IAAAD theory. Crucially this measurement had absolutely nothing to do with radiation.

The coincidence that c was equal to the measured speed of light was first taken up by Kirchoff, who was at the time developing what is now called “circuit theory”. His development of the now well used concepts of inductance, capacitance and resistance stemmed from the IAAAD electrostatic and electrodynamic theories of Ampère, Neumann, Weber and Coulomb.

Using these principles, he was the first to derive the velocity of voltage and current disturbances down a transmission line circuit [12], and found that it was c. This was achieved in 1857, almost ten years before Maxwell began publishing an explanation based on radiating fields.

Kirchoff's calculation gives us the first clue that the Newtonian IAAAD theories can yield a mechanism to explain the phenomena that are traditionally ascribed to electromagnetic radiation. As Weber had first measured it, this constant may just as easily have been presented as its inverse1/ c = 3.3× 10−9 s/m .

It is interesting to consider how theories might have developed if this constant would have been taken to represent a distance related delay rather than a velocity. A delay can be explained by means other than the transport of radiation, for instance the effect of inertia.

However historically, c was always taken to represent a speed. Kirchoff also made a statement [10, 12] that c is the velocity at which charges moving toward each other no longer exert a mutual force. Even though electrons and particle accelerators were not a part of mid-19th century consideration, Kirchoff's claim seems to explain why particles cannot be accelerated beyond the speed of light.

Note that this is now considered to be an observation that is only explicable by the velocity related inertial mass variation predicted by SR, whereas in fact it appears that it is a natural conclusion of Newtonian IAAAD theory.

The electron beam was a newly discovered phenomenon at the end of the 19th century, and the theories that attempted to explain it may have been provided rather hastily and without full consideration. A major success was the measurement of (e/m), the electron charge to mass ratio which yielded another universal constant and gave credibility to the proposed particle nature of this new entity.

However there was an urgent need to find a force law to fit the new discoveries. This was not going to be an empirically derived Newtonian IAAAD formula because it would require the measurement of the force between two isolated and moving electrons.

Even today, this result is still not known. Such a desperate situation opened the door for a new force law to be developed via theory rather than by experiment. To fully appreciate why we now use the modern Lorentz force law, we have to look back to Maxwell's Treatise [4] again.

He was aware of Grassmann's law which was, of the IAAAD laws, the greatest rival to Ampère’s. This law involved a vector cross product, which Grassmann himself had a role in inventing, and as a result did not in general predict Newtonian attractions or repulsions between pairs of elements.

However it did predict that the mutual force between two complete circuits would obey Newton's 3rd law. Unfortunately the only experiments by which Maxwell was able to judge between the two laws was a force measurement between a pair of complete circuits and thus he was forced to conclude that either law may be correct, however he noted that Ampère’s law was “undoubtedly the best, since it is the only one which makes the forces on the two elements not only equal and opposite, but in the straight line that joins them”.

Given that we no longer use Ampère’s force law, one can only ponder why scientists threw away a principle as stunningly powerful and empirically tested as Newton's 3rd law without a solid argument or experiment. The reason probably lies in Lorentz's desire in the early 1890's to be the first to publish a force law for the free electron.

Electron beams were observed to move in circular trajectories when influenced by magnetic fields, so he sought a force law which placed the force always perpendicular to his velocity dependent current element, and found that Grassmann's law did just this.

Therefore Lorentz combined the Grassmann, Biot-Savart and Coulomb laws into one equation, redefined the current element as a moving charge, and was able to predict the force on the electron beam. However in this bold step, he threw away the strong connection between Newtonian physics and electromagnetism.

The experiments that Maxwell had hoped for, that could separate the predictions of the Ampère and Grassmann laws, have now been performed [6] and they favour the Newtonian Ampère law. The manifestation of these discoveries is that the magnetic force component at the core of the presently used Lorentz force formula is incorrect.

The other pressing issue of the day was the resolution of the results of the MichelsonMorley (M-M) experiment, which attempted to detect the ether wind. Maxwell's theory was dependent on the existence of the ether to support its waves, and thus one should have been able to perceive it by comparing the relative speeds of light parallel and perpendicular to the ether wind that was caused by the earth's orbital velocity of 30 km/s.

This was expected to produce a positive result based on the 1859 findings of Fizeau and Foucault [13] who had measured the velocity of light in moving water and had discovered that the moving medium did affect the measured speed of light. Famously, the M-M experiment produced a much

smaller effect than was expected and thus was taken by the physics community to represent a null result. This plunged late 19th century physics into turmoil. Between Fitzgerald in Ireland and Lorentz in Holland, they developed the equations known as the Lorentz transformations, which define how the events in one inertial frame are interpreted when viewed from another one.

Since viewing involved light processes, these equations naturally included the speed of light. They seem to predict that an object moving through the ether with a velocity v, actually contracts by the factor , 1 − v 2 c 2 , and this was initially Fitzgerald's explanation for the quoted null result of the M-M experiment, because it predicted that the path length of the experiment parallel to the ether wind was shorter by just the amount required to hide the difference in velocities.

This explanation was accepted until the arrival of Einstein's famous 1905 paper [14]. To remain brief he simply made the assumption that there is no ether and thus without the ether wind, the null result of the M-M experiment is expected. He was worried however that removal of the ether meant that motion of material objects could no longer be considered with respect to the ether frame.

Consequently no observer could ever be considered stationary and all motions must be viewed as relative to another body or abstract frame of reference. Einstein used his famous light postulate that the speed of light is constant and independent of the motion of the source to re-derive the Lorentz transformations, however he interpreted them rather differently.

He concluded that it was not just objects that contracted but that space and time were constantly distorted by relative motions. Regardless of his admiration for Mach, he made no use of Mach’s Principle nor the concept of a reference frame defined by the relatively static distant universe.

By removing the ether, special relativity greatly weakened the Maxwellian wave theory which requires energy transfer by waves through space. This conceptual conflict necessitated the invention of electromagnetic field energy and was fulfilled by the notion of energy containing photons and a return to an era where light again was both a particle and a wave.

Einstein eventually won his Nobel prize, not for his work on relativity theory but for his role in the discovery of the photoelectric effect, which along with the Compton effect convinced people that photons were indeed a reality. It is less well known that Schrödinger [15] published a classical solution to the Compton effect which did not require photons, and Planck and others developed similar non-quantum interpretations of the photoelectric effect [16].

To conclude this chain of events it can be said that even during the quantum revolution and the discoveries that occurred throughout the remainder of the 20th century, our conventional model of light is still a blurry mix of particle and wave properties which are separate from the properties of matter and somehow mechanically transfer energy across empty space at a finite speed which is dependent on the environment.

As well, an analysis of any electrical machine that is driven by the magnetic force between two sets of coils can demonstrate that the relativistic electromagnetic field energy model violates the principle of energy conservation and therefore cannot be correct.

As an example of this problem, let us consider an induction motor in which there is no electrical connection between the stator and rotor coils. According to modern field theory, the momentum and energy that drives the motor is radiated from the stator and flies across the air

gap arriving at the rotor, thus giving it momentum. The momentum gain per second of the rotor can be measured mechanically and this must correspond to the rate of electromagnetic momentum arriving at the spinning coils. Running in parallel to research on electrodynamics and light, recent confirmation by Aspect [17] of the non-local nature of quantum mechanics predicted by John Bell [18] has yielded clues that instantaneous interactions between distant particles do indeed occur. This has caused great debate because these interactions appear to be inconsistent with the local relativistic concept of travelling photons as the only method of distant particle interaction. It will now be shown that such instantaneous interactions are still consistent with observed electromagnetic signalling delays, but nevertheless imply that the travelling photon model is incorrect.

They were positioned such that the source and detector were next to each other but were not allowed to interact with each other as if separated by a screen and the reflector was a known distance away. The source and reflector were allowed to interact and the reflector and detector as well. The net torques on the source elements were ignored as it was considered that they were being driven by a strong power supply. This could be multiplied by the length of the time step to give the change in angular velocity which was then added to the existing velocity to give the new velocity. This new velocity was then also multiplied by the time step to give the change in angular position, so that all of the elements could be set into their new directions, and then the algorithm was repeated for successive time steps.

After each step, the sine of each element was averaged for each block of elements and stored. The source elements were rotated in phase at a fixed angular frequency and the results for the three element blocks over time were plotted. Calculations have been performed using thousands of elements in each block and several hundred time steps, however these results are still very crude because it is unrealistic to perform this calculation with such a small number of elements, which are attempting to model all of the atoms in a real conductor.

An Enhanced Synthesis of IAAAD Newtonian Forces

In the last 330 years, four IAAAD force laws have been empirically discovered. When Newton proposed his universal law of gravitation, he used Kepler’s laws to deduce that there was a force of attraction between widely separated objects that had an inverse square dependence. He also was aware that the accelerations of objects that resulted from any forces were proportional to their masses. He could therefore bravely predict that it was the same force that guided the planets in their orbits that also led all objects on earth to fall toward its centre with an acceleration that did not depend on its mass. This proposed universal law of gravitation was finally awarded laboratory confirmation in 1798 by Henry Cavendish. Between Newton’s death and Cavendish’s experiment, two more inverse square laws, describing the interaction of distant particles were discovered in the laboratory. John Michell in 1750 discovered that the accelerations of pairs of magnetic poles behave as if a Newtonian inverse square law is acting between them which is proportional to the product of the two pole strengths. The primary difference between this force and the gravitational case is that the force can be attractive or repulsive depending on whether the poles are the same polarity or not. A very similar law was also empirically discovered by Priestly and Coulomb which described the attraction between electrostatically charged objects. It was difficult to measure the quantity of charge on their test bodies, but they were able to demonstrate with remarkable accuracy that the force had an inverse square nature. When Ampère in 1822 proposed the first law of electrodynamics, he naturally based his empirical discoveries on the now well endorsed Newtonian inverse square framework.

His law was deduced from experiments with metallic conductors, and like the other three laws, has never been shown to be incapable of predicting an experimental result. Recently, Ampère’s law has been successfully applied to high density plasmas, for which it is equally successful [6].

If this same law can be responsible for the effects now ascribed to electromagnetic radiation as well, then there truly is no need for the concepts of field and field energy. In this chapter, we have presented evidence demonstrating that apart from the gravitational paradox which is now resolved by the universal fractal structure, there has never been any foundation for dismissing the IAAAD paradigm represented by these four laws.

The only blemish on this schema is that we still do not have an IAAAD force law for the interaction of two isolated moving charges. However, until we can truly “observe” these charged bodies without a layer of theory between us and them, this law will be hard to find.

However, it is the next potential prize in this rediscovered and burgeoning paradigm. Our proposed force of inertia is supported every time that we observe that Newton’s nd 2 law is obeyed. It therefore cannot be falsified in a paradigm that supports Newtonian IAAAD forces and Mach’s principle.

It is interesting that these laws seem to indicate that the two fundamental dimensional constants of nature, c, the speed of light and G, Newton’s gravitational constant get their measurable values from the matter distribution of the universe and perhaps even it’s acceleration of expansion.

This seems more satisfying from a philosophical viewpoint than the conventional description that these are simply the properties of free space or a vacuum. The explanation of radiation with Ampère’s force law and the addition of our proposed law of Machian inertia seem to almost complete a Newtonian IAAAD paradigm.

Philosophers and theoretical physicists have a desire to unite these laws into one universal mechanism. However from an experimental physicist’s perspective, it seems pointless to look for a single concept that explains all of these interactions between different attributes of matter.

Reduction of the present system to a single principle would undoubtedly obscure the practical usefulness of this family of force laws. Moreover, we do not know what empirical discoveries lie ahead. If new ideas fit this Newtonian inverse square format, then they can easily be included in the paradigm.

So far, every physical discovery regarding the interaction of like objects has fitted this pattern, so we feel reasonably confident that as our knowledge of the properties of matter increases to more and more detail, so will this family of force laws. If there is a unifying principle in this universe, it appears to be Newtonian inverse square IAAAD forces.
