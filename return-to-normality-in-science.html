Return to Normality in Science

N. Vivian Pope

For the NRIG (Normal Realist Interest Group)

RETURN TO NORMALITY IN SCIENCE Professor Carver Meade, of Caltech, USA, has written that 'The last seventy years of the 20th century will be seen in history as the dark ages of science'. But neither have the first years of this 21 st century brought any spectacular enlightenment.

Indeed the gloom has got even greater. Normal Realism is an attempt to find a systematic logical-analytic way out of this conceptual murk. Normal Realism is a relatively new and updated form of phenomenalism in the long tradition of Locke, Berkeley, Hume, Kant and Mach.

As Encarta describes it, phenomenalism is the contention that 'the objects of our knowledge are the same as the objects of perception.' In other words, phenomenalism requires that all our knowledge of physical objects is direct knowledge in which the informational elements of that knowledge and those of the object itself are one and the same.

In the 1920s phenomenalism, as an approach to natural philosophy (since known as Physics), flourished with the sense-datum theories of eminent science-philosophers such as Ernst Mach, Ludwig Wittgenstein and Alfred Ayer. However, its progress soon came to an end with the pre-eminence gained by Einstein's Theory of Relativity.

In that theory, it is axiomatic that the light by which we see objects at a distance travels through space (qua void or vacuum) at a constant speed c which is the same for all observers and cannot be exceeded by any other physical influence. Plainly, if that 'Second Postulate' of Einstein's is correct, then as a scientific approach to nature, phenomenalism is left for dead.

Strangely, though, the celebrated chemist, Gilbert Lewis in 1927, noticed something about the Theory of Relativity. This was that this same theory that seemed to have demolished phenomenalism had actually confirmed it. What Lewis noticed was that although Einstein's Second Postulate, in the way it is stated, seems to separate us from distant objects by a time that is inexorably delayed, in fact, the intrinsic time – called the proper time – of the quantum light elements themselves, which Einstein called 'photons', is instantaneous.

As Lewis had discerned, according to Relativity a photonic interaction between a light-source and receiver, regardless of distance, is literally a quantum touching. This is in conformity with Newton's Third Law of equal and opposite action/reaction, in which the events of the so-called 'emission' and 'absorption' of the photon are indistinguishable. That is to say, its time of transit between the emitting and absorbing atoms is zero, hence quite literally a quantum jump.

What Lewis had established, in effect, was that according to Relativity, the true description of those quantum light-elements, in their own intrinsic nature fitted precisely the main requirement of phenomenalism that the informational elements of our knowledge of an object (the sense/instrument data) and those of the object itself are one and the same.

However, in general, it was too much to swallow that light could be, as Einstein's Second Postulate made it appear, both time-delayed and instantaneous. Regarded as completely contradictory, it was forgotten that in some phenomena, instantaneity and time-delay can exist logically together, that is, simply as complementary components of the same action.

Everyone knows, for instance, that action, in a movie, consists of a delayed time-sequence of instantaneous photographic frames or 'stills'. No-one ever regards this as in any way 'contradictory' or remotely puzzling. What, then, makes it seem so in Einstein's theory?

It is that in the way Einstein phrased his Second Postulate, it seems to forbid any such logical juxtaposition, thereby making it seem, notoriously, that Relativity and Quantum Theory are fundamentally and irreconcilably at odds. (this conflict is known among physicists as the 'EPR paradox' ) Normal Realism settles this bogus conflict by producing, independently of Einstein, a logically consistent and coherent phenomenalist version of relativity which is the same in all mathematical respects as Einstein's relativity, except that it is made very much simpler and more conceptually economical by dispensing with Einstein's Second Postulate.

This removes the barrier of 'light-separation' between object and observer, thereby reconciling quantum instantaneity and relativistic timedelay at a stroke. Members of the NRIG will b1e familiar with the way in which Normal Realism does this. It has been called the 'Cinematic Model' of relativistic quantum interaction as referred to in all the talks and seminars that have been arranged by Penny Gower, since the very first one in the Library at Briton Ferry.

Also, the dedicated reader may find, in the Research Publications of the Department of Mathematics, Keele University, the following report by Dr. Anthony Osborne: I have worked on the philosophical side of relativity with N.V.Pope over a number of years.

We have been concerned with a new philosophical approach to Special Relativity in particular, which has addressed a number of issuesofcurrent concern, such as the EPR paradox on action-at-a-distance. We have had problems in finding an outlet for this work since it is difficult to convince physicists that philosophy is important, and traditional philosophers would argue that it is not their place to question physics.

However, our work is summarised in our Physics Essays papers,1 which have been well-received by certain groups, particularly in Canada. We still have a lot of work to do in this area. 2

This cooperation with Anthony Osborne and the Mathematics Department of Keele has since been given (by an associate, Jon Blay) the acronym POAMS (the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis), many publications of which have since appeared and can be accessed on what is now the POAMS website, www.poams.org What creates the situation in which, as Osborne says, 'it is difficult to convince physicists that philosophy is important' is that, typically, physicists cannot see this 'new philosophical approach' as anything more than just another ad hoc physics theory, to be thrown into the eclectic mix with all the rest.

However, phenomenalism cannot sensibly be conceived in that way. As Michael Duffy says:3 Basically ALL PHYSICAL THEORIES are phenomenological. They cannot be anything else. … We are all information processing devices, sorting, correlating and interpreting the signals received by our senses.

Our interpretation of ourselves is a construct itself, of course. Once we have got over the initial 'hurdle' of escaping solipsism 4 and accepting that there are others like us with whom communication is possible, we can accept also that we cannot just make up any old interpretation of our sense data: not if we are to survive, make sense of things, and communicate with others.

Idiots may do so – but then idiots, in the original sense of the word, are entirely private persons. Therefore we assume an objective source of our data, which can be described and investigated by our constructs. It is like making a map, revising it, and improving it through new surveys made with better instruments.

We use models and theories to relate the data together, but these are provisional. They cannot be anything else! The concepts from which the models are built cannot be absolute, or permanent glimpses of reality, whether the concept is 'photon', 'ether', 'geometry', 'atom', 'string', or 'dimension'.

The usefulness of these notions is tested in practice (which always involves turning our experience into provisional symbols and words). A common error is to assume that the words and concepts which have been around a long time refer to Reality whilst the new ones don't!

These words of Duffy's express very clearly the essential nub of phenomenalism, as ever was. But, of course, this way of thinking in which reality is ultimately a matter of correct, most economical, best tried and tested use of language5 is inconceivable. Indeed, it is sometimes is reacted to as a sheer affront to standard orthodox physics. This, of course, is because, according to that orthodoxy we never perceive physical objects as they 'really' are – that is, as 'God' might be presumed to see them – but only as second-hand, subjective and depleted 'images' of things and events that are separated from us by the time that light takes to travel from those objects to us. This is why Normal Realism has its own home-grown phenomenalistic rendering of relativity in which, at the ultimate quantum level, there is no mediation between observation and reality, whether in the form of 'ether', 'waves', 'particles', 'waveparticles' or whatever. Instead, the ultimate sensory/instrumental 'data' of the 1920s phenomenalists are identified, in Normal Realism, with Planck's action quanta, h, as in modern quantum phenomenology.

The Normal Realist philosophical synthesis, therefore incorporates not only Relativity and Quantum Theory but also the long-divided Physics and Philosophy, all within the context of a logically consistent and theoretically unadulterated phenomenalist approach to Natural Philosophy. In this way, with 'light' restored to its original commonsense meaning of what we see6 as opposed to the purely theoretical (indeed altogether metaphysical) 'something' travelling invisibly in space, some of that illumination may be shed on the current 'Dark Age' that Professor Mead talks about.
