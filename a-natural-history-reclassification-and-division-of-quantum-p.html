A 'Natural History' Re-Classification and Division of Quantum Particles and Voids

N. Vivian Pope — c. 2010

The space outside the atom – that is, ordinary everyday space – is different from the void that NR places inside the atom because ordinary outside space is a projection out of statistical numbers of quantum 'blips', or pixels. These pixels are discrete and intrinsically unconnected. In other words, the succession of quantum pixels does not form a continuum. This means that the discontinuities between the discrete quanta are the true voids that there have to be in order to avert the consequence of an Eleatic stasis. 'In themselves', these true voids are quantityless, quality-less and dimensionless. Nothing can be positively predicated of them. All that can be predicated of them is in the negative sense that they are the ultimate gaps, or discontinuities in what there is – which means, of course, that in themselves they are nothing, not just 'void' but null-and-void: in James Jeans' words: 'nothing, not even empty space'.

Problem: How can the quantum be both the pixel and the gap between pixels?

What is the 'pixel', anyway? It is the energy transferred, in a proper-time-instantaneous quantum jump in an energetic transaction between an atom in a light-source and another atom in the eye or some other instrument of observation. The energy is released in a quantum jump from a higher electron orbit-level to a lower one in the source atom, accompanied immediately (in accordance with the law of angular momentum conservation) by the reverse process in the recipient atom. These electron levels are quantised in integer multiples of the angular momentum quantum $h/2\pi$, and the frequency of the energy transferred is measured in fractions of a constant $cR$ as described in the Balmer formula for the spectral frequencies of the illumination ($cR$ is known as the Rydberg frequency constant, or upper limit of the Balmer series).

The gaps between these orbit levels are true voids in the sense that there are no physical parameters to describe anything existing or happening between them. They are, in the terms we have been using, 'null-and-voids'.

So, what about the pixels themselves? These are the true quanta, of magnitude $h$ and frequency $f = e/h$, where $e$ is the energy transferred between the two interacting atoms.

Next, there are the masses involved in those energy transitions, namely, what are customarily called the 'electron' and 'proton' (that is, in the basic hydrogen atom, for simplicity). These are not the solid and permanent entities we normally associate with the description of 'particles'. They are simply the dimensions without which they would not be bits of angular momentum but bits of something else which is indescribable. In other words, at this quantum level, these masses are not real masses but pure formalities. Insofar as these 'particles' are discrete and irreducible, they have no 'insides' that it is sensible to talk about or even consider. Those 'insides', therefore, are no more than 'voids', but not in the same sense as the voids that are gaps between quantum levels previously described.

These different 'voids' have dimensions, i.e., the cross-sectional radii and the duration, or statistical 'half-lives' that they manifest in Particle Physics experiments. Otherwise, they are more suitably described as hollows, more analogous to bubbles in a liquid than solid 'particles', with the 'liquid' being the World around them, the World we understand as real in the Normal Realist sense. And, with that World around them, these 'hollows' have no potency or dynamic in themselves. Unlike solid and determinate particles, all their potency and energy comes from their surroundings, just like the eyes of hurricanes and the centre of whirlpools.

And what are these 'surroundings'? They are, of course, the ordinary matter, space and time of the World as we know it. But now there is a problem of semantics. In the language we are constrained to use this ordinary space is also called a 'void'. Thus we have, altogether, one word to describe four distinguishable things:

i) the 'void' that is the discontinuity between discrete quanta;
ii) the 'void' that is the gap between the electronic orbit levels in the atom;
iii) the 'void' that is the 'inside' of a mass 'particle', or any other measure that is fundamentally discrete and irreducible;
iv) the 'void', so called, that is just ordinary space, or vacuum.

Which of these 'voids', then, is the true void, or nothing that the Eleatics were talking about? Well, we can rule out iv), that is, ordinary space, because that is part of the substance, or the Eleatic what is, of the normal real world.

So, what about 'voids' i), ii) and iii)? Well, i) and ii) are virtually the same, because the gap between the discrete quanta and the gap between the electronic orbit levels is the same gap, there being no case of having the one without the other. That is to say, the gap that separates the orbit levels in the atom is the same gap that separates the pixels.

So, what about iii)? These 'voids' have dimensions, i.e., cross-sectional radius and duration. As identifiable hollows, therefore, in the Eleatic what is, albeit that they are more analogous to bubbles in a liquid than 'particles'.

So, with iii) and iv) combined, the remaining choice is between the combined i) & ii) and the combined iii) & iv). That is to say, the true opposite of the true Eleatic substance in the combined iii) & iv) – that is to say, the true Eleatic void – is the nothing, or what is not in the combined i) & ii).

In other words, the true void that Democritus inserted into the Eleatic One in order to avert the consequence of stasis is the void that separates the discrete quantum pixels from one another – that breaks up what would otherwise be an Eleatic continuum, of both observational information and physical substance which, in relativistic quantum physics are the same.
