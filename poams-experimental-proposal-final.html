1. Introduction

POAMS is the acronym for the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis. Its principals are N. Vivian (Viv) Pope and Anthony D. Osborne. The Synthesis stems from the natural philosophy of the celebrated nineteenth-century sciencephilosopher, Ernst Mach (1838–1916).

It was Mach’s philosophy which provided the inspiration for Einstein’s Theory of Relativity. It is also basic to POAMS in its up-dated, Neo-Machian form of Normal Realism, which is a kind of radical empiricism, or observationism in which light is not something travelling in space with a ‘constant velocity c’, but in which c is no more than a dimensional constant of observational projection.

This modified view of light has all the physical consequences of Einsteinian relativity, except for Einstein’s Second Axiom postulating the ‘constant velocity of light in space’, which more than just a few physicists now regard as redundant [1]. As its description implies, Normal Realism is anti-theory.

That is to say, its scientific aim is, by the use of commonsense logic, to pare-down all speculative theories to their barest minimum so as to reveal to us nature in its true nakedness. It eschews the tendency which is all too common in physics, nowadays, which is to over-intellectualise physics and cosmology by creating and cherishing theories for their own sake, rather than just concentrating on the bare facts.

Nonsensical theories, like the alleged ‘Big Bang’, ‘dark matter’, ‘black holes’ and the like, especially when fed by the popular press, take on a life of their own as dogmas which become impervious to logical criticism. By contrast, the minimalist approach to physical science which is Normal Realism, is what Mach’s followers called Positivism – or, in some of its subsequent offshoots, Logical Positivism.

Normal Realism takes its own departure from these science-philosophies insofar as its basic ‘sense-data’ are not mental sensations, as in Mach’s philosophy, but the quanta h as manifest in objective spectroscopy, where h is the ultimately irreducible quantum of energy known in physics as Planck’s constant or quantum of action.

No doubt the most well-known – perhaps the only known– spin-off from Mach’s philosophy that is acknowledged by modern physicists is Mach’s Principle. This, in effect, is the hypothesis that every bit of matter (mass) in the universe moves in direct correlation with each and every other.

In that case, some might say, what is the use of ‘gravity’? POAMS proposes that Newtonian ‘gravity’ as an in vacuo force keeping bodies in orbit can be disposed of in favour of an overall nexus of angular momentum in which every body is automatically paired and balanced with each and every other.

However, that is a separate issue which is dealt with elsewhere [2]. In this present paper we lay aside all philosophical issues and concentrate on the purely practical implications of our Angular Momentum Synthesis. The problem with Newton’s seventeenth century description of orbital motion is that it took no significant account of the spin angular momentum of orbiting bodies.

According to Newtonian theory, orbiting bodies are kept in orbit by a hypothetical in vacuo ‘gravitational’ attraction which opposes their assumed tendency to fly off in straight lines as per his so-called Second Law of Motion. Now, in this twenty-first century, POAMS suggests a long overdue updating of that Newtonian theory.

This is in accordance with the law of angular momentum conservation, to include any spin angular momentum an orbiting body might possess in its overall total of angular momentum. This opposes Newton’s hypothesis of an in vacuo ‘gravitational force’ acting between bodies on account purely of their mass, which Newton thought to be the same for all masses regardless of whether or not they spin, swing, vibrate or whatever.

Our proposed experiment, therefore will be to confirm the POAMS prediction of small changes in the value of the so-called ‘gravitational constant’, G for fast spinning bodies, hence of changes in the weight and acceleration of falling bodies. In POAMS, bodies weigh what they do, not because of any fictitious ‘force of attraction’ between them and the earth but simply because with insufficient angular momentum to orbit above the earth’s surface, they seek to orbit the earth’s centre way down below its surface, whence, since that surface prevents them from doing so, they press against it with the reactionary force we call weight.

POAMS predicts, therefore, small changes, not only in the orbital parameters of free-moving bodies, such as NASA’s space-probes, due to spin, but also miniscule changes in the weight of spinning bodies such as gyroscopes on the earth’s surface. Many researchers have, of course, made similar predictions but for all sorts of different and unconnected reasons.

For example, Hideo Hayasaka and his team at Tokai University, Japan, have carried out weight-change experiments with extremely fast-spinning gyroscopes, and Prof. Mordecai Milgrom, of the Department of Condensed Matter Physics, the Weizmann Institute, Israel, has

suggested changes in G as an explanation of the so-called ‘Missing Mass’ anomaly (that there seems to be much more matter in the observed universe than can be accounted for by Newtonian ‘gravitation’). Unfortunately for these independent explanations, apart from quite large differences in their findings, they have no common philosophical basis, hence are no more than speculative and ad hoc. The Milgrom explanation, for instance, offers no experimental evidence for its hypothesis, and the results of the Tokai experiment are altogether equivocal. All attempts, to replicate them in order to confirm the reported results have signally failed. In contrast to these ad hoc theories, the predictions of POAMS for spinning bodies, as well as being presented on a well-worked out science-philosophy basis, are sufficiently unambiguous to make them refutable according to Popper’s Falsifiable Criterion for proper scientific theories. That is to say, if its predictions are confirmed, then the thesis survives. If not, then the theory fails. POAMS accepts that there is no middle way to be contemplated.

2. The Quantitative Predictions

What, then, are the changes in G that POAMS predicts for spinning objects? Our answer is as follows. First, let it be clear what we understand by angular momentum. Angular momentum (symbol, L) is simply straight-line momentum mv on a radius r from the centre of balance (barycentre) between a body of mass m and another body of mass M. For the sake of simplicity, let us assume that the mass M is so large as to make its centre of mass practically indistinguishable from the barycentre between it and the counter mass m. This provides us with the simple formula for angular momentum: $L = mvr$

where m is the mass of the orbiting body, v is its tangential velocity and r its orbit radius, or distance from a theoretically infinite central mass such as, say the earth or the sun. This orbit is assumed circular for simplicity. Now as well as being an amount (i.e., quantity or magnitude) of motion, angular momentum is also a directional, or vectorial quantity. This lends itself to the following depiction. Imagine a wheel of, say, one kilogram mass standing still on the earth’s equator. The earth spins at a surface speed of 465.1 metres per second, and the wheel is situated at a distance of $6.371 \times 10^6$ metres from the earth’s centre. In this case, the diurnal angular momentum of the wheel, in its standing or non-rolling state, will be $L = mvr$ = 1 kg × 465.1 m/s × $6.371 \times 10^6$ m º 2,900,000,000 kg m s⁻¹.

Now imagine that wheel rolling along the equator in a direction due West. Since the earth spins in the opposite – that is, the easterly – direction, the spin angular momentum of the wheel is subtracted from that of the earth. On the other hand, if the wheel rolls along the equator in a direction due East with the rotating earth then its spin angular momentum is added to that of the earth.

Let us now take the case of the wheel rolling in the direction due north, that is, at 90 degrees inclined to the direction of the earth’s spin. In that northerly direction, there will, obviously, be no addition or subtraction of the wheel’s spin angular momentum from that of the earth, and the same goes for the wheel rolling in the direction south.

In other inclinations the amounts add or subtract according to the angles of direction (vectors) of the motion. This is from a maximum addition in the case of the wheel rolling in the same direction as the earth (east), to a minimum in the case of the wheel rolling at right angles to the earth’s spin, i.e., north or south, and then to a maximum subtraction at 180 degrees inclination in the direction due west, opposite to that of the earth’s spin.

Between these full 360 degrees of directional variation, the precise amounts of addition and subtraction of the vector magnitudes are calculable according to the well-known principle of the Composition of Vectors. There is nothing necessarily complex or ‘mathematical’ about any of this.

It is – or, at any rate, should be – completely intuitive. Intuitive, also, is that angular momentum is a conserved quantity. This follows from the fact that a rate (such as a velocity) times a time is always an amount. Now according to the law of conservation of angular momentum, not only is the amount, or magnitude of the angular momentum of a body conserved but so, also, is its direction.

This means that whatever might be the direction of spin – of a gyroscope, say – at the start of its motion-trajectory, barring any interference from outside forces from then on, that direction will be conserved throughout its motion thereafter. So, not only is the magnitude of the angular momentum conserved but so, also, its direction, as the gyroscope typically reveals.

Intuitively evident, also, is that even where opposite spins of a pair of bodies – such as, say, the wheel and the earth – may sum to zero, the bodies obviously keep spinning, albeit in opposite directions, so the magnitude of the overall angular momentum remains the combined angular momentum of both the wheel and the earth together, regardless of the orientations of their vectors. (This has been confirmed in many previous POAMS experiments.) From this it naturally follows that the more there is of the amount of spin angular momentum, the less there has to be of the amount of

This means that from our POAMS point of view, what some have interpreted from these experimental results, as ‘levitation’, or ‘antigravity’, can be no more than the shift between different levels of weight-increase with spin. For the same overall angular momentum, the faster the body spins, the smaller is the amount of angular momentum left for its orbit whence, as we have seen, the greater the amount of spin the closer the body seeks to orbit, if allowed, towards the earth’s centre.

The maximum possible increase in a body’s weight, due to spin, therefore, would be when the spin component of its total angular momentum is so large that its orbital component is reduced to zero. How might this be achieved? The answer is provided by the following analogy.

Suppose that some intelligent submarine creature, living on some middle-level stratum of the ocean, unaware of the limits that apply to ocean height and depth, were to set the datum zero of water-pressure at the intermediate level to which it was habituated, between the surface of the ocean and, say, the abyssal Marianas Trench.

From this that scientifically-minded creature might assume, from his experience, that the pressure of water was inversely proportional to the height above that arbitrarily chosen reference-point, hence that any change in that pressure had to be in height, or ‘levitation’ above that particular stratum.

The same applies to the setting of the datum zero of weight-change at ordinary ground-level where, due to the earth’s diurnal rotation, its angular momentum with respect to the earth’s centre is non-zero. If it were zero, then it would be at rest relative to the earth’s centre, sharing the earth’s orbital angular momentum with respect to the sun and the other planets.

This would be its ‘Marianas Trench’ limit of weight increase above which weight can only decrease. The limit of that decrease in weight is where the spin proportion of the body’s overall angular momentum is at zero and the orbital proportion alternately at maximum – that is to say, where the factor G is at its standard textbook value for nonspinning bodies.

For it to be less, the orbital proportion of the angular momentum would have to be so large as to make the spin angular momentum less than zero, i.e., negative, which is impossible to contemplate in any commonsense-logical way [5].) This limit, by analogy, is the ‘surface’ of the ocean where the water-pressure reduces to zero and beyond which the pressure would be replaced by suction, which is pure nonsense to speculate upon.

This reasoning is supported by the fact that in none of these experiments is a gyroscope actually observed to levitate. De facto, It suffers only changes in weight from one level of weight to another. Only if a spinning object were to lift off from the earth into an orbit above the earth’s surface and orbit in the manner of a satellite could an example of true ‘levitation’ or ‘antigravity’ be validly claimed.

Also, needless to say, interpreting those changes in weight, etc. as due to ‘field-forces’ (gravitational, electrical ‘chronal’ or whatever), or to ‘torsion radiation-fields’, ‘vacuum force’ etc. can be no part of the Angular Momentum Synthesis known as POAMS.

From this standard-physics point of view, therefore, it might seem that the satellite is ‘levitated’ away from its expected orbit, which makes it interesting that in so many of these spin experiments (see following) the results have been reported as the discovery of ‘levitation’ or ‘antigravity’. Our thesis therefore, allows that there is, to some limited extent, what might appear as ‘levitation’. This impression of ‘levitation’, or ‘antigravity’ is enhanced by the fact that the larger the proportion of spin to orbital angular momentum, the greater the limited scope for this ‘levitation’, which feeds the fanciful precept of anti-gravitational space-drives as the propulsion power of ‘UFOs’. In our paper, “An Angular Momentum Synthesis of ‘Gravitational’ and ‘Electrostatic’ Forces”, GED, Osborne and Pope (2003), section 4.1, we assumed a mass of 175 grams.

The only instance of negative G is where the centripetal force on a body against the earth’s surface is negative, that is, when that centripetal force becomes centrifugal, as against the inner surface of a centrifuge. However, in free orbital motion there is no such ‘centrifuge’ that may be sensibly assumed.
