Notice of a New Radical Theory

of The Nature of Spatial Separation

This theory offers a philosophical contribution to modern theoretical science in the form of a different way of thinking about space. Essentially, it denies the classical ideas of both 'void' and 'plenum'. Its suggestion, in outline, is as follows.

There is no 'space', or 'absolute vacuum', as classically conceived, with 'light' and other influences travelling in it to make the connections between otherwise isolated objects. It is all time. And time is relative -- relative, that is to say, to the observer. In other words, all the information out of which the world is constructed appears in a time-sequence at the point of observation, in the way the information in a record-groove arrives at the point of the needle or in a magnetic tape at the pick-up head of a tape recorder.

However, whereas the record-groove or tape time-sequence has only one dimension, the observer-sequence has four. Also, whereas record-grooves and tapes are continuous, the observational sequence is not. The information which comes before the observer, and in the processing of which he projects the world, is quantised in all four dimensions, in action-units of Planck's quantum b.

The four time-dimensions are those of ordinary observation. They are 1), observational distance, or range; 2), the angular up-down dimension called elevation; 3) the side-to-side angle called azimuth and 4), the duration of that whole three-dimensional time-system in the living-time of the observer as measured by some standard (local and relatively stationary) chronometer.

Conventionally, distance is measured in metres. In the new theory, these distance-measures are divided by an empirical conversion-constant, c (unnecessarily and misleadingly called 'the speed of light'), which converts them all uniformly into seconds. So the whole projected time-sphere evolves, relatively to the central observer, with no more question of mechanical conveyance 'through a void' than with a sequence of heartbeats or birthdays.

The 'observer', in this context, is not necessarily a human percipient. It can be any transactor of quantum information in any conventional category, animal, vegetable or mineral. In this way, the theory avoids the subjective idealism of earlier observational philosophy. Since the 'information' it deals with is the sort described by information-theorists like Shannon, it also avoids any taint of linguistic conventionalism.

Objects are distanced from one another not by a 'void' but by reciprocal relativistic projection in terms of the information transacted. So every object is what it is, not absolutely and isolatedly, but relatively to everything else -- including, of course, itself. The system of dimensions which is basically polar, or observer-centred, transforms, therefore, into a relatively interconnected, or holistic system of Cartesian or observer de-centralised dimensions, to provide the ordinary space of draughtsmen and geometers -- except, of course, that this space is now not continuous but quantised and is measured in seconds rather than in metres.

Insofar as any of these objects moves relatively to any other (with a uniform motion, let us say) the distance it travels (s/c) in one or any combination of the first three time-dimensions, is a vector-component which, when squared and added to the square of the duration-component t, (measured by the body X) and then square-rooted, gives (by Pythagoras) the observational 'hypotenuse-time', or vector-resultant t of the motion, which answers precisely to time-dilation in orthodox relativity (when s is rendered as vt).

The consequences of this time-dilation, for relativistic mass and energy are therefore the same as in orthodox relativity. There is, however, no question of any 'inertial space' of classical conception, with momentum in straight lines as in Special Relativity, nor of any non-Buclidean version of this as in General Relativity. In the new approach, all momentum is angular momentum, (empirically GmM/v) an observational quantity which is ultimately quantised in units of h/2π, and the only 'straight lines' are orbits of theoretically infinite angular momentum and theoretically infinite radius.

There is thus no need of 'forces' or other influences operating in a vacuum to explain the observed orbital motions of bodies on their respective moment-arms. The motion is naturally orbital, in the manner described by pre-Newtonian scientist-philosophers like Aristotle. Everything orbits everything else, holistically, with finite, conserved and self-adjusting angular momentum. If all that angular momentum were somehow taken away, the whole system would contract to a point -- not by 'gravitational collapse' but simply due to lack of sufficient angular momentum to give the bodies their distance.

In orbital motion, as conceived by Newton, the spin angular momentum of a body can have no effect on its orbital motion, which is a function of the combined masses of the orbiting and orbited bodies. In the new approach, the spin of a body is part and parcel of the whole angular momentum equation. The spins of the mass-components, with their various direction-orientations relative to one another, accounts for the different strengths and signs of the

various so-called 'forces' to which the orbits of bodies are conventionally attributed in astronomical, atomic and nuclear physics. In other words, these 'field-forces' are 'unified' by getting rid of them altogether.

In a holistically interconnected system of the sort described, everything automatically and immediately affects everything else by sympathetic quantum shifts in angular momentum (in the manner of Bell's theorem). The same immediacy is secured on relativistic grounds by the fact that in photonic interaction the 'speed of the photon' is c, at which its time-standard, according to relativity, is infinite. This means that intrinsically the photon travels any observationally marked-out distance in no time at all. It is an immediate and instantaneous quantum-jump, the observationally extrapolated distance and time of which are related in the constant ratio of units c. So what we conventionally call the 'speed of light c', applied to photons, is a redundant conception and the formulae of relativity are derived much more simply without it (i.e., by Pythagoras, see above).(1)

The energy by which bodies interact is thus some portion of their relativistic mass-energy with respect to each other and to the system at large. So the empirical formula (the Balmer-Rydberg formula) expressing the spectrum of that interaction becomes a quantum derivative of the relativistic time-formula t² = (s/c)² + t², (i.e., with vt substituted for s and solved for frequency = v²/h = 2E/h, where 2E is potential energy F with respect to the centre of the orbiting system). This, consistently, makes the electrodynamical explanation of 'wave generation and conduction' as redundant as 'light-velocity'.(2)

'Atoms', at this fundamental level, therefore, cease to be little isolated 'planetary mechanisms' joined by 'forces' acting at a distance in 'the void' between them. They become just observational quanta -- bits of pure information in the observational category of angular momentum -- and what links them is the observer, no more and no less. They interact, that is to say, in the contexts of observational systems, in the manner described. And when they are not interacting -- that is, when not actually configuring in the observational network -- they are not 'ticking-over' in some metaphysical way, behind the scenes. Since time is no more than a dimensional function of observation, then being unobserved they do not exist in the interim between one quantum-appearance and another -- or, at least, such durations as are allocated to them are measured by the numbers of other observational events, not by any internal time-process of their own.

What we conventionally call 'smashing atoms' is therefore no such thing. It is a kind of 'dimensional numerology', in which the particle-accelerator plays the part of an 'abacus'. At that level of analysis, physics ceases as such and merges with purely formal logic and mathematics. So it makes no sense to suppose that we are seeking 'hidden mechanisms' in the 'quantum interstices'. All we are doing is permutating the possible combinations of basic measuring-units and dimensions. As an entity in itself (as though that concept made any sense) the transition of an atomic particle from one quantum-state to another, is timeless -- which can mean an instant or an eternity depending on the observational context.

Although in essence very simple, this theory and its implications seem difficult to grasp due to subliminal interference caused by the body of socially established precepts surrounding the idea of 'the void', which is assumed to be an absolutely extended and self-sufficient 'constant' behind all our physical observations and theories. These precepts are extremely difficult to lay aside, even for the hypothetical purposes of assessing any logical alternative. Some authorities have more than hinted that for these reasons, such a radical solution to our present conceptual problems, if it were to be instituted, would be socially traumatic and that it is therefore evil even to suggest it. Since modern science and science-education, despite the acceptance of relativity theory, remain so committed to that classical idea of 'space', the unsettling effect on present standards of competence in these areas, they say, would be devastating.

Such a purely 'professional' ordering of priorities, however, is not for everyone -- least of all for those imbued with a true sense of scientific and philosophical curiosity. It is to these thinkers, therefore, that this theoretical alternative is offered -- should it be so fortunate as to pass the censors.

N. Vivian Pope

December, 1988
