From Lightspeed to the Vortex

A Condensed Restatement of the Pope–Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis, with the Recruitment Correction and its Single-Vortex Form
C. F. Milam, after N. V. Pope & A. D. Osborne · DRAFT 2026-07-18 · not for circulation

Abstract

Osborne and Pope’s Light-Speed, Gravitation and Quantum Instantaneity (2007) derives the whole verified content of Special and General Relativity, the hydrogen spectrum, and the dissolution of electrostatic “charge” from two moves only: (i) the constant c is a dimensional conversion factor between the units of observed distance and time, not a speed; and (ii) all free motion is a manifestation of angular momentum, which is holistically conserved. We give a condensed statement of that programme, then make one correction the book did not carry through — the treatment of an extended spinning body as a single angular-momentum accumulator — and re-express the whole scheme in the single-vortex ontology of Normal Realism, in which orbital angular momentum is the winding of a closed vortex and spin is its twist. The correction identifies precisely when and where the sign of a spin-dependent weight change survives or is lost (the swamping crossover, ρ = K_s/K₀), and shows that an extended body recruits a fraction of its ~10²²–10²⁴ internal accumulators into coherent alignment, so that its effective spin energy far exceeds the rigid-body value. We keep to the book wherever the book speaks; we mark every departure.

1. The two moves and the cascade [FORCED]

Move 1 — c is a conversion factor. (Ch. 2.) Observed distance and observed duration are both measured, ultimately, in units of time (the metre is a light-second divided by c). Writing the three spatial dimensions in seconds via c makes the world four-dimensional in a single unit, and a “speed” acquires a geometric length in seconds. Relativistic time-dilation is then nothing but Pythagoras’ theorem on that four-dimensional measure (Ch. 3): a clock in relative motion traces the hypotenuse; its proper time is the adjacent side, \[\tau = \left(1 - v^2/c^2\right)^{1/2} t. \tag{1}\] There is no ether, no light “travelling,” no photon in flight — light is the information by which distant events are registered, instantaneous in proper time and delayed only in observer time (the cone model, Ch. 3).

Move 2 — angular momentum is the sole agency. (Ch. 6.) No free body is ever observed to move in an unbounded straight line; all free motion is curved. Osborne and Pope take this at face value: natural motion is orbital, a manifestation of angular momentum, and angular momentum is holistically conserved. Newton’s in vacuo gravitational force is then as redundant as the ether. Requiring that some natural orbits close forces the radial law to inverse-square (Bertrand’s theorem), recovering the Newtonian orbit without a force in the void.

The cascade. From these two, in order: the Lorentz transformation and \(E=mc^2\) (Ch. 4, \(c^2\) a mass–energy conversion factor); the Balmer–Rydberg spectrum from the atom as a quantum accumulator with fundamental mass unit \(m^\ast = hR/c\) (Ch. 5); the Schwarzschild metric with no field equations — only time-dilation plus the postulate that natural circular orbits are geodesics (Ch. 8), yielding Mercury’s perihelion advance (43.03″/century) and starlight deflection (1.75″, as refraction through the corona, not a bent photon); and finally the refusal of black holes, the Big Bang, and dark matter as extrapolations of the metric beyond its warrant (Ch. 9), with the galactic “missing mass” reinterpreted as neglected spin angular momentum and a correspondingly variable G — the position independently reached by Milgrom’s MOND.

The rest of this paper concerns the hinge of Chapters 6–7: weight, and how spin changes it.

2. Weight as a constraint force; the variable G [FORCED]

In the synthesis a body at rest on the ground is not in its natural state. Its natural (force-free) state is an orbit at some radius \(r_{\rm ff}\) fixed by its angular momentum. The ground holds it off that orbit; the force required is what we measure as weight. Weight is therefore a real, measurable constraint force, not the pull of an in vacuo field (Ch. 6 §6.3). Add angular momentum and \(r_{\rm ff}\) moves; the constraint force — the weight — changes with it. Osborne and Pope make this explicit: an eastward-moving mass (adding to Earth-frame angular momentum) weighs less; Anthony calls the limit levitation.

For a body carrying both orbital and intrinsic (spin) angular momentum the relevant kinetic energy is the sum \[\mathcal{K} = K_0 + K_s, \tag{2}\] with \(K_0\) orbital and \(K_s\) spin. The equation of motion keeps its Newtonian form but with a variable coefficient \(\mathcal{G}\) in place of the constant \(G\) (Ch. 7 §7.3), \(\mathcal{G}\) growing with the spin content. The sign is purely dynamical and we adopt it as canonical [FORCED, O2]:

spin parallel to orbital \(L\) ⇒ \(\mathcal{K}=K_0+K_s\) ⇒ orbit contracts ⇒ more strongly held ⇒ heavier; spin anti-parallel ⇒ \(\mathcal{K}=K_0-K_s\) ⇒ lighter.

We retire the earlier “field-handedness / outward-thinning” language: it is only this same statement — whether the spin adds to or subtracts from the orbital sense — expressed less directly (see §5).

3. The swamping crossover: when the sign survives [FORCED]

The synthesis carries two effects of spin, not one. Beyond the directional \(\pm\) of §2 there is a sign-independent magnitude effect: spin raises \(\mathcal{K}\), hence \(\mathcal{G}\), whichever way it points. Osborne and Pope state this explicitly in their own later correction of Chapter 7 — “the magnitude effect of spin alone, which is the same regardless of the direction” — and note it is “so minuscule … compared to the magnitude of the orbital angular [momentum]” for macroscopic bodies, yet “extremely large” for the electron (Pope, A Critique of Modern Physics, PIRT 2008, notes 3–4; the note there is offered expressly “as a correction of that 2007 text”). What follows only names the boundary between those two regimes. Define the ratio \[\rho \equiv K_s / K_0. \tag{3}\]

  • Direction-sensitive regime, \(\rho \ll 1\) (Ch. 7 §7.5; the macroscopic spinning-body examples). Orbital energy dominates; spin is a signed perturbation: \(\mathcal{K}=K_0\pm K_s\) gives a small but reversible, direction-dependent weight change. To leading order the fractional shift in the force-free radius, hence in weight, scales as \[\frac{\Delta W}{W} \sim \pm\,\rho \qquad (\rho \ll 1). \tag{4}\]
  • Swamped regime, \(\rho \gg 1\) (Ch. 7 §7.6, footnote 24; the atom). Spin dominates; \(\mathcal{K}\approx K_s\) for either sign, so — in Anthony’s own words — “the direction of spin has no effect on the parameters of the orbit.” The effect becomes magnitude-only; the sign washes out. This is why atomic binding (“charge”) looks sign-symmetric in magnitude.

The crossover is the operative fact. A signed, reversible weight change exists only while \(\rho \lesssim 1\). As \(\rho \to 1\) the signed response saturates; for \(\rho \gg 1\) it is gone. Any programme that seeks to drive a weight change by pumping spin must therefore live in, and stay in, the band \(\rho \lesssim 1\). [OPEN] the full \(\Delta W(\rho)\) across the crossover follows from the \(r_{\rm ff}(L)\) relation of §6.3 combined with (2); the qualitative content — perturbative-signed below, saturating-then-symmetric above — is fixed.

4. The recruitment correction: extended bodies are not single accumulators [EXT, with reason]

That the magnitude effect exists is the authors’ own correction (§3). What the synthesis has not carried through is its size for a real, extended body, and this is our one substantive departure; we state the reason. In the worked treatment (Ch. 7 §§7.4–7.5, and the numerical proof in ospope-proof, which sizes the Hayasaka 175 g / 18 000 rpm disc) the spin energy is taken as the bulk rigid-body value, \[K_s^{\rm bulk} = \tfrac12 I \omega^2, \qquad I = \tfrac25 m r^2\ (\text{sphere}),\ \tfrac12 m r^2\ (\text{disc}), \tag{5}\] at the macroscopic angular speed \(\omega\). The body is treated as one accumulator. On that basis the predicted weight change is far smaller than Hayasaka reported — the ground on which the Bureau International des Poids et Mesures (Quinn & Picard) called the Hayasaka result spurious.

But a real ball, disc, or gyroscope has physical extent: \(\sim10^{22}\)–\(10^{24}\) constituent accumulators, each with intrinsic spin, ordinarily randomized so that their vector sum is nil and they contribute nothing to (5). Mechanical rotation does not leave them randomized. By the Barnett effect — the mechanical dual of Einstein–de Haas — rotation magnetizes the body, i.e. it coherently aligns a fraction \(f\) of the internal spins along \(\omega\). Each aligned internal accumulator carries spin energy on the atomic (“charge”) scale, \(K_s^{\rm atom}\sim hcR_\infty\) (Ch. 7 §7.6), which is enormous compared with the bulk rotational energy per atom. Hence \[K_s^{\rm eff} \;\approx\; \underbrace{\tfrac12 I\omega^2}_{\text{bulk}} \;+\; \underbrace{f\,N\,K_s^{\rm atom}}_{\text{recruited, dominant}}, \tag{6}\] with the second term controlling. Three consequences:

  1. Anthony undercounted the spin content, and with it the excursion of \(\mathcal{G}\), by many orders — the “far too small” prediction is an artifact of the single-accumulator assumption, not a statement about nature.
  2. Restored, the recruited internal spin recovers the larger observed effect: on this reading Hayasaka’s result is, in direction and rough magnitude, plausibly correct.
  3. The coefficient that has stood as an undetermined coupling in this programme is exactly the recruitment fraction \(f\) (times \(K_s^{\rm atom}/K_0\)) — a computable efficiency, not a mystery. [OPEN] compute \(f\) for a given material and drive.

The correction is tied to §3. Recruiting internal spin is precisely what raises \(\rho=K_s/K_0\) toward and past unity. So recruitment is the lever that carries a body across the swamping crossover. There is therefore an optimal recruited fraction \(f^\ast\) at which \(\rho\approx O(1)\): maximal signed, still-reversible effect. Recruit beyond \(f^\ast\) and the sign is lost to swamping. This — the interplay of (4) and (6) — is, we suggest, the general principle behind every spinning-mass weight anomaly, and it is stated here without reference to any particular apparatus.

5. The single-vortex form [EXT]

Lightspeed’s machinery is two-body throughout: a mass P orbiting a mass B, carrying orbital \(L\) and intrinsic \(S\). Normal Realism has since moved to a single-substance picture: the elementary object is one closed vortex of the time-substance, and there is no orbiting pair. We show the two-body results survive the move intact, and that the recruitment correction becomes native rather than added.

Two angular rates of one object. A single closed vortex has two independent angular coordinates: the azimuth \(\varphi\) around the loop, and the internal framing angle \(\psi\) of the vortex core. Advancing the phase around the loop by whole turns is the standing-wave (de Broglie) condition \[\oint d\varphi = 2\pi n \;\Rightarrow\; L_{\rm orb} = n\hbar, \tag{7}\] the winding — our orbital angular momentum. The rotation of the core’s framing as one traverses the loop is the twist — our spin. These are not two substances layered together (we do not reintroduce “inner/outer quanta”); they are two rates of the same filament, and both are literally rates of the one substance \(S\) against an angle, in keeping with the Normal-Realist identity \(L = dS/d\theta\): \[L_{\rm orb} = \frac{dS}{d\varphi}, \qquad L_{\rm spin} = \frac{dS}{d\psi}. \tag{8}\]

Conservation is topological. For a closed ribbon (a vortex with a framing) the Călugăreanu–White–Fuller theorem gives \[\mathrm{Lk} = \mathrm{Tw} + \mathrm{Wr}, \tag{9}\] with the linking number \(\mathrm{Lk}\) an integer that cannot change under any smooth deformation. Identifying total angular momentum with \(\mathrm{Lk}\), orbital with the writhe/winding and spin with the twist, holistic conservation of angular momentum becomes conservation of total linking — a genuine invariant, not a postulate bolted on.

What falls out for free. - Spin-½ is a half-twist. A ribbon carrying \(\mathrm{Tw}=\tfrac12\) must be traversed twice — 720° — before its framing returns: the spinor property, with no separate assumption. This half-twist is the same factor “2” that appears in the published fine-structure result \(\alpha = \sqrt{2/N}\) (the planar \(2\pi\) versus solid-angle \(4\pi\)). Spin-½ stops being posited and becomes the minimal non-zero twist of a single vortex. - Charge sign is twist handedness. A right- versus left-handed half-twist is the binary phase-sense that Lightspeed §7.6 already identifies with the sign of “charge.” No charge substance survives; only the handedness of a twist. - Anthony’s sign, recovered (O2). Twist co-oriented with the winding sense adds to the linking (heavier); anti-oriented subtracts (lighter): exactly \(\mathcal{K}=K_0\pm K_s\). What we formerly called “field-handedness” is just twist-versus-winding, and needs no independent derivation.

Recruitment and swamping in vortex language. Each atom is a micro-vortex with its own twist. Randomized, the twists point every way and the net twist is nil — precisely Anthony’s “no internal contribution,” now seen as the \(n{=}1\) rigid limit of a system that in fact has a whole tower of internal windings available. Alignment (Barnett) co-orients the twists so their linking numbers add, producing a macroscopic net twist — the recruitment of §4. Phase advance pumps the winding (\(\oint d\varphi\), i.e. adds writhe), the general form of driving angular momentum into the body. And swamping is spin-lock: when the aggregate twist dominates the winding, the sign of the total linking is set by the twist, and further winding (phase advance) can no longer flip the Earth-frame sense. The three phenomena of §§3–4 are one statement about \(\mathrm{Tw}\) versus \(\mathrm{Wr}\).

6. Scope

[FORCED] — Everything in §§1–2 and the two regimes of §3 is Lightspeed, re-expressed. We assume the book correct and have found no reason in it to do otherwise.

[EXT] — (i) The naming and use of the swamping ratio \(\rho\) as the control variable (§3); (ii) the recruitment correction (§4), which is the single substantive departure from the book, taken only because the book’s single-accumulator treatment of an extended body is demonstrably incomplete and its omission mispredicts a real experiment; (iii) the single-vortex re-expression (§5), which changes the representation, not the physics, and reproduces every two-body result.

[OPEN] — (i) the closed form \(\Delta W(\rho)\) across the crossover; (ii) the recruitment fraction \(f\) and the optimum \(f^\ast\) for a given material and drive; (iii) a first-principles value for the twist–winding coupling that fixes the coefficient in (4).
