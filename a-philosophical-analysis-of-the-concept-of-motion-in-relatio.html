A Philosophical Analysis of the Concept of Motion in Relation to Action-at-a-Distance

A Philosophical Analysis of the Concept of Motion in Relation to Action-at-a-Distance

by N. V. Pope

There are two logically distinguishable concepts of motion. One is that of a particle of matter changing its position continuously with respect to some other particle or body of reference, with a momentum defined by Newton's first law of motion. The other is that of a kinematical or "cinematographic" sequence of discrete events, manifest as a wave of some kind, like that caused by wind through corn or ripples on a lake where there is no uniform translation of the moving bits themselves but only of their transferred kinetic energy.

These two concepts of motion are categorically distinct. It is impossible, for instance, to think of the bits of a rope along which a wave travels as travelling with the wave, or the molecules in a water-wave as radiating with the wave along the surface of a pond. As everyone knows, the bits of rope move vertically up and down, and the water molecules describe little circles around fixed points. To conflate the two motions, the dynamic and the kinematic is, in any ordinary-language sense, what philosophers call a category-mistake,¹ an inevitable source of commonsense confusion.

Of course, not all such sequential or wave-like motion is transverse. In a line of touching snooker-balls, for instance, between the ball that is struck by the cue-ball and the one that flies off at the other end, the transfer of momentum is plainly longitudinal through the medium of the intervening, relatively stationary balls. This, of course, is the way sound travels in air, by compression-waves of this longitudinal sort. Nevertheless, the two motions, that of the balls and of the wave can in no sensible way be conceptually conflated.

Another, allied, category mistake is to speak of the kinetic energy of a particle as though, like the particle, it may travel unsupported in a vacuum. All energy – pure energy – is conveyed by contiguity in accordance with Newton's third law of equal and opposite, instantaneous and in-line action and reaction. This is so even in the case of at-a-distance influences such as light and gravitation, as Gilbert Lewis demonstrated in 1926.²

The basis of Lewis' demonstration is as follows. In Special Relativity, the time taken, relative to the measurer, R, of a particle P in travelling a distance s is given by the familiar equation $$t_{\mathrm{R}} = t_{\mathrm{p}} (1 - (v^2/c^2))^{-\frac{1}{2}} \tag{1}$$ where $t_{\mathrm{p}}$ is the time registered by the particle over that distance, $v$ is the velocity $s/t_{\mathrm{R}}$ and $c$ is the usual relativistic distance-time conversion-factor.³ What Lewis showed, in effect, is that if we think of the light-quantum as a travelling particle – the so-called "photon" – then the proper-time $t_{\mathrm{p}}$ of that quantum, travelling at speed $v = c$ is, from (1), $$t_{\mathrm{p}} = t_{\mathrm{R}} (1 - (c^2/c^2)) = 0 \tag{2}$$

In that case, the proper-distance $s_{\mathrm{p}} = ct_{\mathrm{p}}$ travelled by the quantum is also zero. What Lewis demonstrated, therefore, was the reductio ad absurdum of the conventional precept of the quantum of light as a space-travelling particle. In itself, that quantum interaction between source and sink is an intrinsically distanceless and timeless, irreducible quantum jump.

The same goes, of course, for quanta in general, because since the effect in question is one of basic distance and time, it is the same for radio, gravitation or whatever, as for light. All quantum contacts (Lewis called them quantum touchings) therefore take place precisely in accordance with Newton's third of law of equal and opposite instantaneous and in-line action and reaction, in the way classical and quantum physics requires.

Why, then, has "action-at-a-distance" become such a problem in modern physics? Why did the followers, respectively, of Einstein and Bohr remain so implacably opposed over the so-called "EPR paradox" – the apparent contradiction between the finite-speed and infinite-speed requirements of distant interaction – when light, according to Relativity, is demonstrably both? The answer, we propose, is the strange scholastic attachment to the aforementioned category mistake which currently rejoices in the name of "wave-particle duality". In other words, the "paradox" of finite-infinite speed results, we suggest, from failing to maintain – indeed, from the expedient blurring of, in the name of "complementarity" – the commonsense distinction between the dynamical and kinematical aspects of motion in the case of quantum interaction.

How is that category confusion to be corrected? The answer, surely, is by restoring the ordinary-language distinction. With that distinction correctly observed there can be no paradox in conceiving a wave as consisting of kinematical sequences of proper-time-instantaneous quantum-events, propagating at the finite speed c. There is no paradox because the one is dynamic, by Newton's third law, and the other is kinematic, consisting, like all wave-type phenomena, of observational sequences of those dynamic contacts.

Another consequence of this conceptual readjustment is that, obviously, at the level of quantum contiguity there can be no vacuum or void. That sort of "space", as ordinarily conceived, has to be a consequence of quantum interaction, not its primordial precondition. In other words, space as we observe it has to be an informational (i.e., relative) projection out of observational sequences of those distanceless and timeless quantum-events in the way a TV viewer projects the drama out of patterns and sequences of sporadically lighted screen pixels. To conflate those two categorically distinct aspects of physical interaction is to generate all the paradoxicality, or "spookiness", of the current plethora of experimental evidence of instantaneous, at-a-distance quantum interaction.

Needless to say, this conceptual readjustment, which puts space into light rather than light into space, makes space and time (space-time) relative to the observer in precisely the way that relativistic physics requires, with the constant c, relating the two, being a constant of observation in Bondi's sense of a conversion-factor, (vide Endnote 3) rather than as the notoriously confusing "speed" relative to a vacuum or æther.

Perhaps the best way to think of this is with respect to what we traditionally call "gravitation". In this case, all bodies have instantaneously balanced or reciprocal angular momentum interrelations, precisely as in Mach's interpretation of Newtonian physics.⁴ The only difference is that Newton's spatial continuum is replaced by a discretum consisting of statistical (information-statistical) numbers of quantum elements, each of which is an event common to both emitter and absorber (object and observer, in philosophical terms). This is what keeps things in regular trajectories relative to one another instead of shooting about all over the place like will-o'-the-wisps, because they (and, of course, we) are all interconnected by that statistical, yet overall-conserved proper-time-instantaneous interaction in all the various categories known to physics, from light to gravitation.

In observing something one has therefore to interact with it, by light, gravitation, inertia or whatever; and each of those interactions is a proper-time-instantaneous quantum "jolt" affecting the object and observer simultaneously.⁵ That simultaneity is, of course, absolute, being the same (i.e., invariant) in all systems of reference. That is to say, the fact that A is in proper-time-instantaneous contact with B and B is in proper-time-instantaneous quantum contact with C does not signify that A is in proper-time-instantaneous contact with C. This is because, unlike the quantum contacts themselves which are absolute, sequences of them are relative and differ from observer to observer in accordance with the relativistic formulae. This is why a pair of twins may move in definite trajectories with respect to one another, remaining in instantaneous and reciprocal (optical, "gravitational" and "inertial") quantum contact throughout, yet register different ages in returning together, as in standard relativity.

Quantum contiguity is, of course, not only instantaneous but also longitudinal, in accordance with Newton's third law. It therefore favours the longitudinal magnetic flux density [B(3)] field theory of Evans and Vigier, rather than the transverse-field theory of Faraday and Maxwell. The concept of "field" here is, of course, that of a quantum discretum, as opposed to the classical continuum; and since all quantum interactions are contiguous, this leaves no more room for a self-extended vacuum or void than did the original Eleatic conception of the unbroken One or Plenum. The essential difference is that whereas Leucippus and Democritus sought to break that monistic stasis (for how can there be motion where there is no room for it) by conceptually exploding the Eleatic One into material atoms separated by void, we retain the logical embargo on the "vacuum" and break the same stasis by exploding the "One" into atomic events, or quanta. Needless to say, this runs counter to the space-time continuum conception of Einstein and Minkowski, thereby merging together quantum theory and relativity in a way which Einsteinian continuism makes impossible.

Something else which disappears on this analysis is the determinism which is implicit in the Einsteinian conception of a continuous space-time substratum. Thus we clear the way for a digitalised, hence non-determinist, information-theoretical approach to physics in place of the classical analog approach. This information is transacted not only between human observers in human language but also between observers and their instruments, and between instruments and instruments, in a language that makes no radical distinction between communicating and physically interacting. Since anything and everything is, in that sense, an instrument, this gets rid of the bogey of "Positivism" as physicists currently conceive it.⁶ By the same token, it also gets rid of the opposite and no less circumscribed form of so-called "Realism" that physicists have conceived in reaction to "Positivism". So instead of carrying its Positivist head tucked underneath its Realist arm like some parody of the ghost of Anne Boleyn, theoretical physics may become normalised around the ordinary-language meaning of realism restored to philosophy by the commonsense philosopher G.E. Moore.⁷ Called Normal Realism, this philosophy abandons that traditional hard-and-fast distinction between being and perceiving, which is called dualism,⁸ in favour of an objective, neutralistic relativism of the sort that is implicit in both Relativity and Quantum theory. In other words, in Normal Realism physical reality and our observations of it are no longer coextensive, as in Positivism, nor exclusive as in reactionary Realism. They are classifications of experience which overlap, so that of the things that exist, some are perceived and some are not, as in ordinary commonsense.

The physical implications and predictions of this philosophy of Normal Realism are those currently being explored, in effect, by physicists such as those of the B(3) group and others who have produced experimental testimony to instantaneous action-at-a-distance – or Lewisian quantum touching, which is logically the same. In this way, our experimental physics, philosophically debugged of every last vestige of bogus dualism, may once more become a natural philosophy rather than remain, as at present, an incursion into nature with no more philosophical coordination or understanding than that of woodworm in a beam.
