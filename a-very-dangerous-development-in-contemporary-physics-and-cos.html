A Very Dangerous Development in Contemporary Physics and Cosmology

Viv Pope — c. 2011

Are we on the cusp of a revolution in contemporary Physics and Cosmology? A most disconcerting fact that has only recently been discovered is that there is no such thing as what is commonly called the ‘speed of light’ in space What is called the ‘constant velocity of light in vacuo’ in the Second Axiom of Einstein’s Special Theory of Relativity is a seriously over-enlarged interpretation of the actual observational data that has stunted the growth of physical science for more than three centuries. The basic, unadorned fact, as Olaus Römer was the first to discover in 1676, is that observational distances in space are also times in a constant ratio of units, $c$. This was later confirmed experimentally by the astronomer James Bradley and in areas of experimental physics by the likes of Armand Fizeau and A.A. Michelson. The facile interpretation of this ratio is that it is ‘the velocity of light in a vacuum’, as stated by Einstein. As a point of pure logic, however, from the fact that all velocities are measures of distance-divided-by-time it does not follow that all distances-divided-by-time (like c) are velocities – which is like saying that because all dogs are quadrupeds, all quadrupeds are dogs.

Moreover, an essential characteristic of what is properly called a velocity is that it is the motion of an identifiable material object or particle. A velocity of nothing (no thing) makes no sense. Also, a velocity has to conform, both in magnitude and direction, to the law of the composition of velocities. Neither of these criteria is satisfied by $c$, which is the same (i.e., constant) for all observers regardless of how they move relatively to one another. Since no true velocity can do that, then, logically, the constant $c$ cannot truly be a velocity.

Besides, a particle, truly so called, has mass. A massless particle is impossible to conceive, far less to have a velocity. So the assumed particle of light which Einstein called the ‘photon’ must have some mass, no matter how miniscule that mass may be. Plainly, then, any idea of the ‘photon’ being a physical particle becomes untenable, not least because according to Relativity, anything properly called a particle, no matter how small we may assume its mass to be, becomes infinitely massive at the ‘speed c’, so the ‘photon’, allegedly travelling at that ‘speed c’ has to be infinitely massive, which is, of course, nonsense. Anyway, as for c being a ‘velocity in vacuo’, how can that ‘velocity c’ be measured in a vacuum? The plain fact is that c is the same as measured by each and every observer. But measured relative to a vacuum? No way! With respect to what, then, can that ‘velocity’ be measured when the light is (allegedly) travelling all alone in the void between its source and sink? And if, instead of thinking of light in that way, we think of it as waves, (after Huygens, et al.), then what can possibly wave in a vacuum? Nor is there any ‘ether’ to conduct waves, since ‘ether’ is one of the growing number of things that theorists have postulated but have never detected. All sorts of ways of answering these awkward questions are contrived. For instance, one excuse has been that in its own rest-frame the mass of the ‘photon’ is zero. However, it is axiomatic in relativity that the ‘photon’ is never at rest but always travels at the ‘speed c’. So there can be no ‘rest-frame’ for the photon, which means that this excuse is invalid.

But if c is not a velocity, then what is it? By default, it is a dimensional constant like the ratio 39.37 inches to the metre or the ratio $c^2$ between energy in joules and mass in kilograms. So why do we persist in thinking of light as ‘travelling’, when all true evidences are to the contrary? This is where the ‘danger’ comes in. The philosophical consequences of relinquishing the ‘velocity in space’ interpretation of light are profound and have been explored[1], but this exploration reveals that the implications of this radical change in thinking would be catastrophic for academic administrators and the authors of Physics textbooks. This is because it entails a truly Copernican ‘flipover’ from thinking of light as travelling in space to the opposite logical alternative of thinking of all space and time as being in the light – not light as it is usually thought of as travelling in space but light as it is actually observed, optically or instrumentally, in its full spectral range from deepest infrared to far ultraviolet and gamma.

In relativistic proper time – that is, the time of the interaction itself – the interacting atoms are in direct quantum contact. This means that on the quantuminformational level there is no such thing as distance, a quantum being an irreducible amount of energy transacted in zero proper time. This means that at that quantum-informational level the transactions take place in quantum jumps of pure proper-time-instantaneous quantum action-at-a-distance. What we perceive as ‘distance’ on the ordinary physical level is an observational projection by the observer out of patterns and sequences of informationstatistical numbers of these quantum events, in a manner similar to the way in which distance is projected by the viewer of a video scenario from patterns and sequences of screen pixels.

On the other hand, what Römer, Michelson et al, have measured as the observational time-delay of that interaction is no more than one solution of the relativistic time equation as applied to light on the ordinary observational/experimental level. Like the opposite side of the same coin, the other solution of that same time-equation produces the proper-time of that same interaction at the quantum level, which is zero [1].

This solves at a stroke the chronic dispute between Einstein and Bohr over whether quantum interaction is limited to the ‘speed c, as Einstein taught or whether it is instantaneous as Bohr insisted and which John Bell, Alain Aspect, et al. have proved. Needless to say, this automatically removes, in the simplest possible way, the notorious ongoing conflict between Relativity and Quantum theory.

Also proved is that all the practical consequences of relativity theory can be deduced in an extremely simple way without involving the mind-bending historical rigmarole connected with the customary interpretation of $c$ as a ‘velocity’. In the relevant equations it makes no difference whether c is interpreted as the ‘velocity of light’ or as no more than a dimensional constant – what Herman Bondi has described as a distance-time ‘conversion factor’. This was as far back as 1954, when this present writer, independently, made that same discovery[1]. Judging by an article in the New Scientist[2] it now seems that more and more scientists are coming to that same conclusion, that Einstein’s Second Axiom of Special Relativity stating the constancy of the ‘velocity of light in the vacuum’ is redundant, and that relativity theory is far simpler and more efficient without it.

Much is heard nowadays about the need for a ‘New Physics’. The arguments become more and more strident in view of the continued failure of some of our most expensive laboratories and bits of equipment to detect what they were supposed to detect. NASA’s representative, John Anderson, warns that in the event of having to consider the possibility of a New Physics: We shouldn’t miss these things just because we might have the wrong mindset.’

Fine words! However, our contemporary Physics and Cosmology are now so far gone on their current course of theoretical extremism that it would take little short of a miracle to correct them. What publisher or broadcaster would touch it?
