The Cosmological Cockup Viv Pope verbatim: The Cosmological cockup. What sort of cockup are we talking about, here? The answer is: a LOGICAL cockup. Suppose an expert entomologist were to argue: ‘All ants are insects, therefore all insects are ants.’ Now, surely, anyone can see, by pure commonsense logic, that this argument is false. But now suppose that when you try to point this out, someone says: ‘Well, he’s the expert, here, so he must be right and you must be wrong’. Do you say: ‘Well, that’s true; he is the expert, so he must be right. Or do you say ‘I don’t care who the hell this guy is, even if he appears on ‘Horizon’ or ‘Panorama’ or wherever, as the acclaimed authority in these matters, there is just no way in which that argument can be true,’? Part 1: Now this situation, you’ll say, is highly unlikely. Well, of course it is! But that’s because the fallacy in that argument is so obvious. Well, okay, so now suppose that some other expert presents on television, or say in the New Scientist or the Scientific American, an argument to the effect that ‘All motions of recession on the part of light-sources exhibit a spectral redshift, therefore wherever you observe a spectral redshift you are observing a motion of recession’. Again, do you say ‘Well, he’s the expert, here, so what he says must be true’. Or do you say, as you would to the entomologist, ‘Sorry, sunshine, but that argument is false’. This is on precisely the same point of logic as in the entomology example, namely, that ‘P implies Q’ does not imply ‘Q implies P’. We shall now see how this template applies to some other ‘scientific’ arguments. In preparation, let’s look at another simple example of this fallacy. Consider the argument: ‘If it rains, then we will get wet, so if we get wet, then it must be raining’. Now you don’t need to be a logician to see that this is false, because quite obviously, we may get wet by some other means. We might have been squirted with a hosepipe, gone swimming or whatever without there being any rain at all. But, of course, it may well turn out that our getting wet is actually due to the fact that it rains, so that the statement ‘If it rains we will get wet’ just happens to be true in this particular instance. But the point to note, here, is that the argument as such is not necessarily true (indeed, logically, it is strictly not true at all) hence to assume, that it is, is a fallacy – in the vernacular, a logical ‘cockup’.

-2Now, suppose some expert astronomer such as Edwin Hubble or Vesto Slipher reports that in their observations of distant galaxies, the spectra of those galaxies are redshifted in proportion to their distance – the well-known Hubble Constant. You have to believe it, since these are the accredited experts in their field with the very best means of observing at their disposal. But now suppose another expert tells you that because these galaxies exhibit redshifts in proportion to their distance, they must be moving away from us at speeds of about 20 km/sec per million light-years, which means that the universe is expanding. And this, the expert says, means that some fourteen billion years ago all that matter, all that space and time started out from the same spot in a single microscopic ‘primeval atom’ which exploded in what he says is a universal ‘Big Bang’. Do you say, as so many people do nowadays: ‘How absolutely amazing!’, in the simple-minded belief that what this expert says must be true? Or do you point out to him the logical flaw in his argument, that the fact that those galaxies exhibit a spectral redshift does not necessarily imply that they are receding from us (that ‘P implies Q’ does not imply ‘Q implies P’)? Now the trouble is that this guy, who has plainly not spotted that logical flaw in his argument, may have invested much of his life’s time and energy in an implicit belief that the universal expansion argument is true. Indeed, he may have staked his academic reputation on it, producing theories as to how that expansion occurred, how it is sustained. He may even have lectured and published great tomes on how it all started, how it will end and so on, having trampled over all the chalk lines between physics, metaphysics and commonsense. So now you are going to have the devil of a job challenging this guy’s ideas of Cosmical Creation, the Big Bang and so on. This is especially so if, say, religious bodies like the Church have acknowledged his researches as proof of the Biblical story of the Creation, even sponsoring him with funds and so on, as has actually happened in some cases. To change his ideas now would be too embarrassing, not only for him but also for his sponsors and followers to contemplate. So the question of the truth or otherwise of his basic assumption is no longer an issue. It is now not so much a logical matter as a psychological – even a sociological –matter. And ‘Good logic’, as it is well said, ‘is bad psychology’. But, of course, ‘it was ever thus’! People like Copernicus and Galileo went in fear of their lives for challenging the expertise of the Ptolemaic scholars. However, in the end, ‘Truth will out’. And the truth of the matter, in the case of the Big Bang theory, is that the argument on which it is based – namely that seeing those galactic redshifts is the same as seeing those galaxies receding – is, demonstrably, a fallacy. Well, alright, you may say, fallacious or not, the Big Bang theory is backed up by observational evidence, such as the observed anisotropy of the 3°K background radiation and other ancillary observations.[1] But this is just another example of the same logical, P-impliesQ-therefore-Q-implies-P fallacy. For no matter how well authenticated might be the evidence Q for a theory P, that evidence does not necessarily prove that particular theory. This is because those same evidences, Q, may be implied, or predicted, by some other, entirely different theory. This is recognised in ordinary forensic law. For instance, the fact that all the evidences that the accused was in that place at the time of the offence may not be taken as absolute proof of the crime, but may well be thrown out of court on the grounds that ‘the evidence is purely circumstantial’. Of what, then, does positive proof consist? It consists of excluding all possible explanations of those observational evidences Q, other than that provided by the theory P in question. In other words, a theory may be taken as true on the evidence provided, if and only if it is absolutely certain that no alternative theory makes those same predictions. If it is not absolutely certain, then all the ‘evidences’ for that theory P are purely circumstantial. Circumstantial, in that same way, are all the ancillary evidences adduced for the Big Bang theory and the galactic recession hypothesis on which it is based. The only way in which it can be taken as unquestionably true is when all other possible explanations of the galactic redshift and the ‘evidences’ for it have been excluded. To take it as true, either in ignorance of, or in disregard of, the possibility of explanations of the redshift based on premises other than that of galactic recession makes the Big Bang theory a dogma, by definition. (As the dictionary defines it, a dogma is ‘a point of view or tenet put forth as authoritative without adequate grounds’.) Crucial, then, to the issue of whether the Big Bang theory is true or just a dogma is the question of whether or not there is some viable explanation of the redshift other than that of galactic recession. If there is, and if the recession theorists, for whatever reason, fail to take account of it, then they may justifiably be convicted of dogmatism. Now, admittedly, most of the alternatives to the ‘galactic recession’ interpretation of the Hubble redshift presented so far, have failed. Theories of ‘tired light’, or loss of energy due to ‘travelling such enormous distances’, have had to be rejected because any explanation involving ‘light in transit’ is like ‘refraction’, in that it entails a dispersion, or broadening, of the spectral bands, whereas only a Doppler effect, due to a real motion on the part of the source can retain the integrity of the redshifted spectrum in the way that is observed. But did I say the only explanation? In fact, there is at least one other logical explanation of the galactic redshift. In the same way as the Doppler explanation, this retains the integrity of the spectrum throughout its spectral reddening. It also assumes a real motion on the part of those galaxies. The difference is that this alternative explanation is in terms, not of uniform recessional motion but of random motion in which all the Doppler redshift and blue shift effects of normally distributed motions of approach and recession statistically cancel out. This alternative overall effect would be due to what relativists call time-dilation. Let me explain. Now anyone who knows the very first thing about relativity knows that when a body moves relatively to the observer, the observer sees all processes on that body uniformly slowed. This means that any spectrograph of that body taken from the point of observation manifests a uniform redshift, of just the sort that Hubble and Slipher reported. The difference is that this time-dilational redshift, unlike the Doppler redshift, is independent of the direction of the motion, so that it is the same – that is, redshifted – for motions of approach as for motions of recession, indeed, for motions in any direction whatsoever. (There are no ‘blueshifted’ time-dilations.) In that case, one might validly assume a Steady State point of view, that the Hubble phenomenon is due to an increase in the random motions of the distant galaxies in proportion to their observational distance. Now it is not the purpose of this talk to explain the details of this particular steadystate alternative to the Big Bang theory. Suffice it to say that it exists and is, currently, being systematically explored. To give a full account of that would take more than just one other talk of this kind. The logical point to note at this stage is that regardless of whether that alternative is taken as established or as no more than diagnostic, its very existence as a so-far unrefuted challenge to the Big Bang orthodoxy calls that orthodoxy into question. So there, for the moment, I rest my case, that what I have called the Cosmological Cockup – or at least

-4part of it – is the common acceptance of the galactic recession theory as gospel when logical analysis proves it to be no more than a dogma, and an extremely bizarre one at that! Part 2: What’s that, ‘a part of the Cockup’? There’s more? Indeed, there is – much more. For instance, Newton’s ‘fundamental laws of motion’ contain another different form of fallacy. His famous First Law states that ‘every body remains in its state of rest, or of uniform motion in a straight line, except when it is compelled to change that state under the action of a force’. But then, his Law of Gravitation contradicts this by saying that all bodies attract one another with a force proportional to the inverse square of their separation and the product of their masses’. So what Newton says, in effect, is that no bodies anywhere travel in accordance with his First Law because they are prevented from doing so by his other Law, the Gravitational Law. In this way, a whole plethora of mystical and invisible ‘forces’ and ‘force-fields’ is created to explain why nowhere do bodies move in the way that Newton demands. So, whatever possessed him to invent a ‘Law’ that states, against every evidence to the contrary, that all motion is basically rectilinear, or as it has been called, ‘inertial’? Nothing – but nothing – travels in that way. Objects thrown in the air travel in curves. Balls on a billiard table, pucks on ice, if they travel far enough, are also seen to travel in curves. The billiard table and the frozen lake would be seen to have horizons, with the trajectories of the objects having radius matching that of the earth; and for the balls or pucks to travel in those ways that are familiar to us, they have to follow those curved trajectories. In truth, then, there is no such thing as Newton’s ‘straight-line motion’, hence there is no need for his invisible ‘gravitational force’ counteracting it to form the sorts of motions that are observed. But if there is no such thing as ‘gravitational force’, then what makes bodies move in the way they do, in orbits like those followed by the planets, and in trajectories like those of bullets and cricket balls? To answer that it will be necessary to say something about POAMS. POAMS is the acronym for the Pope Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis. This is an alternative approach to modern physics and cosmology, originating over fifty years ago, that now involves a group of scientists and philosophers. It is an approach which harks back to a pre-modern era of anti-theoretical, anti-metaphysical physics known as Empiricism. Broadly speaking, this is the view that reality in science – in Physics, especially – should be couched in a language which makes the least possible reference to things behind the appearances; in other words, that all theoretical statements should be cashed out, wherever possible, in terms of direct sensory or instrumental data. Now, again, the full description of the POAMS version of Empiricism – or, as it is sometimes called, phenomenalism – is far too much to deal with in a short talk such as this. Certainly, there is no slogan-like encapsulation of it that I can trot out here. Anyone wishing to know more about it may access the full works on the websites, shown at the end of this talk. Suffice it to say for now that POAMS regards Newton’s First Law as unempirical, since nowhere are bodies ever actually observed to move in conformity with it. Any empiricist would say, as Aristotle did, that all motion is naturally curvilinear, and that it takes place in automatically paired and balanced systems of angular momentum, as is observed in the case of the planets and satellites of our solar system and, indeed, of our local system of galaxies. [A picture was shown depicting a pair of galaxies in this kind of balance relation.] This is what distinguishes the POAMS approach to modern physics, which is that in place of Newton’s metaphysical conception of ‘gravity’, it proposes an angular momentum equation

L = mvr = GmM/v This equation (applied to purely circular motion for simplicity) has been proved to be perfectly sufficient in itself to explain the orbital motions of free-moving bodies. As can be seen, it incorporates all the parameters needed for the expression of orbital motion. For instance, it has mass parameters m and M, for the pair of masses concerned, it has a velocity parameter, v, for the motion of the orbiting body m (taking, in this case, the larger mass M as the centre of orbit), it has a radius parameter r, and the same empirical scale-factor G as in the orthodox ‘gravitational’ equation of Newton. Indeed, the only difference between this and the customary equation is that for different sets of values of G, the equation describes the orbits of the so-called ‘electron’ and ‘proton’ in the standard Bohr model of the hydrogen atom and the ‘attraction and repulsion’ observed both in electrostatics and magnetostatics. It also predicts the masses of the ‘electron’ and ‘proton’, which the Newtonian equation cannot do. Now someone is bound to say: But if there is no such thing as gravity, then what is it that makes things fall towards the ground and which, resting there, registers as weight? POAMS answers that they fall in that way because they have insufficient angular momentum to orbit the earth in the way the moon and other satellites do. With such small amounts of angular momentum as they have, due to the earth’s rotation for example, they would normally orbit the earth at a distance somewhere way below the earth’s surface, so they fall towards that surface with an acceleration of 32 feet per second per second. And because that surface prevents them from falling any further they press on it with the reactionary force in newtons which we measure as weight in kilograms. All this is calculable in terms, purely, of the angular momentum equation without involving any of the usual fictitious ‘forces’ of the Newtonian formulism (This can be checked by anyone accessing the literature on the POAMS website.) Now the same applies, not only to the fictitious ‘gravitational’ force but also to all the other classical ‘fields’ and ‘field-forces’ of magnetism, electricity or whatever. So another aspect of the ‘Cosmological Cock-up’ is that a whole plethora of invisible and metaphysical ‘forces’ and ‘force fields’ have been created unnecessarily to explain why, at no level of observation, from astrophysics to atomic and nuclear physics, do bodies behave in obedience to Newton’s Law. The ‘cockup’, in this case, is that an altogether prodigious number of manhours and public funds have been completely wasted on trying to unify all these various fictitious ‘forces’ and ‘fields’ – as, for instance, in the Holy-Grail-like search for the ‘Unified Field’, which enervated and defeated even the great Einstein. This failure is little wonder, since the exercise turns out, on logical analysis, to be as futile as trying to unify all the fairy stories written by the likes of the brothers Grimm and Hans Christian Anderson. Is that it, then? Is that the measure of the ‘Cosmological Cockup’, or is there more? You bet there’s more! Once you begin to pull on this logical loose thread, the whole classical paradigm begins to unravel. So far as POAMS is concerned, this monumental cockup is all down to over-intellectualisation, with social premiums placed on ‘genius’ and general eggheadedness. At the root of all modern astronomy and cosmology are Relativity and Quantum Theory. POAMS achieves everything that Einstein’s Relativity does, plus all that Bohr’s Quantum Theory does – and more, as may be seen in the handouts on ‘What POAMS Has Achieved’, to be circulated at the end of this meeting (some may have already seen it on the POAMS discussion group.) But please don’t imagine that this is by adding more and more esotery to the existing formulism. It is done by paring down the existing theories to the barest

-6possible minimum in a manner consistent with the stated policy of Empiricism. In this paringdown process, much use is made of what has been called ‘Ockham’s Razor’. This is in shaving-off those many hoary theoretical accretions that, so useful in their day, have now long outlived their practical usefulness and, in the name of ‘Physics’, serve only to obscure from us the true face of nature. Two other tools that have been mobilised in this paring-down process, are, i), Philosophical Analysis (sometimes called Linguistic Analysis) and, ii), the ordinary commonsense logic that has so long been despised by the priesthood of the modern Church of Mathematical Physics. In short, we need to get out of the traditional rut and start thinking again – that is, democratically, for ourselves, not as directed by the ‘moguls of Physics’. We need to realise that far too much of what we fancy are bits of ‘scientific knowledge’ are, in fact, no more than dogmas. We need to tune up our logical and commonsense crap detectors, refusing to suppress them when they start bleeping. With our modern technology we have never had so much physical and astronomical information at our disposal; and never have we had less freeminded facility for dealing with it. Subjects like Logic and Philosophy have become less and less a part of the Educational curriculum, having given way to more lucrative subjects like Management Consultancy, Sports Science, Flower-Arranging, Aroma Therapy, the Beatles … etc. etc., all of which testifies to the failure of moribund Theoretical Physics and Cosmology to excite the imaginations of young, honest and forthright, enquiring minds. This splitting-off of Physical Science from Natural Philosophy and Logic is like disconnecting the cortex from the cerebellum in the human brain, or severing the head from the body in animals generally. There are, of course, animals which can operate without a head, running around like headless chickens. Hopefully, this is not the way with too many of us humans. For our human curiosity to be properly served in young healthy minds, physics and commonsense philosophy will have to be reconnected – or, perhaps, reinvented – to become more like the wholesome subject that was once called Natural Philosophy. There are, quietly, moves afoot for doing this. At least one true coming-together of minds, from widely different disciplines in the way that is required, is being conducted in the name of POAMS. This presents a whole new paradigm, in effect, as a replacement for the currently cocked up extant paradigm. What, then, is that new paradigm? ‘The Secret, quick about it, friend’, says the poet Omar Khayyám. Not here, Omar, I’m afraid. That would be like trying to fill a lighter from a petrol bowser or to quench one’s thirst from a fire hydrant.
