New Discussion File: Challenges to POAMS

Dear Viv,

I'm trying to clarify my understanding of POAMS using the following four scenarios. I'd like to see if the way I picture these events corresponds with POAMS, and if not, what the differences are, and whether this perspective is nonetheless plausible.

1) A spacecraft orbiting a planet and taccelerating by firing a conventional rocket tangentially to its orbit. Both before and after tacceleration, the planet and the spacecraft can only move in accordance with the conservation of angular momentum (AM). During the spacecraft's tacceleration, the planet would instantaneously change its motion and AM in accordance with the changing AM of the spacecraft. In other words, the planet would respond instantaneously to the spacecraft's tacceleration, NOT in the delayed distance-time, s/c. In principle, this prediction could be tested observationally.

2) A spacecraft orbiting a planet and taccelerating by firing a 'photon' or action quantum drive tangentially to its orbit. (The 'action quantum' drive could be an array of powerful lasers, for example.) Both before and after tacceleration, the planet and the spacecraft can only move in accordance with the conservation of angular momentum (AM). During the spacecraft's tacceleration, the planet would instantaneously change its motion and AM in accordance with the changing AM of the spacecraft. In other words, the planet would respond instantaneously to the spacecraft's tacceleration, NOT in the delayed distance-time, s/c. The action quanta emitted by the spacecraft's drive may not be consummated for aeons, even though the propulsion is felt instantly by the spacecraft. In principle, this prediction could be tested observationally.

3) A spacecraft orbiting a planet and taccelerating by firing a 'photon' or action quantum drive tangentially to its orbit, but where a body orbiting the same planet receives all of the drive's emitted action quanta. This should be the same as for (2) above, but with the following difference. During the spacecraft's tacceleration, the body should also instantaneously change its motion and AM in accordance with the changing AM of the spacecraft and the planet. However, after the distance-time s/c, the body will receive action quanta from the spacecraft's drive. The directional nature of the action quanta received should taccelerate the body, changing its motion and AM. During this tacceleration of the body, the planet and the spacecraft would instantaneously change their motion and AM in accordance with the changing AM of the body. In other words, the planet and the spacecraft would respond instantaneously to the body's tacceleration, NOT in the delayed distance-time, s/c. In principle, this prediction effect could be tested observationally.

4) A supernova in which a significant fraction of the mass of a star is converted to energy in the form of action quanta. At the instant the mass converts to energy and is emitted as action quanta, all planets in the system would instantaneously change their motion and AM in accordance with the changed mass and AM of the system. In other words, the planets would respond instantaneously to the mass-energy conversion, NOT in the delayed distance-time, s/c. Nonetheless, after the distance-time s/c, a planet would receive a tiny fraction of the total action quanta emitted by the supernova. The directional nature of the action quanta received should taccelerate the planet, changing its motion and AM. During this tacceleration of the planet, the other bodies in the system would instantaneously change their motion and AM in accordance with the changing AM of the planet. In other words, the other bodies would respond instantaneously to the planet's tacceleration, NOT in the delayed distance-time, s/c. In principle, this prediction effect could be tested observationally.

I look forward to discussing this.

Cheers,

Jon

Dear Jon,

Thanks for your last e-mail concerning our argument about the 'different scenarios'. These arguments have unearthed something that I think is important, though perhaps not in the way that might have been expected. The argument keeps cropping up in different forms, suggesting that there is either some deep fault in POAMS or some deep misunderstanding of it. Laying aside the argument we just now had on the 'phone, let's first look at the pieces I told you about that I wrote in response to your 'four scenarios' piece.

Here, though, first of all, is a cut-and-paste of the new section 10(b) of the 'Two Paradigms' that I wrote in answer to the first problem.

10(b) 'RECOIL' IN QUANTUM INTERACTIONS

However, in post-Workshop discussions with Jon Blay, the question arose as to what 'recoil' can mean in this quantum-digital context, since the word suggests photons being ejected ballistically, like bullets from a gun. However, we were reminded that in the quantum-digital paradigm a quantum interaction between atoms – or nucleons in the Mössbauer case – is not an isolated, punctiform event. It is an event involving, in one and the same instance, the two interacting atoms and the (macroscopic) distance between the bodies in which those atoms inhere (witness the two-slit experiment, section 8.1). For the action to take place, all these relevant parameters have to be congruent within the condition of the integrity of h. So although there is no distinction, at the quantum level, as to which is the antecedent and which the consequent ('cause' and 'effect'), the interaction does nevertheless behave as a pair of bodies would in a balanced relation where one of them loses some measure of mass-energy while the other gains it, shifting the centre of moment or point of balance in the direction of the transferred mass-energy. Since this is tantamount to moving the 'emitting' atom in a direction opposite to that of the interaction, that quantum motion conforms to what, in ordinary engineering language, is called 'recoil'.

Now to your 'first scenario'. This does correspond with POAMS. Your second one, however, does not. From the POAMS point of view, it mixes together two incommensurable paradigms. For a start, saying that the action quanta may not be consummated for aeons does not correspond with POAMS. In POAMS the consummation is immediate (i.e., proper-time-instantaneous, with tP = 0) and it is only in relative time (tR = s/c) that the 'aeons' can come in. And, as Alan explained to you at Bristol, since there is no overall or underlying 'God's Time' in which to compare what happens in different places, there can be no 'paradox' presented by the fact that the time registered in transit by the 'quantum itself' is tP = 0 while that registered by the observer of that interaction is the delayed-time tR = s/c.

Another defining difference between the POAMS paradigm and the classical one is that in POAMS, due to the overall conservation of angular momentum, things like rockets can't just shoot off anywhere by reaction – call it 'recoil', 'tacceleration' or whatever – in straight lines in the sort of Euclidean 'inertial space' that Newton envisaged. What I am now going to say is, I suggest, a new development of POAMS following from the response to you in my above section 10(B). When a body sheds some portion of its mass, in the form of light from a laser, its centre of balance (or moment) shifts. In the simplest case, of a pair of interacting atoms, when the emitting atom loses a measure of its mass-energy and the absorbing atom (immediately) gains it, the absorbing atom moves nearer to the centre-of-moment of the two atoms, while the emitting atom moves further away. At the quantum level, these shifts in position are entirely momentary. That is, they don't continue on forever in straight lines, in the direction of the recoil. In angular momentum space, taccelerations are not like that. It is axiomatic in POAMS that there is no free and continuous straight-line motion anywhere. As you know, all motions are correlated, and all changes in motion (taccelerations) involve shifts in the centres of balance between bodies in overall-conserved angular momentum relations. For instance, when the Pioneer space-probe left the solar system, it did not, from then on, travel onwards in a straight line towards infinity. It went into some other orbit – another balancing relation with bodies other than the sun and its planets – around the galaxy if nothing else. And unless the ‘sling-shot’ impulse it was given, which took it past Pluto, was sufficient to enable it to interject with some other orbit around bodies outside the solar system, we may see it come back to visit us, after some millennia, like some super-orbital ‘Halley’s comet’. (In POAMS as in classical theory, in order to leave the solar system altogether, the final orbit has to be an extremely elongated ellipse, much more elongated than that of any known comet. But I find it difficult to see how that ellipse can be so elongated as to enable Pioneer to interject with orbits around other bodies when there is no other body for it orbit around that is within at least four light-years away.)

Now that, Jon, was something I intended to finish and e-mail to you. But then I was interrupted and didn’t do so. In the meantime, I dictated the following to Mary in the car and then wrote it up when we got home. Again, I hadn’t finished it before you ‘phoned. So here it is, as it comes, as you wished.

Look at it this way: at the quantum level, to say that a quantum is emitted is to say, in the same breath, that a quantum is absorbed. You can’t have the one without the other, any more than you can have a one-ended piece of string. So there is no case in which a photum is emitted into the void, leaving its emitter, by reaction, floating forever in the one direction and the action travelling in the opposite direction, for perhaps ‘aeons’ until it happens to hit something. As well as being incommensurate with POAMS, this idea is also incommensurate with standard quantum physics. (Witness the argument that takes place perennially in physics over the conservation of spin angular momentum of the collision-products of particles at great distances, in relation to Bell’s theorem.)

In POAMS, of course, not only is spin angular momentum conserved in this way but so also is orbital angular momentum, all as part of the same package, as you well know. This means, as I say, that in a paired angular momentum relation, irrespective of how far apart or how near the bodies may be to each other and to other bodies, any transfer of mass or mass-energy between the one body and the other, in the form of light-energy – which is instantaneous and automatically consummated – shifts the recipient of that energy nearer to the common centre of balance of the two bodies whilst sending the emitter further away. In both cases this movement, by reaction, is no more than a temporary impulse, not continuous in the manner classically conceived as ‘inertial’. Only on the macroscopic scale, where the atoms concerned are fixed constituents of bodies that may be astronomical distances apart, do the accumulations of atomic angular momentum, with reaction motors and so on, resemble the sort of continuous, so-called 'inertial', recoil motions that are classically envisaged. However, not even in the vastest regions of space (so POAMS projects) do reaction-motors move in the rectilinear way that Newton's 'First Law' demands. When a rocket sheds its exhaust, the particles of that gas go into one set of orbits and the rocket-molecules into another. Those rocket-gases do not have to be received anywhere, in the way that light quanta have to. In itself, the body of exhaust-gas is sufficient, in its angular momentum relation with the rocket, to propel the rocket in the one direction (with respect to the common centre of moment) and the gas in the other, each in an elliptical orbit depending on the amount and direction of the torque. From the above reasoning, however, this is not what happens when the rocket-motor is replaced by a laser, which can create a recoil only when the transmitted quanta are received (which, of course, is proper-time-instantly). POAMS therefore calls into question not only the idea of a 'photon-reaction' motor propelled simply by 'photons' shot out into space, but also the classical idea of 'light-pressure' exerted by an emitting source upon an absorbing body by blasts of 'photons' in a facile analogy with wind on a sail.

In short, if POAMS is correct, then – at least in my understanding of it – the effect of a light-interaction, far from exerting a 'pressure' on the absorber, should be to move the common centre-of-mass of the emitter and absorber towards the absorber. Now that, I think, is a very interesting and testable consequence of POAMS, making it yet another reason for regarding it a physics theory (as opposed to a metaphysical one) according to Popper's criterion of refutability, in the way Colin Evans approved.

None of this is, of course, a direct answer to or criticism of your challenge to POAMS. I mention it only as what seems to me an interesting development of POAMS, the opportunity to record which I felt I couldn't pass up.

Now to your most recent telephone call, which I received before that last piece went off. Here is yet another piece in response to that conversation.

I was surprised at your saying that the fundamental axioms of POAMS have never been stated. I thought these axioms had been made plain in all the various publications. The first is:

1), All unmediated observational distances are times, in the constant ratio of units, c.

This is in complete contrast to Einstein's 'second axiom', that light travels in vacuo at the constant speed c, the same for all observers whether moving or stationary relatively to one another.

Now before discussing the second POAMS axiom, let us remind ourselves of what the basic philosophy of POAMS is. It is the neo-Machian observationalist stance, called Normal Realism, according to which there is no possible way, consistent with empirical physics, in which an observer can be in more than one place at the same instant. (let that be another axiom, if you like.) This means that all dimensions of observation are basically observer-centred and polar in character. There are, altogether, five of these polar dimensions to distant observation. Two of these are the angular dimensions, elevation and azimuth ('vertical' and 'horizontal'), and the third is range (or 'distance'). We will come to the other two in a moment.

First, though, let it be said that POAMS claims no monopoly on this characterising of observational dimensions, which only reiterates a standard truism of commonsense. Where POAMS comes in is with its first axiom (see above) which makes all unmediated distances, or range-measures s, times, in units of s/c. (i.e. where s is in metres, s/c is in units of 3.3 nanoseconds). But the fact that these dimensions of observation are interdependent and all orthogonal (projected at right-angles to one another) means that they all fall mensurationally, under the rule of Pythagoras' theorem.

Again, let us remind ourselves of the details of that. For the sake of simplicity, we may ignore the angular dimensions, elevation and azimuth. This leaves us with three orthogonal time-dimensions. These are: i.) the observational range-dimension s/c travelled by an object (such as Jupiter relative to Römer in his first estimation of c); ii.) the time t_P registered by the object itself in travelling that distance-time s/c relatively to the (called the 'proper' time) – and, iii.), t_R which is the resultant, or relative time of that same motion as registered by the observatory clock when the distance-time s/c is subtracted. That resultant time t_R corresponds exactly, as you know, to what Einstein called 'time-dilation.

The second axiom of POAMS can now be stated. It is:

2) All three distance-time dimensions of position and motion are interdependent and orthogonal.

A corollary of this, of course, is that these dimensions fall under the rule of Pythagoras's theorem.

Anyway, all that is, hopefully, now done and tucked-away in the POAMS archives. My concern with it just now is to show – unless, of course, POAMS is entirely 'up the creek' – how that formula forbids those things you were suggesting.

The axiom that all distances are times in the ratio of units c is completely unequivocal. This means that if there is anything that can be found which definitely proves that there is something that can be observed or measured at a distance s in less time than s/c, that first axiom goes in the skip – followed, automatically by POAMS. In other words, all suggestions or speculations about faster-than-light (so-called 'superluminal') interactions, are logically ruled out by POAMS – which doesn't mean that they cannot happen but only that if any such phenomenon can be ascertained then POAMS is refuted.

With that in mind, let us look at what you were suggesting. You proposed that in the angular momentum synthesis, taken in itself as a thesis separate from the 'Pythagorean' situation, we can say that by the law of angular momentum conservation, if some seismic disturbance occurred here on earth, sufficient to disturb the planet's orbit (in effect a torsional change, or 'tacceleration' as we have called it), that would immediately and instantly perturb the orbits of the other planets. That is precisely in accordance with the predictions of POAMS. What is not in accordance with POAMS is any suggestion that there is some way of observing that perturbation instantly, either from the standpoint of the distant observer or from that of the home observer – since that would confound POAM’s first postulate (that all observational distances (in any direction) are times s/c). It would be tantamount to saying that superluminal effects do exist, which POAMS logically and categorically denies.

According to the basic equation of POAMS, which is the Pythagorean time-formula already described in relation to the POAMS second axiom, all distant events, regardless of whether they be the effects of taccelerations of the sort you describe, be they seismic effects, illuminations from sources on earth, burns caused by lasers, or whatever your ingenuity may devise, unless they are observed by some ubiquitous deity, are observable by human scientists. Plainly, any effect you may conjecture which is not observable cannot signify in POAMS. In other words, no matter how you may express it, to assume that something happens in nature in some wholly inscrutable way, behind the observational scenes, which is somehow inaccessible to observation and measurement is pure metaphysics, so far as POAMS is concerned.

Physics, then, so far as POAMS is concerned, is rational observation and measurement, nothing more nor less. In that case, any distant effect of something I might do here, right now, can be observed by me only at that distance. And since that distance s from me entails an observational time-delay s/c, I cannot observe that effect until that time s/c elapses. Add to that the condition that for the distant observer of that effect, its terrestrial cause occurred in his observational frame, at a distance-time s₁/c away from him, then the total time between my sending of that impulse and observing its effect back here on earth will be the sum of those two intervals – that is s₁/c + s/c which, if s₁ = s, is 2s/c. Indeed, according to POAMS, one’s whole warrant for saying that the interaction was proper-time-instantaneous both ways is that it is observed back here on earth after that time 2s/c. If ever it turned out to be less, say just s/c as you suggest, then one of those observational legs would have had to be ‘superluminal’ and POAMS would have to doff its cap and depart.

That, as I say, shows the tightness of the connection, in POAMS, between its Pythagorean basis and the angular momentum synthesis. If, as you suggest, those two aspects of POAMS can be separated for ‘PR’ purposes, then I might agree. But if this implies that they can be logically separated, then I firmly disagree. And, as I said to you on the ‘phone, if anyone were to misappropriate the angular momentum synthesis – that is, excise it from its philosophical roots and present it in a philosophically isolated way, albeit in a manner more attractive to physicists, then they are welcome. But be assured that I would then be that thesis’s worst enemy. My line of attack would be, as I said on the ‘phone, to ask how that distant instantaneity implied by angular momentum conservation could possibly be observed other than by light in the delayed-time s/c. And if the answer was that it was instantaneous in the outward direction and s/c only in the homeward direction, from the distant effect to the terrestrial observer (that is, involving only one s/c), then I would ask: ‘Then how on earth, uniquely among all other observers, did that distant observer manage to receive that proper-time-instantaneous effect in a time s/c of zero – i.e., superluminally?'

Anyway, let that do for now.

Best, Viv.

Nov. 6th (answer to new telephone discussion)

Dear Jon,

What seems to have emerged from this discussion is that in POAMS the instantaneity implied by angular momentum has to be in proper time, not in observational or relative time. This is for the simple reason that if the instantaneity took place in the observational time tR, then from the Pythagorean equation (which in POAMS is axiomatic) we would have $$t_{\mathrm{R}} = \sqrt{[(s/c)^2 + t_{\mathrm{P}}^2]} = 0$$ from which it would follow that

$$t_{\mathrm{P}} = \sqrt{[- (s/c)^2]},$$

which is nonsense.

This implies that there is no monolithic or 'universal' proper-time. It also implies that if some observational instantaneity of the sort that Van Flandern and Phipps talk about were definitely to be proved, then that basic axiom of POAMS would be refuted and so would POAMS.

Dear Clive,

Yes, this problem of what you call 'fishiness' keeps cropping up, doesn't it. As a matter of fact, your questions, added to many other similar ones, prompted me to write and disseminate to my e-mailing group, a 'consolidation of POAMS' piece, which I have enclosed for you to see. Much of that piece (and I thank you for prompting me to write it) was based on my answer to your letter, which was as follows.

Let us look hard at the situation you describe in your letter, regarding the first two pages of the POAMS Website, headed "What does POAMS stand for?" Your response to this was:

On page 1 you say "every body is automatically paired and balanced with EVERY other". Then on page 2, "each change in the one subsystem (an atom, say) is accompanied by a compensatory change in the angular momentum of some other subsystem (atom) somewhere else, that is, immediately and instantly, without the angular momentum of the system as a whole being at any time disconserved." Now here is something a bit fishy; in the first sentence it is every body paired with every other; in the second one is paired with one other. Which should it be? Personally, I can't see how you can really mean the second one because then you would seem to need some mechanism to determine WHICH of all the other particles in the universe was the one which took up the AM recoil.

Let me unpick this. Take, first, your statement that in the first sentence every body is paired with every other, while in the second, one is paired with one other. Which, you ask, should it be? My answer is to affirm both, that every body is paired with every other and that one is paired with one other. The only thing that makes this seem 'fishy', as you say, is the tacit assumption (contraband in POAMS) that somehow, all this should happen in some monolithic, God's-eye-view way in the sort of time Newton envisaged.

To recall the way I once described it to Ted, if one were to say that there is a social system in which all monetary dealings among a populace were completely and absolutely honest on every occasion, that wouldn't entail, would it, that there is some absolute Overall Platonic Honesty in which all the multifarious dealings are monolithically and instantly connected. In POAMS, angular momentum connections are just like those in the populace described. Every single one involves two transactors between whom the giving and receiving are one indistinguishable (i.e., simultaneous and reciprocal) event. But nowhere in POAMS does it say – or even suggest – that those different transactions have to occur together, all at the same time. Indeed, so far as POAMS is concerned, there is no time at all on the fundamental atomic level other than insofar as it consists of series-upon-series of such individual transactions (angular momentum changes). Moreover, those transactions are intransitive, which is to say that when $A$ transacts with $B$, $B$ needn't also, in that specifically one-to-one transaction, also transact with $C$. Indeed, if these transactions involved the passing of a single smallest coin (analogously to an unsplittable quantum), there is no way in which $A$'s transaction with $B$ could possibly be shared by $C$. One could nevertheless say, without fear of contradiction, that in all these transactions the overall economy was strictly conserved.

So, in POAMS, we may say, also without fear of contradiction, that when atom $A$ swaps a quantum with atom $B$, it cannot, in that same instance, swap that same quantum with atom $C$ – or, indeed with anything else in the universe, which makes it an absolutely discrete quantum event. So with complete equanimity, one may say, in one sentence, that every body is paired with every other and, in the second sentence, that (in any one instance) only one is paired with only one other. Also, with no less equanimity one may say that in all these quantum transactions, the overall angular momentum is conserved, with the corollary that every one of these quantum transactions is instantaneous – proper-time instantaneous, that is.

What 'mechanism', you ask, determines which of all the other particles in the universe was the one which took up the angular momentum recoil. Well, so far as POAMS is concerned, 'determines' is a bad word, here, because all such events, on the fundamental level are absolutely indeterminate, the only laws to which they conform being those of complete randomness in accordance with statistical or stochastic laws like that of the second law of thermodynamics – which, of course, is macro-mechanical, not 'micro-mechanical'. In other words, there is no micro-

mechanical determinacy (no micromechanics, even) in POAMS, so there is no mechanism in POAMS to determine whether your A will transact with B or with C or, as you say, with any other particle in the universe.

The nearest thing to a mechanism we have in POAMS is on the macrophysical level where, in some apparatus like that of the two-slit experiment, only those atoms at locations on the screen that are geometrically congruent with the distant point-source in terms of half-periods of the quanta transacted have any probability of interacting (hence the typical pattern of 'interference'). In POAMS, it is all probabilistic, hence – like the Schrödinger probability function – subject to precise, statistical laws of the sort which ensure the functionings of steam-engines and things.

Anyway, having said all that, it occurs to me that saying 'Every body is instantly connected to every other' might be somewhat misleading, even though on the macroscale it is true (the perturbations caused by each of the planets on the orbits of each of the others testifies to that, and so, undoubtedly, do stars in galaxies and galaxies in their groups). This will be looked at in the next website update, and I am grateful to you for alerting me to that possible need.

Now to where you say:

According to the POAMS rule the change of spin is compensated instantaneously by changes in the spin of B & C and from the symmetry of the whole situation I cannot believe that only one of them will take part but I imagine each will change by the same amount. Now here is the problem:- the changes in A and B are simultaneous; so are the changes in A and C. But the two changes in spin of B and C are not simultaneous.

My answer to that is as already stated; it is that the spins, like atomic interactions, are quantised in irreducible and unsharable amounts, so that the interactions are uniquely one-to-one, hence intransitive.

I'm sorry, but I don't understand your last comment, regarding the POAMS statement "To impute these problems to nature is a fallacy." QUESTION:

POAMS states that all bodies in the universe are instantly linked in a balancing relation of overall-conserved angular momentum. Yet POAMS also states that there is no universal instantaneity, that the proper-times of all bodies moving relatively to one another are different. Can you explain to me how POAMS reconciles these two statements.

Answer

This question is based on a false assumption, namely, that 'the proper-times of all bodies moving relatively to one another are different'. This is not so. In POAMS, for bodies in free motion, that is, with no forces acting on them, there are no differences in their proper-times relatively to one another. So it makes sense to say that angular momentum is conserved instantly throughout that set of bodies.

It is only when a force (or, rather, a torque) is applied to some one or another of those bodies that the proper-time simultaneity between that body and the others is changed. As the relativistic 'Twins' example shows, it is only when the symmetry of motion between the twins is broken by the undertaking of the reversing action by one or the other of them, that relative differences in their proper-times result. There is no such break in symmetry in free orbital motion. In that case, the reversal of the motion between bodies at the extremes of elliptical orbits is perfectly symmetrical, occasioning no change in the proper-time of the one relatively to the other. The situation, so far, is virtually Newtonian.

How, then, does the principle of angular momentum apply to that residual set of bodies whose proper-times, due to the effects of torsional forces on them, become 'out of synch' with that of the 'Newtonian' set? Well, in the first place, at the same (neo-Newtonian) time that the change in angular momentum occurs in one locality, the compensatory 'knock-on' effect of that change on the angular momentum system throughout (i.e., non-locally), occurs instantly. And this connection with other bodies, as felt immediately by an observer at the source of the disturbance (as, e.g., by the reversing twin, of the passengers in the cabin of an accelerating rocket-ship) accounts for what Mach called 'inertia'.

How, then, can the principle of angular momentum conservation include that residual set of bodies whose proper-times are out of synchrony with the 'Newtonian' set. Well, as soon as that disturbance ceases (when the 'inertia' is no longer felt) the changed time of that disruptive source remains, while the synchrony of correlation between events at the source and in the main body is resumed. In this way, due to the vast accumulation of these de-synchronising effects throughout the universe, there can be no permanent synchrony of time-readings as according to some imagined 'cosmical GMT'. Nevertheless, despite these differences in time-readings, in a settled state of angular momentum correlation, temporal synchrony of events is assured.

The fact, then, that torsional effects (POAMS calls them taccelerations) change the readings of clocks and other temporal processes of bodies relatively to one another (not only in POAMS but also in standard SR) may be deemed insignificant so far as angular momentum conservation is concerned. So this, regarding your question, is how POAMS reconciles the two statements, that all bodies are instantly linked in a balancing relation of overall-conserved angular momentum and that there is no overall instantaneity (of clock-readings). In sum, the instantaneity of angular momentum conservation is that of events throughout the system, not that of relatively changed clock-readings.

QUESTION:

Normal Realism says we can only talk about what is observable. So if we cannot observe instantaneous interaction, how can we talk about it?

Answer

In Normal Realism the word ‘observable’ is taken in the ordinary language sense which is not artificially restricted to meaning actually seeing, in the truncated sense of the Logical Positivists and others who took their cue from a dictum of the philosopher George Berkeley: ‘esse is percipi’, or ‘To be is to be perceived’. In commonsense usage I may observe, from the size of my electricity bill, plus the overheating of my house, that I am using too much electricity. No-one is going to challenge my statement by asking me if I actually saw the electricity flowing, or if I actually saw all the various stages between my consuming the electricity and the presentation of the bill.

By that same token, it makes sense, in Normal Realism, to say that one observes in one’s telescope that there are two times by which the motion of a body may be measured. One is the time the observer measures by processes on the body itself between two fixed positions A and B in his reference-frame; the other is the time that the observatory chronometer registers for that same motion over that same distance. This difference is something that is actually observed.

Observed also is that this difference increases systematically as the speed of motion increases. One also observes that there is a formula according to which these differences increase up to a limit where the time registered by the object over the distance would be nil, which means that its speed, in that theoretical limit, would be infinite.

One also observes that according to the formula, the ‘speed of light’ across that distance would be, in itself (i.e., intrinsically) infinite, so that its passage from A to B would be instantaneous. We cannot actually see that instantaneity of light but we see that its instantaneity is implied by the formula, similarly to the way in which my electricity bill implies my over-consumption of electricity.

So, someone might say, in that case, what is there that, by comparison, is not observable? To this, Normal Realism replies, any description of entities that makes no reference to anything that is observable, in the commonsense meaning of the word, that involves either logical contradiction and/or purely fictional and unnecessary entities. In short, ‘observable’ in Normal Realism excludes anything that is logically impossible and/or logically redundant, or over-interpreted.
