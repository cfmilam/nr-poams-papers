ANPA talk 2001

The Keele Project by

Viv Pope

Abstract If one adopts the view that the world is one thing and our observations of it something radically other, then a problem is created as how to connect the two – that is, of how to match our ‘ideas of the world’ to the ‘world itself’. This stems from the way Descartes taught us to think, that is, it terms of a dualistic distinction between a ‘world of matter’ and a ‘world of mind’. Many physicists seek to answer this problem by constructing theories as mental representations of what they conceive in their minds as an underlying ‘external reality’. The irony is that without assuming an independent and completely objective God-like knowledge of that ‘external reality’, there is no way of knowing whether or to what extent the ‘representation’ corresponds with it. As a result, what the theoretician conceives as the ‘external reality’ and his theoretical ‘representation’ of are both ideas in his own mind. So, in contemplating what he thinks of as the ‘external reality’ behind his theoretical representations of it the self-styled ‘realist’ implicitly ‘plays God’. That is, in seeking to match his two idea-systems he is simply trying to match his theory-system to his own underlying prejudices, so that his theory takes on a completely dogmatic and idiosyncratic character which may be immune to rational negotiation. With this loss of true objectivity, physics theories proliferate out of control, so that all contact with commonsense reality is lost. Even among physicists themselves, the jargon developed by one faction may become incomprehensible to another, as in some theoretician’s ‘Tower of Babel’, where nothing but fashion can decide the ‘going rate’. Nor can a common language of ‘Mathematics’ help, since a mathematical theory of physics is always a deduction from premises, and it is observation that has to supply these premises. A Mathematical system whose premises lie nowhere but within itself is, like ‘realism’ no more than just another circular, epistemological prison. Undoubtedly, this is why Physics departments are being shut-down and replaced by other departments, such as, for instance, that of Information Technology. Why is this? The answer we propose is that the old artificial Cartesian division between ‘theory’ (mind) and physical reality (matter) is rapidly disappearing. What remains is the empirical need for correct interpretation (decoding) of sensory and instrumental information, and its articulation in the plainest common language. This calls for a complete paradigm shift, in physics, away from the old mechanistic particle-physics analogs into a digital information-based physics. This paper is based on the nascent paradigm shift away from the classical mechanistic ‘realism’ of Democritus and Descartes into a neo-Machian mode, as befits the modern advances in information-technology.

1. Introduction The germ of the idea for the Keele Project was conceived at the beginning of the 1950s, when I was a very young man and my colleague, Anthony Osborne, was not even born. At the time, I worked as a telephone engineer, although I later went on to university and to lecturing. My original inspiration was a letter I received from Einstein, to whom I had written about a problem I had with what he identified to me, in his letter, as the ‘Clock Paradox’. His answer to my question put me right on that, but the more I studied relativity theory and quantum theory, afterwards, the more I came to realise that there was something radically wrong at the philosophical root of the science in which, till then, I had had implicit faith. To cut a very long story short, I worked out in my own mind what was wrong, and after many years of struggling to get a hearing for those ideas, in 1985 I was referred to the Mathematics Department, at Keele University, England, where I met Anthony Osborne, a young lecturer in the department. He showed an immediate interest in giving mathematical expression to some graphical ideas of mine, and so the Keele Project began. This unusual partnership between a philosopher and a mathematician has resulted in a number of conference presentations and published papers, the most recent of which is ‘An Angular Momentum Synthesis’, to appear soon in Galilean Electrodynamics (see handouts). The full account of the curious social and psychological repercussions of my attempt to obtain a fair evaluation of these ideas can be read in my book Lines from Einstein, an Intellectual Odyssey, available on INTERNET. Perhaps the best way of describing what happened is with reference to a piece which appeared in the New Scientist, 15th August, 1985. This shows how uniquely individual and conditional are our mental interpretations of what we perceive. The article describes how Chambers and Reisberg, of the New School for Social Research discovered that there is a limit to what people can do with mental images. The research made use of ambiguous figures such as the famous duck-rabbit. (SEE OHP. 1)

2. The Question of Paradigms The way in which physics perceives the world depends on the prevailing paradigm of the time. It now seems, from the rapidly burgeoning numbers of conferences convened on the subject, that more and more physicists are becoming dissatisfied with the present standard-orthodox paradigm. They feel that as a producer of understanding it has signally failed, and that there is dire need for a clear-cut philosophical alternative. We at Keele have suggested that this might be found by switching, at the fundamental microphysical level of physics, from an ‘analog’ to a ‘digital’ approach to the subject. By ‘analog’ in this context is meant the classical, mechanistic approach to physics of Galileo and Newton. In this paradigm, everything interacts by pushes or pulls of one kind or another, in a manner conceived by analogy with our anthropomorphic sense of the muscular effort that we experience in our contacts with everyday objects and in our attempts to move them. ‘Digital’, by contrast, means a purely kinematical approach to physics – at least, on the fundamental, microphysical level – in which the ultimate microphysical elements are not mechanical particles but informational ‘blips’. These are analogous to the pixel-events in a video-screen into which the programme scenario analyses, and the pushes and pulls of the mechanistic paradigm are replaced by purely formal elements – no, not of mathematics, as in the Combinatorial Hierarchies, but of information-statistics as studied by communications engineers such as Claude Shannon. Moreover, the informational bits are measured in units of Planck’s constant h, not in the usual ‘binary digits’ of contemporary information-systems. Berkeley, Kant and Mach – and, to some extent, the quantum physicist, Niels Bohr. Another way of describing the switch is from absolutism to relativism. In true relativism, or relationism, nothing exists in isolation. Everything exists only in relation to everything else – the physicists’ cut-down version of which they know as ‘Mach’s Principle’. In this Machian way of thinking, there is no space nor time except in relation to matter and there is no matter (mass) except in relation to space and time. Also, needless to say, it means that there is no time except in relation to space and no space except in relation to time, which is the basis of Einsteinian relativism. This relation between space and time was empirically confirmed by Römer, who was the first to discover, in his astronomical observations, that all distances in space are times in the ratio of conventional units c. Now in order for everything to be related to everything else, in that Whole we call the Universe, things have to be in some way instantly connected. That is to say, they have to be linked in some manner which is independent of those delayed-time interactions classically conceived either as mechanical in the manner of Galileo and Newton or as quasi-mechanical in the manner of ‘fields’ conceived by Faraday and Maxwell. These instantaneous connections, however, cannot be continuous. If they were, then in order to move, a body would, in that same instant, have to move all the other bodies in the universe, simultaneously, which would make motion impossible in the way that Zeno of Elea so famously argued. To ensure mobility, therefore, the instantaneous connections between bodies cannot be transitive. That is to say, an instantaneous connection between atoms A and B and an instantaneous connection between atoms B and C cannot imply, in that same instance, an instantaneous connection between atoms A and C. This means that between the instantaneous connections of A with B and of B with C there has to be a time-disconnection between B’s connection with A and its connection with C. To say, then, that everything is connected with everything else in the way Mach’s principle requires cannot sensibly mean ‘all in the same instant’. Instantaneous action-at-a-distance therefore has to be intransitive. In short, the instantaneous knock-on effect from one atom to another cannot be continuous but has to be discretised (quantised) – which, of course, it eminently is according to quantum physics. This does not conflict with the Machian conception of a universal frame of reference for the measuring of space and time. Let me explain. First, as Osborne and I

have argued, there are very good reasons for thinking of the atomic interaction we traditionally call ‘light’ as consisting of movie-like time-sequences of these instantaneous measures in metres in the ratio c of those conventional units. Meanwhile, at the level of interactions between ordinary masses, like those of solar systems and galaxies, the instantaneous connections which are sporadic on the atomic level are statistically smoothed-out to form the continuous balancing connections of those macrophysical angular momentum systems. These systems, in toto answer, in the Keele thesis, to the universal reference-frame of ‘Mach’s principle’ And this is where we get to the universal frame of reference for length, time and motion. The time of this overall angular momentum frame (the sidereal time) is the datum time of the universe relative to which the times of bodies moving freely with respect to it are, as can be easily demonstrated, time-dilated in the manner described by Lorentz and Einstein. In this way, we can have all our theoretical ‘cakes’ and eat them, without having to suffer the usual mental indigestion. That is to say, we get instantaneous connection, mobility, time-delay, the preferred reference-frame of Mach and the relativistic time-dilation of Einstein, all without any smidgen of conflict. This, as I see it, is the direction that the New Paradigm must eventually take, and it is this direction that has been – and is still being – explored, This is now not only at Keele but also at what has become known as the Swansea Workshop, on whose recent first meeting, at the University of Wales, Swansea, I am scheduled to report at the end of this ANPA session. Thank you. May I now throw this subject open to discussion. ■
