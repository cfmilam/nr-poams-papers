Objective time dilation in orbiting bodies

N. Vivian Pope

IV.3 Objective Time-Dilation in Orbiting Bodies

A major difference between our phenomenalist interpretation of light and the classical interpretation is as follows. In the classical interpretation, light travels from a source by analogy with sound. In still air, for a sound-source travelling in a circle around the recipient every sound-pulse from the source travels the same distance at the same speed, so that there is no difference between the rate at which the pulses arrive at the receiving centre. The analogy with sound implies that light from a source which is travelling in a circle around the recipient, where the radial distance of the source remains constant, does not undergo any change in pitch. That is to say, the analogy predicts that there is no lateral Doppler-effect relatively to the centre of the motion.

In our phenomenological interpretation, light does not travel in any way that is analogous with sound. For instance, in our Pythagorean time-dilation formulae (1) and (3), the period between the pulses is increased in accordance with the distance s over which a body travels. That distance is not measured relatively to the central observer, specifically; it is measured relatively to the source of the measure s, which may be anywhere in the observer's extrapolated reference-frame. That is to say, the squaring, in the formulae, of that distance measure s makes the time-dilation uniformly positive, regardless of whether s itself is positive or negative. In other words, in contrast to the analogy with sound, the time-stretching of the pulses (the relativistic Doppler effect) is the same for a travelling-distance s independently of its direction in the reference-frame.

The measure, then, of the periods of pulses from a light-source in a circular orbit around the observer is not from that source to the centre, as it is with sound. It is the measure of the period s/c between points in the observer-frame where successive pulses appear, as though the observer were located at every point in his observational frame. This means to say that between one light-pulse and the next, the source has moved a distance s around the circle, which stretches the period s/c between the pulses in a way that does not occur with sound. The situation may be summed up by saying that whereas with sound all sounds from a circularly travelling source are received in parallel, in the case of light, all observations of events in motion trajectories are in series.

Bearing this in mind, let us look again at our earlier formula for orbital angular momentum, viz.:

L = mvr = GmM/v \quad (4)

Now it is to be borne in mind that although this appears identical to the standard Newtonian formula there is, in fact, a vital difference. Not only is G in the formula not a constant (see above, Ch. III) but also, like all observational velocities, the velocity v is non-Newtonian – that is, it is the same relativistic v, limited to c, that appears in our time-dilation formula, namely:

t_R = $\frac{t}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} \quad (3)

That being the case, from (4) we have

v^2 = GM/r \quad (13)

and substituting this equivalent expression for $v^2$ in (3) then produces

t_R = $\frac{t}{\sqrt{1 - GM/rc^2}} \quad (14)

In this simple and straightforward way, we move from a time-dilation formula for rectilinear motion, as expressed in (3), to a time-dilation formula for orbital motion as expressed in (14). As can be seen, this is without any reference whatsoever to the usual theories of 'gravitation', etc. The substitution of $GM/r$ for $v^2$ in (13) expresses, purely syllogistically, time-dilation in terms purely of potential energy. This supports our reasoning in Chapter III which makes Newton's 'gravitational' hypothesis completely redundant.

In contrast to the classical 'gravitational potential' of Newton, the sign of the potential energy in (14) is positive. That is to say, the zero of reference for that angular momentum potential, hence the objective zero of time-dilation, lies at the greatest distance from the centre of the orbit, that is, in deep space, whereas for the so-called 'gravitational' potential, the zero lies at the centre of radius – that is, nearest to the central mass M.

Formula (14) therefore predicts that in the case of simple circular orbits there will be a time-dilation, hence a slowing of clocks in the orbits of artificial satellites such as that of the American Global Positioning System (GPS) and the Russian equivalent, GLONASS. This, it must be stressed, is only for ideally circular orbits or for orbits of unit eccentricity (that is, a straight up-and-down motion of a body with respect to M). This is confirmed by the Pound-Rebka experiment, in which clocks placed at different levels in a lift-shaft were found to run slower as they were placed lower in the shaft. Both these situations have been expressly confirmed in discussions with those who are expert in these experimental matters.1

Now the reader will, surely, have already intuited that there is a consequence of this deduction which sets it apart from Einstein's theory. In standard Special Relativity, all motion is relative to the observer and all observers are equivalent as zeros of reference for relative motion, regardless of whatever might be their state of motion or rest with respect to one another. This is plainly not the case with orbital time-dilation. In equation (14) the time-dilation is measured relative to the same reference-zero as that with respect to which the orbital motions are measured, namely, the centre of balance or barycentre of the whole orbital angular momentum system. This answers to the 'universal' frame of reference contemplated by Mach as the reference-zero for rotational motion – what astronomers call 'the reference-frame of the fixed stars'. It is the reference-frame with respect to which, as Mach pointed out, all gyroscopes orientate themselves, and all free orbits are 'gyroscopes' in their way.

This supports and is supported by the fact that if differences in measures of time-dilation among different observers are to be objective, then there has to be an objective scale of values ranging from zero to an open-ended maximum. One of the drawbacks of Einsteinian Special Relativity is that it provides no such objective scale. This has made it seem to all too many of Einstein's 'Dissident' critics that what Einstein called relative time-dilation was really no more than subjective. However, as we have now seen, orbital time-dilation restores physical objectivity to time-dilation by providing an absolute zero for that scale in the theoretically motionless centre-of-balance of all the various masses and motions in the whole angular momentum system, the frame that has been called the angular momentum 'alpha frame'. Whether the extent of this alpha frame is finite or infinite is a moot point, to be discussed in contexts of astronomy and cosmology.2

IV.4 Solution of the Notorious 'Twins Paradox'

One of the most disconcerting and time-consuming – not to say time-wasting – issues concerning Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity has always been, from its very first publication, a continued squabble over what many of its critics have named 'The Clock Paradox' – or 'Twins Paradox', as it is sometimes called. This dispute is fuelled by what is regarded as the lack of an objective zero for the above-mentioned scale of time-dilations. This makes it difficult to distinguish, objectively, which of any two relatively moving time-systems is time-dilated and which, conversely, is relatively time-contracted.

The 'paradox', as it seems to many people, is that without being able to specify a true zero of motion, we seem to have the absurd consequence that as a result of a relative motion between them each twin ends up both older and younger than the other.

As can be seen, this explanation is given by Einstein in terms of classical straight-line 'inertial' motion. However, in section III we saw that there is no such thing as straight-line free motion, that all truly free motion is naturally orbital, with no in vacuo forces being responsible for those curved trajectories. The nearest we get to that rectilinear, or 'inertial' free motion is a circular orbit of near-infinite angular momentum, hence near-infinite radius. In other words, at great distances from one another, bodies behave much as Galileo and Newton imagined, in what they thought of as a self-sufficient Euclidean space, which physicists (e.g., Einstein) still call 'inertial'.

In that approximately classical 'inertial space', for a pair of non-spinning bodies of equal mass to remain separate from each other they would have to share an angular momentum $mvr = Gm_1 m_2/v$, where v is the speed in the (assumed circular) orbit, with regard to the centre of balance or barycentre of the two masses. With unit measures of $m_1$ and $m_2$ (that is, one kilogram each), and $r = 1$ metre, the orbital speed would be $v = \sqrt{Gm_1 m_2/r}$, which is virtually stationary. This confirms that in this remote region of space, where there are no large masses, the situation is approximately that of Newtonian 'inertial space', in which the laws of motion that apply are (approximately) the ordinary Newtonian laws. It is this situation that is implied by the motions that Einstein describes in his letter.

For this artificial scenario involving near-rectilinear motions of small masses, Einstein's solution, as described in his letter, works perfectly, as one might well expect.4 A more realistic solution, however, is provided by time-dilation in orbiting bodies, where it is plain that the objective reference-zero for the scale of time-dilations is the angular momentum alpha frame, described in the previous section.

As already seen, in equation (14), $GM/r$ is the potential energy of an orbiting body with respect to the zero of deep space, so that the nearer to the centre of the orbit radius a body descends, the greater becomes its potential energy, hence the greater its time-dilation according to that formula. This prediction is experimentally confirmed.5 Any similarity between this and the conclusions of Einstein's General Theory of Relativity is no more than coincidental.

This angular momentum alpha frame, which serves as the objective zero for all free motions and consequent time-dilations answers, as we have said, to the Machian frame of the 'fixed stars'. It is a universally preferred frame of reference, the conspicuous lack of which, in standard relativity, chronically sustains the charge of 'subjectivity'. This universally preferred frame is well described as 'the firmament' insofar as that word derives from the Latin word for the heavens, firmamentum, meaning 'firm', or 'unmoving'.
