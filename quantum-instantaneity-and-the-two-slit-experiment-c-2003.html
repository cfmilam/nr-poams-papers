Quantum Instantaneity and the Two-Slit Experiment

Question: In the Thomas Young two-slit experiment, as POAMS describes it (see publications section on website www.poams.org) resonance involves three things: the source atom, the screen atom and the length of the path between. How can this be if, as POAMS says, there is no distance between the two atoms at the quantum level?

Answer: At the quantum level there is no action between the two atoms. The resonance between them is immediate, reciprocal and proper-time instantaneous. The action that takes place between the source and the screen is not between those resonating atoms. It consists of time- sequences of those instantaneous resonances.

To understand this, think of the 'stills' in a cinematographic film. There is no action between points A and B in a still photograph. The action that the viewer sees takes place with the running of the film. It is then the sequences of these stills that constitute the action scenario.

In POAMS it is the same with the time-sequences of the instantaneous quantum stills. Here, for instance, is the Pythagorean time equation:

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = \sqrt{[(s/c)^2 + t^2]} \cdot *$$

In this equation, t is the proper time of an interaction, s is the distance in observer-space over which the action takes place (i.e., the source and screen), t_R is the time of the interaction in that same observer space, and c is the usual dimensional length-time constant of 300,000 kilometres to the second. (In POAMS this is not a 'velocity' but, as Herman Bondi calls it, a distance-time conversion factor.)

Now it is clear from this equation that when the proper time t of the inter-resonance between the two atoms is zero, the observational or relative time t_R of the action is the delayed time s/c. In other words, the equation shows that at the quantum level, t is to t_R as the 'still' is to the action as the viewer sees it in the running of a movie. What the equation tells us is that s is the distance (in ordinary observer-space) from the source to the screen, t = 0 is the proper-time-instantaneous connection (at the quantum level) between the source-atom and the screen atom, and t_R = s/c is the time of the interaction in the experimental laboratory that takes part in the source-screen resonance in the typical two-slit experiment.
