The Simplest Possible Derivation of Relativity

N. Vivian Pope — 2011

There can be no simpler deduction of relativity than the following. It comes straight from ancient Pythagoras, bypassing all the twentieth century rigmarole about 'electromagnetism', 'ether', Michelson and Morley, the 'twins paradox', etc. This is achieved simply by adding one natural dimension to the usual three in Pythagoras' theorem. That theorem then becomes four dimensional. Adding to this the fact discovered by Römer in 1676, that observational distances in space are also times in the ratio of units $c$ transforms the usual geometrical equation into a geometro-temporal equation with all the variables measured in seconds. This Pythagorean time-equation is:

$$t_R = \sqrt{(s/c)^2 + t^2} \tag{1}$$

In this equation, $s/c$ is the observational distance in seconds travelled by a body in uniform motion as seen by an observer, $t$ is the time in seconds seen by that same observer to be registered by that body in travelling the distance $s$ in metres, and $t_R$ is the temporal resultant measured in seconds by the same observer at the point of observation. (Note that all these dimensions are relative to the observer – the same observer in any particular instance.)

Here is a geometrical depiction of that Pythagorean time formula (see Fig. 1) as applied to a simple out-and-back motion of a body from O to P and back to O.

Fig. 1: The Pythagorean Time-Triangle. The outward leg has components $s/c$ and $t$, with resultant $t_R$; the return leg has components $-s/c$ and $t$, with the same resultant $t_R$. The total round-trip time is $2t_R$.

Note that the squaring of the distance term $s$ in (1) makes it uniformly positive, which means that the body travelling from O to P and back to O is as much time-dilated on the way back as on the way out, so that his time overall relative to O is simply:

$$\sqrt{(s/c)^2 + (-s/c)^2 + 2t^2} = 2t_R \tag{2}$$

In other words, right at the root of this Pythagorean relativity there is no question of any 'clock paradox' or 'twins paradox'. It also means, by that same token, that the time-dilation is independent of the direction of motion. Note also that there is no troublesome concept of the 'speed of light relative to the vacuum' as in Einstein's Second Axiom of Special Relativity. (In these Pythagorean equations, $c$ is a dimensional constant, not a velocity.)

Assuming that the two parties to this exercise are twins, the question as to which of them at the finish of the out-and-back motion ends up the older and which the younger, depends on which twin takes the reversing action (at the apex of the triangle in the figure). There is therefore no paradoxical ambiguity implied by this simple relativistic formalism since it is obvious that the greatest dilation (slowing or stretching) of the living time is undergone by the out-and-back observer. In short, it is the twin who has taken the reversing action who ends-up the younger of the two.

This embarrassingly simple, purely geometrical derivation of relativity makes child's play of that theory, as compared with the notoriously abstruse mathematical and electrodynamical version of Einstein's. As for its mathematical content, this Pythagorean derivation is scarcely beyond the level of ordinary schoolboy geometry. Its premises are no more than those of ordinary observational perspective in which distance is extrapolated from optical and other sensory information in the manner described by the early twentieth century physics philosopher, Ernst Mach. Yet on this simplest of foundations is built a whole New Physics which is obtained by following the implications of these premises to their own strictly logical conclusion without any further theoretical intervention.

Should it be doubted that this simple derivation gives the same quantitative result as Einstein's, all we need to do is to express the distance $s$ in these equations as $v t_R$, where $v$ is the velocity of a body relative to the observer of its motion. With this substitution of $v t_R$ for $s$ in (1) and simplifying the result we immediately obtain the typical Einsteinian formula:

$$t_R = \frac{t}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} \tag{3}$$

which is now the Pythagorean purely geometrical formula for time-dilation expressed in terms of motion.

The full implications of this unique derivation and the history of its fifty-years development are as described on the following websites: www.vivpope.co.uk; www.poams.org; www.vivpope.org.

This derivation was the 'Eureka!' inspiration of a young telephone lineman working in the hills and valleys of Wales. This was in 1954, when he was recuperating from a climbing accident at work. As his wife vividly recalls, he dashed in from the garden where he had been dabbling with some drawings shouting 'Mary, I've just discovered the length of a second!'

The effect of this innocent seminal discovery on that man's life has been profound. What was the greatest surprise to both him and his wife was the acrimony it produced when he attempted to publish it. He lost his job as a lineman and became a pariah in general, accused as being on an ego trip in a bid to become 'another Einstein'. In the universities, later, he was described as the 'sh**-stirring Welshman' and his attempts at publication were rejected as a 'dangerous heresy'.

Somehow, in spite of all this, that erstwhile lineman has now in his eighties become an honorary Professor of Relativistic Quantum Physics and member of the Scientific Advisory Board of the International Institute of Theoretical Physics and Mathematics, Prato, Italy. Other authorities who once rejected him are only now beginning to agree with him and the numbers of his detractors are gradually diminishing.

However, in all of those fifty years since his completely innocent Eureka! experience, scarcely a day passes without his contemplating, in chronic amazement, how such a simple, guileless discovery, motivated by nothing but sheer natural curiosity, could upset people so irrationally, and generate such intense acrimony. However, as it is said, 'If you've gone through life upsetting no-one, then it's certain that you have achieved nothing.'

Viv Pope, 30/10/2011
