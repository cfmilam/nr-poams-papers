The Pioneer Spin Omission In Science News, 1978, a team of planetary scientists and physicists at Los Alamos National Laboratory reported, that they had identified ‘a tiny, unexplained sunward acceleration in the motions of the Pioneer 10, 11 and Ulysses spacecraft’. This, they said, suggests that there is ‘some relevant physics that's been overlooked in trying to explain the motions of bodies in the universe.’ It seems, writes Michael Nieto, that there is ‘some systematic effect associated with the spacecraft; some subtle effect associated with our tracking systems, which would be important to know for space navigation; or some manifestation of 'new physics.' Nor has this situation changed significantly at this present time of writing. There can be no anomalies as such in nature. The ‘anomaly therefore has to lie in the ‘gravitational’ theory that NASA employs in tracking its space-probes. As they report ‘Newton's laws of gravity alone – with the sun providing the dominant gravitational force – are good enough for NASA to send spacecraft on planetary rendezvous with near-pinpoint precision. But the anomalous motions of these spacecraft are so small that the researchers had to consider numerous possible causes: perturbations from the gravitational attraction of planets and smaller bodies in the solar system … etc.’ Now we see, straight away, that there is an anomaly associated with this ‘gravitational theory’ of Newton’s that NASA employs for tracking their spaceprobes. This is that Newtonian ‘gravity’ takes no account of spin in an orbiting body. All NASA’s space-probes spin, as they have to in order to maintain their orientation with respect to earth. This is in accordance with the law of angular momentum conservation which ensures the constancy throughout its journey thereafter of not only the angle between its spin vector and that of its orbit but also the magnitude of the angular momentum imparted to the probe at the start of its motion, Something else which that conservation law tacitly demands is that the total amount of angular momentum of an orbiting body must include any spin angular momentum the body might have, which means that for an orbiting body that spins. the more there is of spin angular momentum the less there has to be of orbital angular momentum; and the less there is of orbital angular momentum the smaller is the orbit radius. From this it is perfectly predictable that a spinning space-probe must orbit nearer to its countermass – the sun, say – than if it were not spinning. In short, in its characteristic neglect of the spin of an orbiting body, Newtonian ‘gravity theory’ fails to meet the needs of space-age technology. This is why it generates anomalies such as NASA’s Pioneer anomaly and the allied anomaly of the so-called ‘missing mass’. If, as in extant physics, the spins of space probes and other orbiting bodies such as planets stars and spiral galaxies – indeed, just about everything in the universe – are ignored then what else can we expect but that we need a ‘new physics’ NASA are correct in that. So, what sort of ‘new physics; do we need? What else but one which includes the spin angular momentum together with the orbital angular momentum in the total angular momentum of orbiting bodies. Does such a ‘new physics’ exist or does one have to be constructed from scratch? Fortunately such a one already exists.
