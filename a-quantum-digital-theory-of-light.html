A Quantum Digital Theory of Light

Vigier Conference, York University, Toronto

A Quantum Digital Theory of Light

N. V. Pope

ABSTRACT

In the Theory of Relativity, anything travelling at the speed of light registers no intrinsic (i.e., proper) time nor distance in getting from A to B. In that same theory a photon is a quantum of energetic interaction between one atom and another, travelling at precisely that speed, c. So, logically, as Gilbert Lewis pointed out in 1928, according to Relativity, a photon, in getting from A to B takes no time and covers no distance.

In Quantum Theory, also, there can be no intrinsic time nor distance in a quantum interaction. That interaction has to be instantaneous, because any pause between its emission and absorption would break the law of conservation of action, which forbids that the interaction can be held in suspension for the merest instant, let alone for astronomical periods of time. Moreover, if the quantum is thought of as truly integral and irreducible, then it cannot be supposed to have energy to spare for en route detection between an emitting and an absorbing atom. In that case, putting a detector between the emitter-atom and the would-be first absorber merely creates an alternative absorber ... and so on. So in that theoretical context there can be no question of our being able to detect or measure the progress of a quantum from A to B in vacuo.

Logically, then, Relativity and Quantum Theory are in perfect accord, not only with each other but also with the law of conservation and with Newton's third law of action and reaction (which, like the conservation law, brooks no time-delay). It would therefore appear that for the first time in our history we have the situation in science where everything 'gels'. Yet our accustomed way of thinking about space somehow manages to make this all-attested spatially immediate and instantaneous quantum interaction between distance-separated atoms seem a mystery.

The answer we propose is to switch, at the quantum level of physics, from the usual 'analog' or 'realistic' mode of conceptualisation into a purely 'digital' one. In other words, we argue that at that ultimate datum-level of all observational/instrumental perception there can be no real, universally self-extended 'vacuum' in which light travels, as waves, or particles, 'real', 'virtual' or whatever. Empirically speaking, all we have at that datum-level of physics are those purely stochastic or probabilistic (in any case, informational) quantum events that in optical contexts we rename phota (singular: photum). We argue that it is out of purely informational patterns and sequences of these data that the dimensions of physical space and time (space-time) and all that is described in those terms are relativistically projected – including the so-called 'propagation' and 'bending' of light.

In this way we see no real conflict between the 'Realistic' and the 'Operational' (or 'Positivist') approaches to theoretical physics, as followed, respectively, by Einstein, Bohm and Vigier on the one hand and the Bohr, or Copenhagen, group on the other. Instead of seeing 'Realism' and 'Positivism' as competing world-views we see the need for a proper distinction and separation to be maintained between two radically different uses of language applied to two radically different concept-categories. To think of 'photons' as objects 'travelling' in the space that is relativistically projected out of those ultimate bits of data is thus, we propose, the logical category mistake which, in current theoretical physics, is the source of so many unnecessary partisan conflicts, conceptual conundrums and paradoxes.

Ladies and Gentlemen,

This talk could be unpopular. This is because it has to do with the Philosophy of physics rather than with Physics as such, and many physicists believe that philosophy has nothing to do with physics. Yet there are two clearly distinguishable philosophical approaches to theoretical physics. One is the 'Realist' approach, as followed by Professors Vigier, Einstein and Bohm and the other is the 'Positivist' approach, of the Copenhagen group, after Professor Niels Bohr. Nature, of course, knows no such distinction. So we – that is, my colleague Dr. Anthony Osborne and myself – at Keele University, in England, invite you to consider a third philosophical alternative, a whole new paradigm, in effect. This is to dispense with both 'Realism' and 'Positivism' as competing world-views and follow an entirely neutral course of theoretical enquiry which has been called Normal Realism. The essence of this is as follows.

Now a paradigm, may I remind you, is a kind of thought-template through which the information supplied by our senses is, so to speak, stencilled onto our minds. Clearly defined paradigms are like clean-cut templates whose effectiveness if used on their own is lost if used jumbled together. Take, for instance, the classical paradigm of absolute space and time. For some people this is a subliminal background stencil over which they unconsciously superimpose any suggested alternative. For instance, some see relativity as a mere addendum to our accustomed, Newtonian way of thinking about space and time. Even Einstein did! He talked about relativity but took it as axiomatic that light travels in the classical vacuum at a finite and constant speed c. A clear-cut, no less relativistic alternative, however, is to think of light, not as something travelling inscrutably in a vacuum but as simply what we see. So, instead of thinking of light as 'travelling in space' we may quite logically and legitimately think of the space and the travelling as in the light – in the way that all the space and action of a TV drama are in the informational patterns and sequences of bits of illumination on a flat or panoramic screen.

Now everyone here knows that light is quantised, that it consists of discrete bits of action, or energy-multiplied-by-time, called photons. However, because the word 'photon' suggests a space-travelling particle, it would be more appropriate for present purposes for it to be replaced by a newly-coined word, photum, which is more economically suggestive of a pure optical event.

A photum, then, is an irreducible quantum of luminosity, an informational element of the light-patterns we ordinarily see with our eyes and/or by means of optical instruments. The energy a photum manifests, directly or instrumentally is, in the usual way, hν, where ν is the reciprocal of the photum's characteristic spectral period. Patterns of co-occurring photo are the objects of ordinary perception. The comparative sizes and intensities of these patterns, plus their angular features such as perspective and parallax are the information out of which we 'video-project', as it were, the three dimensions of ordinary space, as shown in this diagram.

Let us, then, call those other material centres observicles. Now for an object or observicle to change its distance, relatively to any such centre of reference, some measure of time has to pass relatively to that centre. So a change in any one or any combination of the three spatial measures is also a time-measure relative to that centre. We can restate, then, the fact, discovered by Olaus Roemer, that all distance-measures are also time-measures in the constant ratio of units c. This constant, which Einstein interpreted as 'the speed of light in vacuo' is thus reinterpreted in Herman Bondi's much simpler way as a conversion-factor for interconverting optical distance-measures in metres into time-measures in seconds. That is, every metre an object travels away from or towards an observicle adds or subtracts 3.3 nanoseconds of time to its duration relative to that centre (an effect well-known to radar people as the Doppler effect).

Let us now turn to the quantum aspect of light? Sixty-nine years ago, a chemist called Gilbert Lewis discovered that if you say, as Einstein did, that light travels, then a strange paradox arises. So the proper-time of any light-interaction, over any distance whatsoever, is nil – which means that far from travelling at 'the finite speed c', light-interaction is intrinsically instantaneous. As Lewis put it, in a one-way quantum light-interaction the emitter and absorber are literally touching, so that the energy is transferred precisely in accordance with Newton's third law of equal and opposite (contiguous) reaction.

So there can be no real vacuum for light-quanta to 'travel in'. The emission and absorption of a quantum are not two separate events. They are one and the same event – which fits precisely what we have said, that it is not that light travels in space but that the space is in the light.

Space, then, according to Normal Realism is no 'primordial vacuum'. It is a relativistic information-projection which we who work on this in England call blocspace. The word 'B-L-O-C', here, is taken from the French, which means roughly what is meant in English by 'B-L-O-C-K' but which means, in international parlance, an inter-communicational whole made up of separate, independent and autonomous entities. This very nicely describes our physical observicles with their polar or observocentric spaces inter-projected from informational transactions with one another. These polar (or perspectival) and Cartesian (or classical) dimensions of blocspace are the two ordinary intertransformable aspects of what commonsense calls real space. The old dualistic distinction between these aspects, which makes the one 'real' and the other 'subjective' therefore disappears, leaving the two to be regarded as equally and inseparably real. This merging of subjective and objective within the concept of the relative enables us to revise our ordinary commonsense conceptions of space to include the empirical knowledge that distances in that space are also times in the ratio of 3.3 nanoseconds to the metre and exclude only the metaphysic of a self-sufficient vacuum through which the information on which all this is based has to travel.

In blocspace, then, all quantum interactions are immediate and instantaneous, in accordance with Newton's third law and the laws of overall conservation. For example, in observing a distant star, that object and the observer (or observing instrument) share the same immediate and reciprocal quantum 'jolt'. Of course, this contact is not the sort the Greeks imagined, with everything perpetually jammed together in what they conceived as the 'Eleatic One'. Nor do we take the opposite, Democritan view of that same unity exploded into bits and spread out in a self-sufficient and primordial 'void', connected together by spatially diluted intermediaries such as 'ether' or 'gravitation'. Like the Eleatic

contacts, ours are of the direct, contiguous kind, which eschew any conception of a void, except that instead of forming a perpetual plenum, our Eleatic-style contacts are piecemeal and sporadic – that is, discrete and indeterminate – in a word, purely probabilistic. This does not mean, absurdly, that atoms randomly skip across space to touch one another and then return to their places, as in some superluminal barn-dance. To think like that would be to superimpose thought-templates in the philosophically forbidden way. To avoid that absurdity is to see it all the other way around; that is, simply to think of the distances between bodies as an informational projection out of those quantum touchings, not as some primordial, pre-extended 'void'.

Blocspace, then, is no continuous vacuum, field or plenum. It is a pure probability potential for instantaneous quantum contact in which, as in the Random Access Memory (RAM) of a computer, all informational elements are, potentially, equally close. It is a phenomenalistic version, one might say, of what David Bohm called 'quantum potential' but without Bohm's 'hidden variables' and 'implicate' continuum – and (it has to be said) without his now out-dated philosophical distinction between 'Realism' and 'Positivism'.

In our alternative paradigm, then, there is no continuous, overall synchrony of existence. Other than in those sporadic and indeterminate, instantaneous quantum touchings, each thing and each part of each thing exists (if that is the word) in isolation so profound that it can scarcely even be called 'locality'. So any question of whata fundamental particle is or does in between any two of its physical manifestations is meaningless. This explains why those particles are undetectable between source and screen in 'interference' experiments of the sort discussed by people like Franco Selleri and Dipanka Home.

On this analysis, then, there is no longer any classically conceived 'Universe' but only, at best, a communally interprojected relativistic manifold, or pluriverse, with three statistical Cartesian dimensions and a purely statistical GMT. In that information-projected, super fine-grained, interactive 'hologram' we call Nature the concept of 'waves' or 'virtual particles' in the vacuum', as the conveyor of information from one place to another becomes redundant and is replaced by that of pure probability-functions of Lewisian, 'quantum touchings'. These propagate kinematically (as in cinematography) among normally distributed blocspace observicles with the mathematical 'wave-like' characteristics described by de Broglie and Schrödinger. And since the distances separating these observicles are also times in Bondi's ratio of units c, those probability-waves travel in information-projected blocspace at the finite and constant speed c, with all those features of refraction, diffraction and so on known to the science of optics. So light does travel, but kinematically, not mechanically – that is, as the purely sequential propagation of instantaneous information-transactions between observicles not as streams of space-travelling photons.

Now there is no contradiction in saying that light-quanta (indeed all quanta) are instantaneous and that waves of them travel at the finite speed c. In all wave-type phenomena, the way the wave-elements move or change and the way the wave moves are entirely different. In a water-wave, for instance, the water molecules describe little circular motions about a fixed point, while the wave travels in an entirely different way,

along the surface with a uniform longitudinal speed. The fact, then, that the elements of a light-wave are instantaneous quantum touchings between distance-separated objects, and that informational sequences of these propagate in holographic blocspace at a finite kinematical speed c presents no paradox whatsoever. Nor is there any so-called 'EPR paradox' in the fact that there are 'spooky superluminal actions-at-distance' maintaining the overall balance of things ahead of the finite speed of light. Of course there are superluminal influences. There have to be, by the laws of physics, according to which all objects have to maintain some sort of direct balancing influence on one another. This 'action-at-a-distance' is provided by those instantaneously shared quantum-interactions which I have renamed phota. In terms of these phota there is an instantaneous quantum potential linking things together holistically – not continuously, in the manner of the classically conceived 'void', 'field' or 'æther' but dis-continuously; that is to say, discretely, or digitally (informationally), on a strictly conserved and immediate, one-for-one basis of quantum exchange.

Now, in thirty minutes I can scarcely explain to you all the various ins-and-outs of this new and radical phenomenological synthesis of relativity and quantum theory. All I can say is that in-depth discussions over many years and in many places have prepared it well for its legitimate appearance on the scientific scene – although the barrier of prejudice which inevitably faces any 'upstart' new theory largely remains to be surmounted. (A handout giving all the various references will be made available at the end of this talk.). I can summarise the theory, however, by saying that it incorporates the four following design-elements: i) phenomenalism in the style of the later, anti-Positivistic works of Ludwig Wittgenstein and Alfred Ayer; ii) Gilbert Lewis's description of quantum touching, iii) Herman Bondi's interpretation of c as a distance-time conversion-factor instead of Einstein's 'velocity of light' and, iv) Occam's Razor, which bids us, regardless of convention, to select the simpler of any two theoretical alternatives. We should always be suspicious of convention, anyway. Once we had angels as travelling intermediaries between heaven and earth. Now, we have photons as travelling intermediaries between things and our perceptions of them. The philosopher John Locke, when presented with this view of light as 'travelling globules', declared that it was as though we were told that light was 'nothing but a company of little tennis-balls which fairies all day long struck with rackets against men's foreheads'. I'm with Locke on that! Like all empiricists, I'm all for abandoning these mystical intermediaries and bringing reality right 'down to earth'. So far as light is concerned, this means seeing things directly, as informational complexes of ordinary light and shade, out of which we communally and holographically (in any event, relativistically) 'video-project' the ordinary everyday world. The fact that these complexes analyse-out, ultimately, to quantum light-pixels or phota is hardly inconceivable nowadays, seeing how we know how all the space, time and action of a video drama is projected out of informational sequences of optical blips.

Besides, what alternative is there but to embrace the current standard, rationalised view of light as made up of 'photons' travelling inscrutably and telepathically at the same speed (can you credit!) with respect to all differently moving observers in an infinitely extended and self-sufficient nothing, or 'void'. Frankly, I find angels and fairies easier to believe in than that! Thank you.]
