Traditional phenomenalism Unfortunately, as an approach to science, phenomenalism has been mostly confined to the science of psychology, with the emphasis placed on introspective modes of perception rather than on objective physics. This is due to the tradition of phenomenalism being shot through with certain fallacies and omissions that are rer.eaied only in retrospect. First is the fallacy in the legacy bequeathed to phenomenalism by one of the foremost in the line of the 'English Empiricists'. This rvas George Berkeley (1685-1753), whose famous dictum on which, for all too long, phenomenalism rvas based, was 's.rse is percipi' (to be is to be perceived). Regrettably, this implied, lor critics such as Sarnuel Johnson, that nothing exists except in the mind of the observer, the view called solipsism. This kept phenomenalism in a virtual strarghtjacket of alleged 'subjective idealism' or 'psychologism' for nigh on two centuries until the fallacy in that dictum was revealed in 1903 by the Cambridge philosopher, G.
