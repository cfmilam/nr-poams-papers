Angular momentum conservation on the atomic level Other challenges to POAMS have been on the question of angular momentum conservation at the quantum level, involving distant interactions between atoms. The nub of POAMS's replies to these challenges is as follows. Let us, then remind ourselves of what, in POAMS, universality and instantaneity of angular momentum mean on the ultimate microphenomenal level; that is, between atoms in what are commonly knor,vh ps light-inferactions, which take place in irreducible, quantum units of ft and corr-eliiibns in terms of hl2n.

Now another thing to be kept firrfily in mii{d, if confusion is to be studiously avoided, is that in POAMS, from its Norfnal Realist stance in the phenomenalist tradition of Mach, there are no atoms to be thought of as absolutely self-sufficient things-in-themselves in the way Democritus imagined. In POAMS, these so-called 'atoms' are no more nor less than ultimate ciphers. The dimensions of mass, size, motion and so on that we attribute to them are not properties possessed by the 'atoms themselves'; they are properties originating in the actual, observable, physical world, from which, in experiments with gtoss substances and so on, our conceptions of those 'atoms' and their assumed 'properties' originate. In the Machian holism of POAMS, therefore, atoms do not exist isolatedly and in themselves but only in relation to one another as terminals of reduction from whole macrophysical complexes. In these holistic relations, the atoms sigrufu as discrete angular momentum quanta whose positions and motions are indescribable other than in their interactions with one another in the context of some large-scale phenomenon in which they configure (such as a particle in a laboratory, or some body of matter manifest in an observatory or some other instrument-system). To talk about what an atom is or does 'on its own' or in relation to another atom, in the absence of some such macrophysical context is just another absurdity so far as POAMS is concerned. From this it follows that in POAMS, when we talk about atoms 'interacting at a distance' we are talking, implicitly, about distances on the observational macroscale.

On the atomic level there are no such distances. This is because distance is defined, basically and essentially, in the only way that is available to astronomers, that is, in terms of statistical numbers of quantum illuminations (comparative brightnesses) angular measures, range measures and - since R<jmer - time measures. From a single quantum or observational pixel, in the eye or soine other instrument, nothing can be gleaned as to whether it originated in some mote a centimetre from the eye or in some far-off galaxy. POAMS publications have been fulsome on that. In any talk, then, about 'atoms interacting at a distance', it is to be understood, consistently throughout, that the 'distance' referred to is the macroscopic distance between bodies in which the atoms are located, not between the atoms themselves. To think and talk, then, in the way that presumes to see individual atoms in a superhuman way, behind all their relational manifestations, existing, separated and interacting in such a way as only 'God' might be supposed to see, is the view called reductionism or reductive realism, which POAMS categorically rejects. The publications of POAMS have been explicit on this also. The intellectual challenge, then, that POAMS presents is that understanding it entails keeping all these distinctions firmly in mind and applying them consistently in the context of the lateral-thinking alternative that POAMS proposes - that is, without one's mind snapping back continually to older and altogether incommensurable, habits of thinking.
