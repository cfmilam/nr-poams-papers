The Pope-Osborne Cone-Model of Observer Space-Time

N. Vivian Pope — 1979

British MENSA, January 1979, reports: 'There is a fierce argument raging in Quark on the subject of a new interpretation of relativity which rejects the concept of light travelling in vacuo.' The centre of this controversy is a Cone-Model depiction of observational space-time dimensions, expressing a new advance on the phenomenalistic (positivistic) approach to physical science.

This is based on a philosophy called Normal Realism, developed at University College of North Wales, Bangor, during the late sixties and early seventies. Its aim, as someone has put it, is to 'write back the observer into the physical equations'. It therefore has implications not only for scientists but also for philosophers of science and sociologists. It incorporates relativity and the quantum in its inception.

In that new unified and holistic way of thinking, all physical events are fundamentally observational events, all ultimately quantised and purely sequential relative to an observer, $O$, with no question of mechanical or any other determinate interaction between them. There is thus, at the basic quantum-informational level, no SPACE in the sense of the classical self-sufficient void, but only TIME, extrapolated by $O$ from the occurrences of observational events. Unlike classical time, however, this observational time has not just one but at least FOUR dimensions, all measured in the same units (e.g., seconds). The four dimensions are:

  1. Duration, as measured between events like $O$'s pulse-beats or clock-ticks — or by clocks remaining in the relative proximity of $O$.
  2. Range, as measured by the distance-time $s/c$ (where $s$ is the distance in metres and $c$ the ratio of conventional metres to seconds) of events in $O$'s observational sphere.
  3. Azimuth (angular dimension, left-right) of events in $O$'s observational sphere.
  4. Elevation (angular dimension, up-down) of events in $O$'s observational sphere.

The resultant of dimensions (1) and (2) is $O$'s Relative Time, or observational time $t_R$, as given by the Pythagorean formula:

$$t_R = \sqrt{t^2 + (s/c)^2}$$

where $t$ is the proper-time (duration) and $s/c$ is the distance-time (range). When depicted as a spatial model in three dimensions (with the two angular dimensions represented as one), this generates a cone in which $O$'s duration dimension runs along the axis and the range dimension opens outwards from $O$. This is the Pope-Osborne Cone-Model of observer space-time.

The surface of the cone represents the locus of all events whose proper-time $t = 0$ — that is, events connected to $O$ by light (for which the proper-time is zero). For these events, $t_R = s/c$, which is the conventional 'light-travel time'. But in the Cone-Model, nothing has 'travelled'; the cone-surface simply represents the relationship between distance and time-delay for instantaneous quantum events.

Inside the cone are events with $t > 0$ — that is, events connected to $O$ by processes slower than light. Outside the cone are events that are not directly connected to $O$ by any single quantum interaction.

The Cone-Model thus provides a visual representation of the unified relativistic and quantum structure of observational space-time, in which the distinction between 'local' (time-delayed) and 'non-local' (instantaneous) interactions becomes transparent. ■
