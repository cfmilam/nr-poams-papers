The Pioneer Anomaly: A Dissident Perspective

N. Vivian Pope & Anthony D. Osborne

The data from the two Pioneer space-probes, 10 and 11, shows an anomalous deceleration in the radial speed of those probes relative to the Earth of about $8.5 \times 10^{-8}$ cm/sec². This so-called 'Pioneer Anomaly' has occupied a great deal of attention in the scientific community, with a variety of possible explanations being offered, including those involving new physics.

In this paper we present an explanation of the Pioneer Anomaly based on the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS). According to POAMS, all free motion is naturally orbital and angular momentum is holistically conserved. A freely moving body carries with it both orbital angular momentum (due to its motion relative to some centre of mass) and spin angular momentum (due to its own rotation). The total angular momentum is the combination of these two components.

The Pioneer probes were set spinning at launch to maintain their stability. In POAMS, this spin angular momentum contributes to the total angular momentum budget of the probe. The key insight is that the spin angular momentum of the probe effectively reduces its orbital angular momentum relative to the Sun, thereby reducing the radius of its orbit compared to what would be predicted by purely orbital considerations. This produces an apparent anomalous deceleration when the probe's motion is measured radially.

The magnitude of this effect depends on the ratio of the probe's spin angular momentum to its orbital angular momentum. For the Pioneer probes, with their known spin rates and masses, this ratio produces a radial deceleration of the correct order of magnitude to explain the observed anomaly.

In summary, the POAMS explanation of the Pioneer Anomaly requires no new physics beyond the postulate that angular momentum is holistically conserved and that all motion is naturally orbital. The anomaly arises naturally from the interplay between the spin and orbital angular momentum components of the spinning probe. ■
