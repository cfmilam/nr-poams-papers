Action-at-a-Distance - Oct. 1996

ACTION-AT-A-DISTANCE

What is the most fundamental unsolved problem of modern physics? Is it the problem of how the universe began, whether it is in a steady state or expanding or how it is constructed in terms of its basic elements? These are deep questions. But an even deeper question which needs to be answered before those others can be even considered, is that of whether, in the context of Special Relativity, there is a universe at all in any meaningful sense of the word (that is, as a unified, integrated system) or whether what we imagine to be 'the universe' is no more than a chaos of chance interactions between otherwise purely localised and dissociate bits and pieces.

The problem, in other words, is that of ACTION-AT-A-DISTANCE. Are distance-separated bodies connected or unconnected? According to Einstein, nothing can travel faster than light. But if so, then no physical connection between bodies can take place in less time than it takes for light to travel between them at the finite speed $c$. This implies that there can be no pre-extended medium for the conveyance of light-waves, that the waves have to make their own medium as they travel through space, which is absurd. Moreover, there is the physical connection between freely orbiting bodies called angular momentum which, in any closed system, is a conserved quantity. But how can the angular momentum of, say, a galaxy be conserved if the ends of the moment-arms on which its stars and so on balance are separated in the Einsteinian way by thousands of light-years? Thus, when one of a pair of objects moves, the balancing influence on the other may arrive when the first body is no longer there! Yet another absurd consequence of this same 'Einstein separation' is its splitting of the definitively unsplittable quanta into widely space-time- separated beginnings and ends. This is in complete contradiction to quantum theory, where it is axiomatic that all interactions between bodies are immediately consummated in complete and irreducible (i.e., integral) units of Planck's constant $h$. So how can the integrity of a quantum interaction possibly be maintained if its beginning and end are perhaps millennia apart?

Einstein's way of solving that problem was to think of those quantum interactions as mediated by 'photons'. These, to borrow a picturesque metaphor coined by Tom Phipps (Jr.) are like little 'Dick Whittingtons' that pack their bags full of action at the emitter and then set out, to travel inscrutably through the vacuum at the speed of light to make their fortune at some distant, purely prospective absorber. Not surprisingly, this creates all sorts of problems and paradoxes, as in the notorious Thomas Young two-slit experiment in which, for light-quanta to behave in that way, those 'photons' would have to be telepathic. For instance, the two-slit experiment makes it plain that in the conduction of light or particle-beams between source and screen, the source and screen are connected throughout. That is to say, between the source and some point on the screen, the paths for those energies, via the two slits, measured in positive and negative half-lengths of the unit $hc/e$ for light or $h/mv$ for particles, interfere as complete and instantaneous geometrical wholes to create the typical fringe-pattern of quantum scintillations or particle-strikes on the screen. So when some alternative absorber (i.e., a detector) is placed at any point in one or the other of those two paths, whether before or after the slits, that path's geometrical capacity for interference is cancelled, leaving all the source-screen interactions to take place via the one remaining path. This makes the experiment, in effect, a one-slit experiment in which, typically, there is no pattern of interference. The 'Great Mystery' of the two-slit phenomenon arises only when we

However, quantum integrity demands that every quantum transition is a complete, irreducible and therefore consummate interaction. That is to say, it cannot 'start out', so to speak, without being immediately and instantaneously assured of an 'arrival'. But if that arrival is delayed due to the assumed 'travelling-time of the photon', then in order to consummate that interaction, either those 'photons' have to be telepathic, as we say, or there has to be some 'spooky superluminal' precognitive wave-like influence 'guiding' them to their destinations, as many physicists currently believe. (Ref. Relevant Publications, No. 23.)

However, in 1926, in an article in Nature [ G.N. Lewis, 'Light Waves and Corpuscles' 117 p.256] Gilbert Lewis reported an irrefutable mathematical consequence of Einstein's theory which Einstein seems never fully to have realised. This is that at the finite speed c, a quantum of light-interaction registers, in itself, no time and no distance between the emitter and absorber. (That is to say, its intrinsic or proper time is zero, so its intrinsic or proper distance, being c times that proper-time, is also zero.) Lewis has thus, in effect, pointed out that in relativity, as in quantum theory, a light-quantum is like any other quantum, an integrated and irreducible, proper-time-instantaneous quantum jump. The quantum interaction is tantamount to what Lewis described as a physical 'touching' between the emitter and the absorber - i.e., in accordance with Newton's law of instantaneous, equal and opposite action and reaction.

But how can that be? How can things apart be also together, in this way? And how can the speed of light be finite while that of its component quanta is infinite? This is the basis of the paradox, called the EPR (Einstein, Podolski and Rosen) paradox, that physicists perennially seek to solve by both speculative and experimental means. There is, however, a simple, purely logical answer. This is to revise the standard interpretation of quantum light-interactions by dispensing with the problematic (and, strictly, unnecessary) Einsteinian way of thinking of them as 'travelling photons' in favour of the Lewisian alternative of thinking of them as being, quite literally, complete and integrated quantum jumps. Further to Lewis' demonstration, we present these jumps as occurring in 'cinematic' sequences, between whole, instantly extended quantum angular momentum configurations, or 'quantum stills'. This is illustrated by the following conceptual model.

The Cinematic Model of Light Propagation

As everyone knows, a movie film is made up of finite-speed sequences of still photographs, or 'stills'. In each of these stills nothing moves, because in them there is, by definition, no motion, only extension - that is, the instantaneous separation and distribution of objects in the dimensions of those stills. Any changes and/or motions of those objects, at whatever rates and speeds, take place only when the stills are sequenced through the projector.

The motions of film objects therefore have the following characteristics:

  1. The motion is quantised. That is to say, it consists of a complete and irreducible distance-displacement in the dimensions of the frame from one photographic frame or 'still' to the next.
  2. Any displacement of an object from one still, or frame, to another has to be both a distance and a time.
  3. The distance by which the object may be displaced between one still and the next is theoretically infinite.

In cinematic motion, then, there is no pure distance travelled by an object only distance-time. The unreachable upper limit of all cinematic motion is the dimension of all-distance-and-no-time and the lower limit is the orthogonal dimension of all-time-and-no-distance. (See Figure 1). From this it follows that since, in a movie-scenario there is no pure space, only space-time, then those space-lengths or distances have to be converted into time-units. That is to say, if the time is measured in seconds and the distances in metres, then those distances have to be converted into seconds by the use of some conversion-factor k with the dimensions of metres/seconds. (see Figure 2).

A movie scenario is thus a space-time discontinuum; i.e., a space-time which is quantised in time-sequences of instantly extended stills or frames. Now that, but for its quantisation or discretisation, is suggestive of something between the instantaneous space of Newton and the space-time of Einstein. It is plain, however, that the real physical world is nothing like that. We and other real objects are not passive entities in some cosmic film-scenario but are independent, interactive agents.

Nevertheless, at the ultimate quantum level of physical analysis, that is exactly how space, time and motion are manifest – as time-sequences of what might be called quantum stills. These stills are the irreducible units of angular momentum, in units of h/2π, in which particles are automatically paired and balanced on opposite ends of instantaneously extended moment-arms r in the relation mvr = nh/2π. The distances between these particles are then the lengths r of the moment-arms of these angular momenta, which may be conceived as the irreducible 'cinematic' elements of space, and the periods t_ang.mom. of their irreducible cycles may be conceived as the cinematic elements of time – analogous to the imperceptible time that a movie-still remains in the gate of the projector between one shutter-obscuration and the next.

Of course, in a mechanical movie-projector, the imperceptible periods in which the stills occupy the gate of the projector between successive shutter operations are all the same, whereas the similarly irreducible periods of the quantum angular momentum cycles are many and various. Moreover, at the quantum level of physical analysis there is no overall space in which these quantum occurrences take place, as in the hall of a cinema, nor any overall time analogous to that registered by the cinema clock. So there is no question of any two quanta occurring at the same time, or alongside one another. They do not, in any overall sense, coexist or co-occur but are characteristically discrete.

A fallacy, then, is to imagine all these quantum stills (quantum angular momentum systems) forming one great, monolithic angular momentum system, or universe. That is to say, there is no unified and continuous angular momentum substratum. These quantum angular momentum systems are, by definition, connected only sporadically (intransitively). To think of an orbiting particle going round and round its countermass interacting with every other particle at every stage in its cycle is to think of it as losing its angular momentum by spiralling down into the nucleus in a burst of extruded energy, with frequency ending in the far ultraviolet. The fact that this so-called 'ultraviolet catastrophe' does not occur means that in A's quantum orbit around B, A is not, in the same instance, in any similar relation with C; and that in any quantum relation with C it is not, in that same instance, in a similar relation with A or D or any other particle. In other words, the orbits of each paired mass and countermass are complete and irreducible cycles occurring independently of those of other quantum systems – which is what being 'discrete' means. A fallacy also is to assume that when such an angular momentum system changes, that change comes from within, due to processes going on, inscrutably, inside the system. By definition, nothing changes within a quantum of angular momentum. Changes occur only between one such system and another, as when in the one, the paired mass and countermass lose a quantum of their angular momentum in relation to one another, so that their orbits jump inward towards their common centre; and the other system, in response, gains that quantum amount so that its orbits jump correspondingly outward. Such a change constitutes, as it were, a quantum of cinematic sequence (quantum jump) from one quantum still to another. Thus, while the (non-zero) 'gate-time' $t_{\text{ang.mom}}$ of the complete irreducible angular momentum cycle is a quantum of stasis, or space, the similarly irreducible shift from the one angular momentum configuration to another is an element of sequence – i.e., action-time $t_{\text{action}}$. It is in those external interactions, and only in those external interactions, which occur in ultimate units of Planck's action-constant $h$, that those angular momentum elements exist relatively to one another.

We may picture the world, then, at its most microscopic, fine-grained level, as an angular momentum discretum consisting of statistical numbers of discrete cinematic quantum-jumps, involving no more than just one pair of particles at a time, from one such instantly extended pattern of angular momentum (quantum still) to another. On the ordinary physical level a body is a statistical ensemble of these microphysical interactions, so that the comparative permanence of these bodies is like that of Heraclitus' candle-flame, which remains steady while its gaseous constituents are continually and rapidly changing. Moreover, the same principle applies to angular momentum relations on the macrophysical level as on the microphysical level, namely, that the bodies concerned are instantly paired and balanced on the ends of their moment-arms – except, of course, that on the macroscopic level the discreteness and intransitivity of the microphysical angular momentum interrelations is statistically 'ireoned-out'. The only manifestation of quantum discreteness, at the macrophysical level is therefore the random, Brownian motion of the parts of bodies which otherwise remain in a state of dynamic equilibrium with comparatively well-defined positions and trajectories.

Thus we have, on the macrophysical level, the sort of statistical overall interconnectedness of masses that is conventionally called 'gravitational'. (The other 'forces', as explained elsewhere,¹ are angular momentum relations of the same kind but whose length-parameters and so on include all sorts of involutions and convolutions such as, e.g., spin.) Quantised also are all the various angular momentum vector-orientations. In this statistical sense, our concept of space answers to the overall instantaneous 'inertial space' of Newton – except, of course, that being a discretum it is neither geometrically nor temporally continuous and its inertial motion is cyclic not rectilinear. (In finite amounts of angular momentum mvr, r cannot be infinite, hence the motion is automatically closed, or cyclic; and because masses in angular momentum relations are automatically paired there is no need to think of Newton's 'gravitational' and other invisible 'forces' being responsible for the orbital motions of masses).

Since bodies in this quantum discretum remain, throughout, statistically interconnected in that proper-time-instantaneous way, then it follows that when one body moves, that movement instantly and immediately affects all the others – statistically, of course – and is affected by them in turn (i.e., reciprocally). This means that the motion of a macrophysical body is instantly and immediately (and, again statistically) inhibited by the presence of other bodies in the way which, as Mach saw it, explains the phenomenon of inertia.

However, on that same ordinary macrophysical (Heraclitan) level of physics these instantaneous angular-momentum balancing-connections are nothing like the 'stills' of cinematography. At this macrophysical level, 'immersed' as we are in the physics scenario – as opposed to god-like cinemagoers – the only distances and times we may contemplate are those of objects relatively to ourselves and to one another. In that case, for an object or observer O, the time taken for any particle P to travel a distance s is the resultant t_R of the rectangular time-components s/k and t shown in Figure 3. The space-time length of this resultant is, by Pythagoras:

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = [(s/k)^2 + t^2]^{1/2} \tag{1}$$

The relative velocity of that particle is then

$$v = s/t_{\mathrm{R}} \tag{2}$$

from which it follows that

$$s = vt_{\mathrm{R}} \tag{3}$$

Substituting vt_R for s in (1) produces

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = [(vt_{\mathrm{R}}/k)^2 + t^2]^{1/2} \tag{4}$$

whence, by simplification we have

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = t[1 - (v^2/k^2)]^{-1/2} \tag{5}$$

which precisely matches the standard formula for time-dilation in Einsteinian Relativity.

From this it follows that there is a finite limit to these relative velocities v. This can be seen if we express (5) in terms of v, which is

$$v = k[1 - (t^2/t_{\mathrm{R}}^2)]^{-1/2} \tag{6}$$

The limits of this equation lie between v = 0, when t_R = t; and v = k, when t_R = ∞t. In other words, the cinematic theory entails a finite, unreachable limit (asymptote), k, of all relative velocities, as in Einstein's Special theory of Relativity (see Figure 4).

As can be seen, then the cinematic model entails, at the macrophysical or observer level, two different times and therefore two different speeds for the motion of an object over a distance s. The one time is the relative time t_R as registered by the observer of the motion and the other is the proper time t registered over that distance by the object itself. The two speeds are therefore s/t_R and s/t, which we may call the Einsteinian and the Newtonian velocities (v and v_N), respectively.

The same applies to the transfer of physical action from one quantum still (stasis element) to another. As has been explained, the ultimate cinematic action-element is an event consisting of the immediate transfer of angular momentum in the form of action (the product of energy and time) from one discrete angular momentum system to another. In relative terms, that transfer takes place (according to the cinematic model) at the finite limit speed k. Meanwhile, the intrinsic time (proper time) t_action of that same transfer, regardless of distance, is always zero, as we see if, in (5), viz.:-

$$t_{\mathrm{R}} = t_{\mathrm{action}}[1 - (v^2/k^2)]^{-1/2} \tag{5}$$

we make v = k and rearrange the equation to express it in terms of the proper-time t of that action-transfer, which gives $$t_{\mathrm{action}} = t_{\mathrm{R}}[1 - k^2/k^2]^{1/2} = 0 \tag{7}$$

Now an action which is immediate and instantaneous obviously requires no mediation or conduction by any intervening agency, call it 'ether', 'field', 'photon' or whatever. In that case, the cinematic theory leads us to anticipate a direct connection between the energy emitted by a source A and absorbed by a sink B, which does not depend in any way on any intervening mechanism of the conventional kind. For instance, if we now take t in these equations as the irreducible, non-zero period t_ang,mon of the cycle in the ultimate quantum still, then it follows that since this cycle is complete and irreducible, all measures t_R in the equation are integral multiples n of that basic cycle. That is to say, t/t_R in this case, becomes 1/n, where n is the series of natural numbers, or integers. Substituting accordingly, in (6) and squaring that equation produces

$$v^2 = k^2[1 - (1/n^2)] \tag{8}$$

Now k is, of course, a constant, and the dimensions of k^2 are E/M, which means that masses and energies are interconvertible according to the formula

$$E = Mk^2. \tag{9}$$

This, of course, matches the E = Mc^2 formula of standard Relativity. Substituting E/M for k^2 in (8) gives:

$$v^2 = (E/M)[1 - (1/n^2)] \tag{10}$$

from which we have

$$Mv^2 = E[1 - (1/n^2)] \tag{11}$$

Substituting for the energy Mv^2 in this formula the symbol e then produces

$$e = E[1 - (1/n^2)] \tag{12}$$

In other words, whatever may be the value of E, for values of n approaching infinity, this formula approximates to e = E. Otherwise, at the quantum level, where these ns are Diophantine, or integral, the formula produces the series of fractions:

$$e = E[0; (3/4); (8/9); (15/16) \dots] \tag{13}$$

Moreover, dividing (13) by Planck's constant h expresses these values as fractions of frequency, ν. Other sets of fractions of the frequency E/h (which may be signified by ν_lun) may also be obtained by using n as the fixed term and including a running term n_1, as in the following equation:

$$\nu = \nu_{\text{linn}}[(1/n^2) - (1/n_1^2)] \tag{14}$$

which precisely matches the Balmer-Rydberg formula for the frequencies of the light-quanta observed in simple spectra. (As anticipated, this formula follows directly from our a priori time- and mass-energy equations, without in any way involving the usual concepts of mediatory electrostatics and field-electrodynamics.²).

Now in the quantum discretum there is, as already stated, no single overall angular momentum continuum. Everything exists in relation to everything else, not as an existential whole or 'universe' but only in discrete and complete angular momentum interrelations between individual pairs of fundamental masses. This means that the existence or permanence, as we experience it, of masses on the ordinary macroscale, is statistical (i.e., Heraclitan) not continuous. On the other hand, at the fine-grained, quantum level there is no such existential continuity, or permanence. For the observer, in ordinary macroscopic time, everything at the fundamental microphysical level is continually 'jiggling' in and out of existence.

At the ordinary physical level, however, there are statistical ensembles of quantum masses which, no matter how transient and dissociate the existences of their fundamental constituents, form relatively stable Heraclitan mass-configurations. These ensembles, which we call physical bodies, are paired and balanced in angular momentum interrelations in the same way that the fundamental particles are paired and balanced in angular momentum quanta h/2π. The only difference, we repeat, is that at the ordinary physical level the discreteness condition which prevents a quantum mass A which is in an angular momentum relation with B having a simultaneous angular momentum with C, D ... etc., no longer holds. That is to say, at the ordinary physical level, every body has a (statistically) continuous and balanced angular momentum interrelation with each and every other, forming those complexes of instantaneously balanced many-body interrelations of the sort we observe, for example, in the motions of planets and satellites. It is the moment-arms of these macroscopic angular momentum interconnections that define the macrophysical distances s, or space, in the quantum discretum.

What we have, then, as the basis of a description of the propagation of light-energy is, an angular momentum substratum, analogous to the classical 'ether', or 'field', in which the world is extended, and distance-separated objects are (statistically) interconnected. (That is to say, there is no question of the ultimate bits of things being localised, or Einstein-separated by the 'limiting speed of light'.

Now the distance between the source and screen in the two-slit apparatus is, of course, just such an instantaneous extension and interconnection. When, as we customarily describe it, a 'photon' is emitted from the source, a quantum of angular momentum has disappeared at a source-atom and appeared at a screen-atom, in a time t_action = 0 (see eqn. (7)). And when this intrinsic time, or proper-time, t = t_action is zero, then according to eqn. (5), so is t_R. The length s - or, rather, s/k - of that quantum jump in the angular momentum discretum is therefore that of pure distance-time, shown in Figure 2. In this pure distance-time of the observer the 'speed' s/(s/k) of that same quantum jump is the finite speed k (see also Figure 4).

In these observer-dimensions, then, the quantum, with its frequency given in (14) seems to 'travel' as a little single-cycle wave-packet ('photon') from source to screen along an extension which, in the customary, Einsteinian way of thinking would be regarded as 'superluminal'. When that transfer is uninterrupted it is, as Gilbert Lewis described it, a virtual 'touching' - that is an exchange of energy in accordance with Newton's third law of equal and opposite, instantaneous action and reaction. Otherwise, any intervening object or detector which effectively intervenes between the beginning and end of that macroscopic distance s appropriates to itself the entire quantum, thus casting its quantum 'shadow' on the screen at the point where the quantum flash would otherwise have appeared.

Now in the two-slit apparatus there are lengths s which, ideally, can be geometrically, traced from some point at the source, through the slits, to where they converge at the screen, and which, if they are not equal, differ by some odd or even number of half-lengths of the intrinsic wavelength λ which, from (14) is $$\lambda = \lambda_{\text{lim.}} / [(1/n^2) - (1/n_1^2)] \tag{15}$$

However, these macrophysical lengths s are not strictly determinate because, where everything at the microphysical level is 'jiggling' in and out of existence, on the macrophysical level there can be no exactly defined geometrical points at any instant and therefore no exactly defined distances between them. At the ordinary macrophysical level, then, everything is fuzzy and statistical, and at that statistical level the angular momentum discretum acts as any other pre-extended quasi-continuous medium, or field, for the conduction and propagation of energy between one body and another – as, for example, in a medium such as air or water. This is, typically, in the form of waves, which, in the discretum are now statistical numbers of proper-time-instantaneous cinematic quantum jumps which, as we have seen, in observer-time seem to 'travel' at the finite and constant rate k. The only difference is that in this case there is no interaction by means of these 'waves' as such. The interaction is purely in terms of the quanta, and the 'waves' simply define the probabilities of those quantum interactions in the manner described by de Broglie and Schrödinger.

There is, then, no interference, diffraction, diminution, entropy or whatever, in a single quantum jump. All these 'wave'-type phenomena are features of the probabilistic wave-function which, in the two-slit apparatus, extends, in its statistical, fuzzy-geometrical way, through the two slits at once. In that case, where the path-lengths between source and screen, through the slits, differ by some even number of units λ/2 the probability of a quantum appearing at that point on the screen approaches zero. By the same token, where the lengths either agree, or differ by some even number of units λ/2 the probability of a quantum of that wavelength λ appearing at that point is enhanced by the square of its statistical amplitude, as defined by the wave-function.

Now these interaction-statistics, which in all other respects are identical to the Schrödinger wave-function, are obviously not the sorts of things that can travel. (To think of statistics 'travelling' would be a howler of a category mistake!) They are simply a standing feature of the quantum discretum at some particular moment – as in the arrangements of the parts of the two-slit apparatus. As such, then, they do not not distinguish between the conduction of energies of wavelength h/mc of the so-called 'photons' and the de Broglie wavelengths h/mv of fundamental particles such as electrons, neutrons and even whole atoms. Predictably, then, where these statistics are interfered with, as in the placing of detectors and so on in the one path through the one slit or the other, the patterns of particle-strikes at the screen-end will be affected in the way that experiment attests.³

The two-slit apparatus can therefore be conceived by the cinematic analogy (hence with no question of 'Einstein separation'), as a Heraclitan whole in which the paths through the apparatus from source to screen via the two slits manifest themselves as instantaneous lengths which interfere geometrically in terms of congruent or incongruent half-wavelengths of the energies conducted along them. The propagation of energy along those paths is the same as in standard quantum wave-dynamics, with all the usual effects of interference, diffraction, reflection and so on but, of course, without the usual paradoxical 'EPR' implications of Einstein separation. This is because at the various stages in their motion these 'light-waves', particle-waves and so on are no longer purely prospective, or 'Dick Whittington-like' in seeking their destinations but are, as it were, anchored at both ends by those angular momentum interrelations which have traditionally been conceived as 'gravitational'. These 'waves' are thus envisaged, not as collections of free-moving and speculatively travelling 'photons' or wave-packets which forge their medium as they go, a conception which is alien to commonsense. Like all real waves, they are undulations of a substratum which is pre-extended like the surface of the water in the case of a water-wave or a wire in the case of a wave which is 'pinged' along it. With that same conceptual modification, the consequences of this cinematic theory are identical not only to Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity, as we have seen, but also to those of current quantum wave-dynamics.. What this cinematic model does, then, is to restore to physics that wholeness and interconnectedness of nature that was so much a feature of classical conception and which has been lost to modern physics due to the Einsteinian interpretation – or, rather, misinterpretation – of how light travels and how quantum connections are made. True, in the cinematic model, that wholeness, implied by the classical term 'universe' is a Heraclitan wholeness – the wholeness of a quantum discretum rather than that of an Eleatic continuum – but a wholeness, nevertheless.

DISCUSSION (bits taken from Blue Anchor notebook)

Difficulties relating to the problem of defining the quantum geodesic:-

Re. the REDSHIFT problem: In the two-slit apparatus the geodesics (tracks) are bent (at the slits). How does this fit – or not fit – the sausage-string (redshift) analogy? If the geodesic between the source-point and screen-point is split in this way, the according to the s.s. analogy there should be the same number of wavelengths (sausages) along both routes, hence no interference.

'Tunnelling': The cinematic model explains how an elementary particle can disappear in one place and appear some place else, as between one quantum angular-momentum still and the next. (customarily called 'tunnelling' – a category mistake). In that case, there is no geodesic of the particle as such. What is it, then, that is interrupted when something gets between the source and the screen? If an interaction has no geodesic, then how can it be interrupted? How can you interrupt a path that does not exist?

The answer to this last question is that although it is true that there is no geodesic of the particle as such, there is a connection between one quantum of angular momentum and another. That connection is via the action-quantum nh, which is, in Gilbert Lewis's terms tantamount to a physical touching. The (null) geodesic, in other words, is the 'touching'.
