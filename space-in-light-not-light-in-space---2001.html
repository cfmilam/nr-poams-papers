Space in Light, not Light in SPace Viv Pope Does it make any sense to think of the actions of objects on a cinema screen as enduring and moving about inthe way we see those objects do in the movie? Of course not, because it is a complete fallacy to assume that the informational elements out of which the phenomenon of motion is generated behave in the phenomenon in the same way that the objects do. Then does it make any more sense to think of the action-quanta, out of informational patterns of which we optically project physical space, time and motion as travelling and Znduring in that same physical space? ln other words, does it make any sense to think of the light on which all our perceptions of physical space are based as being just another object u*ong others in that same physical space? Would it not make more logical sense to think of the space (and the duration and the motion) of physical objects being in the light than of the

light as being in the space?

i

The basic data from which all our knowledge of the positions and motions of distant objects stems are bits of action, which, as we measure them, are quantum bursts of luminiferous energy, in the order of 10-rs joule, sustained for periods in the order of 10-16 seconds.

Einstein called these data'photons', which he envisaged as 'travelling' from one atom to another and from those atoms to ourselves in order to inform us of the presence of those atoms and the objects they constitute. Moreover, Einstein interpreted the measured ratio, c, of metres to seconds in the distance-time dimensions of this action, as a 'speed', namely, the 'speed of light'.

However, c cannot possibly be a speed in any true sense of the word, because a speetl, truly so called is that of an object, which differs with respect to differently movingobservers, which c eminently does not (witness the famous experiments of Michelson and Morley).

So Einstein's conception of c as the'speed'of physical objects called 'photons', which is constant not only for those'photons'with respect to the vacuum but also with respect to everything in that vacuum, whether relatively moving and stationary, creates a complete mystery.

Do the atonls, then, which are held to be the sources of those light-interactions, exist nowhere but in the light? No, because when those atoms are not transacting luminiferous energy in action-units ft they are taking part in reciprocally balancing angular momentum interrelations with other atoms, in units of bar-h, or h,2n.

Alternating between these angular momentum correlations in terms of bar-h and the action-jumps or time-discontinuities between them in terrns of ft, atomic interaction takes place like the action in a movie, which consists of quantum time-jumps between purely distance-extended frames, or 'stills'.

These inter-atomic transactions are not motions in the sense applied to masses in mechanical phenomena. They are altemating static and active quantum events, whose transport from A io B takes place purely in accordance with the law of conservation of angular momentum, which forbids that an atom may lose a quantum of angular momentum without that quantum appearing in some other atom somewhere else that is, instantly, regardless of distance measured on the macrophenomenal scale.

The only condition governing such transactions is resonance between the two parties to it in terms of thefrequency of the energy transacted. No r assumption of mediation, by travelling particles, fields or ethers is logically'necessary.
