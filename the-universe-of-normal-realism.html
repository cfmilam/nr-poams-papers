The 'Universe' of Normal Realism

N. Vivian Pope

The 'universe' of Normal Realism, such as it is (universe, pluriverse, polyverse or whatever), is an angular momentum discretum. This is an overall-conserved, fundamentally quantised, angular momentum nexus in which all free-moving bodies orbit one another in automatically paired and reciprocally balanced angular momentum cycles. This is in accordance with a given angular momentum equation which self-sufficiently explains free orbital motion without need of in vacuo 'gravitational' or other 'forces' [1]. Spin angular momentum is naturally included in this same equation with the consequence that the 'gravitational constant' G for orbiting non-spinning or slow-spinning bodies is different for orbiting bodies possessed of large amounts of spin [1].

Space, in and of itself, has no existence or dimensions. What we customarily call 'space' is defined in terms of distances r between bodies, where r is the radius of an orbit of angular momentum $mvr$, and where there is no angular momentum there is no space or distance between bodies. Nor is there any universal or 'cosmic' overall 'God's time', or time in space itself. Time – real or normal time – is a measure local to the point at which it is measured, being, like space, one of the inseparable dimensions of motion or of physical processes such as growth and decay. Without any such physical motion or process there is no time that it makes sense to speak of in Normal Realism.

This means that there is no way whatsoever of defining any set of measures or dimensions with respect to 'the universe' as a whole. That is to say, there is no meaningful way of describing anything as though viewed from everywhere all at once. All the measures, properties and dimensions of real things, such as mass, length and time, are relative to the observer – or, rather, to some point of perspective occupied by some piece of matter that may or may not be a person (see following). This is the same as in standard relativity but, of course, without Einstein's interpretation of the dimensional constant c as the 'velocity of light in vacuo' [2].

In Normal Realism, therefore, the three normal dimensions of space are primarily and fundamentally observer-centred; that is, point-centred, or polar, in accordance with Machian relativism. The classical grid-space, with its Cartesian coordinates, is no more than a secondary transform of these primary polar spaces, having no existence apart from them. That is to say, without reference to matter there can be nothing, not even empty space. In other words, without reference to real objects any talk of a 'void' is null-and-void.

The 'universe' of Normal Realism, therefore, is nothing like what is classically conceived as such; that is, the sort of monolithic, substantial Unity envisaged by the Eleatic philosophers after Parmenides [3]. It is more like a Universe of Discourse, a Community of Perspectives, at the discrete centres of which are always bits of matter of one kind or another, animal, vegetable or mineral, right down to the level of atoms and atomic particles. These centres are therefore, perhaps, more suitably called interagents, rather than 'observers', as in conventional expositions of Relativity [4].

In this Normalist description, then, of matter, space and time, the connection with Machian Phenomenalism is plain. Phenomena are the primary (a priori) realities, and the 'objective' or 'absolute' grid-space of classical, Cartesian science is no more than secondary (a posteriori). This grid-space has no reality of its own except as a set of geometrical projections between point-centres in a community of mutually interacting or intercommunicating perspectives.

The basic dimensions of Normal Realist physics are therefore measures made from some one or another of these interactional point-perspectives. These dimensions are therefore the usual angular measures, elevation, azimuth and range of ordinary commonsense perception. Needless to say, all these physical measures, including the position, velocity, mass, colour, constitution, shape, or whatever, of physical objects are relative to the observer or to other perspectival centres in the reference-frame of some designated observer. They are all, without exception, observational projections from direct information transacted in proper-time-instantaneous quantum bits between the various centres of perspective [5], be these occupied by observers like ourselves or by instruments – and anything that transacts information in these same quantum bits can be an instrument of observation, hence an 'observer' (or, rather, an interagent) in this relativistic sense.

The 'universe', then, of Normal Realism is neither the solid material unity, or 'One' of the Eleatic philosophers, nor the space-separated 'Many' of Democritan materialism. In fact it is not 'material' in either of those senses. It is more in the nature of what was conceived by that other Greek philosopher, Heraclitus (c. 544 – c. 483 BC), namely, logos as opposed to physis, where logos is more in the nature of language and communication than of matter [6]. And, like language – like any informational system, in fact – this logos is not a continuum, but a discretum consisting, like any communicational network, of a to-ing and fro-ing of strictly separated communications/interactions. (Imagine, for instance, how much information there would be in a meeting of Parliament if, without a Speaker to keep control, everyone started speaking together!)

Plainly, then, this logos is not a 'universe' but a pluriverse, or polyverse of interacting and intercommunicating discrete entities. These range from the simplest interagents, such as atoms, to the more complex communicators like ourselves and other organic beings who possess some measure of autonomy. In all cases, the fundamental unit or 'bit' of information, throughout, is the quantum of action (interaction). Unlike the classical 'universe', then, the logos of Normal Realism is both relative and quantised, in accordance with both Relativity Theory and Quantum Theory, in a unification of those two notoriously divided theories that has so far not been managed in any other way.
