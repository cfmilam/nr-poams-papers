POAMS: Farewell to Field

N. Vivian Pope

POAMS offers a unique solution to the Unified Field problem. By modifying Newton’s first and second laws of motion and Einstein’s second postulate, it dispenses with all in vacuo field-forces and processes traditionally conceived as ‘gravitational’. ‘electrostatic’, ‘magnetic’, ‘electromagnetic’ or whatever. In their place it provides a unified observational action-at-a-distance account of physical separation and motion in which bodies are automatically paired and balanced in overall-conserved and therefore instantly correlated angular momentum interrelations. POAMS is therefore not so much a theory as a non-theory. That is, its aim is to strip away from current physics its age-old accumulation of sheer theoretical clutter. This uncovers, beneath its traditional overlay, the purely logical, ahistorical structure of our knowledge of space, time and matter. It reveals that if only Pythagoras could have known what Römer discovered, many centuries later, that lengths are times in the constant ratio of units c, the sides of his rectangular space-triangles, divided by that constant would have become times and what relativists now call time-dilation could have been commonplace knowledge from the era of Pythagoras. POAMS also reveals how, during that same era, it would have been possible to deduce from that same Pythagorean source, the Balmer formula for the discrete lines of the light-spectrum and, from this, deduce a facsimile of the Bohr atom., thus logically condensing twenty-seven centuries of tortuous theoretical history into a single generation.

What is POAMS? It is the acronym used to identify a group of scientists who support the PopeOsborne Angular Momentum Synthesis. What is the aim of that group? To seek out and remove from physics some confusing and conflicting ideas which have accumulated over the centuries. Like what? Like, for instance, the invention of in vacuo ‘gravitational’, ‘electrostatic’ and ‘magnetic’ forces. POAMS solves the age-old problem of ‘unifying’ these by getting rid of them altogether. How? First, by repudiating Newton’s false assumption (called his First Law of Motion) that bodies in free motion travel in straight lines when, obviously, they don’t. How, then, do bodies in free motion move, according to POAMS? In orbits around one another of constant angular momentum. With Newton’s fictitious straight-line momentum mv replaced by angular momentum mvr, every freemoving body is automatically paired and balanced with each and every other without need of in vacuo forces.

What does that achieve? It enables us to simplify and clarify our understanding of the phenomenon of motion, which is central to the whole of physical science. * * * What about motion in Einstein’s Theory of Relativity? What does POAMS say about that? It corroborates what Einstein discovered about the time-dilational effect of relative motion, but from an independent point of view. What point of view is that? An ahistorical one, which starts with the theorem of Pythagoras rather than with the electrodynamic tradition of Faraday and Maxwell, on which Einstein based his theory. Explain! Pythagoras’ theorem shows how components of movement in the three dimensions of length, breadth and depth produce a resultant which is the square root of the sum of the squares of those orthogonal measures. If Pythagoras had known what Olaus Römer discovered, many centuries later, that observational distance is time in a constant ratio of units, c, then time-dilation could, logically, have been known all those centuries earlier. You mean if Pythagoras had discovered the constant speed of light, c? No, I mean if he had known just the bare fact that Römer discovered, which is that observational distances in space are times in that constant ratio of units, without jumping to the historic conclusion that it was due to the ‘velocity of light’, which has led physics so disastrously through the theoretical thicket that remains associated with Michelson and Morley and Faraday-Maxwell.. How would that discovery, if it could have been made by Pythagoras, have produced time-dilation? By adding that extra, fourth dimension to his famous three-dimensional theorem. Dividing all those length-components by the constant c, Pythagoras could have turned his geometrical three-dimensional triangle into a four-dimensional time-triangle, the resultant, or hypotenuse, of which, for a moving body, would then be precisely the timedilation that Einstein talked about. And this can be demonstrated mathematically? Absolutely. All it needs is to cash-out distances s in the Pythagorean formula in terms of its equivalent vtR, where v is the observational speed of a body and tR is the resultant (hypotenuse) of the observational components s and t of the Pythagorean triangle, where t is the time as read by the observer from the clock of the travelling body. Einstein’s historical formula then appears after a few lines of simple algebra. Again, what does that achieve? Another massive simplification and compression of our scientific history. It gets rid of all the traditional rigmarole about how light behaves in space, in the form of ‘electromagnetic radiation’ when we are not seeing it. It rids us of all the confusing talk

of travelling ‘waves’, ‘fields’, ‘wave-particles’ and so on, plus all the associated confusion connected with the Michelson-Morley experiment as to how the ‘velocity of light’ can be the same for relatively moving observers, all of which over-intellectualises modern physics and makes it the mystique that, for ordinary folk, it has so notoriously become. It seems, then, that POAMS cancels ‘electrodynamics’ altogether. Isn’t that a bit drastic? Without these travelling interconnections, how can we think of bodies as interacting with one another? By what some physicists call action-at-a-distance. In POAMS, the law of conservation of angular momentum is sufficient in itself to ensure the balanced motions of bodies. That same law ensures that any disturbance of the angular momentum in one locality must be compensated immediately by an equal disturbance somewhere else in the system, where the distance-time conditions for direct interresonance are just right. All the observational manifestations of distant interaction between atoms are therefore exactly the same as for traditional wave-theory, but without the fictitious and inscrutable ‘waves in vacuo’ and their associated ‘wave-conducting medium’ – and, of course, without any ‘velocity’ of either ‘waves’ or ‘particles’. Are all such atomic interactions, then, instantaneous? Intrinsically, yes – in their own proper-time, that is. In relative time they take place at the observationally delayed-time rate c’ which is like the sequence-rate of the photographic, instantaneously extended ‘stills’ in cinematography. All this is shown to be a direct consequence of Pythagoras’ theorem. * * * What about Quantum Theory? How does this fit into the POAMS picture? In POAMS, any changes in angular momentum are transacted between one locality and another in ultimate units of Planck’s quantum h, of frequency 1/tR, where tR is the observational transit-time (resultant time) of the Pythagorean time-equation. Expressing tR in terms of integer multiples n of some basic unit t0 creates, by means of the formula, what might be called a relativistic ‘quantum accumulator’. This observational accumulator, or absorber/emitter of light-energy, gains and loses energy in quantum amounts, with frequencies that are precisely those of the spectrum as studied and classified by Johann Balmer. It also corresponds to what, in traditional ‘electrodynamical’ terms (unnecessary for POAMS purposes) was envisaged by Niels Bohr and his followers, as the ‘atom’. But in Bohr’s atom there is a nuclear proton and an orbiting electron. Are you saying that Pythagoras’ theorem produces these, also? By implication, yes, but only as plain masses and countermasses, according to the definition of angular momentum, not as the traditionally conceived ‘charged’ particles. So you are saying that Pythagoras’ theorem, which POAMS places at the root of all this, is not only relativistic but is also quantised – as well as containing all we know about atoms. In principle, yes. Is it credible, though, that such a simple formula can achieve so much?

Why not? That Pythagorean formula, as POAMS presents it, in its angular momentum context, contains all the basic dimensions of physical phenomena, including mass, length and time. So why should so much not be deducible from it? But the triangle to which that formula applies is usually thought of as having straight sides, but in POAMS, these ‘sides’ are orbits. How can this be visualised? In the academic world, geometry and mechanics have evolved as separate subjects. However, in the real world, Geometrics and Mechanics are inseparable. For instance, the straight lines and 180º sum of the angles of the Pythagorean triangle are fictions in two senses. In the first place, other than in any mathematician’s ‘God’s-eyeview’ depiction of the triangle, any measurement of the distance between those points (events) involves motion of some kind; and in the second place, the ‘straight lines’ envisaged by the geometers, such as Pythagoras and Euclid – and Newton, as already stated – were never more than idealisations of motion in circles of infinite radius – of infinite angular momentum. If Pythagoras had only known it, in reality, all the lengths measured between the points of his triangle are curves, being, in the ultimate microphysical analysis, the angular momentum orbits of the idealised Bohr atom in the way POAMS describes. * * * What does POAMS say about the fact of the ‘expanding universe’? It points out that this is not a fact but an inference. What is actually observed is that the spectra of the far galaxies are red-shifted in proportion to their distance. There is more than just one way of interpreting that. POAMS sees it as a reductio ad absurdum of the ‘expansion’ interpretation that it implies that some thirteen billion years ago, the universe was crammed into a space the size of a nut, which all but the most committed advocates of the theory regard as nonsensical. Does POAMS offer an alternative explanation, then, of the Hubble redshift? Yes. First, though, let’s take the case of a local angular momentum system, such as that of our sun and its planets. It is well known that the bodies nearest the system’s centre of mass travel fastest relatively to those that are further away. As a result, the nearer those bodies are to the centre of mass, the more dilated are their times relative to the remainder of the system. In other words, the nearer a clock approaches the centre of mass of an angular momentum system, the faster it travels and the slower it goes, due to time-dilation relatively to the system … The same as in General Relativity, as verified in the experiment by Robert Pound and Glen Rebka, in 1960. But how does that apply to the cosmological redshift? In this way: in a non-local angular momentum system the largest ‘centre of mass’ is, in general, the accumulated mass of the whole system of stars, galaxies and whatever, that lie always at the furthest distance from the observer – in all directions. And if it is true that clocks run slower as they approach the main mass of the system, then the further away from us the distant galaxies are, the nearer they will be to that accumulated mass, hence the slower will be their atomic processes as observed in spectroscopes here on earth – or, indeed, from anywhere at a comparably great distance from them. So you are saying that POAMS interprets the Hubble redshift as a time-dilational effect, increasing with distance in a steady-state universe?
