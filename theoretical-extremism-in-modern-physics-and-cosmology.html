Theoretical Extremism in Modern Physics and Cosmology

N. Vivian Pope

When physical science reaches a stage of stultification, as it has on so many occasions throughout its history, it is a fallacy to assume that the only significant next advance must be made by authorities from within that failing system, which is like trying to lift itself by its own braces. It is scarcely surprising, then, that whenever this happens it takes an outsider, like a Copernicus or a Galileo, often at great personal risk, to put science back on track towards an unimpeded natural advancement.

Many believe that our contemporary Physics and Cosmology are now in that moribund state, with their insoluble anomalies and theoretically overweighted attempts to answer them, which has led to the call by no less than a representative of NASA for a possible 'New Physics'. The trouble is that, given the situation as it stands, there is no way of introducing anything radically new into the system because the editors of leading scientific journals publish only suggestions commissioned and presented from within the extant scientific authority. However, truly radical, revolutionary alternatives of the effective 'Copernican' kind cannot be commissioned. They have to appear spontaneously from 'out of the blue', as it were.

Dissatisfaction with the present system is amply manifest nowadays by the burgeoning of so many anti-mainstream fringe publications and forums among which there is no clue, far less any consensus view as to how to proceed. Besides, what possible Olympian Super-Authority can there be for choosing which, if any, of the proposed solutions is to be accepted as the satisfactory new way forward? With no such higher authority as a reference, confusion reigns supreme.

In view of all this, the difficulty of finding a logical alternative to our present faltering Physics seems insurmountable. To what authority, then, might we turn to judge which is the best solution? What else but the authority of nature itself. This means, not more theory but less theory, placing more stress on the plainest most logical, least theoretical interpretations of natural phenomena. And how is this to be achieved other than by strength of argument. Certainly it cannot be by appeals to extant authority.

But what is meant by 'strength of argument'? Socrates gives us an example of this by what is known as the Socratic method. This is the process of the logical elimination of bum theories. For instance, in a symposium, when the question is raised as to what Justice is, one member advances the hypothesis that it is always to tell the truth. Socrates then asks this proposer whether, if one of their friends dashed in, pursued by brigands, when asked where their victim was, we should say: 'We cannot lie, he's in the cupboard.' This could scarcely be regarded as justice, so ideally, this hypothesis should then be abandoned with alacrity, its proposer thankful to have been disabused of his erroneous hypothesis.

This is the way science once operated, that is, by the method of the logical elimination of failed theories. Not nowadays, it seems! Instead, theories are evaluated and treasured on the basis of their own 'aesthetic' quality and/or their appeal to a 'Gee Whizz' audience ravenous for scientific novelty.

A perfect example of this is the popular theory of the Big Bang. If Socrates were alive now he would condemn this bizarre theory as no more than a reductio ad absurdum of the false interpretation of the Hubble redshift as a recession of the galaxies. He would point out the fallacy in assuming that because all receding light-sources are redshifted, seeing a light-source redshifted is the same as seeing that source receding, which is like saying that because all dogs are quadrupeds, seeing a quadruped is the same as seeing a dog.

These thinkers would then have to suggest some other interpretation of the Hubble redshift, which had no such absurd 'expanding universe' implication, and that would be then analysed in the same logical way. Otherwise, to make that absurdity a positive conclusion and stick to it would have stopped the dialogue in its tracks.

If this had been the modus operandi of the time, then nowadays we would surely not have had any science to speak of. Since this is so obvious, how is it that this unearthing of truth by the logical elimination of error has been so plainly abandoned? This is a question we leave to the sociologists.

From the truly scientific point of view, it has to be realised that this 'recessional' interpretation of the Hubble redshift is not logically obligatory, that all other possible alternatives to this nonsensical assumption of an 'expanding universe' have been simply sidelined in favour of the Big Bang dogma and its entourage of supporting assumptions. These other possible alternatives are there for the taking on the Internet, by anyone whose rational vision is not dazzled by the publicity given more and more to the Theoretical Extremists. All one has to do is to take the trouble to look for such alternatives and to exercise one's own free logical judgement regarding them.

Neville Vivian (Viv) Pope
